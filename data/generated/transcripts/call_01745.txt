Deja Shields (CMO): That's fair. We liked the capabilities. The price was the problem, or at least the way the price was presented. For a small education organization, every recurring line item gets questioned.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Was the issue the total annual amount, the initial implementation, or uncertainty about how the price would grow?

Deja Shields (CMO): All three came up. Our finance director said the package was too expensive for the number of campaigns we send. Our marketing manager looked at the contact count and said we could not afford to pay for people who might never receive an email.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds like one objection, but perhaps there are two different calculations underneath it.

Deja Shields (CMO): Exactly. Finance was looking at the budget. Marketing was looking at the database. Those are not the same conversation, though they both ended with, “This is too expensive.”

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Let's unpack the database question. How many contacts do you actively market to?

Deja Shields (CMO): Around twelve thousand engaged prospects and alumni, depending on the program. The full database is closer to thirty-five thousand because it includes old inquiries, parents, event attendees, and people who have unsubscribed.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): And the commercial proposal used the full database?

Deja Shields (CMO): It used a contact tier that covered most of it. That's where confusion started. The proposal referred to stored contacts, while the product conversation referred to active marketing contacts. People assumed every stored record would be billable as an active audience.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Did the team have a chance to review how archived or non-marketing records would be handled?

Deja Shields (CMO): Not clearly enough. Our operations person asked twice and got two slightly different explanations. Once confidence drops, the finance team assumes the highest possible bill.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I understand. Besides price, what did you need the system to do?

Deja Shields (CMO): Enrollment journeys for prospective students, event follow-up, and some basic scoring. We also wanted a better handoff to admissions. Braze looked flexible, but it seemed oriented toward teams with more technical resources.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How large is the team that would administer it?

Deja Shields (CMO): Two people regularly, with occasional help from IT. We cannot maintain a complicated data model or a dozen custom integrations.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would a smaller pilot have changed the decision, or was the approved budget fixed?

Deja Shields (CMO): The budget was fixed for this fiscal year. A pilot might have helped with confidence, but it would not have made an unaffordable recurring amount affordable.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That's an important distinction. If the package were reduced to the active audience and the implementation phased, would finance reconsider?

Deja Shields (CMO): Possibly, but only if the active-contact definition were written into the order and the upgrade path were predictable. We have been burned by vendors saying, “You can clean the database later,” and then charging us for the mess.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would count as a clean definition for you?

Deja Shields (CMO): A stored contact is just a record we retain. An active marketing contact is someone eligible to receive marketing communication under the subscription rules. We need examples: unsubscribed alumni, duplicate parents, and people in admissions review.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is clear. Were there any concerns unrelated to cost?

Deja Shields (CMO): Reporting. The board wants to know which programs create applications, but our admissions system and marketing system don't share a perfect identifier. We did not want to buy automation and then argue over attribution every month.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): So there was a data confidence issue as well.

Deja Shields (CMO): Yes, but it was secondary. If the price had been obviously within range, we would have worked through the reporting problem. Once the price looked risky, every unresolved detail became evidence against buying.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That makes sense. Did the sales process make the difference between affordability and packaging clear enough?

Deja Shields (CMO): No. To be candid, the phrase “too expensive” got repeated because it was easy. The finance objection was inability to afford the proposed commitment. The marketing objection was confusion about whether we were paying for inactive records. They should not have been treated as the same signal.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I appreciate you saying that. If we revisit later, who would need to be involved?

Deja Shields (CMO): Finance, marketing operations, and admissions. Bring a one-page pricing model, not a generic presentation. Show the contact categories and exactly what happens when a record moves from stored to active.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): And implementation?

Deja Shields (CMO): A phased scope: one prospective-student journey, one event journey, and a basic admissions handoff. If those work, we can expand. No promise that we will reopen this year.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. I will record the feedback and send a concise explanation of the packaging distinction, without assuming a new evaluation.

Deja Shields (CMO): That would be useful. The loss was not simply “small business cannot pay.” We could pay for the right shape of solution; we could not justify the ambiguity around the proposed one.
