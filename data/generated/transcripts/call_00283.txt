Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Hi Jamison, thanks for joining the QBR. How are things at Murphy, Blanda and Christiansen?

Jamison Weimann (Marketing Operations): Steady, mostly. We are coming out of a manufacturing conference season, so the team is tired but we have a decent pile of leads.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That sounds familiar. I would like to review adoption, lead flow, campaign performance, and priorities for the next quarter. Since you are still evaluating marketing automation options and HubSpot is in the conversation, we can also talk about where the current process is helping or falling short.

Jamison Weimann (Marketing Operations): That's fair. The important thing is that we are not looking for another dashboard that nobody opens.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. How often are people using the current workspace?

Jamison Weimann (Marketing Operations): Marketing operations uses it every day. The broader marketing team logs in around campaign launches. Sales uses the alerts, but they don't consistently review the underlying activity.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): So operational usage is strong, while cross-functional visibility is mixed.

Jamison Weimann (Marketing Operations): Yes, although some of that is intentional. Sales reps don't need every webinar click. They need a useful signal tied to an account or a real request.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What signals have been most useful?

Jamison Weimann (Marketing Operations): Form submissions, pricing-page visits when the person is known, and repeat engagement from an existing account. Generic content downloads are less helpful. A junior engineer downloading a safety guide does not necessarily mean the plant is buying anything.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That distinction is valuable. Have you adjusted scoring to reflect it?

Jamison Weimann (Marketing Operations): We adjusted weights, but the model is still noisy. Conference scans are especially problematic because one person can scan several booths and then look highly active.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That could be a good candidate for an exclusion or a decay rule. What happens after an alert is sent?

Jamison Weimann (Marketing Operations): Depends on the rep. Some follow up the same day. Others wait until their next account review. We have no consistent SLA because our sales process differs by region.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would a tiered alert help—urgent for a direct request, digest for lower-intent activity?

Jamison Weimann (Marketing Operations): Probably. But I would want the regional sales leaders involved before changing the workflow. In the Nordics, sales teams are smaller and prefer fewer notifications.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Makes sense. On campaigns, what has performed best this quarter?

Jamison Weimann (Marketing Operations): The customer maintenance reminder had a strong reply rate, but it was sent to a narrow audience. The broad product newsletter had more clicks and fewer conversations. If you only look at click-through, the newsletter wins; if you look at opportunities, the reminder wins.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): So the metric choice changes the conclusion.

Jamison Weimann (Marketing Operations): Completely. Also, one opportunity came from a contact who had almost no measurable digital activity. The rep had a relationship from an earlier plant visit. That lead would look cold in an automated report.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That's an important counterpoint to treating engagement score as a complete buying-intent measure. How are you handling offline activity?

Jamison Weimann (Marketing Operations): Reps can log notes, but adoption is uneven. We are considering a simpler account-level field for recent conversations instead of asking them to record every detail.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That sounds practical. We can help design a workflow that combines marketing activity with rep-entered account context. Would you like to test that before comparing platforms?

Jamison Weimann (Marketing Operations): Yes, but I need the test to be small. Our team is 110 people across operations and production, and marketing is not going to run a six-week science project.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can use two regions, one campaign, and a limited alert rule. What would success look like?

Jamison Weimann (Marketing Operations): Fewer irrelevant alerts, faster follow-up on direct requests, and a report that sales leaders can understand without a training session.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): And if the test does not improve those outcomes?

Jamison Weimann (Marketing Operations): Then we should not expand it just because we already invested time. HubSpot may still be simpler for some teams, and simplicity has value.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Agreed. I will send a proposed test plan with baseline metrics and a review date. Are there any data-quality issues we should include?

Jamison Weimann (Marketing Operations): Duplicate accounts, old contact roles, and inconsistent plant names. Also, some records are shared across business units, so ownership is not always obvious.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I'll include those as measurement constraints. Anything you need from me now?

Jamison Weimann (Marketing Operations): Just the plan, plus a clear explanation of what the comparison is meant to prove. We are still prospecting internally, so there is no decision yet.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. I'll avoid framing it as a predetermined platform decision. Thanks for the candid discussion.

Jamison Weimann (Marketing Operations): Thanks, Amelia. Send the draft and I'll get comments from the regional leads.
