Priya Shah (Account Executive): Carlo, thanks for taking the call. I understand you are evaluating automation for Lebsack Inc, the 126-person education organization in the Nordics. Before we discuss products, what is happening in your lifecycle programs today?

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): We have several programs, but they are mostly manual. A student registers for an open day, someone exports the list, and then we send a reminder from a different tool. It works, but the handoffs are slow.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Which audience matters most?

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): Prospective students first. We also communicate with teachers and existing learners, but the open-day and application journeys are where we feel the gaps.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How many contacts are actively marketed to?

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): Around 900, although the database has more like 3,500. Many are old inquiries or people who attended one event years ago. We do not want to message everyone just because they are stored.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is helpful. What would a successful first journey look like?

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): Registration confirmation immediately, reminders based on the event date, a follow-up survey, and then different content depending on whether the person attended. If they request an application guide, admissions should get a task.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can build that with date-based triggers and attendance data. How is attendance captured?

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): The events team scans a QR code, but the result is uploaded the next day. So “attended” is not real time. There is also a small percentage of walk-ins who are recorded by hand.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then the reminder journey should not assume attendance data is immediate. We can send the follow-up based on registration first, then update the branch when the file arrives.

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): That would be better. I do not want a person marked as absent just because the upload was late.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What are you using now?

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): A CRM, a form platform, and email software. Eloqua is on the shortlist because our parent organization knows it. We are also looking at a lower-cost option.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What will determine the choice?

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): Ease of building, reliable segmentation, and reporting. I need to see which source produced an application, not just how many emails were opened.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How much reporting does leadership expect?

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): They want enrollment contribution. I can give them engagement and conversion rates, but the CRM opportunity—or application—stages are not consistently maintained. That makes attribution difficult.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We should include a data-quality check before promising a full attribution model. Which fields are most reliable?

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): Event registration and email address. Source campaign is mixed. Program interest is better for new forms than old records.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then the pilot should use registration and event date as the stable foundation. We can document the attribution limitations and improve source capture going forward.

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): Yes. Another concern is cost. The proposal is €10,810 annually, which is not outrageous, but we are an SMB and our budget is tight. If the implementation is complicated, we will probably choose the cheaper tool.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Is the recurring fee itself outside the budget, or is the uncertainty about implementation effort the bigger issue?

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): The recurring fee is manageable if we can prove time savings. A large setup fee is harder. We do not have an automation specialist, so I need to know how much I will personally have to maintain.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can scope a small implementation around the open-day journey, train you and one admissions colleague, and leave broader programs for later. How often would you expect to change the journey?

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): Weekly during the event season. I need to change dates and content without opening a support ticket every time.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is exactly the kind of workflow we should demonstrate. We can build a date change, preview the branches, and show the approval process.

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): Please include testing. We have accidentally sent a reminder with the wrong event date before.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can use a test audience, require approval, and add a date validation checklist. Do you need multilingual content?

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): Finnish and English for the first phase. Possibly Swedish later. Translation is handled by our team, so the system just needs to support the variants cleanly.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): No problem. Would admissions need access to build journeys?

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): No, but they need visibility into requested application guides and urgent replies. I want marketing to control the workflow while admissions receives actionable tasks.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can separate those permissions. What would prevent you from moving forward after a successful demo?

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): If the contact pricing counts all 3,500 records, or if the data connection requires custom development, that could stop it. Also, Eloqua's familiarity with our parent organization gives it credibility, even if it is not the easiest tool for my team.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will prepare an active-contact calculation, a no-code integration outline, and a pilot plan with measurable time-saving targets. We should also invite someone from admissions to validate the task handoff.

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): Good. I can bring admissions next week. I am interested, but I do not want to confuse interest with a decision yet.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That's fair. We will use the next session to test the actual workflow and leave the unresolved data and budget questions visible.

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): Perfect. If the journey is easy to maintain and the reports are honest, we may have a case. If it only produces prettier emails, we do not.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Agreed. Thanks, Carlo. I will send the agenda and the contact-model worksheet today.

Carlo Lesch (Lifecycle Marketing): Thanks, Priya. Talk next week.
