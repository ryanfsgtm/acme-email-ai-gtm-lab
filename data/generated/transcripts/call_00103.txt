Monte Romaguera (Sales Operations): We are involved because marketing keeps handing us lists that are not ready. Sales reps get duplicates, old contacts, and sometimes leads assigned to the wrong region. Then sales ops spends the afternoon cleaning it up.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How often does that happen?

Monte Romaguera (Sales Operations): Every campaign cycle. Not always badly, but enough that nobody trusts the handoff. We have around twelve sales regions, and the rules are different for distributors versus direct accounts.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What happens after a form fill today?

Monte Romaguera (Sales Operations): It enters the CRM, gets a score from a basic rule, and then sits in a queue. A coordinator checks it manually. If the company is unfamiliar, we search the website and decide whether it belongs to sales or nurture.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would you want automated first?

Monte Romaguera (Sales Operations): Deduplication, territory routing, and a clear reason for the score. We do not need a flashy journey builder on day one. If the data is wrong, more automation just makes wrong things happen faster.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is a sensible priority. Do marketing and sales agree on the qualification definition?

Monte Romaguera (Sales Operations): Not really. Marketing says engagement means readiness. Sales says a person can download six documents and still have no project. We have argued about this for months.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Could you define a handoff threshold based on both engagement and account fit?

Monte Romaguera (Sales Operations): Possibly. But account fit is complicated. A small distributor can be worth more than a large manufacturer if it covers the right territory. Employee count is not enough.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What data would be needed?

Monte Romaguera (Sales Operations): Industry, country, channel type, existing customer status, and whether there is an active equipment program. Some of those fields are free text. That is the real obstacle.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Would sales operations own the data model?

Monte Romaguera (Sales Operations): We would own the rules, but IT owns the integrations. Marketing owns the forms. Nobody wants to own the messy fields.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): If a platform could enforce required values on forms and synchronise the territory rules, would that address the main concern?

Monte Romaguera (Sales Operations): It could address part of it. We would still need to test the sync with the ERP. Product names and account IDs do not line up between systems.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What is the ERP?

Monte Romaguera (Sales Operations): An older Dynamics installation with several custom tables. I am not saying integration is impossible, but it will not be a weekend project.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How would you measure success after a pilot?

Monte Romaguera (Sales Operations): Fewer duplicate records, fewer reassigned leads, and less time spent checking lists. I would also want reps to understand why a lead arrived. A mysterious score will be ignored.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Would a two-region pilot be realistic?

Monte Romaguera (Sales Operations): Maybe Sweden and Germany. They have different routing rules, which is useful for testing, but the local teams are already overloaded.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Who else needs to participate?

Monte Romaguera (Sales Operations): Marketing operations, one IT integration person, and a sales manager from each region. Procurement comes in later. Security will ask about data residency, especially for European contacts.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Is there a budget or deadline attached to the evaluation?

Monte Romaguera (Sales Operations): There is a budget discussion for next year, but no approved project. The current request is to document options and estimate effort.
