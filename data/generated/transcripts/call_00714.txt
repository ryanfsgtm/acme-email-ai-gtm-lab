Priya Shah (Account Executive): Aylin, thank you for taking the time. I know Kunde LLC has already evaluated a few approaches, so I would like to understand what matters most before we talk about capabilities.

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): That is appreciated. We are a 298-person education company, and our marketing team is small. We need better automation, but I am wary of buying a system that creates another full-time job.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What is the pressure behind the evaluation?

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): Growth has made our process messy. We run enrollment campaigns, professional-learning webinars, and partner programs. The campaigns work individually, but we cannot consistently tell leadership which activities influenced applications or revenue.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): So attribution and reporting are central?

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): Central for me and the executive team, yes. I need a reliable view of pipeline, applications, and campaign influence. My marketing operations manager will ask a different set of questions: can she build a journey quickly, test it, and change a branch without waiting three weeks for IT?

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That distinction is important. What does she use today?

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): Several tools stitched together. She builds emails in one place, pulls audiences from the CRM, and checks webinar attendance in another application. She spends too much time reconciling counts.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): And what does that mean for campaign speed?

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): A simple nurture can take days because every change requires checking three systems. A complex journey can take weeks. The team has become cautious, which protects us from mistakes but also means we miss timely opportunities.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How does leadership currently review performance?

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): Monthly spreadsheets and a dashboard that nobody fully trusts. We can show opens, clicks, and registrations. We cannot explain how a person moved from a webinar to an application or how multiple campaigns contributed.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would a trustworthy report include?

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): Definitions first. Then source, campaign touch, stage movement, and a reasonable influence model. I do not need a magic number; I need a model that we can explain and use consistently.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds like a shared reporting framework rather than a single dashboard.

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): Correct. And it needs executive-level summaries as well as operational detail. If the report is only for me, it will not survive budget season.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would your marketing operations manager need to see in a workflow?

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): Entry criteria, branching logic, suppression rules, test results, and a clear history of who changed what. She wants speed, but not mystery. The worst outcome is a fast journey that sends the wrong message to the wrong audience.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Can you describe a recent example?

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): We had a professional-learning series where attendees were supposed to leave the prospect nurture after registration. The registration feed lagged, so a few people received both the confirmation follow-up and the invitation they had already acted on. It was corrected, but the team now manually checks every launch.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would automated testing and visibility into event timing address that concern?

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): Partly. The process also needs ownership. If marketing operations owns the journey but IT owns the integration and admissions owns the application status, someone must own the end-to-end outcome.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Who would need to participate in a decision?

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): Me, marketing operations, IT, and someone from admissions. The CFO will care about attribution and cost. The admissions director will care about whether the process treats applicants appropriately.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would the team be willing to start with one program?

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): Yes, probably professional learning. It has a manageable audience and a clear event-to-application path. We should not attempt every enrollment journey at once.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would success look like after the first phase?

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): Faster journey changes, fewer manual audience checks, a tested handoff from registration to follow-up, and a report leadership accepts. If we cannot demonstrate those, expansion will be difficult.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What concerns remain besides implementation effort?

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): Cost, of course, and whether the platform is flexible enough for education-specific consent and application statuses. Also, our operations manager is excellent but overloaded. Any plan that assumes she can attend six hours of training every week is not realistic.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can design shorter working sessions around the first program and document repeatable patterns. I would also suggest a separate executive reporting session so the business case and the operator workflow are both addressed.

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): Yes. Those are different conversations. Executives ask whether the investment produces measurable growth. Operators ask whether Tuesday's campaign can be fixed before lunch.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a useful way to frame it. I will prepare a proposal with the phased program, reporting model, testing approach, integration responsibilities, and estimated internal effort.

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): Include a risk register. We have had vendors show the happy path and leave the awkward data timing until implementation.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will include data latency, consent, ownership, and adoption risks. Would you be able to provide a sample campaign and anonymized stage definitions?

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): Yes, though admissions will need to approve the definitions. We lost the last project partly because marketing and admissions used the same words differently.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then we should validate the vocabulary before building the report.

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): Agreed. I want the team to leave the workshop with decisions, not just screenshots.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will send a proposed agenda and two scheduling options. Thank you, Aylin.

Aylin Treutel (VP Marketing): Thank you. I am interested, but we need to be disciplined. A polished demo will not win this on its own.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. The proof should be in the working process and the numbers leadership can defend.
