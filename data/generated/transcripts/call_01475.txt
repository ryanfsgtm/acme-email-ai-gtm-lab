Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Corbin, thanks for making time. I know this is an early conversation for Hegmann Group, so I would rather learn how your team works than assume a package is the answer.

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): That is appreciated. We are a 75-person media company, and our marketing process has grown in pieces. We have a newsletter tool, forms on the website, and a CRM that sales uses inconsistently.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What is the pain you feel most often?

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): We cannot connect audience interest to a useful next action. Someone downloads a report, somebody else attends a webinar, and the information sits in separate lists. The team knows there is interest, but not who should respond.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Is the priority revenue generation, audience engagement, or operational efficiency?

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): Revenue generation is the executive language, but I would start with operational clarity. If we promise a revenue lift before fixing the handoff, people will stop trusting the program.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is a thoughtful starting point. How does a handoff happen today?

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): Usually an editor emails a salesperson and says, “This person downloaded the thing.” Sometimes the salesperson follows up. Sometimes they are already dealing with that account and do not need a generic alert.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): So account context matters more than a single activity.

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): Exactly. A subscriber can open five newsletters because they like the writing, not because they want a sales call.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can distinguish content engagement from commercial intent using multiple signals and account attributes. It would still need calibration with sales.

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): Sales will resist another scoring model if nobody explains it. We have had dashboards where a contact was “hot” for reasons no one could see.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then transparency should be a requirement. Each score can show the activities and rules contributing to it, and we can start with a simple model.

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): Good. What would integration look like with our CRM?

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We would map contacts, accounts, owners, and relevant opportunity fields. I would want to understand duplicate handling before discussing automation.

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): Duplicates are common. Our newsroom collects one email address, events collect another, and company names are entered in different formats.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is a significant implementation issue. We can establish matching rules and an exception queue, but the system cannot safely decide every duplicate without review.

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): I would rather hear that now. Can it preserve separate subscriptions? Our audience newsletter and commercial webinar invitations are not the same thing.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Yes, with subscription categories and preference controls. Someone can opt out of commercial invitations while continuing the editorial newsletter, depending on your consent language.

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): Legal is revising that language. We should not automate the old consent field until they finish.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Agreed. We can design the process with a consent gate and use test records during evaluation.

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): Our board meeting is in six weeks. I would like a credible plan before then, but not a rushed implementation.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would you want to show the board?

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): A current-state map, a proposed lead handoff, and a measurement plan. Maybe one example showing how a webinar attendee becomes a reviewed sales follow-up rather than an automatic alert.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can prepare that. Would sales and editorial participate in the next session?

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): Sales, yes. Editorial may send someone, but they will care about preserving the reader relationship. They do not want every interaction treated as a sales signal.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is a useful counterbalance. We can define separate tracks for editorial engagement and commercial interest, with an explicit transition between them.

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): Please do. Another issue is geography. We work mainly in France and Belgium, but some campaigns go to English-speaking markets.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can segment by language and region, assuming those fields are reliable. Are they?

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): Language is decent. Region is less so. People move, and the CRM is not always updated.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then region should not be the only basis for a critical send. We can add a review step for ambiguous records.

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): That kind of caution will help me get buy-in. What does the commercial side look like at this stage?

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): The opportunity is still discovery, with no competitor selected and a planning close date of May 31 next year. I would treat that date as a placeholder until requirements and ownership are clear.

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): Yes, it is definitely a placeholder. We are not asking for a quote today.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. I can send a discovery recap and a proposed ninety-minute workshop. We can cover data sources, consent, duplicate policy, scoring, and reporting.

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): Ninety minutes may be difficult. Could we do two shorter sessions? One with marketing and one with sales?

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That may be better. The marketing session can cover audience and consent; the sales session can cover routing and follow-up. Then we combine the findings.

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): Great. Include our CRM administrator in both if possible.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will. Before we close, what would make you decide not to proceed?

Corbin Kuphal (CMO): If implementation requires constant manual maintenance, or if the scoring cannot be explained, or if it harms the editorial relationship. A prettier dashboard is not enough.
