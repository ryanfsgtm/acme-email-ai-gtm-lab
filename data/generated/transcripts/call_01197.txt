Eleanore Schroeder-Howe (Implementation Partner): Exactly. We wanted templates, approval steps, and a way to reuse audiences. The sales presentation focused heavily on automation, and I’m not sure our team was ready for the amount of process change behind it.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Can you say more about that process change?

Eleanore Schroeder-Howe (Implementation Partner): Someone has to define the audience, someone has to approve the message, and someone has to handle unsubscribes and exceptions. In the demo it looked simple. In practice, our store managers would have needed clear rules or they would have worked around the system.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Did you have a central marketing owner?

Eleanore Schroeder-Howe (Implementation Partner): Not really. There’s one person who coordinates promotions, but no dedicated marketing operations role. I would have been the implementation partner, and then each region would have supplied content.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That sounds like a potential adoption risk. Were there technical blockers as well?

Eleanore Schroeder-Howe (Implementation Partner): Nothing that killed the project. We had questions about connecting the point-of-sale data and keeping customer preferences current. The bigger blocker was ownership. We could have connected things technically, but nobody had a week-by-week plan for maintaining it.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Did the project have an internal deadline?

Eleanore Schroeder-Howe (Implementation Partner): We originally wanted it live before the holiday campaign. By the time the evaluation got serious, that deadline was too close. Then the budget review happened, and the urgency disappeared.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): If the budget had stayed available, would you have proceeded?

Eleanore Schroeder-Howe (Implementation Partner): That store teams could use approved templates without creating duplicate campaigns, and that the customer preference data would stay accurate. We also wanted reporting that a regional manager could understand without asking an analyst for help.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Were those requirements documented?

Eleanore Schroeder-Howe (Implementation Partner): Some were in the evaluation notes, but not in a formal success plan. That was part of the problem. Different people had different ideas about what “success” meant.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): And changing that would have required more than purchasing software.

Eleanore Schroeder-Howe (Implementation Partner): Much more. Training, approvals, data cleanup, and probably a named owner. We were trying to solve all of that at once.
