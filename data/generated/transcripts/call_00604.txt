Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Stefanie, good morning. I appreciate you taking this before your product launch. How is the release week treating you?

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): Busy, but manageable. We have three people in the room and one person listening from a train, so apologies if somebody disappears.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): No problem. I have Sporer and Sons as a software company in the United Kingdom, about 175 employees. The previous opportunity closed as lost in June at $20,072. I want to understand whether the problem is still relevant, not assume we should reopen anything.

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): That is fair. The problem is still relevant. The timing and internal ownership were the issue.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Tell me what happened.

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): We wanted better onboarding journeys and product-usage follow-up. Marketing evaluated several platforms, but customer success wanted to own the lifecycle logic, while product wanted to own the event data. We could not agree on who would maintain it.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): So the loss was not primarily a feature gap?

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): No. We had too many opinions and no operating model. Also, our engineering team was preparing the launch, and nobody could promise implementation time.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What are you using today?

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): An email platform, spreadsheets for cohorts, and an internal event feed that only product really understands. Customer success exports a list every Monday. It works, but it is fragile.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Which customers need attention first?

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): New trials that have not completed setup, existing customers with declining usage, and accounts approaching renewal without a recent success conversation.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How do you identify those groups?

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): Product gives us a CSV. Sometimes the event names change and the column is blank. Then we ask an analyst to reconcile it.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That sounds like the data contract may be the first project, before journey design.

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): Exactly. The last vendor showed beautiful branching diagrams. They looked impressive, but we could not trust the inputs.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would a practical first phase include?

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): One onboarding sequence, one usage-warning sequence, and clear rules for when customer success takes over. No renewal automation yet.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Who would approve the rules?

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): Me for messaging, our head of customer success for handoff, and product analytics for event definitions. Engineering should not be asked to build ten custom calls.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. We could start with a scheduled file or a limited event feed, then expand once the definitions are stable.

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): That would be easier to defend. I do not want another platform that looks sophisticated in a demo and becomes a weekly manual job.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What is the cost of the current manual process?

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): Two people spend most of a day on the Monday export and cleanup. But the larger cost is missed follow-up. We know some trial users stall and never hear from us.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Can you estimate the volume?

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): Roughly 300 new trials a month, though that will increase after the launch. Conversion is around eight percent. I would not put a precise value on the missed group yet.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is enough to frame a test without claiming a guaranteed return.

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): Good. Procurement will ask for the business case, but I can support a controlled pilot.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What stopped the previous proposal from reaching a pilot?

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): We tried to get a yes from everybody at once. Product asked for real-time events, customer success asked for case context, and marketing asked for flexible content. The scope became impossible.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then the next conversation should have a narrower decision: can one trusted data set support two journeys and one handoff?

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): Yes. And we need an owner after launch. I can own content, but I cannot be the data administrator.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can include an operating guide with ownership, change requests, and a monthly review. Would customer success provide the handoff queue?

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): They can if the queue is small and actionable. A hundred alerts a day would be ignored.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What threshold would be acceptable?

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): Maybe ten to fifteen accounts a week, prioritized by trial age and meaningful usage change. That is a starting point, not a final rule.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will capture it as a hypothesis. Are there privacy or regional considerations?

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): UK and EU customers are mixed in the database. We need consent and suppression behavior to remain intact. Product events are not automatically marketing consent.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Agreed. We should separate product eligibility from permission to communicate.

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): Thank you. That distinction was blurred in the earlier evaluation.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would make you comfortable restarting?

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): A workshop with the three owners, a sample data dictionary, and a pilot proposal that explicitly excludes renewal automation and real-time engineering work.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I can send those. Would a ninety-day pilot be too long?

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): Probably. Sixty days with a two-week setup would be easier. We can measure completion, handoff acceptance, and conversion from the selected cohort.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That gives us clear checkpoints. I will not mark this as an active opportunity yet. I will send a discovery recap and let you decide whether to schedule the workshop.

Stefanie Schneider (Lifecycle Marketing): That is the right approach. We lost momentum last time because the system said we were further along than we were.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will keep the status aligned with the actual decision.
