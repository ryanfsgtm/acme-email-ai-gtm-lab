Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): When did you first notice the change?

Leonard Russel (Privacy): Around the start of the winter sale cycle. It was not every campaign. Transactional messages are generally fine, but high-volume promotional sends have lower inbox placement. Our team initially blamed the subject lines.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Did the issue affect particular domains or regions?

Leonard Russel (Privacy): Mostly Microsoft-hosted domains and some corporate retail addresses. Gmail was less dramatic. We sell across Benelux, so the impact is uneven, but our biggest lists are affected.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How large are those sends?

Leonard Russel (Privacy): The main sale audience is about 1.8 million records, though not all receive every message. On a busy week we send to several hundred thousand contacts. That is why a small percentage change becomes a large commercial problem.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Have you changed sending volume or segmentation recently?

Leonard Russel (Privacy): Yes, both. We added a loyalty segment and increased frequency before the sale. We also changed creative. So there are several plausible causes.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What did your team see in the monitoring data?

Leonard Russel (Privacy): Opens dropped, but we do not trust opens as much anymore. Clicks and attributed revenue also declined for the affected segments. The most concerning part is that unsubscribes rose after we repeated the send.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Did suppression and consent handling remain consistent?

Leonard Russel (Privacy): As far as our audit shows. We had one delayed suppression from the commerce platform, but it was corrected. That incident is not the same as the inbox-placement problem.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. We should examine reputation, authentication, content, and infrastructure together. Are you currently on shared sending infrastructure?

Leonard Russel (Privacy): Yes, and that is the point I have been trying to raise. High-volume customers moved to shared IP infrastructure experience inbox-placement degradation and elevated churn risk. I am not saying shared infrastructure is the only explanation, but we have not had a convincing one.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is a serious concern. Did the timing line up with the infrastructure change?

Leonard Russel (Privacy): Roughly. The migration was a few weeks before the winter campaigns. There was also a new authentication policy, so correlation is not proof. But our deliverability team sees a cluster among the largest senders.

Leonard Russel (Privacy): Account-level diagnostics, the relevant IP-pool information, complaint and bounce comparisons, and a mitigation plan with dates. We also need to know whether a dedicated configuration is available and what it would cost.
