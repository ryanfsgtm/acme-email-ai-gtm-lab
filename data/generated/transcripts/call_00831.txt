Priya Shah (Account Executive): Absolutely. The proposal is for Bailey - Graham Marketing Automation at 167,377 annually. Since the opportunity is now closed, I also want to understand where the process landed and whether there is anything useful for a future cycle.
Ole Torphy (Implementation Partner): We did not move forward. Pardot remained the safer procurement choice for the team, even though our implementation group liked parts of your platform.
Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. Was “safer” mainly about price, existing licensing, or implementation risk?
Ole Torphy (Implementation Partner): A combination. The headline price was not wildly different, but once we included services, training, data work, and internal support, your number felt less predictable.
Priya Shah (Account Executive): The proposal included onboarding and a phased deployment. Did the line items make that hard to compare?
Ole Torphy (Implementation Partner): Yes. Our finance team wanted one view: subscription, required services, optional services, renewal assumptions, and any increase after the first year. We received those pieces, but not in a format they could approve quickly.
Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is fair. What did Pardot provide that was easier?
Ole Torphy (Implementation Partner): Their account team mapped the package to our existing Salesforce contract. Procurement could see the incremental amount almost immediately. We have a large education operation, forty-two thousand employees on paper, and a lot of approval layers. “Almost immediately” matters.
Priya Shah (Account Executive): Did the size of Bailey - Graham create any special legal or security requirements?
Ole Torphy (Implementation Partner): Standard enterprise review, data processing terms, regional hosting questions, accessibility, and a vendor questionnaire. None was a deal breaker alone. The issue was timing. We had a March 15 close target, and the committee wanted a recommendation before the summer planning window.
Priya Shah (Account Executive): Did we miss that date because of procurement, or had the business decision already shifted?
Ole Torphy (Implementation Partner): The business case was still positive, but procurement would not sponsor a package they could not normalize. Our marketing operations lead was enthusiastic. Finance was neutral. The executive sponsor asked, “Why change if the current tool is already approved?”
Priya Shah (Account Executive): Was there a quantified benefit attached to the change?
Ole Torphy (Implementation Partner): Some. We estimated better campaign governance and less manual list work, but the savings were not audited. The team could describe benefits; they could not defend a hard payback period.
Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds like a value case problem more than a product problem.
Ole Torphy (Implementation Partner): Mostly. There was also concern about implementation ownership. We would have needed our central team plus regional marketing teams. People worried the first wave would become another shared-services project with unclear accountability.
Priya Shah (Account Executive): We discussed a pilot for two regions. Why did that not reduce the risk?
Ole Torphy (Implementation Partner): It helped conceptually, but the pilot still needed architecture review and procurement approval. Some people heard “pilot” as “extra cost before the real cost.”
Priya Shah (Account Executive): Did anyone object to the annual amount itself?
Ole Torphy (Implementation Partner): One finance reviewer called it high compared with the immediate campaign volume. That was not the only objection, but it gave the committee a simple reason to defer.
Priya Shah (Account Executive): If we had supplied a single commercial workbook with three-year pricing and implementation assumptions, would that have changed the outcome?
Ole Torphy (Implementation Partner): It might have improved the process, but I would not say it would have won the deal. The existing Pardot relationship was comfortable. A clearer workbook would have removed friction, not created urgency.
Priya Shah (Account Executive): That distinction is helpful. Was there a specific approval threshold?
Ole Torphy (Implementation Partner): Any new vendor above the department threshold needed executive review. The amount was above that threshold. We could not split it across regions without looking artificial, and nobody wanted to do that.
Priya Shah (Account Executive): Did legal or IT request concessions?
Ole Torphy (Implementation Partner): Legal wanted local language in a couple of clauses. IT wanted a named escalation path. Those were manageable. The unresolved item was internal capacity for migration and governance.
Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would make a re-engagement credible in six or twelve months?
Ole Torphy (Implementation Partner): A defined implementation plan with customer responsibilities, a fixed first-year services ceiling, and an executive-level outcome tied to reporting or governance. Also, show how the platform coexists with what we have instead of presenting replacement as the only path.
Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is very clear. Would marketing operations still be the right internal champion?
Ole Torphy (Implementation Partner): Yes, but they need finance and the executive sponsor involved earlier. The champion can explain why the system is better; they cannot approve the risk alone.
Priya Shah (Account Executive): I appreciate the candor. I will record that the loss was not simply price, but procurement comparability, incumbent comfort, and uncertain internal ownership.
Ole Torphy (Implementation Partner): That is accurate. And please do not take “lost” to mean nobody liked the product. It means the organization did not have enough reason to absorb the change this year.
