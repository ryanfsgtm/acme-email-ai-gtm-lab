Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Hi Zaria, thanks for joining. Before we start, is Amsterdam still getting that rain you mentioned last week?

Zaria Parker (CMO): It is, and the office heating is either tropical or nonexistent. We’re all wearing coats indoors. How about you?

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Sunny for once. I’d like to understand what Doyle - Gleason needs from marketing automation, where the current process breaks, and what a sensible next step would look like. I know Marketo has been part of the comparison.

Zaria Parker (CMO): Yes. We looked at Marketo seriously. The opportunity ultimately closed as lost, but we’re still learning from the evaluation because our needs haven’t disappeared.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What prompted the search originally?

Zaria Parker (CMO): Growth without adding another coordinator every time we win a client. We’re a professional-services firm with a small marketing team. We need to nurture prospects between conversations, keep event follow-up from falling through the cracks, and give partners visibility without asking them to live in a system.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How many people are involved in campaign work today?

Zaria Parker (CMO): Four in marketing, plus several partners who approve content or follow up with accounts. The four-person number sounds manageable until someone is out sick or three proposals land in the same week.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What happens after an event registration?

Zaria Parker (CMO): A spreadsheet export, then someone cleans it, then an email goes out. Sometimes the partner gets a list. Sometimes the list waits because we’re unsure whether consent covers the follow-up. We lose momentum.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Is that mainly a tooling issue or a process issue?

Zaria Parker (CMO): Both, but process first. We have tools already. They don’t talk well, and nobody wants to own the messy middle. A new system could make that better or just give us a more expensive place to be disorganized.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That’s an important distinction. What would you want the system to automate without approval?

Zaria Parker (CMO): Basic reminders, event confirmation, a short educational sequence, and notifications when someone asks for a consultation. Anything that sounds like a commitment on behalf of a partner should need review.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): And what should never happen automatically?

Zaria Parker (CMO): Sending a prospect a case study from the wrong practice area. We work across legal, finance, and technology clients, and a generic segment can be misleading. Also, don’t repeatedly contact people who have asked to pause.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How are pauses tracked today?

Zaria Parker (CMO): In three places, which is the problem. One person’s notes, an email inbox, and sometimes the CRM. A contact can be marked inactive in one place and still receive a campaign from another.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What CRM are you using?

Zaria Parker (CMO): A lightweight CRM. It’s useful for opportunities but not great for marketing history. We have duplicate companies, abbreviated names, and contacts who work with several practices. Marketo could probably handle the scale, but scale isn’t our immediate issue.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What is the immediate issue?

Zaria Parker (CMO): Reliability. If I schedule a campaign, I need confidence that the right people get it, the wrong people don’t, and the team can explain what happened. Our current reporting tells me sends and opens, but not whether a partner actually followed up.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Would partner activity need to be logged manually?

Zaria Parker (CMO): Ideally not every detail. A simple status would help: accepted, contacted, not relevant, or needs review. We don’t want a large adoption project.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would make adoption realistic for a small team?

Zaria Parker (CMO): Templates that reflect our actual work, short training, and someone who can answer questions during the first month. We don’t need a hundred-page manual. We need to know what to do on Tuesday afternoon when an event list arrives.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That suggests a focused first use case. Which event or practice area would you choose?

Zaria Parker (CMO): Technology consulting, probably. The audience is defined and the follow-up journey is reasonably repeatable. Legal is more sensitive and partner-led.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What metrics would determine whether the pilot worked?

Zaria Parker (CMO): Faster follow-up, fewer duplicate contacts, fewer unsubscribe complaints, and evidence that partners saw the high-intent requests. I’m less interested in a prettier open-rate chart.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How fast is fast enough for a consultation request?

Zaria Parker (CMO): Same business day. If someone fills out a form at 10 a.m. and hears from us on Friday, the automation failed even if the email technically sent.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Who would approve a purchase?

Zaria Parker (CMO): I would sponsor it, but finance would need a clear cost case and the partners would need confidence that it won’t create noise. The prior evaluation lost momentum because we were comparing features before agreeing on ownership and budget.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Is budget the primary obstacle now?

Zaria Parker (CMO): It’s a constraint, not the only obstacle. Marketo was more than we wanted to operate. A less expensive tool could still fail if it requires too much administration. Total effort matters.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): If a system were selected, when could the team realistically start?

Zaria Parker (CMO): Not during our annual planning cycle. Perhaps the next quarter, provided the pilot scope and owner are agreed first. I don’t want another launch where everyone says yes in a meeting and nobody has time afterward.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That’s clear. I’ll summarize a pilot around technology consulting events, consent and suppression governance, same-day routing, and a lightweight partner status workflow. Does that reflect the need?

Zaria Parker (CMO): Yes. Add data cleanup as a prerequisite, though. If duplicates stay unresolved, automation just makes the confusion faster.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Good point. Anything else?

Zaria Parker (CMO): A rollback plan. If the first sequence performs badly, we need to pause it without disabling every other communication.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I’ll include that. Thanks, Zaria. This was helpful, especially the distinction between buying software and building a dependable process.

Zaria Parker (CMO): Thank you. And if the rain stops, I’ll consider that a successful discovery call.
