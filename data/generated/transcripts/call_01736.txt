Marcus Chen (Account Executive): When you say it works, where does it break down?

Paula Moen (Demand Generation): Follow-up timing, mainly. A prospective student may download something and wait several days before anyone notices. Also, our reporting is fragmented. I can tell you how many form fills we had, but connecting that to an enrolled student is difficult.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Are those inquiries all handled by you, or do admissions and program teams participate?

Paula Moen (Demand Generation): Admissions owns the personal follow-up. I own campaigns and list preparation. Program directors sometimes ask for their own audience, and that is where things get messy. They’ll say, ‘Can you send this to people interested in leadership?’ and I have to build the list by hand.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What systems are involved today?

Paula Moen (Demand Generation): A website CMS, a form tool, spreadsheets, and our student information system. There’s also a basic email platform. The names vary by department because some tools were adopted independently.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Got it. Are you looking to replace the email platform, or to connect the pieces around it?

Paula Moen (Demand Generation): I’m not sure yet. Replacing it would be attractive, but I don’t want to create a large IT project. Our operations lead is supportive in principle, though she has limited time.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That’s helpful. What would a successful first phase look like?

Paula Moen (Demand Generation): A clean inquiry journey. Someone fills out a form, receives useful information, gets routed to the right admissions person, and we can see whether the person engaged. It would be nice if a program director could request a segment without me spending an afternoon filtering rows.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): And what would you measure?

Paula Moen (Demand Generation): Response time, inquiry-to-application rate, and unsubscribe rate. Maybe influenced enrollment later, but I don’t trust our current data enough to promise that metric immediately.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That’s a sensible sequence. How quickly would you want this in place?

Paula Moen (Demand Generation): Ideally before the next major enrollment push, but I need to understand the effort. We have no dedicated marketing-operations person.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Our typical starting point is a small data and lifecycle review, followed by a pilot for one program. We’d map fields, define consent handling, build a few journeys, and train the people who need to use them. Would a pilot be easier to socialize internally than a full replacement?

Paula Moen (Demand Generation): Probably. People are cautious because previous software projects promised a lot and ended up as another login.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What happened with those projects?

Paula Moen (Demand Generation): We bought a reporting add-on once. The dashboard looked good in the demonstration, but nobody agreed on definitions. Another vendor gave us a complicated scoring model. It produced numbers, but admissions didn’t use them.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): So adoption and shared definitions matter as much as functionality.

Paula Moen (Demand Generation): Exactly. And I don’t want to be the only person who knows how it works. If I’m out, the process stops.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Who else should be involved in evaluating this?

Paula Moen (Demand Generation): Our operations lead, maybe someone from admissions, and eventually the person responsible for the student system. The executive director will want a short business case, but she won’t join a technical workshop.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. Is there an existing budget or would this be planned during the next cycle?

Paula Moen (Demand Generation): There’s some budget for demand generation, but the amount isn’t fixed. I’d need pricing that doesn’t assume an enterprise rollout. Our annual revenue is healthy for our size, but we are still careful with recurring software.
