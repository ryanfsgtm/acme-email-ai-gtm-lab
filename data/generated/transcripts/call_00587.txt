Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): How are those motions separated today?

Ricardo McLaughlin (CMO): Not cleanly. The same website visitor may download a developer guide, attend a webinar, and later ask for pricing. We score those activities, but the score is not trusted. Sales sees a number; marketing sees a person with context.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What happens when sales does not trust the score?

Ricardo McLaughlin (CMO): They create their own lists. Sometimes that is reasonable. Sometimes it means three reps contact the same account because each list came from a different report. We only discover the overlap when someone complains.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Is the issue primarily identity resolution, routing, or the definition of a qualified lead?

Ricardo McLaughlin (CMO): Yes, all three. The qualified-lead definition is the political part. We have an MQL threshold, but it does not account for account fit. A student at a large university can score higher than an operations director at a target company. The dashboard says the student is hotter.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That is a good example. Would you want scoring to combine individual behavior with account characteristics?

Ricardo McLaughlin (CMO): Eventually. First I want a reliable foundation. Our data is split between Salesforce, a product database, webinar software, and a warehouse. The warehouse is authoritative for usage, but marketing cannot query it directly without an analyst.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): How long does a typical campaign take from idea to launch?

Ricardo McLaughlin (CMO): A simple email can go out in a day. Anything involving product behavior takes two to three weeks because someone has to extract a file, clean it, and check permissions. By then the campaign idea has lost its momentum.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What campaigns are waiting today?

Ricardo McLaughlin (CMO): An onboarding sequence for teams that invite additional users, a renewal-risk series, and a re-engagement program for trial accounts that never activated. The onboarding sequence is the priority because customer success believes it could reduce support tickets.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What would success look like there?

Ricardo McLaughlin (CMO): Fewer “how do I invite my team?” tickets, higher workspace activation, and a clear comparison against the current cohort. We can measure those. I do not want to use open rate as the headline.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Agreed. Who owns the event definitions for invitation and activation?

Ricardo McLaughlin (CMO): Product analytics owns the definitions, customer success owns the support taxonomy, and marketing owns the communication. That sounds collaborative, but it means no one owns the whole journey.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): If we mapped that journey, which system should trigger the message?

Ricardo McLaughlin (CMO): Ideally the marketing system receives a trusted event. But our product team is concerned about sending too much data outside the warehouse. Security will ask about retention, access, and regional handling. We sell in Canada and the United States, with a small European customer base.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We would need security involved early. Are there requirements around consent and suppression that are especially important?

Ricardo McLaughlin (CMO): Suppression is the land mine. We have global opt-outs in one place and product-notification preferences in another. A customer can opt out of marketing but still need a service message. If the model is too simple, we either annoy people or miss a necessary notice.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That distinction should be explicit in the design. How many people would administer the system?

Ricardo McLaughlin (CMO): Four regularly, maybe six occasionally. None of them is a full-time operations specialist. I need something our team can maintain after implementation, not a beautiful machine that requires consultants for every change.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What would make a project fail internally?

Ricardo McLaughlin (CMO): A long data project with no visible wins. Also, if sales thinks marketing is changing routing without agreement, we will lose support. We need a pilot with a small audience and a shared definition of success.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Would the activation campaign be a reasonable pilot?

Ricardo McLaughlin (CMO): Probably. We have a stable segment of new workspaces, and customer success is motivated. But we need to confirm the event is complete enough. There are cases where a user invites a teammate from a mobile screen and the event arrives late.
