Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Brock, good afternoon. I see you have joined from somewhere with a heroic amount of wind noise.

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): I am in Copenhagen for two days. The wind is part of the local procurement process, apparently. Give me a second—there. Better?

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Much better. We planned to review adoption, regional performance, and the renewal decision. I also want to understand the concerns raised by your operations group so we can separate product gaps from process gaps.

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): That is fair. The short version is that the platform works, but the organisation around it does not always. We have five regions, four different definitions of a qualified lead, and a central team that thinks a dashboard can settle an argument.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What is the most consequential disagreement?

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): Sales says marketing sends too many names. Marketing says sales ignores the names. The system is faithfully recording the quarrel, which is not the same as solving it.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Have you established a common acceptance criterion?

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): We drafted one. Then the Nordics objected because their average deal is different, and Germany objected because the local team does not want central scoring. It is not a technical dispute, even when it arrives wearing a technical hat.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That distinction matters. Usage is strongest in the UK and Netherlands, correct?

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): Yes, especially for event follow-up and account alerts. The smaller offices use the basic campaigns. France is barely using it because their CRM migration is still unfinished.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Are the high-usage teams seeing measurable value?

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): They are seeing faster follow-up, and the UK team reports more visibility. I would hesitate to put a clean revenue number on it. Attribution is a political object here.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Understood. What does your finance partner need to see for the $441,234 programme to remain justified?

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): A credible operating model and fewer duplicate tools. Finance is not asking only whether this platform performs. They are asking why we have this, HubSpot in two acquired units, and several local email tools.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Is HubSpot being considered as a replacement or as a consolidation vehicle for the acquired units?

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): Both, depending on whom you ask. The acquired teams already know HubSpot. They say migration would be quicker. Our central team says the governance and enterprise controls are better in your environment. I have heard both arguments in the same meeting.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): If you had to identify the decision criteria, what are the top three?

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): Integration with our CRM, regional governance, and administrator effort. Campaign features are table stakes now. Also, we need a path for the acquired data without creating another long migration programme.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can map those criteria and run a focused comparison. I do not want a generic feature battle. Would a pilot migration of one acquired unit help?

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): Maybe, but the local team has no spare capacity. They are already working weekends. A pilot that depends on them may become a test of exhaustion.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Then the first step may be a data and process assessment, with their involvement limited to validation.

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): That sounds more realistic. But I need to be candid: the opportunity is marked lost in the commercial system because the current term was not approved. I do not want this QBR to become a ceremonial attempt to revive it.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I appreciate the candour. We can treat it as an evaluation of whether there is a future programme, not assume a win.

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): Exactly. If the answer is HubSpot for those units and something else centrally, I would rather make that explicit than force a single answer.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What would make a split model unacceptable?

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): Inconsistent consent handling, fragmented reporting, and no reliable ownership. If we cannot explain which system is authoritative, we will have bought complexity.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can document system-of-record responsibilities before discussing licensing. Has privacy or legal raised any particular concern?

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): They are concerned about regional data flows, but they have not issued a prohibition. They want a clear register of where campaign data sits and who can access it.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will add that to the assessment. On adoption, would executive sponsorship help, or is the problem more local capacity?

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): Both. The central mandate gets attention for two weeks. Then local priorities win. A smaller number of repeatable plays would probably outperform a giant transformation message.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Which plays should we preserve?

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): Event follow-up, renewal-risk alerts, and a simple executive account brief. The account brief is not flashy, but the partners actually read it.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is useful counterweight to the assumption that more automation is always better. What should we stop?

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): The weekly score email. People forward it around without context, then we spend half a meeting explaining why a score changed.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can pause it and replace it with a monthly review based on agreed definitions.

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): Maybe. Do not change it until we see the impact. Some teams rely on it as a prompt even if they misuse it.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Fair. We will measure before and after rather than declare it useless.

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): Another practical issue: the renewal date in the record is May twenty-six next year, but our board review is in February. The commercial sequence needs to start earlier.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will note February as the planning checkpoint, with a decision review before that. Do you want procurement included now?

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): Not yet. Bring me a one-page operating model, the regional data-flow questions, and an honest comparison with HubSpot. Then I can decide whether procurement should spend time.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will send those items next week and include the limits of what we can conclude from current usage data.

Brock Gottlieb-Boyle (CMO): Good. If the answer is no, say no. We have enough programmes that survive because nobody wants to write the last paragraph.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Agreed. Thanks for the direct discussion, Brock.
