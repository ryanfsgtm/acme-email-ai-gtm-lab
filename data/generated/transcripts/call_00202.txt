Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Frankie, thanks for making time. I know procurement calendars are never exactly generous.

Frankie Walker (Procurement): No problem. I am between two meetings, so if the dog starts barking, that is why.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Completely understood. I would like to understand the purchasing process, the business problem, and what would make a marketing automation project acceptable internally. Could you start with why this is being considered?

Frankie Walker (Procurement): Marketing says the current process is too manual. They want better campaign follow-up and fewer spreadsheets. From procurement’s point of view, though, we need a measurable requirement before we compare vendors.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What measures are they using today?

Frankie Walker (Procurement): Mostly email volume, response rates, and whether a campaign went out on time. The reporting is inconsistent across teams. Some numbers come from the CRM, some from a spreadsheet, and some are, well, remembered.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That makes attribution difficult. How many people would use the system initially?

Frankie Walker (Procurement): Maybe twelve, possibly twenty if regional offices join. We are not trying to automate every process on day one. Finance would want approval for anything with a recurring charge.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can start with a focused implementation and expand. What is the timeline?

Frankie Walker (Procurement): Marketing says this quarter. IT says they have no capacity this quarter. Those statements are both true, which is the problem.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would IT need to review?

Frankie Walker (Procurement): Security questionnaire, user provisioning, data flows, integrations, and support terms. Procurement also needs at least two comparable quotes. A verbal preference does not create a purchase order.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Are there incumbent tools we need to account for?

Frankie Walker (Procurement): We use a CRM, an email platform, and a reporting tool. None is perfect, but replacing one creates work. The business case has to cover that friction.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): If the new platform reduced manual campaign work, how would you quantify the benefit?

Frankie Walker (Procurement): Hours saved per campaign, probably. But I would not promise savings until Marketing Operations validates the baseline. We have a habit of estimating the best case first.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Good distinction. Would a proof of concept help?

Frankie Walker (Procurement): It could, provided it has a fixed scope and no automatic conversion into a long contract. Also, someone has to own the test. If that person leaves, the project stalls.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Who is the likely business owner?

Frankie Walker (Procurement): The head of marketing has interest, but the operations manager knows the workflows. Neither has formally accepted ownership yet.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then an agreed owner is an early milestone. We can provide a sample implementation plan, security materials, and pricing at the likely user count.

Frankie Walker (Procurement): Please include renewal terms and implementation fees separately. Hidden first-year costs are what make procurement look difficult later.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Absolutely. Are there budget limits I should work within?

Frankie Walker (Procurement): There is a rough ceiling, not an approved budget. I would rather see a realistic number than a discounted number that expires after the pilot.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. What would cause you to recommend not proceeding?

Frankie Walker (Procurement): No internal owner, unclear integration responsibility, or a business case based only on enthusiasm. If those are addressed, we can evaluate fairly.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will send a discovery summary with those points and ask for the IT questionnaire. We should also schedule a session with Marketing Operations.

Frankie Walker (Procurement): Yes, but I need to check calendars. Tuesday may work; I will confirm rather than promise.
