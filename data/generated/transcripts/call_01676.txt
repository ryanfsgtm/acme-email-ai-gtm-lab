Elmer Mante (Sales Operations): Correct. We can send emails already. Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How are you reporting on that today?

Elmer Mante (Sales Operations): Mostly CRM reports and exports. Once a month I pull data, normalize it in a spreadsheet, and send a slide deck. It’s good enough for a meeting, not good enough for somebody asking, “What changed since Tuesday?”

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. When you say good enough, is that because the reporting requirement is modest, or because the teams have accepted the workaround?

Elmer Mante (Sales Operations): The second one. People have accepted it because no individual step is unbearable. But we lose hours, and the numbers are debated instead of used.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Let me ask about the operator side. Who builds journeys or manages campaigns?

Elmer Mante (Sales Operations): Two marketing coordinators, with occasional help from regional marketers. They’re not technical specialists. They need templates, approvals, and a way to test without accidentally emailing the full audience.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Are they testing manually today?

Elmer Mante (Sales Operations): Usually. One person sends to herself, then someone else checks it. Sometimes a test list. We’ve had the usual issues—wrong personalization token, a link that points to a staging page, that sort of thing.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): And what happens after a form submission?

Elmer Mante (Sales Operations): Depends on the region. A high-priority inquiry may create a CRM task. Others sit in a shared queue. There’s no consistent timer or escalation. If the contact is already known, matching can be messy.
