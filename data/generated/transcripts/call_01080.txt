Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Leone, thanks for taking the call. I understand you are evaluating marketing automation, and I would like to start with the technical constraints rather than jump into features.

Leone Schneider (IT): That is preferable. I was asked to assess whether it would create more support work than value. Marketing is enthusiastic; IT is expected to make the enthusiasm safe.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): A familiar division of labour. What is the current process?

Leone Schneider (IT): Mostly email campaigns assembled manually, with contact lists exported from the CRM. Altenwerth - Halvorson is a software company, but the marketing function is small and the process has grown by habit.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Where does it break down?

Leone Schneider (IT): Duplicates, unclear ownership, and too many spreadsheets. We also have prospects in several European countries, so consent and preference handling need to be consistent. I do not want a second database drifting away from the CRM.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Which system should be authoritative?

Leone Schneider (IT): The CRM for account and contact identity. Campaign activity can live elsewhere if it syncs cleanly and we can explain the direction of every update.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What integration would be required first?

Leone Schneider (IT): The CRM and our form provider. Maybe webinar data later. We have a small engineering team, so custom middleware is not realistic for an initial project.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How would you define a successful pilot?

Leone Schneider (IT): One form, one nurture path, and a clear unsubscribe test. We need to see that a preference change propagates correctly and that a deleted or suppressed contact does not reappear from an old import.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can design those as test cases. How many people would administer the system?

Leone Schneider (IT): Two from marketing and one IT administrator. Agencies may need temporary access, but we would not give them broad permissions by default.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is workable. What security documentation will procurement request?

Leone Schneider (IT): Hosting location, encryption, subprocessors, access logging, incident notification, and deletion. Also whether support can view customer data without us knowing.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I can provide the security materials and explain the support-access controls. Your legal and procurement teams should validate whether the contractual terms meet your requirements.

Leone Schneider (IT): Naturally. Documentation is evidence, not approval.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How does data enter the system today?

Leone Schneider (IT): CSV imports from marketing, CRM syncs, and web forms. The CSV route is the dangerous one because field names change and someone can upload a full customer export when they meant to upload fifty prospects.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We should restrict import permissions, use a mapped template, and test with a small file. Would required fields solve that?

Leone Schneider (IT): Partly. Required fields prevent blanks; they do not prevent the wrong file. We need a review step and a documented rollback procedure.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Agreed. What about reporting?

Leone Schneider (IT): Marketing wants engagement dashboards. IT wants to know whether reports are expensive, whether they expose personal data, and who can export them. Those are not the same question.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can separate operational dashboards from unrestricted data access and review report-sharing defaults. I will include export permissions in the access design.

Leone Schneider (Customer Success Manager): Our biggest concern is support capacity. If every campaign requires IT, the project will fail even if the software works.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then the pilot should include ownership boundaries: marketing manages approved campaigns, IT manages identity and integrations, and exceptions have a ticket path. We can document training needs before launch.

Leone Schneider (IT): That sounds reasonable. I should mention that the opportunity is not committed. Finance rejected the first cost estimate, and marketing is revising the scope.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Thanks for clarifying. We can keep the discovery proportional and avoid assuming a purchase. What would make the revised proposal credible?

Leone Schneider (IT): A small implementation plan, named responsibilities, integration limits, and a total cost that does not hide agency or data-migration work.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will prepare those inputs and a pilot outline. We should also include the unsubscribe and suppression tests you described.

Leone Schneider (IT): Yes. If those fail, the rest does not matter.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. Shall we schedule a technical workshop with marketing and the CRM owner?

Leone Schneider (IT): Send me the agenda first. I want to ensure the right people attend, not another general product call.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will send it with the security pack and the draft test cases. We can decide afterward whether a pilot is worth pursuing.

Leone Schneider (IT): Good. That is the right next step.
