Priya Shah (Account Executive): Hi Jamey, it’s good to see you. How have things been since our last review?

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): Busy, but in a constructive way. We opened two new service lines, and marketing has been trying to keep pace without turning every campaign into a compliance committee meeting.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds familiar. Before we get into the slides, anything urgent you want to put on the table?

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): Reporting. Our leadership team thinks the platform is generating more engagement than it actually is because the dashboard mixes marketing emails, appointment reminders, and a few operational notices. The headline number looks impressive, but I don’t trust it.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): So the immediate issue is confidence in the measurement, not necessarily delivery performance.

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): Correct. We work in healthcare, across France, and the distinction between a marketing message and a service message is important. A patient opening an appointment reminder should not be counted as a campaign success.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How are those messages separated today?

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): In theory, by programme and sender. In practice, one team inherited another team’s templates. Some operational messages use the same sender identity as marketing. We are cleaning that up, but the reporting has to survive the transition.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would separate workspaces or sending domains help?

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): Maybe. I’m hesitant to create more silos. We already have a CRM, a scheduling system, and regional databases. Every new boundary makes a future integration harder.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a reasonable concern. Let’s look at the current account structure and decide whether the separation should be logical, technical, or simply reporting-based. What has gone well this quarter?

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): The professional referral campaign worked better than expected. We had a clear audience and one person accountable for follow-up. The response rate was not spectacular, but the downstream consultations were higher than the previous quarter.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Do you have the consultation figure?

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): Approximately eleven percent above the prior quarter for that segment. I’m saying approximately because the regional teams record outcomes differently. One logs an appointment, another logs a completed consultation.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That definition mismatch could explain some of the dashboard disagreement. Would a shared outcome taxonomy be useful?

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): Yes, but it needs to be small. If you give us twenty-seven lifecycle stages, the teams will choose whichever one sounds nicest.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can start with a few agreed states and map the local values underneath. I’ll ask our analytics specialist to help with that.

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): Good. The other success was the preference centre. Patients and professional contacts can now choose topics rather than opting out of everything. It reduced blanket unsubscribes in one region.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That’s encouraging. Did the change introduce any operational issues?

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): A few. The preference labels are not equally clear in French and English, and one team interpreted “service updates” as promotional. We need a review with legal and patient communications.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I’ll note that as a content and governance action. On adoption, how comfortable are the regional marketers?

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): The experienced users are comfortable. New users still build campaigns by copying old ones, which preserves bad habits. We need a practical playbook, not another product tour.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What should the playbook cover first?

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): Audience selection, consent, template classification, approval, and post-send analysis. Also what not to put in free-text fields. We still see notes that should never be in a marketing system.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is important, especially given the nature of your data. We can provide configuration guidance, but your privacy team should define the permitted fields and retention rules.

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): They have defined them, broadly. The problem is enforcement. People are under pressure and paste a whole CRM note into a campaign brief.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Could we add required fields and remove free-text access for some roles?

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): Possibly. I need to know how much that affects the existing workflows. If it adds five clicks to every campaign, adoption will fall.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can test a lighter permission model with a pilot group. Would you prefer to optimise for strict control or speed?

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): Different levels by role. The central team can have more control. Regional teams need speed within a safe set of templates and audiences.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That suggests a tiered governance model. Let’s make it a design item rather than a binary choice.

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): Agreed. What about the integration roadmap? We have postponed the scheduling feed twice.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Is the delay technical or prioritisation-related?

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): Prioritisation. The scheduling team is migrating systems. They do not want another dependency until that is complete. We can continue with nightly aggregate data, but we cannot promise real-time events.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That may be enough for reporting and some educational campaigns, but probably not for immediate follow-up. Which use case depends on real time?

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): A reminder after an information request. We currently handle it manually. It is useful, but not worth derailing the migration.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then we should separate the roadmap into what can run on current data and what waits for the scheduling integration.

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): Exactly. Our finance director wants a value story before approving more work. If we show that the referral programme produced eleven percent more completed consultations, we have a stronger case than saying we sent more emails.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can build the next review around completed outcomes, with a caveat about regional definitions. I’ll also propose a dashboard that excludes operational messages from marketing engagement.

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): Please do. And include data quality as a metric. I want to see missing consent status, unmapped outcomes, and stale records—not just open rates.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That’s a better health view. Any concerns about support or response times?

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): No major concerns. A ticket took longer than expected last month, but the answer was correct. I care more about recurring issues than one slow reply.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We’ll review the ticket pattern, not just the average response number.

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): Good. For next quarter, our priorities are reporting separation, the playbook, preference-language review, and a realistic integration plan.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I have those four. I’ll send the recap with owners and proposed dates, and we’ll schedule a working session with privacy and analytics.

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): Include patient communications, too. They are the people who catch the confusing wording before we do.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Absolutely. Thanks, Jamey. Enjoy the rest of your afternoon.

Jamey Nicolas (VP Marketing): Thanks, Priya. Speak soon.
