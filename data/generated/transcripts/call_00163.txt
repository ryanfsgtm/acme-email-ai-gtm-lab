Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Morning, Kenyatta. Thanks for joining. I know you’re in Canada and this is early, so I’ll keep the conversation practical. I’d like to understand what Haley Group needs from marketing automation and whether the current setup is helping or creating more work.

Kenyatta Schamberger (CMO): Morning. Coffee is present, at least. We’re a media company with about one hundred and twenty-eight people, and our marketing team is small. We publish across several brands, so the problem is less “we need more emails” and more “we cannot coordinate everything without stepping on each other.”

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That sounds familiar. What happens today when a brand wants to promote a webinar, newsletter, or sponsorship?

Kenyatta Schamberger (CMO): Someone builds a list in the CRM, someone else has an old spreadsheet, and the brand manager has a separate audience in the newsletter tool. Then I’m asked whether the numbers are accurate. Usually they are approximately accurate, which is not the same thing.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Approximately accurate is a dangerous reporting category. How do you define success for the automation project?

Kenyatta Schamberger (CMO): Better segmentation, fewer manual imports, and a clearer view of which campaigns create qualified conversations. We also want a consistent preference centre. Our readers may follow one publication but not another, and a global unsubscribe should not accidentally wipe out every legitimate relationship—or the reverse.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): So subscription preferences need to work at both brand and global levels. Who owns the decision when a brand manager wants to email someone who has opted out of that brand?

Kenyatta Schamberger (CMO): Compliance should win, obviously, but people are not always looking at the same screen. That is the operational issue. We have enthusiastic teams who will find a workaround if the approved path takes too long.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I appreciate the honesty. We should design the approved path around the real workflow, not an imaginary disciplined organisation. How many users would build campaigns, and how many would only need reports?

Kenyatta Schamberger (CMO): Maybe eight active users, plus executives who want dashboards. The sales group is smaller, but our partnerships team also touches leads. Access needs to be simple enough that I don’t become the help desk.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can start with a few role templates, then adjust after observing usage. What does your current lead handoff look like?

Kenyatta Schamberger (CMO): A form submission creates a notification. If it looks interesting, marketing forwards an email to a salesperson. There is no shared definition of “interesting.” A sponsorship lead may be valuable even if it never fits a traditional sales score.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That suggests separate lifecycle definitions for advertising, subscriptions, and partnerships. Would the first project need scoring across all three?

Kenyatta Schamberger (CMO): No, and that is one of my concerns. Vendors often promise one beautiful score that nobody trusts. We need a modest starting model and a way to explain why a person moved stages.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can begin with transparent rules—activity, declared interest, company fit—then review false positives and false negatives. The system should support human override with a reason, not pretend the score is objective.

Kenyatta Schamberger (CMO): Good. Our audience is unusual. A journalist who downloads a media kit is not a sales lead, but a brand relationship. A small advertiser may be more valuable than a large company that only reads an article.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is a useful counterpoint to standard B2B qualification. Which reports are currently hardest to produce?

Kenyatta Schamberger (CMO): Attribution. We can count opens and clicks, but we cannot confidently say a campaign influenced a renewal, sponsorship, or inbound brief. The board asks for revenue impact, and we give them activity charts.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Do you have opportunity and revenue data in the CRM today?

Kenyatta Schamberger (CMO): Some of it. Renewals are recorded, but sponsorships are sometimes handled through finance and never connected to the original contact. So I don’t want a promise of perfect attribution from day one.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Sensible. We can define a minimum viable attribution model and label gaps. A campaign can show influenced activity without claiming it created revenue. Then finance and sales can improve the linkage over time.

Kenyatta Schamberger (CMO): Exactly. I also need onboarding support. We have tried tools where the first month looked smooth because a consultant did everything, then the system collapsed when they left.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can set up a handoff plan: administrator training, campaign templates, naming conventions, and a weekly office hour for the initial period. I’d also like one person from each brand in the design sessions so the templates don’t favour the biggest publication.

Kenyatta Schamberger (CMO): That may slow the start, but it will prevent arguments later. One of our smaller brands has the most engaged audience, even though it has the fewest subscribers.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is precisely why raw volume should not determine the design. What would make you say after ninety days that the program is working?

Kenyatta Schamberger (CMO): We can launch a campaign without spreadsheet surgery, preference changes are visible and respected, and the weekly report takes less than an hour to prepare. For commercial impact, I’d accept a directional result, provided the definitions are honest.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): And what would make it fail?

Kenyatta Schamberger (CMO): If the data migration creates duplicate contacts, if teams cannot tell which audience they are using, or if every change requires an administrator. Also, if the implementation assumes our brands behave identically.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I’ll capture those as design requirements. I should mention that the opportunity record shows this as a closed-lost project, so I’m not going to present today as a purchase decision. My role is to understand what would need to change before a future conversation is useful.

Kenyatta Schamberger (CMO): That’s fair. We have not selected a competitor; we simply did not feel ready to commit. Timing and confidence matter more than another feature list.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then the next useful step is a short workshop with marketing operations, one brand representative, sales, and whoever owns finance linkage. We can map audiences and define the initial metrics before discussing a new commercial proposal.

Kenyatta Schamberger (CMO): Send me a one-page agenda. If it is focused on our actual process, I’ll get the right people. If it is another generic demo, I won’t.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. I’ll send the agenda and a draft data map. Thanks for the candid discussion.

Kenyatta Schamberger (CMO): Thank you. And please write down “approximately accurate” so nobody forgets it.
