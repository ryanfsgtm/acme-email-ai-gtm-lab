Dee Schaefer (Implementation Partner): Both, unfortunately. I coordinate the CRM work, plant onboarding, and the outside integrator. Marketing owns the content, but they ask us whether every form, routing rule, and sync is safe.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What prompted the search now?

Dee Schaefer (Implementation Partner): Lead follow-up is inconsistent. A contact downloads a maintenance guide, and sometimes the plant salesperson sees it the same day. Sometimes it arrives in a weekly report. We also have product lines that should not be marketed to every customer.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): So speed and control are connected. What would a good first use case look like?

Dee Schaefer (Implementation Partner): A post-inquiry sequence for replacement parts. Three emails, then a task for the right regional representative. If the contact is already in an active service case, the sequence must stop. If the company has multiple facilities, the owner should route based on facility, not headquarters address.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is more nuanced than a standard lead nurture. Where does the facility information live?

Dee Schaefer (Implementation Partner): In the CRM for some accounts, in an ERP field for others, and in free text for the rest. We have been cleaning it. “Free text” is doing a lot of work in that sentence.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That could be a launch risk. Would you prefer to wait until the data model is perfect?

Dee Schaefer (Implementation Partner): No, because it will never be perfect. I want a controlled fallback. If facility is blank, route to a central queue and flag it. What I do not want is silent misrouting.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can model that with conditional branches and exception reporting. Who would build and maintain the journeys?

Dee Schaefer (Implementation Partner): Initially, our marketing operations analyst and the integrator. Later, I want plant coordinators to update approved content without editing logic. Permissions are important. One enthusiastic coordinator can break a live workflow in ten minutes.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would a library of locked templates help?

Dee Schaefer (Implementation Partner): Yes, if the language can still vary by product line. We have five major lines and different compliance notes. Reusable structure is good; forced identical copy is not.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What is the procurement team asking for besides price?

Dee Schaefer (Implementation Partner): A clear services boundary, data processing language, renewal terms, and evidence that the implementation estimate is realistic. They are nervous about professional services expanding after signature.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How much internal time could Kub - Wolff commit in the first month?

Dee Schaefer (Implementation Partner): Maybe six hours a week from marketing operations, four from CRM, and ad hoc plant input. That is not enough for an open-ended discovery project. We need decisions in workshops, with owners named before anyone leaves.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would you want us to run those workshops?

Dee Schaefer (Implementation Partner): I want a joint model. Your team can provide the framework, but Kub - Wolff has to make the policy decisions. We have bought software before where the vendor documented every question and nobody answered them.

Dee Schaefer (Implementation Partner): The integrator wants clean APIs and fewer custom objects. Marketing wants a visual builder and less dependence on developers. Both are reasonable. They are optimizing different parts of the same program.
