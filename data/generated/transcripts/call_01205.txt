Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Emilio, thanks for taking the time. Before we dive in, how is the weather in Toronto?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Cold enough that our office attendance is mostly theoretical. We’re managing.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I appreciate it. I’d like to understand what prompted the marketing automation review, what is working today, and where Eloqua is falling short.

Emilio Kulas (CMO): We’ve outgrown the patchwork. We’re a media business with several brands, and campaigns are planned centrally but executed by different teams. The central team wants control; the brands want speed.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What does the patchwork look like in practice?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Eloqua handles some nurture programs, our CRM handles sales ownership, and regional teams use spreadsheets for event follow-up. We also have an agency running paid acquisition. Data arrives late, and nobody trusts the handoff report.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): When you say arrives late, are we talking hours or days?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Usually a day. Sometimes three. After a conference, the event team sends a spreadsheet on Monday, marketing uploads it Tuesday, and sales sees assignments Wednesday if nothing breaks.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That delay sounds expensive. What happens to those leads during the gap?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Some are contacted manually. Others disappear into a queue. The sales leaders say marketing sends them names without enough context, while marketing says sales ignores the names.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How do you measure whether a campaign is successful?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Pipeline contribution, influenced revenue, registrations, and response rates. The problem is attribution. A person may download a report, attend a webinar, click an ad, and then be claimed by three teams.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): So the issue is not merely sending email; it is establishing a usable account and contact history.

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Yes, although email execution is still painful. We have journeys that are technically sophisticated but require a specialist to change one sentence. That creates a bottleneck.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Who currently maintains those journeys?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Two marketing-operations people. They support all our brands, and one is on parental leave next quarter. We need simpler administration.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would a simpler process look like for the brand teams?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Preapproved templates, reusable segments, and a request workflow. They should be able to launch a standard campaign without touching the scoring model or breaking compliance rules.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That suggests centralized governance with delegated execution. Are there regional consent requirements we should account for?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Canada and Europe are the most sensitive, but our rules are global. We need suppression lists, unsubscribe handling, and a clear record of why someone was eligible.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Are all of your brands using the same CRM instance?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Mostly. One acquired business still uses a separate system, and that is one reason the integration discussion gets complicated.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would that acquired business be part of the first phase?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Probably not. We want to prove the operating model with the main brands first. That’s a counterpoint to the usual “integrate everything on day one” advice.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is a sensible boundary. What does the main-brand database look like?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Roughly 180,000 contacts, but the active marketing audience is smaller. We have duplicates, stale records, and old event lists that should be archived.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Do you have a deduplication owner?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Not really. IT owns the CRM technically, marketing owns usage, and both assume the other team is cleaning it.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then a migration without data governance could reproduce the same problem faster. Would you consider a staged cleanup before expanding automation?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Yes, but not a six-month data project. The business wants visible improvement this quarter.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can define a minimum viable cleanup: required fields, duplicate rules, consent status, and a small set of lifecycle values. More comprehensive enrichment can follow.

Emilio Kulas (CMO): That’s closer to what I need. We have a board review in six weeks, and I want to show faster event follow-up, not a perfect database.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What target would satisfy the board?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Cut event handoff from three days to same-day for standard events. Improve accepted lead rates by giving sales the event, content, and engagement context. And reduce the number of campaign changes that require operations.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Do you have a baseline for accepted lead rates?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): About 31 percent, though the definition changed last year. I wouldn’t make that number a hard benchmark until analytics validates it.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Good qualification. We can use operational metrics first and revenue metrics once the definitions are stable.

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Exactly. Vendors love to promise revenue uplift from automation. We need to be honest about what the system can influence.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would prevent a purchase?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): A long implementation, forced migration of every brand, weak reporting, or a system that only a technical specialist can operate. Also, we’re not replacing our CRM.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. Would your finance team evaluate this against the current Eloqua spend or against the cost of the manual work?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Both. Eloqua is expensive, but replacing it is not automatically cheaper once services and retraining are included.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That’s important. Do you have a renewal date?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): The contract discussion is later this year. We are evaluating now so we’re not negotiating under deadline pressure.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Who else needs to participate?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Marketing operations, sales operations, IT security, and one brand leader who will challenge any central model. Her objections will be useful.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I like that approach. For the next session, I suggest mapping one event campaign from intake through sales acceptance, then testing a standard nurture change.

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Include reporting definitions. If the dashboard cannot distinguish registration, attendance, engagement, and accepted lead, the demo won’t help.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll build the agenda around those stages and bring an integration specialist. We can also discuss the separate acquired-business system, but we’ll label it phase two.

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Good. Don’t let phase two sneak into the first estimate.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): It won’t. I’ll send a summary with current-state pain, desired outcomes, constraints, and open questions.

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Add that our teams are distributed and not all brands will adopt at the same pace. A rollout that assumes uniform behavior will fail.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Noted. Adoption sequencing is part of the design, not an afterthought.

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Then we have a useful next step. Send me the agenda and I’ll bring the operations lead.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll do that tomorrow. Thanks again, Emilio.

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Thank you, Marcus. And if you can make the next meeting shorter than the weather forecast, you’ll already have a point in your favor.
