Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Cicero, thanks for meeting with me. I know this is close to the end of the year for many education teams, so I appreciate the time. How is the week going?

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): Busy in the predictable way. Everyone wants to fill next term’s pipeline before they disappear for the holidays. We are trying to promote three programmes with one small demand-generation team.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That gives us a useful place to begin. What are you hoping marketing automation would improve?

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): Follow-up and visibility. We collect enquiries from open days, paid search, partner schools, and the website. They all land in different places. A person can attend an open day, download a guide, and speak with an admissions adviser, but we do not see that as one journey.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Which system is the source of truth today?

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): Our CRM is supposed to be. In practice, admissions keeps notes in the CRM, marketing has campaign spreadsheets, and the website platform has its own form records. We reconcile them when a report is due.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What happens when an enquiry is ready for admissions?

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): Sometimes it is routed immediately. Sometimes the person is added to a general newsletter because nobody knows which programme they asked about. That is the embarrassing part. We do not want a generic message going to someone who asked about a specific course.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What would define a qualified enquiry for your team?

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): Programme interest, start term, location preference, and whether they are a domestic or international applicant. But some of those questions are not asked on every form because the admissions team worries about making the form too long.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Could you collect the minimum fields initially and ask the rest progressively?

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): Maybe. Our web manager is concerned that progressive profiling sounds simple until it breaks on mobile. We have a lot of mobile traffic.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is worth testing rather than assuming. We can start with the current forms, use the information already available, and run a small experiment with one programme. Would the business accept a pilot that does not redesign every form?

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): Yes, provided it does not create another report that only marketing can read. Admissions needs to understand the queue and the next action.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Then the workflow should show the owner, programme, urgency, and reason for the recommendation in plain language. What response time are you targeting?

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): For open-day attendees, one working day. For general downloads, three days is acceptable. International enquiries sometimes need a different process because the adviser may be in another region.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): So the routing rules should vary by source and applicant type. Who owns those decisions?

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): Admissions operations owns them, but they are not on this call. I can bring them into a requirements session. I do not want to invent rules that they later reject.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Good. I will make that a next step. What concerns do you have about adopting a new platform?

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): Migration. We have years of enquiry history, but not all of it has reliable consent. Also, our previous automation project produced duplicate records. People still remember that, especially the admissions staff who had to clean it up manually.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We should separate historical reference from marketable contacts. A migration plan can preserve records for reporting while excluding anyone whose permission is unclear. We can also test deduplication on a sample before any larger import.

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): I would need the privacy officer involved. There are different consent expectations for prospective students and for people attending public events.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Absolutely. We can document the consent sources and the permitted communication types before building journeys. What would the first useful journey be?

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): Probably open-day follow-up. The timing is clear, and admissions knows what the message should achieve. A reminder, a programme-specific resource, then a task if the person has not engaged.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Would the task go to an admissions adviser or a central queue?

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): Central queue first. Assignment by programme can happen later. We do not have enough advisers to create a complex territory model.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is a sensible first version. We can add programme context without making assignment overly clever. How would you measure success?

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): Attendance-to-application progression, adviser response time, and whether people stop receiving irrelevant general messages. I would also watch unsubscribes, but not treat every unsubscribe as a failure.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is a thoughtful distinction. Some people unsubscribe because they have chosen a different path, and suppressing them may be the correct outcome. We can report on source, programme, and consent status rather than only campaign engagement.

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): Exactly. One vendor showed us a dashboard full of clicks and called it success. Our director asked how many applications resulted, and the room went quiet.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We should define the handoff and the reporting before building the dashboard. Otherwise we will measure activity because it is available.

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): Agreed. There is another complication: our academic calendar. Engagement falls sharply during exam periods, then spikes near application deadlines. A journey that looks weak in May may be fine in September.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can annotate the reporting with term dates and compare like periods. We should avoid making a platform decision from one unusual month.

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): Good. Budget is also limited. The opportunity is marked lost, and I cannot reopen a purchase discussion without a clearer scope. If the pilot requires a large implementation, it will not proceed.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Then I will propose a bounded discovery and pilot plan, with an explicit effort estimate. We can review whether your existing team can administer it after training. If not, that is a constraint to solve before commercial approval.

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): I would want admissions operations, the web manager, and privacy in the next conversation. Finance can join once we know the shape of the work.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will send a requirements worksheet focused on sources, consent, routing, and outcomes. We can use the open-day journey as the example and leave the other programmes for a later phase.

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): That feels manageable. I am interested, but I do not want another system that promises a single view and gives us another reconciliation project.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is a fair test. We will treat data quality and ownership as part of the solution, not as footnotes. Thanks for the candid discussion.

Cicero O'Connell (Demand Generation): Thank you. Send the worksheet after the holidays, and I will coordinate the group.
