Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Presley, good to see you. How have things been since the last review?

Presley Hoppe-Brekke (Procurement): Busy, mostly. We’re in the middle of our summer planning cycle, so I appreciate keeping this focused.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Absolutely. I’d like to review the program status, outcomes against the original goals, and the commercial path for the marketing-automation initiative. The opportunity is currently marked closed and lost with a July fifteenth close date, so I want to make sure our records reflect what actually happened.

Presley Hoppe-Brekke (Procurement): That’s accurate as far as procurement is concerned. The project did not proceed with your proposal. It doesn’t mean the business has abandoned automation altogether.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That distinction is useful. Could we start with the business outcome you were targeting?

Presley Hoppe-Brekke (Procurement): Gutkowski wanted better campaign coordination across retail teams in the UK. Marketing had several disconnected processes, and procurement was asked to evaluate a platform that could reduce manual work and improve visibility. The problem was that the requirements kept shifting.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Shifting in what way?

Presley Hoppe-Brekke (Procurement): Initially, they wanted campaign scheduling and lead handoff. Then store-level segmentation became important. After that, legal raised questions about consent records and data retention. By the time we compared the final proposals, the team was evaluating a broader operating model than the one discussed in the first meetings.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That explains some of the changes we saw. Did our proposal fail on functionality, commercial terms, or process?

Presley Hoppe-Brekke (Procurement): A combination. The price was not the only issue, but it was visible. Your offer was also harder to compare because some services were described in the implementation section and others in the subscription section. Our committee wanted a simple three-year view.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): So packaging created uncertainty even where the underlying capability might have been acceptable?

Presley Hoppe-Brekke (Procurement): Yes. And your implementation estimate assumed that Gutkowski would provide clean data and a dedicated administrator. We don’t currently have either. The business team liked the workflow demonstration, but they didn’t want to commit to a program that required a lot of internal cleanup before anyone saw value.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What did the winning alternative do differently?

Presley Hoppe-Brekke (Procurement): It was not a direct one-for-one comparison. One option was an extension to an existing platform, which made the procurement process easier even though the marketing capabilities were narrower. Another vendor offered a phased start with fewer teams. The committee considered risk and adoption, not just feature coverage.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Was there a named competitor we should record?

Presley Hoppe-Brekke (Procurement): No single competitor was selected in the final record. Please don’t enter one just to fill the field. The decision was between doing a smaller extension, postponing, or choosing a different platform later.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. How did our evaluation process feel from your side?

Presley Hoppe-Brekke (Procurement): Your team was responsive, but too many conversations began with a demonstration before the requirements were stable. We had three different stakeholder groups and each heard a slightly different story. Procurement received the commercial details late, which made it harder to build the comparison.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That’s fair. Did the retail teams identify any positive results from the pilot work?

Presley Hoppe-Brekke (Procurement): They did. The campaign calendar was easier to understand, and the handoff notifications were clearer than their current email process. One regional manager said the ability to see who owned a follow-up was useful. But those benefits were not measured against a baseline, so they remained qualitative.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): If the project reopens, what would need to be different?

Presley Hoppe-Brekke (Procurement): First, one accountable business sponsor. Second, a requirements document signed off before demonstrations. Third, a realistic data-readiness plan with named internal owners. Fourth, pricing that separates subscription, onboarding, optional services, and renewal assumptions. Finally, legal needs the consent and retention answers early.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would a smaller initial deployment be acceptable?

Presley Hoppe-Brekke (Procurement): Potentially, but only if the phase-one scope is genuinely useful. A pilot that produces another isolated database will not be approved. It needs a clear exit criterion and a decision about how it connects to the rest of the business.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How far out should we think about timing?

Presley Hoppe-Brekke (Procurement): Not this quarter. The current budget is committed, and the team is dealing with a broader retail systems review. We might revisit in the next planning cycle, but I don’t want that treated as a forecasted opportunity.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll record it as a possible future evaluation, not an active pipeline commitment. Is there any documentation you want from us now?

Presley Hoppe-Brekke (Procurement): A concise closeout summary would help. Include what was demonstrated, what remained open, the commercial structure, and the assumptions about data and staffing. Please don’t send another full presentation.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I can do that. Before we finish, were there concerns about vendor support or implementation risk beyond the staffing assumption?

Presley Hoppe-Brekke (Procurement): Support coverage was a question, not a rejection point. Implementation risk was more about our readiness. People sometimes say the project was lost because procurement blocked it, but that’s too simple. The business had not agreed on the operating model.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That context matters. Is it all right if I ask whether procurement would participate earlier next time?

Presley Hoppe-Brekke (Procurement): Yes. Bring us in before the commercial structure is fixed. We can tell you what needs to be comparable and what language will create review delays.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Helpful. I’ll send the closeout summary by Friday and mark the opportunity outcome as lost without naming a competitor. Thank you for being candid, Presley.

Presley Hoppe-Brekke (Procurement): No problem. The relationship is fine; the project simply wasn’t ready to move. Enjoy the rest of your day.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): You too.
