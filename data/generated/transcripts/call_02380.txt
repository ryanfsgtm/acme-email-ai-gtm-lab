Priya Shah (Account Executive): Tamara, thanks for joining. Before we dive in, I saw your office is in the United States, but I won’t assume that means sunny. How is your week going?

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): Busy and reasonably sunny, so I can’t complain. We are a 79-person financial-services firm, and marketing operations is basically me plus a part-time coordinator. I’m looking for leverage, not a giant transformation programme.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a useful frame. What prompted the search now?

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): Our current tools can send campaigns, but they do not give us a reliable view of the buyer journey. We have website forms, event lists, and CRM records in separate places. I spend too much time deduplicating contacts and building reports that nobody trusts.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What does a good outcome look like six months from now?

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): A prospect fills out a form, gets the right follow-up, and the sales owner sees the history without me manually writing a summary. I also want to know which campaigns create useful conversations, not just clicks.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How are leads routed today?

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): A form notification goes to a shared inbox. If it is obviously a request, someone assigns it. If it is a research download, it may sit there. We have a round-robin rule in the CRM, but it ignores industry and sometimes sends a regulated-finance inquiry to the wrong person.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can build routing around region, account type, product interest, and explicit intent. Would you want marketing to control the rules?

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): Marketing should own the campaign logic, but sales operations should approve assignment rules. I don’t want to be blamed for a territory dispute.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That division makes sense. What data is available today?

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): About 9,000 contacts, though only perhaps 2,500 are currently marketable. Some records are old clients, some are prospects, and some are people who opted out years ago. The CRM has account relationships but inconsistent job titles.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can start with a data audit and keep suppressed records out of active campaigns. We would map the lifecycle stages and agree on what qualifies as marketing-ready.

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): Please emphasize suppression. In financial services, compliance reviews are not theoretical. We need a clear history of consent and a way to prove why an email was sent.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can show consent status, subscription preferences, and campaign history in the contact record. We should also involve your compliance reviewer in the workflow design.

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): Good. Another problem is nurture. We have one generic newsletter. It is easy to send, but it is not relevant to a small-business owner versus a treasury director.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How many audiences would be manageable initially?

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): Two. Small-business finance and mid-market treasury. If we start with six segments, I will be the person maintaining six segments.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then two journeys with shared components would be a sensible first release. We can add branching later if the data supports it.

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): Exactly. I’m wary of demos where every possibility looks simple because the presenter is doing it.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Fair criticism. In the next session we can configure one example using your actual fields and show the admin steps. That will reveal the effort more honestly.

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): I’d appreciate that. We also need a dashboard for leadership. They ask for ROI, but our opportunity stages are not consistently updated.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can separate operational metrics from financial attribution: delivery and engagement, lead acceptance, meeting conversion, and influenced pipeline with a confidence note. Would that be credible internally?

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): Yes. I would rather show “data incomplete” than manufacture a precise return number. Our CFO will notice.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Who besides the CFO will be involved?

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): The marketing director, sales operations, IT, compliance, and two sales leaders. The final business owner is the marketing director. IT will review integration and security, while compliance reviews consent and retention.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): And the budget process?

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): We have budget allocated for this financial year, but the purchase is not automatic. We need a business case and a pilot plan. The current opportunity amount is $20,682, and I’m treating that as a working estimate, not approved spend.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. We can prepare a proposal with the platform, implementation assumptions, and an optional phased rollout so the decision team can see what is essential.

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): Phased is preferable. Phase one should include the CRM connection, data cleanup rules, two nurture journeys, and basic routing. Advanced scoring can wait.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would you use to judge the pilot?

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): Time to launch a campaign, reduction in manual list work, response time to high-intent forms, and whether sales actually uses the activity history. If reps ignore it, the technology has not solved anything.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Could we interview two sales users before configuring the view?

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): Yes, and choose one enthusiastic user and one sceptic. Otherwise you will only hear the people who already want a new system.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That will make the design better. What concerns might stop the project?

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): Data migration risk, compliance sign-off, and the hidden administration burden. Also, if the integration requires a custom development project, we may pause.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I’ll bring a solutions consultant to validate the integration and identify what is standard configuration versus custom work. We’ll document assumptions instead of burying them in the proposal.

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): Thank you. Can you include training for the coordinator? I do not want the system dependent on one person.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Yes. We can include role-based training, a short operating guide, and a review after the first campaign.

Tamara Mohr (Marketing Operations): That sounds reasonable. I need the next meeting to be practical, though. A broad overview will not help me decide.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I’ll send a draft agenda: contact and consent model, one routing path, one nurture journey, reporting definitions, integration questions, and implementation estimate.
