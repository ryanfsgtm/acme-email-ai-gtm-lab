Priya Shah (Account Executive): Pearl, good to see you. How is the spring retail calendar treating you?

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): Badly, but predictably. We have three launches, two loyalty pushes, and someone wants a flash sale in the middle of it.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds busy. For this QBR, I would like to understand how the automation program is performing, what happened after the Marketo decision, and where renewal risk might be hiding.

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): The Marketo migration is the obvious place to start. We went live last quarter with the implementation partner, and I expected more disruption than we actually had.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What made the rollout manageable?

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): The partner. They were certified, knew the migration path, and forced us to clean up ownership before anyone touched the workflows. It was still stressful, but we had a person who could answer, “What happens to this old program?” without opening six tickets.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is helpful context. Our general impression is that Marketo migrations tend to be risky. Was your experience uniformly smooth?

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): No. The launch was faster than our original estimate, but the first reporting cycle was messy. The partner could move programs; they could not magically resolve our inconsistent campaign naming. We spent two weeks reconciling numbers.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): So the implementation partner reduced execution risk, but governance still mattered.

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): Exactly. I would not tell another company that certification alone guarantees success. It gave us a competent guide, not immunity from bad source data.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I also wanted to ask about data residency. Has that become a material concern for your teams?

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): Yes, especially with our Nordic and central European operations. Our privacy counsel has started asking vendors whether customer data can remain in the EU. EU-only data residency is low-volume overall but rising recently and commercially significant in European enterprise deals.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Is that a requirement for every account or more of a procurement filter?

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): More of a filter today. Most of our business can proceed with the standard arrangement, but our larger regulated customers increasingly want EU-only processing and a clear list of subprocessors. If a vendor cannot answer quickly, the deal may stall before marketing is even involved.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Has your own organization changed anything because of that request?

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): We have added residency and access questions to our vendor review. We are not moving every workload tomorrow. The change is that privacy is now involved at the beginning rather than at the final signature.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. Let us talk deliverability. Have you seen any change in inbox placement since the migration?

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): We did see a dip during the first high-volume promotion. High-volume customers moved to shared IP infrastructure experience inbox-placement degradation and elevated churn risk.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Was that your situation?

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): Partly. We were on shared infrastructure for one segment, and reputation weakened during the promotion. But the bigger issue was our list hygiene. We had old loyalty contacts and a burst of low-engagement addresses. After suppression and throttling, placement improved.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): So shared infrastructure was a contributor, not the only cause.

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): Correct. People like a single explanation, but deliverability was a combination of volume, engagement, content, and infrastructure. We also learned that the accounts with the most revenue at stake were not always the accounts sending the most messages.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds like a renewal-risk question.

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): It is. We now monitor inbox placement and complaint trends for our biggest customer programs separately. A small degradation in a high-value program deserves attention sooner than a noisy dip in a low-value campaign.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What operational changes have you made?

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): Pre-send checks, gradual volume ramps, suppression rules, and a weekly review between revenue operations and lifecycle marketing. We also documented who can approve an exceptional send. Before, the answer was basically whoever was awake.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How are the business teams responding?

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): Better than expected. The first time we paused a campaign, people were annoyed. Then they saw that the alternative was explaining a missed launch to the executive team. The process has more credibility now.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Any unresolved concerns with the platform?

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): Reporting still needs work. Our migration preserved activity, but the historical taxonomy is uneven. We can report forward cleanly; comparing this quarter with the same quarter last year requires caveats.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would a taxonomy project help?

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): Yes, but it is not glamorous. We need standard campaign types, channel names, audience definitions, and ownership. If we automate bad labels, we just create bad reports faster.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Agreed. Would you consider expanding the partner's role for that work?

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): Possibly. They understand the technical structure, but our internal team needs to own the definitions. We should not outsource the meaning of our funnel.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a sensible boundary. For the next quarter, what would make the program successful?

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): Stable deliverability during the summer promotion, cleaner executive reporting, and a documented privacy answer for European prospects. If we can do those three, I will call the migration a success.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will capture those as the QBR actions. We can provide a deliverability review, a reporting-taxonomy workshop, and the current residency documentation for your privacy team.

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): Send the residency material first. That question is coming up in two active enterprise evaluations.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Will do. For deliverability, should we include the partner?

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): Yes, but keep the meeting focused. Our partner is excellent at implementation details and less interested in debating commercial priorities.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Noted. Anything else before we close?

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): One small thing: do not frame the migration as “done.” It is operational now, but we are still tuning it. That wording matters because people stop checking systems they think are finished.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Fair correction. We will describe it as live and under optimization.

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): Good. Then I have no other fires for today.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will send the action summary tomorrow.

Pearl Fahey (Revenue Operations): Thanks, Priya. And if the flash sale appears, I am forwarding it to you.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will add it to the risk register.
