Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Wendy, thanks for meeting. I saw the account is in discovery around marketing automation, with Customer.io listed as another option. I would like to understand the workflow before discussing whether our approach fits.

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): That works. We are a twelve-person healthcare practice, and I am the only person doing marketing operations. I need something manageable, not a second full-time job.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What are you trying to improve?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): Appointment reminders, educational newsletters, and follow-up after an inquiry. Right now, reminders are partly manual. The front desk keeps a spreadsheet, and I export lists from our scheduling system.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What happens when a patient changes an appointment?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): The scheduling system updates, but the spreadsheet may not. We have caught a few mismatches before an email went out. That is why the clinical director is nervous about automation.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That concern is reasonable. Would the first phase need appointment reminders, or could it begin with educational content?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): Educational content first would be safer. Appointment reminders are more valuable, but they require a reliable integration and approval from compliance.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Who owns compliance approval?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): The practice administrator coordinates it. The clinical director reviews language. We cannot put diagnosis or treatment details into marketing messages.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Understood. What information can safely be used for segmentation?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): General interest, source of inquiry, and whether someone has opted in. I would avoid detailed clinical categories unless counsel says otherwise.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That gives us a narrow starting point. How are inquiries captured?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): Website form, phone calls, and referrals. Phone inquiries are typed in later, sometimes with missing source information.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Could a required source field help?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): It could, but the front desk will choose “other” if the form is annoying.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Then adoption is part of the design. We could use a short list and permit an optional note.

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): Yes. The fewer required fields, the better.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): How do you measure success today?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): Mostly newsletter clicks and calls reported by staff. We do not have a clean line from an email to a booked appointment.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What would you trust?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): A tagged link and a simple booking confirmation. I do not need a complicated attribution model.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can start there. What made Customer.io attractive?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): It seems flexible and less expensive. A consultant also told us it would be quick to launch.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What makes our option worth comparing?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): Support and governance, maybe. I need somebody to answer when an integration behaves strangely. But I do not want to pay for features we will never use.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is fair. We can scope an initial package around one audience, two content journeys, consent, and reporting. We should not include appointment automation until the data path is tested.

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): Can we test with fake records?

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Yes, and then with a small internal audience. Production activation should wait for compliance sign-off.

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): Good. Another issue: our newsletter list includes old contacts. Some have no recorded consent.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Then list hygiene comes before sending. Do you know how many are affected?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): Not yet. Maybe a third, but that is a rough estimate.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We should verify rather than build the plan around the estimate.

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): Agreed.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What would the administrator need to see before approving a project?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): Cost, implementation time, security answers, and a clear statement of who owns the system. If it all comes back to me, she may say no.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Who could share ownership?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): The front desk can own intake quality. I can own campaigns. The administrator can own policy and budget.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is a workable model. Would a working session with those three people help?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): Probably, but not until I have a concise proposal. They will not attend a broad demo.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will prepare a workflow diagram, assumptions, and a list of open compliance questions. Is there a decision date?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): We are exploring this now. The March date in the opportunity is old; it should not be treated as a deadline.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will mark timing as unconfirmed. What is the next concrete action on your side?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): I will ask IT for the scheduling-system integration documentation and audit the consent field. If those are impossible, the project may stop at newsletters.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is a useful boundary. I will send the recap and a sample test plan.

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): Please include how unsubscribes are handled across channels.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will. Anything else?

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): Keep the language plain. We have clinicians in the review, and technical jargon slows them down.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Understood. Thanks, Wendy.

Wendy Hegmann (Marketing Operations): Thank you. I am interested, but we need to prove the safety and workload first.
