Junior Stracke (Marketing Operations): We have a user UUID in the application and a contact ID in Salesforce. The mapping is stored in a service table, but it is not exposed through the current Segment destination.

Junior Stracke (Marketing Operations): Parent account plus workspaces. One customer can have several workspaces, and usage is meaningful at workspace level.
Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Would marketing target the person, the workspace, or the parent account?
Junior Stracke (Marketing Operations): Usually the person, but the message needs workspace context. We’ve had incidents where an admin received usage information for the wrong workspace.
Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That’s a data-isolation concern. We should include workspace ID in the event payload and enforce it in audience construction.
Junior Stracke (Marketing Operations): Can your platform prevent a person from entering two workspace journeys?
Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): It can enforce a person-level frequency limit, but that is not the same as workspace-level exclusivity. We’d need an explicit suppression or arbitration rule.
Junior Stracke (Marketing Operations): Explain the difference in practical terms.
Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): If a person belongs to Workspace A and B, a person-level limit may block the second message globally. A workspace rule can decide which workspace owns the next communication, or permit both with separate limits.
Junior Stracke (Marketing Operations): We need the latter, but our business rules are not written down.
Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Then that becomes a design decision, not an integration detail. Who can approve it?
Junior Stracke (Marketing Operations): Product operations, probably. I can propose the options, but I can’t choose on their behalf.
Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Understood. Now event volume: what is your peak daily count?

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Which events are activation-worthy?
Junior Stracke (Marketing Operations): Trial started, integration connected, report shared, and usage dropped. We also have dozens of debugging events that should never leave the warehouse.
Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We can whitelist the four event types and discard the rest at the source or destination. Do you need historical backfill?
