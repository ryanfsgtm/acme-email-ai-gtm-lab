Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Hettie, thanks for taking the call. I know lifecycle marketing is usually juggling several campaigns at once. How is the week going?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): Busy but manageable. We are a team of four supporting a retail business with one hundred four employees, so everyone owns at least three things. I can give you twenty-seven minutes before our planning meeting.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That works. I would like to understand your current marketing process, what you want to improve, and whether ACME would fit. I saw that the earlier opportunity is now closed-lost, so I am treating this as discovery rather than assuming a purchase decision.

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): That is fair. We looked at automation because our abandoned-cart and post-purchase programs are too manual. The loss was not because nobody cared; the business case got squeezed and we could not get a clean answer on pricing.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): When you say pricing, was the issue the annual amount, the implementation cost, or the contact model?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): Both, honestly. At one point we thought ACME charged for every contact stored in the database. Then someone explained it might be active marketing contacts only, but by then finance had decided the number was outside our comfort zone. So there were two different concerns mixed together.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That distinction is important. Could you talk me through the numbers you were using?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): We have around eighteen thousand customer records, but only maybe six thousand receive marketing messages in a normal month. Another three thousand are suppressed or inactive. Finance saw the eighteen thousand figure and compared it with the annual quote. Marketing was looking at the six thousand.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): If the stored records and active marketing audience were priced differently, would the affordability concern disappear?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): No. It would reduce the concern, but not eliminate it. Our budget is tight, and the return would need to be visible quickly. The six-thousand-contact scenario might be possible. A price based on all eighteen thousand was not.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That helps separate the issues. What return would the team need to demonstrate?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): More completed purchases from cart recovery and fewer hours spent building lists. We currently export a file, clean it, check suppression tags, and upload it. That takes two people most of a day whenever we launch a larger campaign.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How frequently does that happen?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): Twice a month for major campaigns, plus smaller sends. The cleanup is not always a full day, but it is enough that people dread it. We also make mistakes—usually not catastrophic, but embarrassing.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What kind of mistakes?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): A customer who purchased yesterday receiving a “complete your first purchase” email, or a regional offer going to the wrong store group. Those are data and timing problems, not a lack of creative ideas.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What systems are involved?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): The ecommerce platform, a loyalty database, and our CRM. The ecommerce platform has order events, the loyalty database has preferences, and the CRM has customer service history. None of them agrees perfectly on the customer ID.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Which source should control marketing eligibility?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): Loyalty preferences should control consent. Orders should control purchase status. The CRM should provide service flags. We have not formalized that, but that is how we think about it.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is a useful starting model. Who would own the integration and audience rules?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): I would own the rules, but I am not an engineer. Our ecommerce agency could help with the connector, although they charge separately. That implementation cost is another reason finance hesitated.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): If we created a phased plan, what would be the smallest useful first phase?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): Cart recovery and a post-purchase welcome series. The audience is understandable, and we can measure revenue and unsubscribe rates. I would not start with every loyalty segment.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How long would you need to evaluate results?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): Probably six to eight weeks. We have seasonal variation, so two weeks would be meaningless. The board is asking for a quick answer, but the business does not behave on a neat demo schedule.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That makes sense. Would finance consider a pilot if the active-contact definition and implementation costs were explicit?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): Possibly. They need a ceiling. They do not want a low starting quote that becomes a surprise when inactive records are included or when the agency invoices us.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I can document the questions for the commercial team: what counts as stored versus active, how suppressed contacts are treated, what happens if the audience grows, and which connector work is included. Would that help?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): Yes, especially the stored-versus-active explanation. That was the part people kept misunderstanding. But please do not write that price was the only reason we stopped. Even the lower estimate may have been hard for us this year.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. Apart from budget, what could prevent adoption?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): Staff capacity. We cannot buy a system that needs a full-time administrator. Also, the retail team wants local-store reporting, and if that requires a complicated data model, we may delay.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Could local-store reporting be a later phase while the first phase uses region and campaign?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): Yes, if we agree that before signing. We have been burned by “easy later” promises that became mandatory setup work.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will separate required launch configuration from optional expansion. What would a credible internal decision path look like now?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): First, finance reviews a corrected pricing scenario. Second, the ecommerce agency estimates integration. Third, I run a business case using our current manual hours and cart-recovery baseline. Then our marketing director decides whether to request budget.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Is the marketing director engaged?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): Yes, but cautiously. She supports the problem, not necessarily this product. If the numbers are vague, the project will stay on the backlog.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What baseline can you provide?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): We have a rough cart-abandonment rate and monthly recovered revenue from manual sends. The data is imperfect because the tracking code changed in March. I can provide ranges rather than pretending precision.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Ranges are better than false precision. I will send a discovery summary with the two pricing concerns separated, the phased use case, integration questions, and the decision path. Could we reconvene after finance and the agency respond?

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): Yes, but not next week. Give me two weeks. If the corrected model still exceeds our ceiling, we should say that plainly and stop spending time.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Agreed. I will propose a meeting in two weeks and leave the date flexible until you have the estimates.

Hettie Cormier (Lifecycle Marketing): Good. The conversation was more useful than the previous pricing call because it recognized that “too expensive” was not one clean objection.
