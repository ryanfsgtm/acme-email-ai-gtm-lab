Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Carissa, good morning. Before we start, I see the dashboard says rain in Brussels. Is that accurate, or is it just the dashboard being dramatic?

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): It is accurate. We’ve had rain all week, so at least the dashboard is getting one thing right.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Fair enough. For today’s QBR, I’d like to review performance, hear what has changed at Nienow - Wunsch, and agree on priorities for the next quarter. I know you’re evaluating ActiveCampaign as part of the broader review, so I’ll make space for that too.

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): Yes, the evaluation is still open. We’re a ninety-four-person healthcare company, and the team has grown without our processes growing with it. We need to decide whether to deepen the current setup or simplify.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What has gone well since the last review?

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): The onboarding sequence is much more consistent. New inquiries receive a response quickly, and regional teams can see which contacts are active. That has helped. The reporting is less settled, though.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What feels unsettled about it?

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): Different teams define conversion differently. One region counts a booked consultation, another counts a qualified lead, and a third counts a completed intake. The platform is showing numbers, but we are not always discussing the same number.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That sounds like a measurement-governance issue rather than a missing chart. Would a shared lifecycle definition help?

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): Yes, but it needs sales and clinical operations involved. Marketing can propose it, but we cannot declare the official definition alone.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Let’s note that as a cross-functional action. How is campaign execution going for the regional teams?

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): Better in the Benelux group. They use the templates and follow the approval process. The smaller teams still build one-off sends because they feel the central process is slower.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Is the slowdown caused by approvals or by template flexibility?

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): Both, probably. Compliance wants review for patient-facing language, but not every internal update needs the same level of scrutiny. We have not separated those paths clearly.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Could we create different approval tiers?

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): Possibly. I don’t want a system where people click through a warning just because there are too many warnings. The controls need to match the risk.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Agreed. What would you like to improve in the next quarter?

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): First, attribution. Second, better segmentation based on service interest. Third, a way to identify contacts who need human follow-up without asking staff to monitor five reports.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): For the follow-up use case, would an alert in the CRM be preferable to an email?

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): Usually, yes. But some coordinators do not live in the CRM. If the alert only appears there, it will be missed. We need to understand the actual workflow, not the ideal one.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That’s helpful. We can map the roles before choosing the notification path. Are there privacy or security changes we should account for?

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): Our privacy advisor is reviewing retention. We also want to be more precise about consent for educational content versus commercial follow-up. A person may agree to one and not the other.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Does the current data model distinguish those purposes?

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): Partially. Some forms do, some still use a single checkbox. We need to fix the source forms; no platform can make an ambiguous collection practice magically clear.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That will affect the expansion plan. What additional volume or capability are you considering?

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): More regional journeys and possibly event-based triggers. But I’m cautious about adding triggers before the data definitions are stable. More automation can multiply confusion quickly.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would a controlled pilot with one region make sense?

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): Yes. The Benelux team is organized enough to test it, but it should include a less mature team eventually. Otherwise we only prove that a capable team can use the system.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Excellent point. We can use Benelux for the initial design and then test the process with another region before rolling it out.

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): Exactly. Also, please separate the business case from the product comparison. ActiveCampaign may be cheaper in one configuration, but migration effort and reporting continuity have value too.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. What would leadership need to see for a decision?

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): A baseline of current response times, a realistic estimate of hours saved, the effect on qualified consultations, and the compliance work required. I don’t want a forecast built entirely from assumed uplift.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can build the baseline from the last two quarters and label assumptions separately. Would a decision in June still be realistic?

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): Possibly. The opportunity is in evaluation, not approved. Procurement will need a recommendation, and our privacy review could change the scope. I would rather make a sound decision in July than force a June signature.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That’s reasonable. For next steps, I’ll prepare a performance summary, propose the lifecycle-definition workshop, map notification workflows, and outline a one-region pilot with a second-team validation.

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): Add the consent-purpose cleanup and retention review. And include the current subscription cost beside any expansion estimate.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Will do. Is there anything you would not want us to change yet?

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): Do not change the onboarding sequence until we understand why it is working. We have a habit of redesigning the part that is finally stable because a new feature appears.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a very useful constraint. We’ll preserve the working sequence and focus the experiment elsewhere.

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): Good. If the analysis is grounded in our actual operations, we’ll have a productive decision conversation.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Thanks, Carissa. I’ll send the recap with owners and open questions this afternoon.

Carissa Hand (VP Marketing): Thanks, Amelia. And good luck with the Brussels weather dashboard.
