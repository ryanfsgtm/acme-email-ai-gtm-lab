Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Good morning, Rosendo. Can you hear me clearly? I had a slightly heroic battle with my headset before this call.

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): I can hear you. We had the same battle here, although ours involved a projector and three people insisting it was muted. How are you?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Doing well, thank you. I appreciate you making fifty minutes. I understand Gleason, Lowe and Kuphal is an education organization in France, with roughly 1,774 employees, and that you lead marketing. Before I jump into anything, what prompted you to take a look at marketing automation now?

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): The short answer is that our campaigns have outgrown the way we run them. We have several schools and program teams, different audiences, and a central marketing group trying to coordinate everything through spreadsheets, email tools, and some manual exports. It works, but it is slow and we cannot confidently explain which programs are creating qualified opportunities.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): When you say several schools, are they operating as separate brands or under one parent identity?

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): Both, really. There is a common corporate identity, but prospective students and institutional buyers usually recognize the school or program first. We need local flexibility without losing a central view. That tension has been with us for years.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): And where does the process break most visibly?

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): Lead capture is inconsistent. A webinar registration might land in one list, an inquiry from a landing page in another, and an event attendee in a spreadsheet. Then someone reconciles them. Sometimes the person is already in our database under a slightly different email address. We spend time cleaning rather than planning.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What happens after the data is cleaned?

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): Sales or admissions receives a file. There is no elegant handoff. They may get it daily, or twice a week, depending on the team. We do have a CRM, but not every program uses it in the same way. The result is that follow-up varies a lot.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Is the concern speed, visibility, or both?

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): Both. Speed affects conversion, but visibility is the more embarrassing problem. The board asks which campaigns influenced enrollment or partnership revenue, and we can produce activity counts. We cannot always produce a reliable story from first touch to outcome.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That makes sense. If we were successful, would the first milestone be a unified lead process, campaign attribution, or automated nurture?

Rosendo Waelchi (Account Executive): Unified lead process first. Attribution is important, but if the underlying stages and ownership are inconsistent, a prettier dashboard will not help. Then nurture for people who are interested but not ready.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Who would need to participate in the decision?

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): Marketing owns the initiative. IT will want to understand integrations and security. Admissions and the program directors will need to agree on definitions. Finance will look at the business case. Procurement comes in later, but they are not usually the first obstacle.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What systems would we need to connect?

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): The CRM, our website forms, event registration, and email. We also have a learning platform, though that may be phase two. There are a couple of old databases that should probably be archived rather than integrated. I am saying that as someone who has been asked to integrate them twice.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Fair warning received. How do you define a qualified lead today?

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): It depends on the audience. For a student, it could be an application-ready inquiry with a particular program interest. For an institution, it could be a meeting request from a target account. We have broad categories, but not a shared scoring model.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would a shared model be welcome, or would local teams resist it?

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): They would resist a rigid model. They would welcome useful signals. If the system says someone is hot because they opened an email six times, people will lose confidence. If it says the person attended a program session, visited the pricing page, and requested a conversation, that is more credible.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): So behavior plus context, with human review.

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): Exactly. And consent needs to be handled properly. We operate in France and across Europe, so we cannot treat every address as fair game just because it appears in a file.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What is your current email volume and how sophisticated are the journeys?

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): Moderate volume, but many one-off sends. We have welcome sequences for a few programs and reminders for events. Testing is mostly subject lines. We are not yet doing mature branching journeys. The team knows what it wants to do, but the operational overhead stops us.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): If we started with one journey, which would prove value fastest?

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): An inquiry follow-up journey. Someone asks for information, receives a useful response, gets an invitation to a relevant session, and is routed to the right person when intent is clear. That is common enough to measure.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What metric would you put on the first dashboard?

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): Time to follow-up, accepted handoffs, progression to a meeting or application, and eventually influenced revenue. I do not want open rates treated as the headline.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. What would make an implementation fail internally?

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): If it becomes a central marketing project that everyone else experiences as extra administration. Also if we migrate everything before agreeing what should be retained. And if the first launch tries to cover every school.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): A phased rollout, then. One audience, clear ownership, and a migration policy.

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): Yes. We need a practical plan, not a grand diagram. There is a little concern that automation will make communication impersonal, especially for high-value programs.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is a reasonable concern. Automation can handle timing and routing while leaving the important conversations to people. We should identify where human intervention is mandatory.

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): I like that distinction. One question: can local teams have their own templates and permissions without creating chaos?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Yes, with a governed structure: shared fields and lifecycle rules, local content blocks, approval workflows, and visibility for the central team. We would validate the exact model in a workshop.

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): Good. We are not looking for a platform simply because it has more buttons. We need adoption.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): For next steps, I suggest a technical discovery with IT, a process session with admissions and two program teams, and a value review using the inquiry journey. Does that fit?

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): It does. I will bring our CRM administrator and someone from admissions. I cannot promise the program directors will all attend; getting three calendars aligned is a sport.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can start with two and circulate the output. I will send a recap with the assumptions we heard, the open questions around data and consent, and a draft phased approach.

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): Please include what the migration effort looks like. That is likely to be the first question from IT.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Absolutely. We will separate active records, historical data, duplicates, and the archives. Any final concern before we close?

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): Just measurement. If we cannot show improvement within a quarter, enthusiasm will fade.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then we will define baseline metrics before launch and set a ninety-day checkpoint. Thank you, Rosendo. This was helpful.

Rosendo Waelchi (VP Marketing): Thank you, Marcus. Send the recap, and we will keep moving.
