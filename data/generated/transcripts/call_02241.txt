Julien Kiehn (Demand Generation): Segmentation first. We have retail buyers, suppliers, and partners mixed in the database. Then the active campaign list gets exported, cleaned, and uploaded. Julien Kiehn (Demand Generation): Usually two newsletters a month, plus product announcements and event invitations. In a busy period maybe eight sends. We are not doing sophisticated journeys yet, although that is what management keeps asking about.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What are you using now?

Julien Kiehn (Demand Generation): A mixture of our CRM, a low-cost email tool, and spreadsheets. The email tool is fine for sending, but it does not understand our customer status. We can see opens and clicks, but acting on those signals is clumsy.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): And who would own a new system?

Julien Kiehn (Demand Generation): I would be the day-to-day owner. Our commercial director would approve it, finance would check the numbers, and sales would want visibility into leads. We have sixty-four employees, so nobody wants a six-month implementation project.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. If we could automate the list building and give sales a usable handoff, what would success look like?

Julien Kiehn (Demand Generation): Less time preparing campaigns, fewer accidental sends, and a way to tell whether a contact is actually engaged. We also need consent and unsubscribe handling to be reliable. I don't want to discover after the fact that an old supplier list was included.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can walk through those controls. Let me ask about the database itself. Roughly how many records do you have?

Julien Kiehn (Demand Generation): About twenty-two thousand total, though I doubt more than a few thousand are genuinely active. This is where I got confused in the pricing material. I saw a number for marketing contacts and assumed we would pay for all twenty-two thousand.
