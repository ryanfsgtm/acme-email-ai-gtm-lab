Rod Torphy (Revenue Operations): That’s the part I care about. The builder looks familiar, but migration is where projects acquire a personality.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Especially with a Marketo instance that has years of inherited programs. Before we talk mapping, how many active programs do you believe you have?

Rod Torphy (Revenue Operations): Around eighty active, maybe one hundred and twenty if you count the paused ones. Our CRM says something different from the admin console.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That discrepancy is common. We would inventory programs, assets, fields, and dependencies, then classify them as migrate, redesign, archive, or investigate.

Rod Torphy (Revenue Operations): “Investigate” could become the largest category.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): It often does at first. The useful part is making the uncertainty visible before anyone promises a cutover date.

Rod Torphy (Revenue Operations): We have scoring rules, regional routing, and a few old webinar programs nobody wants to touch. I’m worried that a clean demo hides the ugly pieces.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): It should not. We can run a sample extraction and identify unsupported or ambiguous behavior. For scoring, we’d compare current outcomes with the proposed model rather than translating every rule mechanically.

Rod Torphy (Revenue Operations): That sounds less comforting than “one-click migration,” but more believable.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): It’s also where implementation support matters. Are you planning to do the migration entirely with your internal team?

Rod Torphy (Revenue Operations): Initially, yes. We’re a 156-person professional-services firm. The marketing-operations team is capable, but it is not large.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I want to introduce Anika because she’s joining as the implementation partner on the technical side. Anika, could you describe how you handled the last Marketo migration?

Anika Vogel (Implementation Partner): Sure. The last customer had more than two hundred programs, and the risk looked intimidating in aggregate. We did not move everything at once. We certified the data mappings first, rebuilt five high-value journeys, and ran them in parallel for two weeks.

Rod Torphy (Revenue Operations): What happened to the launch date?

Anika Vogel (Implementation Partner): It moved by about ten days during planning, then the actual launch was earlier than the revised date. We also avoided carrying over several obsolete triggers.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is the pattern we’re proposing here: narrow the first release and use a certified partner for the migration review.

Rod Torphy (Revenue Operations): I can see the appeal, though partner involvement adds cost and another relationship to manage.

Anika Vogel (Implementation Partner): It adds a line item, yes. It can also reduce the amount of internal time spent reverse-engineering inherited behavior. We would document every decision so your team is not dependent on us forever.

Rod Torphy (Revenue Operations): That last bit matters. We don’t want a managed-service arrangement by accident.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): The opportunity is 17,504 and currently marked closed-lost against Marketo. I know the commercial outcome is already recorded, so today is a retrospective and capability review, not a promise that the deal is reopening.

Rod Torphy (Revenue Operations): Correct. The loss was not simply “Marketo is too hard.” We paused because our board wanted a lower implementation risk and we had an internal hiring gap.

Anika Vogel (Implementation Partner): That distinction is important. A complex platform does not make every migration unsuccessful. The implementation conditions change the result.

Rod Torphy (Revenue Operations): We also had one small business unit migrate from Marketo two years ago without a partner. It took longer than expected, but the team eventually got it working.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That’s a useful counterexample. It suggests partner support is an intervention, not a requirement for every organization.

Rod Torphy (Revenue Operations): Exactly. But we can’t assume that success transfers to the current team; the people who did it have left.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then the next diagnostic would be staffing, program complexity, and data quality—not just competitor history.

Rod Torphy (Revenue Operations): What would you measure before deciding?

Anika Vogel (Implementation Partner): Number of active dependencies, percentage of contacts with usable consent and lifecycle fields, volume of custom scoring, and availability of an internal owner. I’d also test one regional journey because regional exceptions tend to expose hidden assumptions.

Rod Torphy (Revenue Operations): We have German and Swiss variants, so yes, regional testing is necessary.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can create a migration-readiness worksheet with those measures. Do not treat the score as a forecast by itself; it is a way to identify work.

Rod Torphy (Revenue Operations): Good. I’d like the worksheet and an example of the certification criteria.

Anika Vogel (Implementation Partner): I’ll send both, including what would cause us to stop a pilot.
