Rex Krajcik (CMO): The immediate expectation is less manual campaign work. We have six business units, and each one has its own spreadsheet logic. The central team spends too much time stitching lists together before anyone can send a message.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): When you say stitching, are you mainly combining contact lists, checking consent, or deciding which audience belongs in each campaign?

Rex Krajcik (CMO): All three, honestly. Consent is the part that makes people nervous, but the audience definition is where the hours disappear. Someone asks for “German buyers who attended an event and have not heard from us in ninety days,” and we start with four exports.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is a useful example. Does the team already have a standard definition for a marketing-ready contact?

Rex Krajcik (CMO): Not one definition. One division counts anyone with an email address. Another excludes old contacts. A third keeps people in the database for reporting but does not want them in campaigns. That is why the stored-versus-usable distinction came up during the buying process.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can help document that distinction before building workflows. Who owns the final rules?

Rex Krajcik (CMO): I own the business decision, but our regional marketing leads will write the practical rules. Legal needs to approve the consent language. IT will help with the data feed, although they are not interested in becoming campaign administrators.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Understood. On the current plan, are you expecting one shared workspace or separate regional operations?

Rex Krajcik (CMO): One workspace with regional permissions. We want shared templates and reporting, but the Austria team should not accidentally email the Netherlands list. We have had close calls with that in the past.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We should include permission design in the kickoff, then. What would make you say the implementation is working by the end of the first month?

Rex Krajcik (CMO): A repeatable campaign can be built without me. Also, the central team should be able to see why a contact entered an audience. Right now, if a director challenges a segment, we can only say that somebody uploaded it on Tuesday.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): So auditability matters as much as speed.

Rex Krajcik (CMO): Yes. Speed without confidence just moves the embarrassment closer to the send button.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Fair point. We can start with one campaign pattern, perhaps event follow-up, and then make the audience logic visible. Would that be representative enough?

Rex Krajcik (CMO): Probably. Our events team has a campaign coming up in March. It is not the largest program, but it has a clean registration source and several regional variations.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Excellent candidate. What systems would supply the registration and customer information?

Rex Krajcik (CMO): The event platform, our CRM, and a finance file for customer tier. The finance file is the ugly one. It is updated weekly and the labels are not consistent.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can flag that as a data-normalization task rather than treating it as a campaign problem. Do you have someone who can explain the finance labels?
