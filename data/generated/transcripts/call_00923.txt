Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Elmer, thanks for joining. I know the account was marked closed-lost, so I’m treating this as a learning and handoff conversation rather than a sales restart.

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): That’s appreciated. We bought a small package, then decided not to expand. I can explain why, although some of it is simply timing.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Let’s start with the original goal.

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): We wanted fewer manual exports. Our consultants generate leads through webinars, referrals, and a website form. Revenue operations cleans the data, assigns an owner, and sends a weekly file to sales.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What made the process difficult?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): Different teams use different definitions. A “qualified enquiry” to one manager is just a person who downloaded a guide to another. We also have duplicate contacts and incomplete company names.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Did the platform help with those issues?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): Partly. The form capture was straightforward, and the notifications worked. But our consultants wanted to change the workflow constantly. Every small request became a discussion about permissions, data fields, or whether the automation should run.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): So control became friction?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): Yes, and the opposite was also true. When we loosened access, someone changed a rule without telling sales. Two leads went to the wrong regional queue. It was fixed, but confidence dipped.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How did the team measure success?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): Response time and accepted leads. We looked at open rates, but nobody trusted them enough to use as a primary measure. The sales managers wanted opportunities, not clicks.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Were opportunity stages reliably maintained?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): No. That was a major weakness. Some consultants updated the CRM after a meeting; some kept notes elsewhere. A dashboard could show a neat funnel, but the underlying stages were stale.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Did that affect the decision to expand?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): Absolutely. We couldn’t tell whether the automation improved conversion or merely made activity easier to see. The small team also didn’t have time for a formal measurement project.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What did users like?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): The visual overview. People could understand the sequence without reading code. And the ability to stop a workflow was useful when a webinar was cancelled.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would have made adoption easier?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): A narrower starting point. We tried to support three service lines at once. Each had different intake questions and ownership rules. We should have picked one service and documented it properly.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That’s a useful retrospective. Were there any objections from privacy or IT?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): Privacy asked about consent and retention. We answered most of it, but deletion was not obvious to the team. IT disliked another integration to monitor, especially because we have no dedicated marketing systems administrator.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Did the commercial terms contribute?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): Somewhat. The amount wasn’t impossible, but it was hard to justify while our data quality remained poor. We decided that process cleanup had to come first.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): If you were starting again, what would you define before configuration?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): A single lifecycle definition, required fields, owner rules, and an exception process. Also, one person with authority to approve changes. We had too many stakeholders and no clear tie-breaker.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Would training have addressed that?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): Training helps with buttons. It doesn’t resolve organisational disagreement. We needed a decision on what counts as an accepted lead.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That distinction is important. Did the lost opportunity reflect dissatisfaction with the product itself?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): Not primarily. We saw useful functionality. The gap was between the promise of a cleaner process and our readiness to maintain one.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Were there specific features you didn’t use?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): Advanced personalisation, mainly. It looked attractive in the demonstration, but our volume was low and our content library was thin. Simple, relevant follow-up would have been enough.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Did anyone request more complex branching?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): A consultant asked about different paths for public-sector and commercial enquiries, but we never implemented it. The data needed for the split was often missing.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That’s a good example of a plausible requirement that wasn’t operationally ready.

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): Exactly. We were seduced by future possibilities. The weekly export was ugly, but it was understood by everyone.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would trigger a future reconsideration?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): More volume, a stable CRM process, and someone accountable for lifecycle operations. If those change, automation becomes more valuable. Before then, another purchase would probably create more work.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Is there anything we could have done differently during the evaluation?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): Show the maintenance burden honestly. Demonstrate a bad record, a missing owner, a revoked consent, and a failed integration. We mostly saw the happy path.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That’s fair feedback. How should I describe the outcome internally?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): Closed-lost because of readiness, data quality, and ownership ambiguity, with product fit only partially tested. Please don’t say we rejected automation entirely.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I’ll record it that way. Any final advice for the next account team?

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): Qualify the operating model before promising sophistication. A small professional-services team may gain more from dependable routing than from a dozen clever journeys.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. Thanks for being candid, Elmer.

Elmer Hettinger (Revenue Operations): No problem. The conversation was useful. If our process matures, I’ll reach out, but there’s no active project now.
