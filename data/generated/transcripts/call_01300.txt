Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Rylan, thanks for making time. I saw the weather in Toronto is doing its usual thing.

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): It is raining sideways, which feels appropriate for a systems evaluation.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Then let us keep the conversation dry and practical. What triggered the review of your lifecycle stack?

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): We have grown to nearly nine hundred employees and our programs have grown sideways too. Customer.io handles some messaging, the CRM handles some sales activity, and product data sits elsewhere. We can launch campaigns, but coordination is fragile.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Which coordination problem hurts most?

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): Audience eligibility. A customer can be in a product-qualified segment, a renewal segment, and an onboarding segment at the same time. We need priority and suppression rules that people can actually inspect.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Are those rules documented today?

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): Some are documented. The important ones are in people’s heads, which is not ideal when someone takes vacation or changes teams.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would you want to keep from Customer.io?

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): Its event flexibility. Product marketers can respond quickly when engineering exposes a useful event. We do not want a platform where every new behavioral signal requires a month-long ticket.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): And what would you change?

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): Governance, reporting, and handoff. We need a shared lifecycle model and better visibility into why a person entered or left a journey. We also need to know whether sales acted on a high-intent signal.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How is ownership divided?

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): Lifecycle Marketing owns the framework. Product Marketing owns several programs. Sales Operations owns routing. Data Engineering owns the warehouse feeds. The boundaries are reasonable on paper; in practice, they overlap.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would a central model with delegated program ownership fit?

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): Probably, but only if delegated teams cannot quietly redefine lifecycle stages. We have had local definitions of “activated” that made executive reporting meaningless.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That sounds like a governance decision we should surface early. What data sources are required?

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): CRM accounts and contacts, product events, subscription status, support signals, and consent. The last one is especially important because we operate in financial services and cannot treat consent as a checkbox added at the end.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What is your current consent architecture?

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): The preference center is separate. We pass status to the messaging tools, but timing is not always obvious. If a person changes preference at 10:02, the team wants confidence that a 10:03 send will not go out.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We should map the propagation path and define the control point. That is more useful than simply saying “the systems integrate.”

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): Agreed. Security will ask about auditability too.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Who needs to participate in the evaluation?

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): Marketing Operations, Data Engineering, Security, Sales Operations, and a product marketer who will challenge every assumption. Finance will care about contact volume and implementation effort.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What is the evaluation timeline?

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): The opportunity is still open. We are collecting requirements now, but the close date on the record is not a commitment. We need a technical workshop before we can say whether the timeline is credible.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I appreciate the distinction. Would a workshop centered on one lifecycle use case be manageable?

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): Yes. Take product-qualified accounts. Show ingestion, eligibility, suppression, sales notification, and reporting. If that works, we can discuss other journeys.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would count as failure?

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): If the audience cannot be explained, if consent changes are delayed or opaque, or if sales receives a notification without an accountable next step. Also if implementation requires us to redesign every data pipeline first.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can document prerequisites and distinguish must-have changes from later improvements. What concerns you about a platform switch?

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): Change fatigue. People have already heard that a new tool will solve everything. I need an honest migration plan, training by role, and a clear decision about what stays in Customer.io during transition.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): A staged coexistence period may reduce disruption, though it can temporarily increase operational complexity.

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): Exactly. “Run both” sounds safe until someone has to reconcile two suppression lists.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We will include that tradeoff. Do you have a preferred next step?

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): Send the workshop agenda and a data-source questionnaire. I will bring the owners together. Please do not send a generic feature deck.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will keep it focused on the product-qualified-account flow and governance questions.

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): Good. If we can make that concrete, I can get the right people engaged.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Thanks, Rylan. Stay dry if possible.

Rylan Tromp (Lifecycle Marketing): I make no promises.
