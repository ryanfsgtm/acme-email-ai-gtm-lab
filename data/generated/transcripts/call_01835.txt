Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Alexander, thanks for meeting. I know privacy reviews tend to multiply once people start asking for examples.

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): They do. I brought notes from Marketing and Legal, so we may discover three different definitions of “ready.”

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That sounds like a useful place to begin. What prompted the team to revisit its marketing process?

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): They want better follow-up for prospective students and alumni. Today, lists are exported, emailed around, and sometimes re-uploaded. Privacy is concerned about that movement, while Marketing is concerned about missing the right audience.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Which concern is more urgent?

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): Both, but the immediate risk is uncontrolled copies. If a spreadsheet has an old consent status, nobody knows which version is authoritative.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What system currently holds the authoritative status?

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): The student information system should. In practice, updates arrive through a nightly feed, and one alumni database is maintained separately. That gap is why people make local files.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): So the process issue may be as important as the platform choice.

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): Exactly. A new tool will not magically correct ownership. We need a rule for which system wins when values disagree.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How are requests to withdraw consent handled now?

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): Usually by email to the privacy inbox. Someone forwards the request to Marketing, and then Marketing removes the person from the next list. It is not elegant, and sometimes the request arrives after a campaign has already been prepared.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What timing would be acceptable?

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): Our policy team has not set one universal number. For promotional email, we want prompt suppression; for legal retention, we may need to keep a record that the request occurred. Those are different actions.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That distinction should be reflected in the workflow. Suppression, deletion, and retention should not be treated as one button.

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): Correct. And we need an audit trail without exposing more personal information than necessary.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would a useful audit record contain?

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): Request date, decision, responsible role, action taken, and completion date. Not a giant dump of the person’s history in every report.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. What does Marketing need from the new process?

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): Segmentation by program interest, country, and language. They also want automated reminders, but they have not agreed whether the reminder is marketing or administrative communication.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That classification could change the consent rule.

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): Yes, and they keep asking me to solve it after the campaign is designed. I want the purpose documented first.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): If you could choose one pilot, what would be safest and most useful?

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): A small alumni re-engagement journey using records with current consent and a single language. We could test suppression, access, reporting, and deletion without involving every department.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Why alumni rather than prospective students?

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): Prospective-student data has more complicated acquisition sources and sometimes includes minors. Alumni is not risk-free, but the population and purpose are easier to define.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That sounds like a carefully bounded first step.

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): It is, although Marketing will complain that it is too small to show revenue impact.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Could the pilot measure process outcomes as well as campaign outcomes?

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): Yes. Time from request to suppression, number of uncontrolled exports, and reconciliation errors are meaningful. A campaign can perform well while the underlying process remains poor.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What access concerns should we consider?

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): Marketing users need to build drafts, but only a small group should publish. IT needs administrative access without becoming the owner of content. Privacy needs visibility into the log, not necessarily permission to edit campaigns.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can map those roles during discovery. Are contractors involved?

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): Possibly for the initial campaign work. That means offboarding and export permissions need attention. Contractors should not be able to download the whole database simply because they need one segment.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Agreed. Least-privilege access and filtered exports should be part of the design.

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): Also, please avoid saying “the platform is compliant” as if that ends the discussion. Compliance depends on configuration, purpose, contracts, and our process.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will frame it as controls the platform can support, then identify what your organization must decide and operate.

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): Good. That makes the conversation productive.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What could stop the project?

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): An unclear data map, no owner for consent definitions, or a demand to launch before Legal signs off. Budget is not the first blocker.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): And what would move it forward?

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): A workshop with Marketing, IT, Legal, and the alumni office. We need a field inventory, purpose statement, consent mapping, role matrix, and pilot success criteria.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I can coordinate that. Would you prefer a ninety-minute session or two shorter ones?

Alexander Gleichner (Privacy): Two shorter ones. The first should settle data and purpose; the second should review the workflow and controls. Otherwise the legal questions consume the whole meeting.
