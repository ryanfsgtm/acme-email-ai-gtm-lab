Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Ethelyn, thanks for joining. Before we start, are we still expecting anyone from marketing or procurement?

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): Marketing may join later. Procurement is not on this call. I was asked to assess whether the proposal creates privacy work that the business case is not acknowledging.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That makes sense. The opportunity was closed as lost after the Marketo comparison, so I’d like to understand what drove the decision and whether there is a useful path forward.

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): The decision was not based on one feature gap. Marketo is already familiar to our organization, and familiarity has value. Your proposal looked easier for marketing to use, but I could not establish that it would be easier for privacy.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What information was missing?

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): Data-location details, retention controls, consent records, and the difference between an unsubscribe and a full deletion request. The answers existed in pieces, but they were spread across product pages and sales explanations. I needed a control-level view.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How many systems would be involved?

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): At least CRM, the website, product telemetry, and our support platform. We have 1,119 employees, so the volume is not trivial. A contact can interact with several business units, and consent is not always collected in the same interface.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Does marketing currently use a centralized preference center?

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): Partly. Some preferences are centralized; event registrations and partner imports are less consistent. The dangerous assumption would be that one checkbox represents every purpose. It does not.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would you need to see in a demonstration?

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): A complete journey from collection to activation to deletion. Show what gets stored, who can access it, how a person changes a preference, and what happens downstream. I do not need a glossy campaign example.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. Would a sample contact record be enough?

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): Not by itself. Show an actual event: a person withdraws marketing consent, support still needs to contact them about an open ticket, and sales has a separate legitimate business interaction. The system must distinguish those purposes.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is a good scenario. How did Marketo address it in your review?

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): Marketo did not make it simple, but our administrators know the current configuration. We have documented workarounds. Switching platforms would mean revalidating every integration and historical consent mapping.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Was implementation effort the primary reason we lost?

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): It contributed. The business sponsor wanted a faster operator experience, but the migration plan treated privacy review as a checklist near the end. For us, privacy has to shape the design at the beginning.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): If the migration were phased, would that reduce concern?

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): Potentially. Start with a region or a low-risk audience. Do not import every historical record immediately. Define deletion and preference behavior first, test it, then expand.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Are there regional requirements that would affect the pilot?

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): Yes. The United Kingdom has its own expectations around consent evidence and marketing communications. We also have customers in the European Economic Area. I am not saying the platform cannot support them; I am saying the operating procedures must be explicit.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would count as evidence of readiness?

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): A data-flow diagram, a retention matrix, role descriptions, audit-log examples, and a written answer for subprocessors. Also, explain whether administrators can export personal data and how those exports are controlled.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can provide those materials. Would your team review a security packet before a technical workshop?

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): Yes, if it is tailored. Generic security language will slow us down. I need to know which answers apply to the proposed architecture.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll ask our solutions consultant to prepare an architecture outline. On the marketing side, the expected gain was campaign speed. How would you balance that against review requirements?

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): Speed is useful only when the controls are reusable. If each new journey requires privacy to inspect every setting manually, marketing will eventually bypass us. Approved templates, purpose-specific fields, and clear permissions would let both teams move faster.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): So governance can be an accelerator if it is built into the workflow.

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): Yes, but do not oversell that. Templates drift. Someone must own them and review them periodically.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Who should own that process?

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): Marketing operations should own day-to-day configuration. Privacy should define requirements and audit periodically. IT needs to own integrations. Those responsibilities were not clear in the original proposal.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is an important gap. Did price affect the outcome?

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): The 65,257 amount was significant, but I would not call price the deciding factor. The cost of a privacy incident or failed migration is much higher. The issue was that the proposal did not make the risk legible.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): If we could show the controls and a phased rollout, would you support reopening the discussion?

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): I would support another review. I would not promise approval. Marketing needs to confirm the workflow benefits, and procurement will compare the total migration cost with keeping Marketo.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is fair. I’ll send the requested materials and suggest a workshop with you, marketing operations, and our solutions consultant. We can use the consent-withdrawal scenario as the test case.

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): Include the deletion request and the support-ticket exception. If the demonstration only shows a clean contact, it will not answer my questions.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Agreed. One last question: is there a deadline we should work toward?

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): No immediate deadline. The prior close date was October 15, but the need is ongoing. If you send a complete packet, I can review it in about two weeks.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll do that. Thanks for being specific, Ethelyn.

Ethelyn Fay (Privacy): Thank you. Specificity is what was missing last time. We can continue if the next conversation starts with the data, not the headline features.
