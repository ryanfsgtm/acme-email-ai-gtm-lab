Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Ezra, thanks for meeting. I know procurement calendars are never quiet in April.

Ezra Smith (Procurement): They’re especially loud this week. We’re reviewing renewals across three systems, and everyone says theirs is strategic.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then I’ll keep this practical. I’d like to review what your team is getting today, what you would need to renew, and whether expansion is justified. How has the platform performed for manufacturing campaigns?

Ezra Smith (Procurement): Operationally, it’s stable. The team sends fewer one-off emails and has more repeatable programs. The finance director, however, asks whether those programs produce attributable pipeline.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That’s fair. What reporting does she expect to see?

Ezra Smith (Procurement): Revenue by campaign, influenced opportunities, and a view by business unit. She doesn’t need a tour of every workflow. She needs to know whether the spend is defensible.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can prepare an executive report focused on attribution, influenced pipeline, and business-unit rollups. Who will use the day-to-day workflow views?

Ezra Smith (Procurement): Marketing operations. They care about building journeys, testing subject lines, and getting changes live without filing a ticket. Different audience, different definition of value.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That distinction is useful. For renewal, we should satisfy both: an executive view that connects programs to pipeline, and an operator view that makes journey building and testing fast. Would you agree?

Ezra Smith (Procurement): Yes, though I don’t want two separate products or two separate reporting projects.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can use the same campaign and opportunity data with two presentation layers. The executive dashboard can be summarized, while operators retain the detailed workflow and test results.

Ezra Smith (Procurement): Can the executive dashboard handle offline distributor activity? A lot of our manufacturing leads come through distributors, not forms.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): It can include manually uploaded or CRM-linked activities, but attribution quality depends on consistent source fields. I don’t want to promise a perfect answer where the data is incomplete.

Ezra Smith (Procurement): Good. Procurement hears “360-degree attribution” and later discovers three spreadsheets.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We’ll document the coverage and the gaps. For operators, what is currently slowing them down?

Ezra Smith (Procurement): Approvals. They can build a journey, but a small wording change waits for legal review and then someone has to rebuild the test. They say the workflow is easy until governance enters.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can create a reusable approval path and test variants before legal approval, with sending disabled. That would let them compare subject lines without creating an uncontrolled campaign.

Ezra Smith (Procurement): That sounds useful, but our legal team may still require a screenshot of every version.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can export the variant summary and approval history. I’ll confirm whether screenshots are supported or whether the export is the better audit artifact.

Ezra Smith (Procurement): Please confirm. “Should be possible” is not a procurement answer.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Fair correction. I’ll give you a documented answer with the renewal proposal.

Ezra Smith (Procurement): Speaking of the proposal, the current amount is 7,709 ARR. What would expansion cost?

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I need to separate the options. One is additional users and training. Another is the attribution package. A third adds the distributor data connector. I don’t want to bundle all three before we know which problem matters most.

Ezra Smith (Procurement): The distributor connector is the least certain. We would need the regional teams to standardize their files first.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then it may be a later phase. If the immediate business case is reporting plus workflow enablement, we can scope those and leave the connector as an optional item.

Ezra Smith (Procurement): That would make approval easier. The board dislikes paying for capability we cannot operationalize.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How many operators would need access?

Ezra Smith (Procurement): Six regular users, maybe four occasional reviewers. The reviewers mostly inspect results; they don’t build journeys.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I’ll price six full seats and four review seats if that distinction is available. Training should focus on journey templates, testing, and approval handoffs.

Ezra Smith (Procurement): Include office hours for the first month. We have a new lifecycle manager starting in June.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Good timing. Would she own the operating model, or just the campaigns?

Ezra Smith (Procurement): Both eventually. She’ll need to explain results to the VP Marketing and still be able to change a workflow at nine in the morning.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then the onboarding should pair executive reporting with hands-on build sessions. We can schedule one session around attribution definitions and another around template construction and testing.

Ezra Smith (Procurement): I’m glad you’re separating those. The last vendor demo showed a beautiful dashboard, but our operators left unsure how to create anything.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): A dashboard is not a workflow. Both need to work for renewal to be worthwhile.

Ezra Smith (Procurement): What commitment do you need from us before you draft the proposal?

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): A list of the six operators, two representative campaigns, and the business-unit fields used in your CRM. We can then validate the reporting scope.

Ezra Smith (Procurement): I can provide the user list and campaigns. The business-unit fields are messy; some teams use plant codes.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is a risk to attribution. We’ll treat field cleanup as a prerequisite or explicitly exclude those records from the first report.

Ezra Smith (Procurement): Put both options in writing. I need to know the cost and timeline if cleanup is ours versus yours.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I’ll include that comparison. I’ll also add the approval-history export question as an open technical item, not bury it in assumptions.

Ezra Smith (Procurement): Good. If the proposal stays within our renewal budget, I can route it to finance. Expansion above that needs the VP’s approval.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): When should I send it?

Ezra Smith (Procurement): By next Friday. We have a committee meeting the following Tuesday.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I’ll send it Wednesday so there is time for questions. We’ll include a base renewal, a reporting-and-workflow expansion, and the connector as an optional phase.

Ezra Smith (Procurement): That structure is workable. No promise of expansion yet, but renewal is active if the reporting answer is credible.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. I’ll follow up with the requested list today.

Ezra Smith (Procurement): And I’ll send the campaigns tomorrow. Thanks, Luis.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Thank you, Ezra. We’ll make the business case as useful to finance as the workflows are to marketing operations.
