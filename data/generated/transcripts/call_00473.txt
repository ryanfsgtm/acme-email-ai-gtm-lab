Priya Shah (Account Executive): Tony, thanks for joining. I know procurement calendars are never exactly spacious. How has your morning been?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Busy, but this is a good time. We are reviewing several marketing operations requests, and I am trying to separate a genuine requirement from a feature wish list.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is exactly the right lens. I would like to understand the current process, who needs to approve a change, and what outcome would justify the investment. I can show capabilities afterward if they map to a real problem.

Tony Orn (Procurement): The request came from our client-services marketing group. They want more consistent nurture across professional-services campaigns. Today they export lists, create separate emails, and report manually. It works, but it is slow and difficult to audit.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How many campaigns are active in a normal month?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Maybe six or seven, depending on proposals and events. We are not a giant marketing department. That is why I am cautious about buying an elaborate system.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. Where does the process break most often: audience selection, approvals, follow-up, or reporting?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Approvals and ownership. A campaign can sit with a partner for two weeks because nobody knows whether the copy is final. Then someone asks procurement to explain why the launch moved.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): So visibility and accountability may matter more than sending volume. Who owns the business case?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Originally our marketing director, Dana. She sponsored the request in June. She has since moved to another internal project and has not attended the last two meetings. The people attending now are coordinators and an analyst.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds like a change in decision coverage. Are they still authorized to define requirements, or are they gathering information for someone else?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Gathering information, mostly. They can tell you what buttons they like. They cannot commit budget or approve a process change.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Helpful distinction. I can tailor a discovery walkthrough around the basic workflow first. There is also advanced journey branching, where different paths respond to role, engagement, service line, or event behavior. For example, a prospect who downloads a tax briefing could receive a different sequence from someone attending a webinar.

Tony Orn (Procurement): That sounds attractive, and I am sure marketing will ask for it. But I am not sure it is a requirement. We currently have one audience and a handful of templates.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Right. It would be an option, not a prerequisite. What would the first phase need to do?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Import approved contacts, route a draft through review, send one or two messages, and show who approved what. If it cannot do that simply, advanced paths will not save it.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Agreed. Let me ask about systems. Is your CRM the source of truth?

Tony Orn (Procurement): For clients, mostly. The event list is in a separate spreadsheet, and some practice groups maintain their own contact lists. There is no universal taxonomy.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then integration scope could become the first hidden cost. Would procurement require a fixed implementation quote?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Yes, or at least a cap. We cannot approve an amount and discover later that every practice group needs a custom project.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can propose a bounded pilot around one practice group. Who would provide the contact mapping and approve the templates?

Tony Orn (Procurement): The analyst could map fields. A partner would need to approve copy. Again, the partner has not been on these calls.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Do you have a target launch date?

Tony Orn (Procurement): We had June, then July, then “after the busy season.” The latest internal note says September, but nobody has confirmed it. I would not put that date in a purchase order.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Has the opportunity been pushed because of pricing, or because the project itself is losing momentum?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Momentum. Price is not irrelevant, but we have not reached a price negotiation. The meetings keep turning into tactical questions about templates and whether a button is in the left menu.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a useful signal. If the sponsor reappears, what decision would you need from her?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Confirm the practice group, approve the pilot budget, name the partner owner, and accept the September or later timeline. Without those, procurement cannot move.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will send a short pilot outline with those decisions clearly marked. Would it help to include a comparison against Eloqua?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Eloqua is not actually selected. It is the incumbent reference point from another team. A comparison could be useful, but do not frame this as a competitive bake-off. We may simply defer it.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. If we schedule another meeting next week, who should attend?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Invite Dana, but I cannot promise she will come. Include the analyst and me. If Dana declines again, that tells us something, though I would not put it quite so bluntly in the calendar note.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will keep the wording neutral: “pilot scope and ownership review.” Is there a date that works?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Tuesday is possible. But if the director is travelling, it may be another information-gathering session.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then I will avoid presenting it as a commercial decision. I appreciate the candor. Before we close, is advanced journey branching something you want in the pilot estimate or left as a future option?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Leave it as a future option. Put the basic workflow in the estimate, and show branching as an optional line so nobody mistakes interest for commitment.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Done. I will send the bounded scope, assumptions, approval roles, and the optional branching line. We will hold Tuesday provisionally.

Tony Orn (Procurement): Good. And if the date moves again, do not treat that as a procurement objection. It is an ownership problem.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Clear. Thanks, Tony.

Tony Orn (Procurement): Thank you.
