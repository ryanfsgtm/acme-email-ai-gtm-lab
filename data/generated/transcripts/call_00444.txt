Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Norbert, thanks for taking the call. Before we get into systems, how are things at Gutkowski, Wehner and Koss?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): The spring enrollment cycle is starting, so busy. We are trying to make the emails more relevant without creating fifteen versions of every message.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That tension comes up often. What prompted you to look at marketing automation now?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Our prospective-student journey is mostly manual. Admissions exports a list, marketing edits it, and someone checks the list against a spreadsheet. It works until two people update the spreadsheet at the same time.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What happens when the process fails?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Usually a duplicate email or a student receiving a reminder after they have already booked a visit. We catch most of it, but “most” is not a comforting word in admissions.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Which audiences are in scope first?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Prospective undergraduate students and their parents. Later, continuing education and alumni. We do not want to put alumni into the first build.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Good boundary. Are students and parents stored as separate contacts?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Usually. Sometimes a parent uses the student's email, and sometimes a student uses a personal address before switching to a school address. Identity matching is one of our messier problems.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Does the admissions system hold the authoritative status?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Yes, for application status. Marketing owns email consent, although the consent capture happens in several forms and event systems.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Then the automation needs clear ownership rules. We can use admissions status to stop reminders, but marketing should control whether a message can be sent.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): That sounds right. We also need a pause when someone requests a counselor appointment. The counselor should not get a prospect who is still receiving a generic “book a call” message.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Would the appointment system expose that event quickly?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): In theory. In practice, the integration runs every few hours. During peak weeks, that is too slow.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We should test the actual delay rather than rely on the vendor description. A two-hour delay may be acceptable for one journey and unacceptable for another.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Exactly. The visit-reminder journey needs same-day suppression. A newsletter can tolerate delay.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What reporting does leadership ask for?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Enrollment influence, source performance, and whether communications increase campus visits. They are careful about attribution because a student may attend an open house before filling out a form.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Would a multi-touch view be useful, or do they need a single campaign answer?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Both, unfortunately. The board wants a simple number, while the enrollment team wants to understand the sequence.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We can provide a headline metric with an underlying journey view, but we should define the attribution window first.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Please include that in the discovery notes. Our current reports quietly use different windows.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): I will. How large is the initial audience?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Roughly 18,000 prospective students and associated contacts. But not all are marketable. Some have opted out, some are inactive, and some are international contacts with different consent requirements.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That means a count alone will not define the implementation. We need status, consent, relationship type, and region.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Yes. And we need a way for admissions staff to see why someone is in a journey without asking marketing every time.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Would a contact timeline and journey membership be enough, or do they need to edit enrollment?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): View first. If they can edit things, someone will accidentally enroll a person twice.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Sensible. We can design separate permissions for viewing context and changing automation fields.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Security will ask about that. They will also ask where the data is stored and how deletion requests are handled.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): I'll include those questions for our technical and legal teams. We should not answer them from memory.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Appreciated. Another concern is tone. Our messages have to feel supportive, not like retail marketing. The platform team sometimes demonstrates with aggressive urgency language.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): The system does not require that tone. We can model a calm, service-oriented journey and test the copy with admissions before launch.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Good. We also have accessibility requirements for email, including contrast, alt text, and plain-language review.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We can add accessibility checks to the build checklist. Who signs off on content?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Marketing owns copy, admissions owns factual accuracy, and our accessibility lead reviews templates.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That is a clear approval chain. What would a successful first ninety days look like?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Fewer duplicate reminders, faster suppression after appointments, less spreadsheet work, and a report people trust enough to use in planning.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): How will you measure fewer duplicates?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): We can compare the last two enrollment cycles, but the old data is incomplete. We can at least measure duplicate incidents prospectively.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Good. We can label the historical comparison as directional and use the prospective incident count as the primary measure.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): That level of honesty will help when we present it.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): For next steps, I propose a data workshop with admissions and IT, a sample journey map, an integration-latency test, and a security questionnaire.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Add the consent inventory. We need to know which forms are authoritative before anyone builds an audience.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Absolutely. I will add it. Are there any dates we need to work backward from?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): The next open house is in six weeks. We do not need the whole program live, but a small reminder pilot would be useful.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): A pilot is realistic if the audience and suppression rules are approved early. We should not make the open house date a promise until the data workshop is complete.
