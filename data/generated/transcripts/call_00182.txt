Adella Abernathy (Privacy): Provenance. Someone knows the list came from an event or a partner, but nobody can immediately show the notice that was presented, the lawful basis, or whether the person opted out afterward. It becomes a reconstruction exercise.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): And do you have a centralized suppression list today?

Adella Abernathy (Privacy): We have several. That is the problem. A person can unsubscribe from one newsletter and still appear in an event audience managed elsewhere. Marketing says the systems are connected, but connected is not the same as governed.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a useful distinction. Would your desired workflow be a single contact preference record shared across campaigns?

Adella Abernathy (Privacy): Ideally, yes, though I am cautious about calling it single unless the source of truth is explicit. We have regional teams and different brands. The same person may consent to one publication but not another.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): So preference management needs to preserve purpose and scope rather than treating consent as one global yes or no.

Adella Abernathy (Privacy): Correct. Also, legitimate interest assessments are not interchangeable with consent. I do not want a salesperson selecting a broad segment and assuming the system has solved the legal analysis.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): ACME can store preference fields and apply suppression rules, but your organization would still define the lawful basis and campaign policy.

Adella Abernathy (Privacy): Good. Vendors sometimes imply the checkbox is the compliance program.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Where is your data hosted now?

Adella Abernathy (Privacy): Different answers depending on which system you ask about. The newsletter tool is in the United States, our CRM has European hosting, and event vendors vary. I need to know the hosting region, subprocessors, transfer mechanism, and support-access model.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I can provide the current documentation. Are there particular retention periods for media contacts?

Adella Abernathy (Privacy): Marketing has an informal rule of three years after the last engagement, but it is not applied consistently. Some records are retained because they attended a conference; others because a business card was scanned. We are reviewing that policy.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would automated inactivity rules help, or could those create problems?

Adella Abernathy (Privacy): Both. An automated rule is useful if it is transparent and reversible, but deleting a record can remove evidence needed for an audit or a pending request. I would want a legal hold or exception process.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. How are access requests handled?

Adella Abernathy (Privacy): Privacy coordinates them, but we depend on system owners. We need search, export, correction, and deletion capabilities. We also need a log showing what was done. If a platform only gives me a pretty dashboard, that is not enough.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Could your team administer those requests directly?

Adella Abernathy (Privacy): Maybe for straightforward cases. We have only 127 employees, and privacy is not a large department. We cannot become the help desk for every campaign field.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That suggests role separation: marketing manages ordinary fields, privacy can review or override sensitive actions, and an administrator handles configuration.

Adella Abernathy (Privacy): Potentially. I would need to test the permissions. A common failure is that the person who builds a segment can also export it.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can demonstrate that configuration. What would marketing consider a successful outcome?

Adella Abernathy (Privacy): Faster campaign preparation and fewer manual reconciliations. They also want engagement reporting by audience and channel. I do not want privacy requirements to make the system unusable, because then people create workarounds.

Adella Abernathy (Privacy): English is the default, but we have teams working with different markets and notices. The platform should support localized forms and messages. Consent language cannot be quietly changed by a local administrator without a record.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would approval workflows for forms and consent text be useful?

Adella Abernathy (Privacy): Yes, provided they are not so slow that everyone bypasses them. I want version history and a way to identify which notice was active when a person submitted a form.
