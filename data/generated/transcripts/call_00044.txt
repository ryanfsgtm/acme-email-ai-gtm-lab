Priya Shah (Account Executive): Lenore, thanks for making time. I know the renewal review is arriving during a busy implementation cycle.

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): It is. I have one plant manager waiting for a migration answer, so if I glance away, that is why.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. I want to review the current program, the renewal decision, and whether an expansion makes sense for Vandervort Group. I have the account at 74,463 employees in U.S. manufacturing and an opportunity amount of 217,331. The current opportunity is marked closed-lost, so I would like to understand what happened rather than presume a simple budget issue.

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): It was not simple. The platform worked for the teams using it, but the buying committee did not agree on what “working” meant. Marketing operations cared about journey building and how quickly they could test a change. Executives wanted a reliable view of contribution to pipeline. Those conversations were happening in separate rooms.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Can you give me an example of the operator concern?

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): A coordinator wanted to clone a nurture, change two steps, and run an A/B test before the weekly production meeting. The old process required a ticket and a developer. She said the delay made testing pointless. For operators, workflow speed was not a nice-to-have; it determined whether they experimented at all.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): And the executive concern?

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): The CMO asked which journeys influenced qualified pipeline by division. We showed engagement and opportunity association, but the report did not align with finance's booked-revenue view. He said, “I can see activity, but I cannot defend the number in an operating review.” That became the headline objection.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): So the same program was judged on workflow speed by practitioners and attribution by executives.

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): Yes. Neither group was wrong. We made the mistake of presenting the same value story to both groups.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would you have shown the executives earlier?

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): A reporting framework with definitions, not just a dashboard. Which opportunities count as influenced? What is the date window? What happens when an opportunity has no campaign association? We needed finance and marketing operations in one workshop.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Did the team have enough data quality to support that?

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): Not at first. Division codes were inconsistent, and some plants used a local campaign naming convention. But the operators were willing to clean it because they could see an immediate benefit. The executive team saw a long project before seeing a trusted number.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is helpful. Where did expansion enter the conversation?

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): We considered adding two regions and more product lines. The operators were excited because reusable journeys could reduce their backlog. Then the board review focused on revenue attribution and implementation risk. Expansion became associated with more reporting uncertainty, not more capacity.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Were all divisions using the same workflow?

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): No. Some needed complex product education journeys. Others mostly needed event follow-up. A single global design would have been too rigid, but completely separate workspaces would have made reporting difficult.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How did permissions affect that balance?

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): Central operations controlled audience rules and publishing. Regional teams could change approved content. That was mostly right. The exception process was still slow because every unusual request had to be reviewed manually.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Could expansion have been phased by persona and use case?

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): Yes. If you had proposed “first, executive reporting for the existing program; second, operator workflow for two regions; third, broader rollout,” it might have felt manageable. Instead, the proposal bundled new regions, additional seats, and a reporting package. It looked like a large commitment before the original value was agreed.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Was pricing the decisive issue?

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): Pricing was visible, but trust was decisive. The amount was material for any team, yet the committee could have funded it if the outcomes were clear. We had champions, but they were not aligned on the proof required.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What did the implementation team want from us during renewal?

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): A working session with the CMO's analyst and our journey builders together. Show the analyst how a new journey is built and tested. Show the executives how the resulting events roll into a governed report. Same data, two views.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would a pilot be credible at this scale?

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): Only if the pilot has an operational metric and an executive metric. For example, reduce journey launch time from ten business days to three, while producing a monthly influenced-pipeline report that finance can reproduce. If we measure only sends and clicks, the executives will dismiss it.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): And if we measure only revenue?

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): The operators will disengage because revenue takes too long to mature. They need fast feedback on build speed, testing cycles, and handoff quality.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is the distinction I wanted to capture. Is there a technical issue that could still block a future renewal?

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): Identity resolution across divisions. A contact may appear under a plant, a parent account, and a distributor relationship. If we cannot explain the joins, the attribution report will remain disputed.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can include an identity-mapping assessment before commercial expansion. Would that help?

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): Yes, but it needs a named owner on our side. We have learned that “the data team will review it” means nobody owns it.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Who should be involved in the next meeting?

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): Me, the marketing operations lead, the CMO's reporting analyst, finance transformation, and one regional journey builder. Five people, not twenty. We can bring a readout to the wider committee.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would cause you to recommend staying with the current footprint?

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): If the reporting definitions remain unsettled, I would not expand. More regions would produce more activity but not more confidence. We may renew a smaller footprint while we fix governance.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): And what would justify expansion?

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): A repeatable report, faster operator testing, and proof that regional teams can work within shared guardrails. If those three are visible, the investment becomes easier to defend.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I appreciate the specificity. I will prepare two tracks: an executive attribution workshop and an operator workflow session, with a proposed phased plan.

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): Good. Please do not call it a “feature review.” That phrase makes everyone expect slides instead of decisions.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will call it a value and operating review.

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): Better. Also, the current lost status should not be read as “no interest.” There was interest. We failed to connect the people who needed different evidence.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will make that clear in the account notes. Thanks, Lenore. I hope the plant manager gets her migration answer.

Lenore Konopelski (Implementation Partner): Me too. Send the draft, and I will add the right people before it goes to the committee.
