Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Raoul, thanks for joining. Before we jump into the demo, how is the end-of-year rush treating you?

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Busy, but not catastrophic. Software companies like to pretend December is quiet; ours is mostly people trying to use the remaining budget before everyone disappears.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That sounds familiar. I understand you are evaluating marketing automation for demand generation. What would you most like to see?

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Campaign orchestration and reporting. We have email tools, a CRM, and a lot of spreadsheets between them. I can launch a campaign, but answering what happened afterward takes longer than the launch.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Let us start with a campaign journey. Suppose a prospect downloads a technical guide. What happens today?

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): They get an email, usually. Then a marketer checks the list, exports something to the CRM, and asks sales whether the person is known. If the person is already in an opportunity, the process becomes manual because we do not want to create a second lead.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can branch based on CRM status and account ownership. I will show a simple version first. Here is the entry event, then an immediate delivery, a three-day wait, and a branch for engagement. Can you see the screen?

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Yes. The layout is straightforward. Can the branch also check whether the account is already in an open opportunity? That is more useful than an individual score for us.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Yes, assuming that opportunity status is available from the CRM connection. We can suppress promotional steps and send a relevant update to the account owner instead.

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Good. Our sales team does not want marketing sending generic nurture messages to active evaluation accounts. They will complain, correctly.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What data does your CRM reliably contain?

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Company and owner are generally reliable. Industry is less reliable. Job title is all over the place. Product interest is often in notes, which is not ideal for automation.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then we should not make industry or title hard gates in the first design. We can use form selections and account status, then improve enrichment later.

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): That is sensible. Show me reporting. I need to distinguish a campaign that generated form fills from one that influenced an existing pipeline conversation.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Here is the campaign dashboard. It shows delivery and engagement, influenced contacts, sourced opportunities, and revenue values when the CRM provides them. We can filter by program, region, or product line.

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): The revenue view is the part I worry about. If three campaigns touch one opportunity, I do not want the dashboard presenting three pieces of sourced revenue as if we won three deals.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can separate sourced, influenced, and multi-touch measures. The attribution model needs to be selected explicitly. The system will not resolve the business definition for you.

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Good answer. Our CFO will challenge any number that looks too neat.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Can I ask about your campaign volume?

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Maybe twenty major programs a quarter, plus smaller product emails and partner activity. The challenge is not just volume; it is that every team wants its own naming convention.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): A central program taxonomy would make reporting much easier. Would you control that centrally?

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): I would like to, but product marketing will resist if it feels like a bureaucratic form. We need defaults and validation, not a fifteen-field submission process.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can make the required fields minimal and use predefined values. Optional details can be added later. The same principle applies to templates: lock the global elements, leave approved modules editable.

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): How granular are permissions? Our regional marketers should be able to build campaigns, but they should not alter the unsubscribe footer or the global tracking parameters.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Those can be restricted to administrators. Regional users can work within shared templates and submit for approval. We can also limit access to regional audiences if your data model supports that separation.

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): It does not always. Some strategic accounts have contacts in several countries. I would rather have account-based visibility than an overly simple country wall.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can design around account ownership and approved audience membership, then review exceptions. I would want your CRM administrator involved before we finalize the permission model.

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Agreed. Another question: can the platform handle a product webinar with registration, reminders, attendance, and a follow-up path?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Yes. Registration can write to the CRM, reminders can be scheduled based on event time, and attendance can branch the follow-up. No-shows can receive a recording message, while attendees receive a summary and next-step option.

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Can sales receive a task only for people who attended and asked for a conversation? We have too many low-quality tasks today.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Yes, if the registration or post-event form captures that intent. We can also route based on existing account ownership.

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): That would be useful. I do not need every attendee treated as sales-ready.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would you need to validate before moving forward?

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Data synchronization, attribution definitions, permissions, and the effort to migrate templates. We have several years of assets, but I do not want to bring all of them over. We should archive most of them.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is usually healthier. We can start with current evergreen assets and two active programs. What timeline are you considering?

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): If the business case holds, a pilot in the next quarter. But the date is not fixed. December budget conversations are not the same as an approved project.

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): That sounds right. And please include the implementation assumptions. The hidden cost in these projects is usually the customer’s time.
