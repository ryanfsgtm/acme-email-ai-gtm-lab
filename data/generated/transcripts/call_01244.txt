Connor Brown (Privacy): Marketing wants automation for a manufacturing business of roughly one hundred fifty employees. They say the current process is too manual. My concern is that “automation” becomes a reason to reuse contacts for purposes they never understood.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Which data sources are under discussion?

Connor Brown (Privacy): Website forms, trade-show scans, CRM contacts, service inquiries, and a distributor list. The distributor list is the uncomfortable one because provenance is inconsistent.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Do you currently have separate consent records for those sources?

Connor Brown (Privacy): In theory. In practice, the CRM has a consent field, the event vendor has another field, and a spreadsheet has free-text notes. The records do not line up reliably.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What would happen if the system received conflicting values?

Connor Brown (Privacy): It must default conservatively. I do not want a missing value treated as permission. Nor do I want an old opt-out overwritten by a newer import simply because the newer file is incomplete.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is a technical and governance requirement. Who owns the final decision on campaign eligibility?

Connor Brown (Privacy): Marketing operations proposes it; I review the legal basis and retention. The business owner signs off. People sometimes assume privacy is the campaign owner, but we are not.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): How long are contacts retained today?

Connor Brown (Privacy): It varies by source and purpose. We have not completed the schedule for every marketing segment, which is itself a problem.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Would a pilot be acceptable if it used only website contacts with documented consent?

Connor Brown (Privacy): Potentially. But the pilot must prove that a contact can be suppressed, exported, corrected, and deleted. A happy-path send is not evidence of readiness.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What reporting do you need from the system?

Connor Brown (Privacy): An auditable history: source, consent value, timestamp, campaign enrollment, suppression, and who changed the record. I also need to know whether administrators can see more than they need.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Are role-based permissions important?

Connor Brown (Privacy): Yes, but roles alone are not enough. We need to understand support access, vendor access, logs, and whether data leaves the approved region.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): The opportunity record shows a technical validation stage and an amount of eight thousand seven hundred forty-eight annually. Has a purchase decision been made?

Connor Brown (Privacy): No. The date in the system is already past, and it should not be read as an approval. We are still validating whether the design is lawful and workable.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Thank you for correcting that. What documentation should we provide first?

Connor Brown (Privacy): Data-flow diagrams, hosting and residency details, subprocessors, deletion behavior, encryption, access controls, and the contract terms. Marketing can look at the interface later.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Would you prefer a technical workshop with security, or written answers before that?

Connor Brown (Privacy): Written answers first. A workshop is useful only after the basic questions have been answered consistently.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): How do you handle legitimate-interest assessments today?

Connor Brown (Privacy): Case by case. The problem is that marketers want a reusable rule, while the legal basis depends on audience, geography, and message. We cannot make the tool decide that without oversight.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That distinction should be reflected in the workflow. A system may enforce a documented rule, but it should not invent one.

Connor Brown (Privacy): Exactly. Also, do not assume every contact wants a marketing journey. Service inquiries may be appropriate for a response but not for a promotional nurture.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is a good counterexample to a single “engaged contact” category. What would make you reject the solution?

Connor Brown (Privacy): If deletion is only a soft flag, if suppression is not propagated across campaigns, if consent history is overwritten, or if the vendor cannot explain privileged support access.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What would make you comfortable enough to recommend moving forward?

Connor Brown (Privacy): A constrained pilot with documented inputs and negative tests. Try an opted-out contact, a conflicting record, a missing consent value, and a deletion request. Show me the logs.
