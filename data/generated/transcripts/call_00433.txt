Priya Shah (Account Executive): Ted, thanks for joining. I know you are calling from the UK, so we will try not to run over into your evening.

Ted Conn (IT): It is already evening, but the kettle is doing its part. I have about thirteen minutes before I need to leave.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will be concise. This is a renewal and expansion conversation for the marketing automation account. The current opportunity is closed lost to ActiveCampaign, so I want to understand the technical and operational reasons before we wrap the relationship.

Ted Conn (IT): The main reason is that the platform was being used as a fairly expensive email sender. We had automation features, but the marketing team mostly built a message, selected a list, and pressed send.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Was that because of capability gaps, or because the team did not have time to use the broader features?

Ted Conn (IT): Mostly time and ownership. We are a media group with roughly a thousand employees across several brands. Every brand had its own calendar. Nobody owned the shared journey design, and IT was asked to keep the integrations alive, not to run marketing.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is useful context. Which integrations were stable?

Ted Conn (IT): CRM sync was stable enough. The event platform was less reliable. We also had a warehouse feed that arrived late on Mondays—well, technically Sunday night, but after the campaign team had gone home.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Did those delays affect renewal confidence?

Ted Conn (IT): They contributed. The bigger point was adoption. We paid for segmentation, nurture, testing, and behavioral triggers. The team used broadcasts and occasional welcome emails. ActiveCampaign gave us a smaller package that matched what they actually wanted to operate.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would have needed to change for expansion to make sense?

Ted Conn (IT): A named business owner, a standard integration pattern, and evidence that journey building would not become another IT project. We also needed better permission boundaries. One brand administrator could see assets from another brand, which caused some nervousness.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): The permission model can be configured by workspace and role. Was that tested in production or only discussed during evaluation?

Ted Conn (IT): Discussed, then tested briefly. We never completed the full matrix. That is on us too, although the handoff between sales and implementation was not smooth.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Can you describe the handoff issue?

Ted Conn (IT): Sales said our existing setup could be expanded quickly. Implementation started with a discovery questionnaire and found that our consent fields were inconsistent across brands. We paused. Then the project resumed, but the marketing lead changed jobs.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds like the initiative lost momentum rather than failed technically.

Ted Conn (IT): Correct, but from an IT perspective, momentum is part of success. We cannot call something successful if the owner disappears and the work becomes a queue item.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Did the replacement marketing lead participate in the renewal discussion?

Ted Conn (IT): She joined one call, mostly to ask for email templates. She did not want a platform redesign. The director who approved the original purchase did not attend any of the last three meetings.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I see. Was there a specific expansion use case that remained attractive?

Ted Conn (IT): Transactional recommendations were interesting, and the sports brand wanted a triggered series around subscriptions. But those were ideas, not funded projects. The immediate request was simply, “Can we keep sending newsletters?”

Priya Shah (Account Executive): And the answer was yes, but at lower cost elsewhere.

Ted Conn (IT): Yes. Also, ActiveCampaign was familiar to two of the brand teams. Familiarity reduced training risk.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): If we had offered a narrower renewal, would that have changed the decision?

Ted Conn (IT): Maybe, but not enough. The procurement process favored a lower-cost option, and our internal adoption numbers did not support defending the larger contract. We had positive sentiment about the interface, but sentiment was not the business case.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What reporting did leadership actually review?

Ted Conn (IT): Opens, clicks, unsubscribes, and monthly send volume. There was no trusted attribution model. IT could show delivery and system health, but not whether a journey influenced subscriptions or advertising revenue.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a missed opportunity. Did anyone ask for an attribution workshop?

Ted Conn (IT): We asked for a reporting example. The response was a generic dashboard. Our finance partner wanted brand-level contribution and campaign-to-subscription visibility.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. What should we preserve in the account record?

Ted Conn (IT): Record that the platform was not rejected because it was unusable. It was rejected because our operating model never expanded beyond email blasts, and the cost became hard to justify.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will record that. Is there any technical debt ActiveCampaign will create that we should be aware of?

Ted Conn (IT): We will lose some sophistication in event-based orchestration. That is a trade-off we accepted. We are also keeping the warehouse feed disconnected for now, which may limit reporting, but it reduces implementation work.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): So the smaller solution fits the current maturity, even if it is not the most capable option.

Ted Conn (IT): Exactly. Do not interpret the move as a universal platform preference. For a team with a real lifecycle function, your system might be the better fit. We just did not have that team.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That distinction matters. We will close the renewal and expansion as lost to ActiveCampaign and document the adoption, ownership, permissions, and attribution issues.

Ted Conn (IT): Good. Please also remove the old integration credentials after the transition. I will send the retirement date separately.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Absolutely. Thanks for being direct, Ted.

Ted Conn (IT): No problem. Enjoy the rest of your day.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): You too. Bye.
