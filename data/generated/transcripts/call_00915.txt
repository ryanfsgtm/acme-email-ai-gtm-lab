Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Myrna, hello. Thanks for taking the call. I know you’re in procurement, so I’m not going to presume there’s a buying project. I’d like to understand what prompted the conversation and whether there’s a useful next step.

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): That’s a good starting point. We’re a software company with about 93 employees. I was asked to look at options because our marketing team says the current automation tool is limiting them, but I’m still at the information-gathering stage.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What do they mean by limiting them?

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): They want better segmentation and easier reporting. They also complain that every change requires someone technical. Procurement hears “we need a new platform,” and then asks whether the problem is the platform or the process.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Sensible question. What works well today?

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): The current tool is inexpensive, familiar, and already connected to our CRM. It is not a disaster. The team can send newsletters and basic nurture sequences.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): And where does it fall short?

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): Data is copied between systems. Campaign members do not always match CRM statuses. Reports are assembled manually before the monthly leadership meeting. There’s also a backlog of ideas because nobody wants to risk breaking a live workflow.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Is the main cost the staff time, or are missed opportunities more important?

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): Staff time is easier to prove. Missed opportunities are harder to quantify, though marketing believes slow follow-up affects conversion. I would need evidence before putting that into a business case.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Who owns the decision?

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): No single person yet. The marketing director sponsors the review, finance checks the spend, IT checks integration and security, and procurement handles terms. The CEO may weigh in if the proposal is material.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Do you have a budget range or renewal deadline?

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): The current contract does not renew until later in the year. We could change earlier, but there is no pressure to do so. Any proposal needs to explain migration effort, not just license cost.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That’s important. What would make the migration unacceptable?

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): A long period where both systems have to be maintained manually, unclear ownership of data cleanup, or a requirement for consultants every time we change a segment. Our team is small and already stretched.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Would you prefer a self-guided implementation?

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): Not automatically. Self-guided can be cheaper, but only if the documentation is good and the scope is controlled. A few hours of expert help may be more efficient than a month of trial and error.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What data would be included initially?

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): Active prospects and customers, campaign subscriptions, lifecycle stage, product interest, and engagement history if it can be migrated cleanly. We have old contacts that should probably be archived rather than imported.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How are subscriptions currently defined?

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): That is one of the unresolved issues. Marketing has newsletter status, product updates, event communications, and regional preferences. Some records are “unknown” because the original source did not capture enough detail.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Would a platform need to enforce that unknown cannot enter a campaign?

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): It would help, but policy and process matter more. A clever rule is useless if someone exports a list and sends it outside the system.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Agreed. So controls need to cover both automation and exports.

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): Yes. Also, we operate in the Benelux, but our product team is distributed. Language and regional ownership may complicate templates.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Are there particular workflows marketing wants to improve first?

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): Trial conversion and event follow-up. The team wants a signal when a trial user is engaged enough for a sales conversation, but sales does not trust the current score. Event follow-up is more straightforward because attendance is explicit.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): A narrow event pilot could produce a clearer comparison. Would marketing accept that?

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): Possibly. They want the broader capability, but I could make a pilot easier to approve if it does not create a second reporting model.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can discuss mapping the pilot metrics to your existing CRM stages. I should mention that I’m a customer success manager, so I can help with adoption and implementation planning, while a commercial owner would handle a formal proposal.

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): That’s fine. I actually prefer knowing who handles which part. Too many vendors blur discovery, sales, and delivery.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would IT need before participating?

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): Architecture overview, authentication, API limits, data residency or transfer information, and a list of subprocessors. They will also ask how failed syncs are surfaced.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I’ll include those in the request for the next session. What would finance need?

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): A comparison that includes migration, training, internal time, and the cost of staying with the current tool. “More features” will not be enough.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That gives us a useful evaluation framework. If the pilot shows reduced manual reporting and cleaner follow-up, would that support a broader review?

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): It would support it, not guarantee it. We also need to know whether the marketing team will actually use the features. Adoption is a procurement risk, even when the software is capable.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. We can include an adoption plan with named owners, training, and a check-in after the first campaign.

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): Please do. Also, don’t promise a date before IT reviews the integration. Our last project slipped because the business team committed first.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I won’t set a delivery date yet. I’ll send notes, a discovery checklist, and two options for a follow-up with marketing and IT. We can use the event workflow as the concrete example.

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): That works. I’ll speak with the marketing director and ask for their current process map. It may be embarrassingly informal, but that is useful information too.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Informal processes are often the most valuable thing to see. Thanks, Myrna.

Myrna Bayer (Procurement): Thank you, Luis. Send the materials, and I’ll come back once I know who can join the next meeting.
