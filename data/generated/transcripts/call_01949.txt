Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Ofelia, thanks for taking the call. I know this is an unusual pairing—a customer success manager leading discovery—but I want to understand what happened with the marketing automation evaluation at Weissnat, Barton and Kuphal.

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): It is unusual, yes. We closed the opportunity as lost last month, so I'm not expecting a restart conversation today. I agreed because the team should learn from it.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That's exactly the purpose. The account is enterprise healthcare in ANZ, and the opportunity was valued at 121,513. What problem were you originally trying to solve?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): Fragmentation. We have a large organization, several service lines, and regional teams using different campaign tools. Leadership wanted a common operating model and better visibility into demand.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What made that urgent?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): A board reporting cycle. We could report activity, but not confidently explain how marketing programs connected to pipeline. The pressure was real, although the requested timing was not realistic.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What did realistic timing look like to you?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): Eighteen months for the full operating model, with a controlled first phase. The proposal discussions kept circling back to a rapid rollout. Our team heard “go live” before we had agreed on governance.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Was governance the main reason you chose not to proceed?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): It was one of them. Security and privacy were not blockers by themselves, but the review surfaced questions about regional consent, data retention, and account ownership. We did not have answers internally, either.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What other factors were involved?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): Cost, internal capacity, and the fact that our CRM cleanup was incomplete. People often say the platform was too expensive, but that is too simple. We could have funded it if we had believed the organization was ready to use it well.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): So budget was not an automatic no?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): No. The business case was plausible. The issue was sequencing. We would have paid for capabilities that our teams could not govern consistently.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Which teams were most affected by that concern?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): Regional marketing, sales operations, IT, privacy, and our field teams. Brand was supportive in principle but worried that local groups would publish inconsistent messages.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Did the product evaluation include the regional users?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): Some of them. Not enough. The central team participated heavily, and then we discovered that two regions had different consent practices and separate event systems. That should have happened earlier.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What did the central team value most in the proposed approach?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): A unified view, reusable journeys, and the possibility of reducing manual list work. The demonstration was convincing for those use cases. It was less convincing as a plan for cultural and process change.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Did anything in the evaluation increase confidence?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): The data mapping workshop helped. Once we looked at actual fields, we stopped speaking in generalities. We could see that duplicate accounts and inconsistent ownership would make routing unreliable.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Was that a product limitation?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): Mostly not. It was an organizational data problem. The platform could expose it, but it could not decide which of our conflicting records was correct.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That's an important distinction. How did the opportunity finally get decided?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): Our executive steering group decided to pause and standardize the underlying process first. Another initiative already owned part of the CRM cleanup. They chose to let that run before committing to automation.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Did a competitor win the business?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): No named competitor replaced it. We kept some existing tools and expanded internal reporting. There was no clean competitive displacement.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would have changed the outcome?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): A smaller first phase with explicit exclusions, regional consent owners identified, and an implementation plan tied to our readiness rather than a generic launch date. We also needed a credible estimate of internal staffing.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How small would that first phase need to be?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): One region, two campaign types, and a limited set of known accounts. It should prove governance and handoffs, not promise a global transformation.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Would that have made the value too narrow for an enterprise program?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): Not if the expansion criteria were clear. A small pilot can be valuable when it answers the questions executives actually have. A small pilot with no path to scale is just a demonstration.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What metrics would you have used?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): Consent accuracy, campaign launch time, accepted sales handoffs, duplicate suppression, and the quality of pipeline reporting. Open rates would be secondary.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Did your team feel the evaluation process was too sales-led?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): At moments. The people we met were responsive, but the process naturally emphasized capability. We needed more challenge around whether we were prepared for the operating model.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is useful feedback. Were there any aspects you would preserve if the project returned next year?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): The data workshop, the transparency about assumptions, and the role-based training discussion. We would also want security and privacy involved earlier, not brought in at the end as a gatekeeper.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would you tell another healthcare organization considering a similar program?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): Do not begin with the dream-state journey map. Begin with ownership, consent, and the definition of a qualified handoff. If those are unclear, automation simply makes disagreement faster.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is a strong summary. Has the internal situation changed since the loss?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): Somewhat. We have a new data-governance working group and a clearer regional consent matrix. We are still not ready for a broad implementation, but the next evaluation should be more disciplined.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Would you consider reopening the conversation eventually?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): Possibly, but not as an automatic continuation. We would run a fresh requirements process and invite several stakeholders. The previous proposal should inform that process, not dictate it.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. I'll document the decision factors as readiness, sequencing, governance, and capacity—not simply price or product fit.

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): Please do. And include that the business need remains. “Lost” does not mean the problem disappeared; it means our timing and operating conditions did not support that purchase.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will include that distinction. Is there anything you want removed from the summary?

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): Avoid saying the regions resisted change. They asked reasonable questions, and the central team had not done the preparation. That wording matters.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Agreed. I'll describe the gaps without assigning blame.

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): Good. I have another meeting in two minutes, and this was more useful than I expected.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Thanks for the candor, Ofelia. I'll send the notes for correction before sharing them more broadly.

Ofelia West (VP Marketing): Please do. If I see a mistaken assumption, I would rather fix it while it is still a note than after it becomes a lesson everyone quotes.
