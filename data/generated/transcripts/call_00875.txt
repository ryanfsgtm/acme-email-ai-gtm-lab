Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Madisyn, thanks for staying with me through the reschedule. I know privacy reviews are rarely improved by one more calendar invitation.

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): They are improved slightly by coffee, but not by much. We have forty minutes, and I want to leave with a clear view of whether expansion is supportable.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is the goal. The account is a mid-market professional-services company with more than thirteen hundred employees. The current contract is approaching its decision point, and the opportunity is marked closed-lost against HubSpot, so I want to understand what did and did not work before discussing any future path.

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): The privacy team did not reject the product outright. We rejected some assumptions made during the rollout. Those are different things.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Which assumptions caused the most trouble?

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): That one global workspace would be convenient for everyone. Daniel, Carroll and Russel has several brands and a mix of internal teams and agencies. People wanted shared templates and a single performance view, but they also wanted local control over audiences, consent language, and retention.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That sounds like a configuration problem. Would separate workspaces solve it?

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): Partly. But separation creates reporting and governance work. If every brand invents its own field names, consolidated reporting becomes unreliable. If everything is central, local teams may access data they should not see.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): So the design needs a boundary between isolation and aggregation.

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): Yes. We have agencies working on behalf of two brands, an internal group serving three, and one acquired business with a different consent history. The marketing team says they need speed. Privacy says speed without traceability becomes an incident review.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I hear you. The proposed expansion included additional user seats and more campaign volume. Could it also include a workspace design workshop?

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): It would have to. I want an inventory of brands, processors, purposes, data categories, and administrators. I also want to know whether a user can build in one workspace and report across several without receiving raw contact access everywhere.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): The platform supports workspace-level access and shared reporting in some configurations. I do not want to promise the exact combination before our technical specialist checks it.

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): Good. We had a previous vendor tell us that “role-based access” answered everything. It did not answer who could export a list.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Export permissions need to be explicit. We can review download, API, and report-sharing rights separately. What about reusable templates?

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): Reuse is desirable, especially for mandatory footer language and approved disclaimers. But a template copied into another brand must not silently carry the wrong controller name or retention notice.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Could required fields force the user to confirm the local entity before sending?

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): Possibly. A forced field helps, but it is not a substitute for review. Also, agencies should not be able to modify the locked compliance sections.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can demonstrate locked sections and approval routing. Would your team participate in a test with masked records?

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): Yes, if the test includes the awkward cases: a contact with multiple subscriptions, an acquired brand with old consent language, an agency user who changes an audience, and a request to delete data across workspaces.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is more useful than a perfect happy path. We can include audit trails and show which administrator changed what.

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): Include retention too. The campaign team wants to retain historical engagement forever because it helps their models. Our policy does not say forever.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can map retention schedules to data categories and campaign purpose, subject to your policy. For historical reporting, we may need an aggregated or anonymized approach rather than identifiable records.

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): That is the conversation I wanted. Marketing often says “we need the old data,” but when asked whether they need names, dates, or just totals, the answer changes.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. On the commercial side, the prior proposal was around ninety-nine thousand two hundred fifty-nine euros annually. I will not ask you to approve that number today. First we need a design that your team can defend.

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): Correct. The lost decision was not simply price. HubSpot looked familiar to some users, and familiarity reduced resistance. But familiarity is not evidence that the workspace and retention model fits us.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would a limited renewal or controlled pilot be easier to support than an immediate broad expansion?

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): A controlled pilot, with a named owner and a stop condition. Use one brand, one agency, and a limited set of campaign purposes. Do not include the acquired business until we understand its consent history.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would the stop conditions be?

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): Unauthorised cross-workspace visibility, an export path that cannot be restricted, missing audit events, or inability to honor a deletion request within the agreed process. A failed template check should pause sending but not necessarily end the pilot.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is a helpful distinction. I will write those into the evaluation plan. We also need the marketing operations lead, the agency manager, and someone from records governance.

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): Add legal counsel only for the final interpretation questions. If they join the first workshop, we will spend the entire hour defining terms.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Sensible. I will schedule a ninety-minute design session and send a data-flow worksheet beforehand. You will review the workspace and retention assumptions.

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): Yes. And please do not call the summary “global access with local exceptions.” That phrase made the last proposal sound simpler than it was.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I will call it “isolated execution with governed consolidation,” unless that is too polished.

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): It is polished, but at least it is accurate enough to test.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then that is our next step. No expansion approval today, but a defined pilot and clear privacy gates.

Madisyn Lehner (Privacy): Exactly. Send the worksheet by Friday. If it arrives without the deletion scenario, I will send it back.
