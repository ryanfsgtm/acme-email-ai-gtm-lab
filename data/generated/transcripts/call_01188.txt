Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Usually an email alert, sometimes a manual spreadsheet, and then a debate. If the person is from a known software company, someone responds quickly. If not, it waits. There is no consistent service level.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): So the problem is partly prioritization and partly handoff. Which system holds the customer records today?

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Salesforce for accounts and opportunities. The marketing data is split between Salesforce, our website platform, and event files. Some records have a company domain; some have only a personal email. We also have duplicates from acquisitions.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): How many people would need to work in the marketing process?

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Six in demand gen and maybe ten sales users consuming the results. The company is over a thousand employees, but the operating group is much smaller.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What would a successful first use case look like?

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): A person attends a technical webinar, visits two product pages, and asks for pricing. I want the system to recognize that as more urgent than someone who downloaded a general trend report six months ago.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is a good example. Would sales agree that those signals indicate urgency?

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Some would. Our enterprise team cares about account context; the smaller-market team cares about speed. If we make one universal model, someone will complain.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Then we may need separate views or thresholds by motion, with shared definitions underneath. Before we go there, what information is reliable enough to use?

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Event attendance is usually reliable. Page visits are less reliable because of offices, VPNs, and shared devices. Job title is often outdated. Opportunity status is reliable-ish, assuming sales updates it.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): “Reliable-ish” is an important data category. We can use page activity as a supporting signal rather than a standalone trigger. I would avoid presenting it as proof of individual intent.

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Good. Our privacy team will ask the same question. We operate across Europe and North America, and they do not like invisible profiling.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We should include privacy in the design workshop. We can document what is collected, the purpose of each signal, retention, and how a person can opt out.

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): The opt-out process is another mess. A person can unsubscribe from marketing email but still appear in an event follow-up list.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That should be treated as a priority. Suppression needs to apply consistently across campaign sources, including manually uploaded event lists.

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Can we block an upload if it contains someone suppressed?

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can explore validation and suppression rules, but I would want to confirm the exact import behavior. We should not assume an automated block exists without testing it.

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Fair. I appreciate the distinction between possible and confirmed.

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Mixed. Marketing wants control. Sales wants fewer notifications. Regional teams worry that a central model will erase local nuance. IT is busy and will not take on a project that requires constant custom coding.

Raoul Bauch (Demand Generation): Salesforce, the website forms, and our event platform. The partner-referral files can remain manual initially if we have a clean template.
