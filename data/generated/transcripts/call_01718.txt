Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Bettye, hello. Thanks for joining. I realize this is outside your normal morning window, so we’ll keep the conversation focused.

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): No problem. It is early here, but I have coffee. I was expecting a discovery call, although I know you are our customer success contact rather than the account executive.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Correct. I’m here to understand the operational needs and make sure any recommendation is grounded in what your team can actually implement. If we get into commercial details, I’ll bring in the right colleague.

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): That works. We are reviewing marketing automation because the current setup is difficult to scale. The plant groups have different calendars, different audiences, and occasionally different ideas about what “qualified” means.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): When a campaign is launched today, what are the main steps?

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): Marketing requests an audience. Sales operations checks the account list, marketing builds the email, and someone from IT verifies the integration. Then the campaign goes live. For a simple announcement, it is manageable. For a nurture, the review cycle can take weeks.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Where does it slow down most?

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): Exceptions. A contact may belong to a distributor, a direct account, or an inactive territory. The rules are not difficult individually, but they are scattered. A person may qualify for a message in one system and be suppressed in another.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Is suppression managed centrally?

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): Not reliably. We have a central preference record, but some regional lists are maintained locally. That is one of the reasons I am cautious about making a change quickly.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would you need to trust a new process?

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): Clear ownership, an audit trail, and a way to test before sending. Also, the system must handle our distributor relationships. Manufacturing is not a single funnel. A lead that looks cold may be attached to a long-standing channel partner.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That nuance is important. How do you measure success today?

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): Delivery and response rates are easy. The harder question is whether a campaign contributes to an opportunity. Sales says the timing is helpful, but the reports do not show a clean connection. Leadership wants pipeline evidence.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Do sales leaders trust the current attribution reports?

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): Some do, some do not. One region uses first touch, another uses last touch, and a third has a spreadsheet with weighted influence. We keep saying we will standardize it.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): If the system could produce a common report, would that solve the issue?

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): It would help, but reporting alone is not enough. If the journey is still hard to build, people will work around it. We need both control and speed.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What kinds of journeys are most important?

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): Post-event follow-up, dormant account reactivation, and technical content for prospects who are not ready to speak with sales. We also want different paths for engineers, plant managers, and procurement.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How much variation is acceptable before the team feels overwhelmed?

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): That is the question. We have a tendency to design very clever journeys and then fail to maintain them. A simpler path with reliable governance may be better than an elaborate one nobody understands.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Have you evaluated other platforms?

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): We have had an introductory conversation with Eloqua. They appear strong on enterprise orchestration. The concern is implementation effort and whether our smaller regions would actually use the system.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would a fair comparison include?

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): A real workflow, not a slide deck. Use an event follow-up with a distributor exception, show the approval path, show suppression, and show what a sales manager sees afterward.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can prepare that. Who else should attend?

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): Marketing operations, one regional marketer, IT integration, and someone from sales. Legal may join for the preference discussion. Please do not schedule a two-hour demo with twenty people.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Noted. Would forty-five minutes be enough for a scenario-based session?

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): Yes, followed by a separate technical review if the first session is useful.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): There is one commercial detail I should clarify. The existing opportunity is currently being evaluated, but no decision has been made. The amount in the record is a planning figure, not an approved budget.

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): That matches my understanding. We are not selecting a vendor this month.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. I’ll document the use case and coordinate the scenario session. Before we close, what would make you stop the evaluation?

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): If the solution cannot show who changed a journey, or if regional teams must ask central operations for every adjustment. Also if the integration requires a large custom project.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): And what would move it forward?

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): A credible pilot, clean preference handling, and reporting that sales accepts. We need evidence, not enthusiasm.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is a good standard. I’ll send a one-page summary and two scheduling options.

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): Please include the distributor case in the summary. It is where generic demonstrations usually fall apart.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will. Thanks, Bettye. Enjoy the rest of your morning.

Bettye Collins (Sales Operations): Thanks, Luis. We’ll see whether the coffee was worth it.
