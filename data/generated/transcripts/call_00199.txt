Priya Shah (Account Executive): Kaden, good morning. Before we get into software, how are things going with revenue operations at Schowalter?

Kaden Hane (Revenue Operations): Busy. We are a forty-person retail company, so everyone owns three processes. I maintain the CRM, fix routing, and somehow answer questions about campaign invoices.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a broad job description. What made marketing automation worth discussing now?

Kaden Hane (Revenue Operations): We are losing follow-up requests. A store manager fills out a form, the email goes to a shared inbox, and then somebody remembers it three days later. We need a basic nurture process and better ownership.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How many people would actively use the system?

Kaden Hane (Revenue Operations): Maybe six. Marketing, two salespeople, me, and whoever covers operations when I am out.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): And what does success look like in the first quarter?

Kaden Hane (Revenue Operations): Fewer unworked leads, a clear handoff, and reports that do not require me to export three spreadsheets. We are not expecting sophisticated account-based orchestration.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is helpful. We can start with forms, routing, a short welcome sequence, and a dashboard. Let us talk about your audience size because that often affects the plan.

Kaden Hane (Revenue Operations): This is where I get confused. We have around 8,000 records in the CRM, but only perhaps 1,200 people we would actively market to. The quote seems to price the whole database.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): The plan may distinguish stored contacts from active marketing contacts. Do you know which figure the proposal used?

Kaden Hane (Revenue Operations): I do not. The salesperson said, “You have eight thousand contacts,” and finance heard the larger number. So now everyone thinks the annual price is too high.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds like a definition problem as much as a price problem. We should separate records you retain for history from people eligible to receive marketing. How many of the 8,000 are former customers or duplicate store records?

Kaden Hane (Revenue Operations): At least half are inactive, duplicates, or suppliers. Another thousand are old event attendees. We keep them for reporting, but we would not put them into a nurture without consent review.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then the initial tier could be materially different. Would the business approve the purchase if the active-contact count fit the budget?

Kaden Hane (Revenue Operations): Possibly, but not automatically. We have a hard ceiling. Finance told me we cannot spend more than about nine thousand this year unless the board approves it.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a true affordability constraint. If the correct active tier still exceeds nine thousand, what would happen?

Kaden Hane (Revenue Operations): We would stop. We are not going to borrow from store operations for a marketing platform.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. If it fits, what else could prevent a decision?

Kaden Hane (Revenue Operations): Implementation time. I can give you the field map, but I cannot spend every afternoon cleaning the CRM. Our data is messy, and people will blame the automation when a record is wrong.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We could define a narrow launch: one form, one routing rule, one sequence, and a list of approved active contacts. We would document exclusions rather than attempt a full cleanup.

Kaden Hane (Revenue Operations): That is more realistic. I also need to know whether unsubscribes update quickly enough. We have seasonal campaigns, and the store teams are sensitive about customer complaints.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can review the consent flow and test suppression before launch. I will not ask you to take that on trust.

Kaden Hane (Revenue Operations): Good. Another concern is ownership. If a lead is routed to a salesperson and nobody responds, I need an escalation report, not another notification.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can create a task-aging view and an escalation after a defined period. Who would own the first response?

Kaden Hane (Revenue Operations): The regional sales coordinator. She is not on this call, so I need her agreement.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is an important next step. Would you be comfortable bringing her into a workflow session?

Kaden Hane (Revenue Operations): Yes, if the pricing is clarified first. I do not want to involve her in a project that finance has already rejected.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will send two scenarios: one based on 1,200 active marketing contacts and one showing what happens if that number grows. I will also list what remains stored but not marketed.

Kaden Hane (Revenue Operations): Please make the distinction very obvious. The last proposal used “contacts” for everything, and that caused the argument.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will. I will also include the estimated implementation effort and the suppression test. After you review it, we can decide whether to bring finance and the coordinator into the next call.

Kaden Hane (Revenue Operations): That works. I am interested, but I cannot call this approved. Budget and capacity are both real constraints.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Completely fair. I will not mark it as committed. Thanks for explaining the situation so clearly.

Kaden Hane (Revenue Operations): Thank you. Send the revised numbers, and I will tell you whether there is a next meeting.
