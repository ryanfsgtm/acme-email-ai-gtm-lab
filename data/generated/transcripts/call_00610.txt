Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Kristy, thanks for meeting. I know privacy reviews are rarely the shortest calendar item. How are things in Sydney today?

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): Warm, and my inbox is worse than the weather. I have thirty minutes, though I may need to pause if legal sends the revised transfer assessment.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can use the time to map the implementation controls and leave a clear list of decisions. Your opportunity is in technical validation, so I do not want to treat implementation assumptions as approvals.

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): Good. The business wants to use Customer.io as a comparison point, but my questions are about processing, consent, deletion, and access. Marketing can decide whether the interface is attractive.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Let us start with data flows. Which systems would provide contacts and activity?

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): CRM, the ecommerce platform, and our loyalty database. The loyalty database contains purchase history, so it cannot simply be treated like a newsletter list. Some fields are optional, and some are collected for service purposes rather than marketing.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can define separate audiences and field mappings. Would you expect purchase history to be copied into the platform?

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): Not by default. Marketing may need a broad category or eligibility flag, not every transaction detail. Data minimization is part of the design, not something we document after launch.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Understood. We will put a minimized schema in the implementation workbook. How should consent changes reach the system?

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): As close to real time as practical. If someone withdraws consent in the preference center, they must not remain eligible because a nightly batch has not run yet.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We need to verify the connector behavior and fallback suppression. I will bring our integration specialist to the next session.

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): Please do. Also, do not describe the fallback as guaranteed until we see the configuration. We have had vendors use “automatic” very loosely.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Fair. Next topic: access. Who will create segments and journeys?

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): The central lifecycle team, regional marketing, and an agency. The agency should not see every region's customer data. We need role separation and probably separate workspaces or carefully scoped permissions.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can design permissions around those groups and test them with sample accounts. Would privacy approve a sandbox using production-like data?

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): Only if it is masked or synthetic test data. Do not copy live customer records into a demo environment for convenience.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Noted. For deletion requests, do you need a record removed immediately from all systems?

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): We need a documented process that distinguishes deletion, suppression, and legal retention. A customer-service record may have a retention obligation, while marketing eligibility must stop immediately. Those are not the same action.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That distinction should be in the runbook. We can identify the source of truth and the downstream confirmation for each action.

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): Yes, and include failed jobs. A process that works only when every API call succeeds is not a control.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We will add monitoring, an exception owner, and evidence of completion. What about regional transfers?

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): Legal is still assessing them. I need the hosting locations, subprocessors, transfer mechanism, and incident-notification language. The fact that the vendor has a security certification is helpful, but it does not answer the transfer question.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will send the subprocessor register and data-location documentation. Our counsel can respond on notification language.

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): Good. The implementation date should remain provisional until legal signs off. Marketing has already circulated a target launch, which is premature.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will label it as a planning target with dependencies. We can still prepare mappings and training material while the legal review continues.

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): Training is fine if it uses masked examples. I also want a short module explaining what data users must not export into campaign notes.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is a useful control. We can include prohibited-field examples and a review checklist before a journey is published.

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): And make the checklist practical. If it takes twenty minutes for every small correction, people will work around it.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can separate low-risk edits from new audiences and new data fields, with different approval levels.

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): That sounds workable, though I want to see the actual role matrix.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will send the workbook today, request the technical follow-up, and leave hosting, transfer terms, deletion behavior, and consent timing as open decisions.

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): Add the Customer.io comparison only if it helps document the control differences. I do not need a feature bake-off.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Understood. We will focus on the operating model and evidence. Anything I missed?

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): The agency contract. Procurement needs to know whether agency users are covered by the same restrictions. I will send that question after this call.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Please do. Thanks, Kristy. We have a plan for the work, but not yet a go-live approval.

Kristy Steuber (Privacy): Exactly. Send the follow-up, and I will coordinate with legal.
