Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That sounds like a visibility issue. What happens after the first inquiry today?

Cordelia Koss (Sales Operations): The inquiry lands in our homegrown database. A coordinator reviews it, assigns a counselor, and sends a few templated messages. Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How do you know whether a campaign is creating qualified inquiries rather than just volume?

Cordelia Koss (Sales Operations): We mostly export a monthly file and compare inquiry counts with enrollment deposits. It is slow, and the dates do not line up cleanly. Marketing claims that a campaign worked because it produced many forms. Admissions says half of those forms were students looking for a different program. Both sides have a point.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would a better handoff look like?

Cordelia Koss (Sales Operations): At minimum, a common definition for lifecycle stages, a required program field, and a visible source history. I also need assignment rules that do not put a healthcare program inquiry with a counselor who handles technology programs. Right now, someone checks a queue twice a day.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can discuss routing based on program, geography, and inquiry type. Are there compliance or consent requirements that affect outreach?

Cordelia Koss (Sales Operations): Yes. We work with adults and occasionally younger learners through partner programs, so consent and guardian information can matter. The database has a checkbox, but it is not always clear what the person agreed to. Also, unsubscribes are handled differently by our email tool and our database.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That sounds risky. Is the issue that people are receiving messages after opting out, or that you cannot prove what was sent?

Cordelia Koss (Sales Operations): Mostly the second, but we have had complaints about the first. A person can unsubscribe from a newsletter and still receive a counselor’s manual follow-up because the counselor does not see the suppression status. That is one reason I am involved in this project.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. We should include suppression synchronization in the evaluation. How much of your current process is in the homegrown system versus separate tools?

Cordelia Koss (Sales Operations): The database stores the contact and inquiry record. Email is in a basic platform. Forms are managed by the website team. Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Which metrics would leadership care about?

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Does the current database support stage aging?

Cordelia Koss (Sales Operations): Technically, yes. Practically, no. There is a status field, but counselors update it inconsistently. Sometimes “contacted” means an email was sent; sometimes it means the person replied. That destroys the report.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): So part of the project is process design, not just software configuration.

Cordelia Koss (Sales Operations): Exactly. A beautiful automation built on ambiguous stages would only produce faster nonsense.
