Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Cleveland, thanks for joining. Before we dive in, are we still expecting anyone from the ActiveCampaign side of the evaluation?

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): No, this is our internal onboarding group. The purchase didn’t go through, but we wanted to review what we learned and close the loop on the project.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Understood. I saw the opportunity was closed lost in April, and ActiveCampaign was listed as the competitor. I’ll treat this as a retrospective, not as a deployment session.

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): That’s right. We need to understand why the project stalled. We’re a 746-person retail organization, and the evaluation had broad support but never became operationally safe.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What felt unsafe?

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): Data ownership. Marketing wanted faster journey building. IT wanted tighter controls. Store operations wanted regional flexibility. Those needs were all reasonable, but no one could agree on the first release.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Did ActiveCampaign win because it solved that disagreement?

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): Partly. Their team presented a smaller initial scope and made the migration feel less disruptive. We were comparing feature lists while they were talking about a first campaign in two weeks.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That’s useful. From your perspective, what did our process miss?

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): We had several good demos, but the people building the journeys weren’t in the first technical conversation. The executives heard reporting language; the operators heard configuration language later. By then, confidence had slipped.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Can you give me an example?

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): The executive team wanted a clear view of campaign influence and regional revenue. Our practitioners wanted to test journeys, suppress store-specific audiences, and change workflows without waiting three weeks for a ticket. We discussed both, but not in the same meeting.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): So the value case was split by persona.

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): Exactly. Our VP wanted a board-ready answer. The operators wanted a tool that wouldn’t slow them down.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Was there a technical blocker with the CRM or commerce systems?

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): No single blocker. The integration was possible. The concern was what happened when a store changed ownership, a customer belonged to multiple regions, or a promotion needed a same-day exclusion.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Did we test those edge cases?

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): Not deeply enough. We showed a clean customer record. Our real data is not clean. There are duplicate households, shared emails, and regional fields that are sometimes manually maintained.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That would make a staged validation important.

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): Yes, but our timeline was already compressed. Procurement asked for a recommendation, and the team interpreted that as a request for a complete answer. We should have proposed a small data slice and two journeys.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What were those journeys?

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): A post-purchase follow-up and a lapsed-customer reactivation flow. Both had clear business owners. Instead, we debated the entire lifecycle architecture.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Did price decide the outcome?

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): It mattered, but it wasn’t the decisive point. The opportunity amount was 98,965, and leadership could have supported it if the risk case were clear. ActiveCampaign’s proposal was easier to explain internally.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Did the team believe our platform was too complex?

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): Some people did. Others liked the flexibility. That’s the contradiction: flexibility appealed to experienced operators, but it looked like governance work to everyone else.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What would have changed the decision?

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): A joint workshop with Marketing Operations, IT, and an executive sponsor. We needed to define the minimum data model, agree on regional rules, and show how reporting would be consumed. Also, a named implementation lead would have helped.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Was there an implementation partner involved?

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): No. We discussed partners, but the cost and procurement process made that feel like another project. In hindsight, the absence of a partner increased the burden on our internal team.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That’s an important distinction. The platform itself wasn’t the only variable.

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): Correct. And I don’t want the lesson to be “simple always wins.” Our most sophisticated regional team actually preferred your workflow builder. They lost influence because the central team was worried about rollout risk.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That weakens a general conclusion that complexity alone caused the loss.

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): Yes. The issue was mismatch between complexity and readiness. If we had a partner or a smaller pilot, the same flexibility might have become an advantage.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What should we document for future evaluations?

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): Start with the business decision, not the feature inventory. Put executive reporting and operator workflow in one agenda. Test bad data and exceptions. Name the person who owns implementation. And don’t leave the practitioners until the end.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): I’ll capture those points. Any unresolved concern you still have?

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): Data retention and regional consent remained unclear in our notes. We also never finalized who would approve suppression rules. Those weren’t the reason we lost, but they would have become issues later.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That’s fair. Thank you for being candid, Cleveland.

Cleveland Quigley (Marketing Operations): Thanks, Nina. The review was useful, even if the answer is already closed.
