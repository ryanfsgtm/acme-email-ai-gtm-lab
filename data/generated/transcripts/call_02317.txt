Priya Shah (Account Executive): Was that primarily a product decision, a commercial decision, or a confidence issue?

Ova Stehr (VP Marketing): A little of all three. ActiveCampaign is less ambitious, but the team knows it. We had a small group maintaining newsletters and nurture sequences, and they could make changes without waiting for someone technical.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): When you say waiting, where did the friction show up?

Ova Stehr (VP Marketing): Reporting and permissions. The dashboards looked polished, but our regional managers wanted different views. We also spent too much time deciding who was allowed to alter a journey. It became a governance discussion every time a campaign changed.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is frustrating. Did the platform fail to support those views, or was the configuration work not completed?

Ova Stehr (VP Marketing): Probably the latter, but the distinction did not help us during the quarter. We needed the report for a board meeting, and the answer was, “We can build that.” We heard that several times.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Fair. I also see the contract value was 23,428, and expansion had been discussed. What made expansion unattractive?

Ova Stehr (VP Marketing): We had not proven the basic operating model. Adding more contacts or regions before proving reporting would have felt irresponsible. The sales team was enthusiastic about new journeys; finance asked why the existing ones were not producing a clean pipeline view.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What did the existing journeys produce?

Ova Stehr (VP Marketing): Opens and clicks, some form activity, and a few useful alerts. But the attribution was messy. When a prospect attended a webinar and later spoke with sales, we could not agree on whether marketing should get credit. It sounds like a small issue, but it made the expansion case weak.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Did ActiveCampaign solve attribution, or did you decide to stop measuring at that level?

Ova Stehr (VP Marketing): We stopped measuring at that level for now. That is not necessarily a victory for ActiveCampaign. It is just easier to operate.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That distinction matters. If we were to revisit this later, what would have to change first?

Ova Stehr (VP Marketing): A named owner for the reporting model, a short implementation plan, and commercial terms that do not assume we will immediately scale. Also, I would want a practical demonstration using our Canadian and U.S. segments, not a generic slide deck.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I can commit to the demonstration and a scoped re-entry plan. I cannot promise the old pricing remains available, especially after the close date, but I can ask our commercial team to separate the base use case from future expansion.

Ova Stehr (VP Marketing): That would help. Another issue was support response time. Nothing was disastrous, but a question could sit for two days while a campaign deadline passed.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Were those questions submitted through the regular support channel or through your account team?

Ova Stehr (VP Marketing): Both. The regular channel was fine for “how do I do this?” questions. It was less good when the question involved data structure and our deadline was tomorrow.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. We should have set escalation expectations earlier. Did anyone on your side receive formal enablement?

Ova Stehr (VP Marketing): We had an onboarding series, but attendance was uneven. Two people changed roles, and the person who understood the original setup left. So there was institutional memory loss as well.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a confounder we should own, though it does not erase the experience. Would a focused administrator workshop have changed the decision?
