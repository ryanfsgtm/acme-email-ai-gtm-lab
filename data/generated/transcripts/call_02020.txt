Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Nina, thanks for meeting. I understand privacy is involved in the marketing automation review. I’d like to learn what your team needs and where the current process creates risk.

Nina Langosh (Privacy): Sure. We are a financial-services firm with a large employee base and several jurisdictions. Marketing wants faster campaign execution, but privacy is concerned about purpose, consent, and vendor boundaries.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What is happening today?

Nina Langosh (Privacy): Lists are assembled by regional teams and sent to marketing operations. Some are well documented. Others arrive with a note saying “existing relationship,” which is not a sufficient legal explanation by itself.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Does the proposed platform replace that process?

Nina Langosh (Privacy): It could make the process more visible, but visibility is not compliance. We would still need a lawful basis, suppression rules, retention, and evidence of who approved an audience.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would you want to see in an evaluation?

Nina Langosh (Privacy): Clear field-level controls, auditability, deletion behavior, and a way to prevent a region from using another region’s list accidentally. Also, we need to know whether the vendor’s standard settings are actually the settings we would receive.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can walk through permissions and audit logs. Is the principal concern customer data leaving your environment?

Nina Langosh (Privacy): That is one concern, but not the only one. Internal access can create the same practical issue. A trusted employee exporting a list is still a data event.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. Are there particular campaign types that should be excluded?

Nina Langosh (Privacy): Cross-sell campaigns involving sensitive financial indicators should not be in the first phase. General educational communications may be suitable if the audience rules are documented.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): So a narrower pilot could be acceptable.

Nina Langosh (Privacy): Possibly. But do not frame it as “privacy approved the pilot” until we review the exact data flow. The word pilot has a habit of becoming production by Friday.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is fair. Who else needs to participate?

Nina Langosh (Privacy): Security, marketing operations, and the data-protection officer for the relevant region. Procurement will also ask about subprocessors and breach notification.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What about Eloqua, the other vendor in the review?

Nina Langosh (Privacy): Eloqua has an advantage because some teams already know it. Familiarity is not the same as fit, though. We are comparing operational maturity as well as features.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would make a solution fail the review?

Nina Langosh (Privacy): Inability to prove deletion, unclear subprocessor changes, broad administrator access, or an audit trail that omits exports. Also, claims that every jurisdiction can use one universal configuration.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Could a universal baseline with regional controls work?

Nina Langosh (Privacy): Maybe, but only after classification. Our contracts vary, and some customer records have stricter handling terms. A single global rule may be convenient and still wrong.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What timeline is realistic?

Nina Langosh (Privacy): I can review documentation in two weeks. A decision would take longer because the business wants a comparison with Eloqua and the architecture group meets monthly.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That helps. Would it be useful if I sent a data-flow diagram, retention details, permission examples, and the subprocessor list before the next meeting?

Nina Langosh (Privacy): Yes, provided the documents distinguish current functionality from planned functionality. We have been burned by roadmap language presented as if it were available.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I’ll label those separately. Is there any evidence that the current process is causing actual customer harm?

Nina Langosh (Privacy): We have had near misses and inconsistent suppression, but I am not aware of a confirmed customer complaint tied to this workflow. That does not make the control gap acceptable; it means the case for change is preventative.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That distinction is important. If the first phase used only approved educational audiences, would that address part of the concern?

Nina Langosh (Privacy): It would reduce scope, not eliminate review. We would still test access, deletion, and the approval record.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I’ll send the packet and propose a joint session with security and marketing operations.

Nina Langosh (Privacy): Good. Please do not schedule it as a final approval meeting. Call it a technical and privacy review.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Agreed. Thanks for the clarity.

Nina Langosh (Privacy): Thank you. The safest answer today is that we have a path to evaluate, not a decision.
