Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Carole, thanks for joining. I understand you support implementations for Shanahan Group, so I would like to hear how projects are run from your side rather than assume the marketing team speaks for everyone.

Carole Cummerata (Implementation Partner): That is sensible. I work across client programmes, and Shanahan brought me in because their marketing operations team is overloaded. I am not the final budget owner, but I influence the technical recommendation.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What prompted the evaluation now?

Carole Cummerata (Implementation Partner): They have grown quickly across media properties and regional brands. Leads arrive from forms, events, partners, and some old lists. The team can send campaigns, but handoffs to sales are inconsistent. A lead may be followed up in one region and ignored in another.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): How are those handoffs managed today?

Carole Cummerata (Implementation Partner): A CRM, several spreadsheets, and email. I know, not elegant. The CRM is authoritative in theory, but campaign context often lives elsewhere. Sales says marketing sends too much; marketing says sales does not work the good leads.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What would a successful first phase accomplish?

Carole Cummerata (Implementation Partner): One intake model, clear qualification rules, and visibility into response time. I would also like reusable journeys for the different brands, because rebuilding the same logic twelve times is expensive.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Are the brands governed centrally?

Carole Cummerata (Implementation Partner): Partly. Central marketing owns standards, but each brand has its own tone, consent language, and sometimes its own CRM fields. That is where implementations become messy.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Would you need separate workspaces or shared governance with local permissions?

Carole Cummerata (Implementation Partner): I do not know yet. Separate workspaces give comfort, but they can create duplicate data and reporting. I want to test the operating model before making that decision.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That makes sense. Which systems would need to integrate?

Carole Cummerata (Implementation Partner): The CRM first, webinar and event tools second, and probably the data warehouse later. The warehouse team will not accept a connector that creates unexplained changes in production.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can map objects and ownership, then run a controlled test. What volume should we plan for?

Carole Cummerata (Implementation Partner): Around seven hundred employees, but the reachable contact universe is much larger and changes by brand. I would rather size from actual records and growth than use employee count as a proxy.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Good point. How is consent captured and audited?

Carole Cummerata (Implementation Partner): Differently by property. Some forms are modern; others are inherited. We need a way to preserve source and preference history. If the system merely stores a current checkbox, legal will reject it.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What reporting does leadership expect?

Carole Cummerata (Implementation Partner): Pipeline influence, conversion by source, and speed from inquiry to first action. They also want brand-level views without losing the consolidated picture. The definitions are not agreed, which may be a bigger problem than the software.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I agree. Before configuration, we should define the measures and owners. Who would participate in a technical validation?

Carole Cummerata (Implementation Partner): Marketing operations, CRM administration, one regional brand lead, security, and me. Sales should join for the handoff rules, though they may complain about another workshop.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can keep it focused around two real journeys: an event lead and a website inquiry. We will document what happens, what syncs, and where exceptions need human review.

Carole Cummerata (Implementation Partner): That would be useful. I also need implementation effort and an honest migration view. There are old records with missing consent, and nobody wants to discover that in week six.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can segment the migration: active records, suppressions, incomplete records, and archival data. I will not assume everything should move.

Carole Cummerata (Implementation Partner): Good. Timing is flexible, but the technical validation needs to happen before the July commercial checkpoint. The opportunity is still open, and I am not saying Shanahan has selected a vendor.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Understood. I will send a discovery summary, questions for the CRM owner, and a proposed validation agenda. What is the next decision point?

Carole Cummerata (Implementation Partner): After the technical session, I will recommend whether to proceed to a scoped pilot. Budget approval comes from the marketing and operations leaders together.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That gives us a clear path. Anything I missed?

Carole Cummerata (Implementation Partner): Only that the implementation partner needs access to enough detail to configure properly, but not unrestricted access to every brand. We should solve that early.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Agreed. We will include partner permissions in the design. Thanks, Carole.
