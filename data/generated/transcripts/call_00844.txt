Priya Shah (Account Executive): Allan, good morning. Is now still convenient? I know you have a board-prep session later.

Allan Hudson (CMO): It is convenient, yes. I have forty-five minutes before that, and I would like to leave with a workable first-month plan. We are a small education organization, so our implementation team is basically three people wearing six hats.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is helpful context. The agreement is closed, and the annual value is £11,699. Today I would like to confirm goals, audiences, ownership, integrations, and the first launch. What made this the right time to implement?

Allan Hudson (CMO): We have been sending newsletters manually, and our event follow-up is inconsistent. Someone remembers to email attendees, someone else remembers to call, and then nobody knows which version of the story is true. We need a simple operating rhythm before we attempt anything ambitious.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What is the first audience you want to bring over?

Allan Hudson (CMO): Prospective students and professional-development leads. Existing alumni are important, but I do not want them mixed into the first migration. We have roughly 8,000 contacts in different spreadsheets, though I suspect the usable number is lower.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How are those contacts currently classified?

Allan Hudson (CMO): Some by course interest, some by event attendance, and some by the person who happened to own the spreadsheet. We have duplicate records and missing consent dates. The database is not a disaster, but it is certainly not a polished asset.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then a staged import with a consent review is sensible. Who can make decisions about field definitions?

Allan Hudson (CMO): I can decide on marketing fields. Our admissions manager owns the operational definition of an active applicant. She needs to be involved because a “lead” to us might mean someone who downloaded a brochure, while an admissions record means something much more serious.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can keep those as separate lifecycle stages. I also recommend agreeing on the handoff rule before building automation. What should trigger admissions follow-up?

Allan Hudson (CMO): A completed application, obviously. For earlier signals, perhaps two event attendances or a request for a call. I do not want to send a salesperson after every brochure download. That would annoy people and create work we cannot service.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a good boundary. We can start with high-intent triggers and leave scoring for a later phase. Do you have an email provider or CRM that must connect on day one?

Allan Hudson (CMO): We have a student information system. It is not a modern API product. There is an export every night, and our operations person uploads a file. If your team expects real-time synchronization, we will disappoint each other.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): A nightly file can be a valid first step. We should document the cut-off time and what happens when a row is malformed. Would you prefer the marketing platform to be the source of truth for campaign activity while the student system remains the source for enrolment status?

Allan Hudson (CMO): Yes, that sounds right. I want to avoid copying every field into every system. Keep the ownership clear.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): For the first launch, I suggest one welcome sequence for new enquiry contacts and one follow-up sequence after an information session. How many emails feels acceptable?

Allan Hudson (CMO): Three for the welcome sequence over ten days. The event follow-up could be two messages, one immediately and one a week later. But the copy must be reviewed by admissions; marketing can write it, but we should not imply a course guarantees an employment outcome.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can set content approval before activation. Who supplies brand assets and sender details?

Allan Hudson (CMO): Our communications coordinator has the logo and templates. I will own the sender domain configuration. She is on leave next week, so do not make the kickoff dependent on receiving a perfect template.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can use a plain approved layout for the first test and refine the design later. What is your target launch date?

Allan Hudson (CMO): If we can test with internal addresses by the end of March, I would be happy. Public launch in early April, assuming consent and deliverability checks pass.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That gives us enough time. For training, we can do two ninety-minute sessions and a recorded walkthrough. Which users need access?

Allan Hudson (CMO): Me, the communications coordinator, admissions, and one part-time events assistant. The events assistant should not be able to change global subscription settings by accident.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can assign a restricted role for that user. Do you want local teams to create their own lists?

Allan Hudson (CMO): Enquiries, event registrations, applications, and eventual enrolment. I know the last two will not be attributable perfectly on day one. I need a useful trend line, not a claim that every enrolment came from one email.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can build a first dashboard around source, campaign engagement, and handoff status, with a note on attribution limits. Do you have a baseline for response time today?

Allan Hudson (CMO): It varies. Some event attendees hear from us the same day; others wait a week. That inconsistency is one reason for the project. A reasonable first target would be acknowledgement within one business day.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Good. I will include that as an operational measure. We also need to decide whether old contacts with unclear consent are excluded or reviewed manually.

Allan Hudson (CMO): Excluded from marketing until reviewed. We can keep them in a restricted holding file, but I do not want a clever import rule to create a compliance problem.
