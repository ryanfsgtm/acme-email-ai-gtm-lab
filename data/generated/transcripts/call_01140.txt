Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Anne, thank you for joining. Before we talk about the platform, could you describe how marketing works today at Jacobs, Wisoky and Moen?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): It’s a small software company, so “marketing” is me, one coordinator, and occasional help from sales. We use Mailchimp for email, a CRM for opportunities, and a lot of manual exports. I know that sounds untidy.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): It sounds common. Where does the manual work hurt most?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): Reporting and follow-up. Someone downloads a guide, and we may not know whether they’re an existing customer, an active prospect, or just a student. Then sales asks why the lead wasn’t routed, and marketing asks why sales never followed up.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That suggests two problems: identity and process. How are leads currently qualified?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): Mostly by a score in Mailchimp and a quick look at the company domain. It isn’t sophisticated. We sell to other software firms, so a lot of visitors look like good fits on paper.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What would make a lead genuinely useful to sales?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): A relevant role, a company in our target size range, and an actual project. A click by itself isn’t enough. We’ve had people click every link in an email because they were testing it.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That’s a good counterexample to simple engagement scoring. What happens after a lead is deemed qualified?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): An email goes to a sales alias. Sometimes it gets assigned. Sometimes it becomes a task that nobody owns. We don’t have a formal SLA.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): If the system created an assigned CRM task with an escalation timer, would that solve it?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): It would solve the visibility problem. It wouldn’t create sales capacity. We have five account executives, and they’re already busy. A flood of alerts could make things worse.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): So volume control matters. Would you prefer a higher threshold or a staged review queue?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): A staged queue. Marketing can review the first signal, then sales gets fewer, better alerts. We also need to exclude customers from prospect nurture. That sounds obvious, but our lists are not clean.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): How are customer and prospect statuses stored?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): In different fields depending on who entered the record. Some customers are marked “active,” others “closed won,” and one old segment uses “subscriber.”

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That will need normalization before automation. What would success look like six months after launch?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): I want to know which campaigns create conversations, not just opens. I want sales to trust the handoff. And I want my coordinator to stop spending Fridays cleaning CSV files.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What’s the current database size?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): About eight thousand records, but only maybe three thousand are active marketing contacts. The rest are old, suppressed, duplicates, or people we don’t understand.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That distinction is important. Are you concerned about pricing based on stored versus active contacts?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): Yes, and I want the definitions in writing. We’ve looked at another system where the sales pitch sounded affordable until we counted every historical record.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We can model the tiers using your actual file and show which records are stored, marketable, suppressed, and archived. We should also document how a contact moves between those states.

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): Good. I don’t want a generic calculator.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What other objections have you heard internally?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): Cost, obviously. We’re an SMB. The finance lead says marketing automation is expensive compared with Mailchimp. But there’s also confusion about what gets counted. Sometimes people say the product is too expensive when they really mean they don’t understand the contact model.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That’s useful to separate. Would a pilot with a limited segment help finance evaluate value?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): Maybe, though a pilot can understate the implementation work. We need to understand the full cost, including cleanup and training.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Agreed. We can include one-time migration effort, ongoing subscription, and internal hours. What systems must integrate on day one?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): CRM and the website forms. Product usage would be nice, but engineering won’t prioritize it this quarter.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Then product usage should be phase two. For forms, do you have consent source and timestamp today?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): Sometimes. The old forms only have a checkbox, no source detail. We’ll need a compliance review before rebuilding them.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We’ll include that as a discovery workstream. Are there regional requirements to consider?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): We’re UK-based, with some EU customers. Our privacy lead will want to review retention, deletion, and unsubscribe synchronization.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Understood. Let’s return to sales follow-up. If a prospect visits a pricing page twice, is that enough to alert someone?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): Not necessarily. A researcher might do that. If the same person submits a technical question or requests a meeting, then yes. Context matters more than a raw score.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We can combine behavioral activity with explicit intent and firmographic fit. How would you like attribution handled?

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): Separate first-touch source from influenced pipeline. We have referrals and partner introductions that should get credit even when marketing later sends an email.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That can be preserved. We’ll avoid treating an email open as sourced pipeline and let you define meaningful influence.

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): Good. I’m open to the solution, but we lost confidence in the previous evaluation because the demo skipped the messy parts.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Then the next step should use your data: a contact sample, duplicate examples, current lifecycle values, and one real handoff scenario. We’ll return a proposed model and a cost view.

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): I can provide that. I’ll also bring finance and our CRM administrator. They’ll challenge the numbers, which is useful.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Excellent. We’ll schedule a working session rather than another presentation.

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): That’s the right format. I’m not committing to a purchase today, but I’m willing to continue if the model is transparent.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That’s a fair next step. I’ll send the data request and agenda this afternoon.

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): Thanks, Nina. And please include the stored-versus-active contact definitions in the agenda. That is where the internal debate keeps getting stuck.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): I will. Thanks for the candid discussion.

Anne Anderson (VP Marketing): Thank you.
