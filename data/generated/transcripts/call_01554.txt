Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Claire, hello. Thanks for making time. I know this is an unusual pairing since I normally support existing customers, but I was asked to help understand the operational requirements before the team revisits automation.

Claire Lang (Sales Operations): That's fine. I had wondered whether this was a renewal conversation. We are not currently using your platform, so I assume it is exploratory.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Correct. I have the background that Borer and Sons is an education company in the Benelux, around one hundred and eighty-eight employees, and that the prior opportunity closed lost last summer. I would like to understand what changed and what would need to be different.

Claire Lang (Sales Operations): The short version is that sales operations was asked to support more campaigns without adding headcount. We looked at automation, but the business did not agree on ownership. Marketing thought sales should define the follow-up; sales thought marketing should qualify the leads.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That sounds like a process issue before it is a software issue. How are leads handled today?

Claire Lang (Sales Operations): Web inquiries go to a shared mailbox. Someone exports them once or twice a day, removes obvious duplicates, and assigns them in a spreadsheet. If the spreadsheet is open by somebody else, the update waits. It is not elegant.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What happens to a lead after assignment?

Claire Lang (Sales Operations): The rep gets an email. There is no consistent service-level timer. Some reps call the same day; others wait until their next planning block. We can see the delay only if someone audits the sheet.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): If you had an alert after a defined period, would managers act on it?

Claire Lang (Sales Operations): Maybe. We tried a reminder in another tool, and it generated so many notifications that managers muted it. The threshold must account for school holidays and regional schedules.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can define business hours and escalation rules, but I would want your team to agree on the exception policy first. A reminder cannot solve a disputed definition of “late.”

Claire Lang (Sales Operations): Agreed. Another requirement is language. We work in Dutch, French, and English. The team does not want three completely separate processes.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): The workflow can be shared while templates and routing messages vary by language. Which language determines assignment?

Claire Lang (Sales Operations): Territory first, language second. A Dutch-speaking prospect in the French territory should still go to the territory team, with the right message template.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is clear. How clean is the data going in?

Claire Lang (Sales Operations): Mixed. Company names are inconsistent, and some school districts share a domain. A simple email-domain deduplication rule would merge records that should remain separate.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then deduplication needs to use more than domain. We could start with a review queue rather than automatic merging. That protects the account structure while the rules are tested.

Claire Lang (Sales Operations): Good. We have been burned by overconfident automation. People hear “automatic” and assume “correct.”

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What reporting would make this worthwhile for you?

Claire Lang (Sales Operations): First-response time by territory, assignment backlog, and conversion from inquiry to qualified meeting. I do not need a huge executive dashboard. I need to find the broken step on Tuesday morning.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can provide operational views and a weekly summary. Would you also need campaign attribution?

Claire Lang (Sales Operations): Eventually. For now, attribution would distract us. We do not even have a consistent source field. Some reps type “website,” others type “event,” and one writes “Claire's list,” which is not a source.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then source governance belongs in the first phase. We can offer a controlled list and make the field required at the relevant stage.

Claire Lang (Sales Operations): That may meet resistance. Sales dislikes required fields when they cannot see the benefit.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Could the benefit be shown through the weekly conversion view? If the field drives a report they use, the requirement has a purpose.

Claire Lang (Sales Operations): Possibly, but we need a manager sponsor. Our previous project had a sponsor who moved departments, and the work lost momentum.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Who would own this now?

Claire Lang (Sales Operations): The VP of Sales would need to sponsor it. I can coordinate requirements, but I cannot mandate behavior across the territories.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then the next meeting should include the VP or a delegated sales leader. We can map the current process and agree on two measurable outcomes.

Claire Lang (Sales Operations): Yes, although timing is uncertain. We are in the middle of the academic-year planning cycle, and everyone is stretched.
