Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Matilde, thank you for taking the call. I understand you are exploring marketing automation for Jaskolski and Sons, but the previous evaluation ended without a decision. I would like to understand what changed and what did not.

Matilde Sanford (CMO): That is a fair place to start. We are a 196-person healthcare company, and the last project became one more initiative competing for attention. Nobody formally rejected it. It simply lost oxygen.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): When you say lost oxygen, was that budget, ownership, timing, or confidence in the project?

Matilde Sanford (CMO): A mixture. The budget was available in principle, but the marketing operations lead left. The replacement was tactical and focused on campaigns already promised to the business. Our executive sponsor also moved to another division.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): So the problem was not only the product evaluation; the internal conditions changed.

Matilde Sanford (CMO): Exactly. We had a close date on the calendar, then moved it twice, and eventually accepted that we were not ready. The phrase was “let us revisit when the team stabilizes.”

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is helpful context. What is the current team structure?

Matilde Sanford (CMO): I have one demand-generation manager, one content person, and a shared CRM administrator. We can execute campaigns, but we do not have someone dedicated to designing a large automation program from scratch.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What is the first business outcome you would need to see?

Matilde Sanford (CMO): Better follow-up for prospective patients and referral partners, with appropriate consent handling. We have several service lines, and leads are distributed unevenly. Right now, a request can sit in a spreadsheet until someone notices it.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How quickly does someone need to respond?

Matilde Sanford (CMO): Same business day for high-intent inquiries. For lower-intent educational content, a slower nurture is fine. The issue is that we do not distinguish them reliably.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can use source, service interest, and engagement to create different routes. Before we talk about advanced journey branching, how are those fields maintained today?

Matilde Sanford (CMO): Source is reasonably good. Service interest is inconsistent because it is entered differently by three teams. Consent is the bigger concern. Healthcare language makes everyone nervous, even when the campaign is not about treatment.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That caution is appropriate. We would want legal or compliance to define which data can be used for marketing and which should remain out of scope. The platform should not become a place for clinical information.

Matilde Sanford (CMO): Correct. I am not asking for patient records. I want a clean process around legitimate business inquiries and referral relationships.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Who would approve the process?

Matilde Sanford (CMO): Compliance would review the rules. The CRM administrator would own the connection. I would sponsor the business case, but I will not be the person building every workflow.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That distinction matters. If the day-to-day operator is not in the evaluation, we risk choosing something that looks impressive but is difficult to run.

Matilde Sanford (CMO): Yes. The last vendor demo was all executive dashboards. My team needed to know how to fix a broken enrollment or change a message without opening a ticket.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Let me show one example. A contact enters from a referral form, receives an acknowledgement, waits one business day, and then routes to an owner if the inquiry meets the agreed criteria. The operator can inspect why a contact did or did not enter the next step.

Matilde Sanford (CMO): That is closer to what we need. Can the owner be assigned by service line and geography?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Yes, provided those fields are available and governed. The routing can also create a task rather than merely send an email.

Matilde Sanford (CMO): Good. We have had automation that sends notifications but never creates accountability.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would success look like after ninety days?

Matilde Sanford (CMO): Ninety percent of high-intent inquiries acknowledged within fifteen minutes, same-day assignment for the rest, and a report that tells me where the process breaks. I would also want the team to launch a simple nurture without outside help.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Those are measurable. What might prevent the project from starting this time?

Matilde Sanford (CMO): Ownership again. If we buy before hiring the new operations manager, the tool may sit unused. We are interviewing candidates now, but I cannot promise a start date.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would a phased launch reduce that risk, or would it create more work?

Matilde Sanford (CMO): A narrow phase could help if it is genuinely narrow. One inquiry route, one consent model, one reporting view. I do not want a six-month transformation program disguised as a pilot.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. We can define the first phase around referral inquiries and leave the other service lines for later. I will include implementation assumptions and the skills required from your side.

Matilde Sanford (CMO): Include an estimate of internal hours. Our team needs to see the opportunity cost, not just the subscription.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I will. There is also an advanced journey-branching option for more complex paths, but it may not belong in phase one.

Matilde Sanford (CMO): Show it as a later option, not a requirement. The priority is reliability and adoption.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That makes sense. What is the procurement path now?

Matilde Sanford (CMO): Finance reviews the business case, compliance reviews data use, and then our COO approves. The previous process stopped before the final approval because the sponsor disappeared and the close date kept moving.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I will document named owners for each stage and avoid setting a close date until those owners confirm timing. Would you invite the CRM administrator to the next session?

Matilde Sanford (CMO): Yes, and compliance should attend at least part of it. If the operator and compliance reviewer agree on the first workflow, the project has a chance.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I will schedule a working session with those participants, bring the phased proposal, and include the failure-reporting example. We will leave the larger roadmap separate.

Matilde Sanford (CMO): Good. I am interested, but interest is not approval. We need the team and the controls in place.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. I will send the recap with open questions and no assumed commitment.

Matilde Sanford (CMO): Thanks, Marcus. That is a sensible next step.
