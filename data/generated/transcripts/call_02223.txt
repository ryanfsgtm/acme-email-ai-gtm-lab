Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Reinhold, thanks for meeting. I understand you are evaluating marketing automation from a privacy and governance perspective, so I would like to learn how decisions are made before discussing product details.
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): That is correct. Marketing asked me to assess whether the proposed system can be operated within our consent and retention requirements. I am not the budget owner.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That distinction is helpful. Who owns the business case?
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): The commercial director does, with input from marketing. She is traveling this week, so I am collecting answers rather than approving anything.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. How are campaigns handled today?
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): Mostly spreadsheets, a retail email tool, and manual exports from the loyalty system. It is not elegant, but the team knows where the files are.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What prompted the review now?
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): A new loyalty program and the need to coordinate store promotions with online orders. Marketing believes automation will improve timing. My concern is that convenience can hide a weak legal basis.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Which consent questions are most important?
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): Separate consent for promotional email, profiling, and sharing data with franchisees. We also need proof of when consent was captured and what wording was displayed.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can discuss audit fields and purpose-level preferences. How are those recorded today?
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): In several places. Website forms use one checkbox, the loyalty app uses two, and stores sometimes collect paper cards. The paper records are later entered by staff. There are gaps.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would a phased implementation help, beginning with digital records and leaving paper imports for review?
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): Possibly. But I do not want “phase one” to become a permanent excuse for incomplete records.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Fair. We would define acceptance criteria for each phase. What retention period applies to inactive loyalty contacts?
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): The policy says twenty-four months after the last relationship, but “relationship” is disputed. A purchase, an email click, and a store inquiry may have different meanings.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That should be resolved by your policy owner before automation rules are finalized. We can expose the dates, but we should not invent the rule.
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): Exactly. Also, deletion requests must propagate to the marketing platform and downstream systems. A monthly batch is probably too slow.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What response time do you require?
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): Usually thirty days legally, but our internal target is ten business days. The operational issue is verifying that deletion did not remove a transaction record we must retain.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can separate suppression from transactional retention, with documented interfaces. I would want your data protection lead and the system owner in a technical workshop.
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): I can arrange that, but not until the director returns. She is the executive sponsor for this review, although I cannot say how much time she will have.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Should we wait for her before scheduling a workshop, or could the technical team define the questions first?
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): Schedule a preliminary session. Keep it exploratory. Please do not call it approval or a launch meeting.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Noted. What would make the evaluation fail?
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): Inability to preserve consent history, unclear processor responsibilities, no reliable deletion workflow, or default settings that encourage broad targeting. Security review is separate but connected.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): And what would make it viable?
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): Narrow purpose controls, evidence that a user can manage preferences, transparent exports, and an implementation team willing to accept “not yet” when a rule is unresolved.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is a useful standard. Are there particular campaigns we could use for a controlled test?
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): A store-opening announcement would be too simple. A loyalty reactivation campaign is more representative, but it includes contacts with mixed consent histories.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That makes it a good test if we use synthetic internal records or a fully reviewed subset.
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): Use internal records first. We have had test sends go to real customers before, and I do not want a repeat.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I will record that as a hard requirement. How will the commercial team judge success?
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): They will look at revenue and response rates. I will look at whether the system can explain why each person was eligible.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Those measures can coexist, but the reporting must retain the eligibility logic. We should include an export showing audience membership, consent state, and the rule version.
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): Yes. Versioning is important because teams change a filter and later cannot reconstruct what happened.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I will propose that in the workshop agenda. One question: is the opportunity already committed?
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): No. Marketing is interested, but finance has not approved a purchase. The target date has moved twice because the director was unavailable.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Thanks for clarifying. I will treat timing as provisional and avoid making assumptions in the follow-up.
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): Good. Please send a short summary with open questions, owners, and the documents you need. I can provide the consent policy and retention schedule. The commercial case should come from marketing.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I will do that today and suggest a preliminary technical session after the director’s return. We will keep the next step focused on evidence and requirements.
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): That works. I am interested, but interest is not approval. If the sponsor does not engage, the project may remain an assessment.
Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. Thank you for being precise about that.
Reinhold Mante (Privacy): Thank you. Send the notes, and I will correct anything that overstates our position.
