Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Bertrand, thanks for joining. I saw that Smith - Greenholt has been using the marketing automation package since the March close. I’d like to understand what prompted the evaluation and what has changed since then.

Bertrand Hauck (Marketing Operations): Sure. We were trying to get campaigns out of disconnected spreadsheets. The team is seventy-six people overall, but marketing operations is small, so every manual list upload became a bottleneck.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How small is the operations team?

Bertrand Hauck (Marketing Operations): Three people, plus a part-time analyst. We support the Nordic region, so language and consent rules complicate the simple version of the story.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Walk me through a typical campaign before the purchase.

Bertrand Hauck (Marketing Operations): Product marketing would send a spreadsheet, sales would add names, and I would normalize fields before uploading anything. Then someone would notice a country or language issue after the email was queued. We had to stop and re-check it.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That sounds risky and slow. Was the primary driver speed, governance, or reporting?

Bertrand Hauck (Marketing Operations): Governance first. Speed was the visible pain, but we could not defend how some segments were assembled. Reporting came third. Leadership wanted attribution, though the data was not clean enough to support it.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What made you comfortable moving forward?

Bertrand Hauck (Marketing Operations): The review step and the ability to keep regional permissions visible. We did not want a black box that sent faster but made compliance harder.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): And now?

Bertrand Hauck (Marketing Operations): Campaign assembly is faster for the repeatable programs. I would not say everything is automated. One-off campaigns still need manual review, particularly when a country manager wants an exception.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How much time are you saving?

Bertrand Hauck (Marketing Operations): Maybe a day per standard campaign, sometimes less. The bigger improvement is fewer last-minute corrections. We have not quantified the correction rate properly, so I don't want to invent a percentage.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That's fair. Are sales users engaging with the system directly?

Bertrand Hauck (Marketing Operations): Some are. Regional sellers mostly consume the alerts and ask for segments. They do not build journeys themselves. That is fine for now, but it means our team remains the bottleneck for unusual requests.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would self-service be a goal?

Bertrand Hauck (Marketing Operations): Maybe for templates and approved audiences. Not for consent logic. If everyone can edit that, we create a different problem.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. What is still frustrating?

Bertrand Hauck (Marketing Operations): Naming. We have old campaign names in the reporting view, and people cannot tell which are active. Also, attribution is being discussed as if the new system solved it. It improved capture, but the CRM opportunity fields are not always completed.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): So the platform can collect the touch, but the downstream process determines whether leadership trusts the revenue report.

Bertrand Hauck (Marketing Operations): Exactly. The tool is not the whole operating model. I have said that twice, actually.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I’m glad you did. What would you prioritize over the next quarter?

Bertrand Hauck (Marketing Operations): Clean up naming, define two approved self-service audience types, and get regional managers to complete opportunity fields. If we only add more automation, we will automate ambiguity.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That phrase is going in my notes. Are there any concerns about performance or support?

Bertrand Hauck (Marketing Operations): Support has been responsive. There was a delay during a launch week, but it did not affect the send. I would like clearer escalation guidance when an audience is blocked close to deadline.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I can arrange a short escalation review and bring a reporting specialist for the attribution question. Would you include the regional managers?

Bertrand Hauck (Marketing Operations): Two of them, yes. The others will want a broad promise that we are not ready to make.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Good to know. Before we wrap, is the outcome still considered a win internally?

Bertrand Hauck (Marketing Operations): Yes, with conditions. We won back time and reduced corrections, but we have not earned automatic trust in every report. That takes process discipline.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I’ll summarize the win and the conditions, then send the proposed agenda.

Bertrand Hauck (Marketing Operations): Please do. And use “standard campaigns,” not “all campaigns.” The distinction matters.
