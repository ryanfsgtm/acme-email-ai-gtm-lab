Michael Hand (Privacy): Yes, although I should say at the outset that I am not the campaign owner. I advise marketing and the partners when personal data is involved. My concern is less “can they send an email” and more “can we demonstrate why they were allowed to send it.”

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That distinction is helpful. How are campaigns handled today?

Michael Hand (Privacy): Mostly manual lists and spreadsheets. Marketing exports contacts from several places, someone cleans them, and a person checks suppression requests before a mailing. There is a small CRM, but it is not the single source of truth. We also have contacts maintained by individual practice groups.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): So the same person could exist in multiple lists?

Michael Hand (Privacy): Absolutely. Sometimes with a slightly different company name, sometimes with a personal address and a business address. We have tried deduplication, but it becomes difficult when a partner says, “I know this person; keep them on my list.”

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That sounds like an area where automation could reduce effort while preserving a review step. What happens when someone withdraws consent?

Michael Hand (Privacy): We record it in a suppression file. The problem is timing. If an export was made yesterday, I cannot be certain the old file was discarded or that every local copy was updated. It is not necessarily a malicious process; it is just distributed and human.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. Would you need a full audit trail for every send—source, consent status, changes, and who approved the audience?

Michael Hand (Privacy): Yes, but “full” is doing a lot of work there. We need retention rules, access controls, and something a privacy reviewer can understand without asking an engineer to reconstruct it. We also need to know where processing takes place and whether our client data is used for anything beyond the campaign.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): The platform can keep activity history and separate operational data from model-training use. I do not want to overstate that before our security team confirms the exact configuration, though. Would a documented data-flow review be a prerequisite?

Michael Hand (Privacy): It would be one of them. We would also need a data-processing agreement and probably a questionnaire covering sub-processors, incident notification, deletion, and international transfers. Because we are in France, our legal team will ask questions that marketing may not anticipate.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That makes sense. On the business side, what is marketing hoping to improve?

Michael Hand (Privacy): Faster follow-up and more consistent communications. Different practice groups are sending similar messages, and there is no reliable view of whether a contact already received something. They want journeys and segmentation. I am not opposed to that. I just do not want “better segmentation” to mean collecting more information than necessary.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Could we begin with a narrow use case—existing business contacts, explicit consent, and a limited set of fields—then expand after review?

Michael Hand (Privacy): That would be more realistic. A broad rollout would be rejected. We have had tools proposed before that looked straightforward in a demo and became complicated once regional rules and partner-owned lists entered the picture.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would success look like for you after, say, the first quarter?

Michael Hand (Privacy): I would want to answer three questions quickly: why was this person contacted, what permission or lawful basis supported it, and how do we stop future contact if they object? If those answers depend on a spreadsheet somebody keeps on a laptop, we have not solved the problem.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): And for marketing?

Michael Hand (Privacy): They would probably say fewer manual exports, clearer reporting, and the ability to schedule a campaign without waiting three days for list preparation. But I should be clear: partner preference carries weight here. A partner can delay adoption even if the privacy case is sound.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Is there a particular partner group that would sponsor this?
