Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Owen, thanks for meeting this late. I saw the calendar invite had moved twice, so I assumed either procurement is very busy or someone is trying to avoid me.

Owen Bode (Procurement): Procurement is busy, but I can confirm the second theory is only partially true. We have three supplier reviews running and one store opening that has turned into four store openings.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then I appreciate the time. I understand you're evaluating marketing automation for Effertz - Steuber, and that Marketo is part of the comparison. I would like to understand the business case, the buying process, and the requirements procurement will use to assess the options.

Owen Bode (Procurement): I am not the daily user, so I will give you the commercial view first. The business wants fewer manual campaigns, better follow-up on promotions, and a clearer picture of which channels contribute to store and online sales. My concern is that software projects often begin as “simple automation” and end as an expensive consultancy relationship.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What has made this project important now?

Owen Bode (Procurement): The marketing team is small, but the calendar is not. They manage seasonal promotions, supplier-funded campaigns, loyalty communications, and local store activity. Lists are built manually, approvals happen in email, and someone has to remember to stop a campaign when stock runs out.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How often does that create a material problem?

Owen Bode (Procurement): Not every week, but often enough. A promotion can continue after the product is unavailable, or a local offer can go to the wrong customer group. Those are not always dramatic incidents, but they waste budget and make the brand look disorganized.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What systems are involved?

Owen Bode (Account Executive): The ecommerce platform, a customer database, the point-of-sale reporting system, and an email service. Store teams also send spreadsheets with local customer information. There is no clean, single source for every permission and preference.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Which system does the business consider authoritative for consent?

Owen Bode (Procurement): That is one of the questions I need answered. Marketing says the customer database; IT says the ecommerce platform; the legal team says it depends on the channel. I am not going to pretend procurement can resolve that in a spreadsheet.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That sounds like an important implementation dependency. If the source of truth is unclear, automation could amplify the disagreement.

Owen Bode (Procurement): Exactly. And vendors usually say, “We can integrate with all of them,” as if connection equals governance.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Fair criticism. We can document the source per consent type and make the conflict cases visible. For discovery, what outcomes would justify the investment?

Owen Bode (Procurement): Reduced agency spend, fewer hours assembling lists, and better conversion from customer communications. I also need evidence that the team can use the system themselves. If the benefit depends on consultants building every journey, the financial model is wrong.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Do you have a target for savings or revenue improvement?

Owen Bode (Procurement): I have a budget ceiling, not a guaranteed revenue number. Marketing is being asked to prove efficiency first. They estimate around one day per week could be recovered from list preparation and reporting, but the estimate is not audited.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What is the budget ceiling?

Owen Bode (Procurement): I can discuss the range after we establish the required scope. The current proposal is around fourteen and a half thousand annually, which is not unreasonable for our size, but the add-ons are where these things become difficult.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. We should separate included capability, implementation services, data work, and optional modules. What commercial terms matter most?

Owen Bode (Procurement): Predictability. Annual price, renewal treatment, user limits, contact limits, integration charges, and any required onboarding package. Also, I need to know whether a price increase at renewal is capped or merely described as “standard.”

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I'll include those items in the proposal. Are there security requirements that could affect the shortlist?

Owen Bode (Procurement): Yes. Vendor security review, access controls, deletion handling, audit logs, subprocessors, and where data is hosted. We have a standard questionnaire. A supplier that avoids answering it gets removed, regardless of how good the demo is.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is clear. Who signs off on the technical and legal portions?

Owen Bode (Procurement): IT security reviews controls, legal reviews data processing and marketing permissions, finance reviews the business case, and the head of marketing owns the operational recommendation. I manage the commercial process and the final comparison.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Is Marketo a serious alternative or mainly a benchmark?

Owen Bode (Procurement): Both. The marketing director knows it and believes the ecosystem would be safer. I am less interested in brand recognition than total cost and time to value, but I cannot ignore stakeholder confidence.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would make our proposal credible against that familiarity?

Owen Bode (Procurement): Show the actual workflows, not a generic automation diagram. Take one seasonal promotion, one local-store campaign, and one abandoned-cart sequence. Show how consent, inventory status, approvals, and stopping rules work. If the answer is “a consultant configures that later,” it will not land well.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That's a useful test. Would the team provide sample data and workflow owners?

Owen Bode (Procurement): They can provide sanitized examples. They may not agree on owners yet, which is itself part of the problem.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can mark ownership as an open decision and show the consequences. How should success be measured during a pilot?

Owen Bode (Procurement): Time to launch a campaign, percentage of sends with documented approval, opt-out handling, list-preparation hours, and reporting effort. Revenue would be informative, but a short pilot cannot prove attribution perfectly.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I agree. We can include operational measures alongside campaign results. What timeline are you working toward?

Owen Bode (Procurement): The team wants something in place before the next major seasonal cycle. That is not a firm date because the commercial review is not complete. If legal or security takes longer, the timeline moves.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What are the next procurement steps?

Owen Bode (Procurement): Written requirements, a workflow demonstration, security questionnaire, commercial proposal, reference discussion, then approval. We may ask for a short proof of concept, but do not assume that means free implementation.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I wouldn't. We can propose a bounded evaluation with defined inputs and outputs.

Owen Bode (Procurement): Good. Another point: we are a small organization. A feature-rich system that requires a specialist administrator may be less valuable than a narrower system that people actually use.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We'll address staffing assumptions explicitly. How many users would need access at launch?

Owen Bode (Procurement): A few marketing users, one operations person, selected sales or store-management viewers, and IT administrators. Store teams should not all become full users just because they contribute local data.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That distinction can affect licensing and permissions. We'll model it separately.

Owen Bode (Procurement): Please do. And don't hide integration costs in a footnote.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. I'll send a discovery summary, the commercial checklist, and a proposed demonstration agenda. You can then confirm whether the marketing and IT stakeholders should join.

Owen Bode (Procurement): Send it to me first. I want to make sure the agenda reflects our process before I put another meeting on six calendars.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Will do. Thanks for the direct feedback.

Owen Bode (Procurement): Direct is cheaper than a failed implementation. Talk soon.
