Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Rashad, thanks for joining. I understand you lead demand generation across a large healthcare organization, so I will keep the overview practical. How are things today?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): Complicated is the honest answer. We have regional marketing teams, service lines, and a central operations group. Everyone wants better nurture, but nobody agrees on whose data is clean.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What is the business problem leadership is asking you to solve?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): Pipeline visibility and conversion. We generate a lot of activity, but executives see disconnected reports. A campaign looks successful in one dashboard and irrelevant in the revenue report.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Is the reporting problem caused by definitions, integration, or behavior?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): All three. Our CRM has opportunities, the web team has analytics, and regional teams keep local lists. Some leads are duplicated. Some opportunities are created months after the first interaction.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How many people would be involved in a standard campaign?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): Potentially hundreds across the organization, though only a smaller operations group would build campaigns. We have 60,920 employees overall, but that is not the user population for this project.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That distinction will matter for licensing. What does the current handoff from marketing to sales look like?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): A lead reaches a threshold in one team, gets exported or synced, and then sales may accept it. The threshold is not consistent. One region considers a webinar attendee qualified; another requires a meeting request.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would standardizing the threshold be politically difficult?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): Yes. The regions have different buying cycles and regulatory concerns. A single global score sounds tidy but could produce nonsense.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Then a common framework with regional adjustments may be better than one universal number. What data cannot be centralized?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): Patient information and some clinical details. The marketing program would focus on professional audiences, referral relationships, employers, and institutional buyers. We need a strict boundary.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Who approves that boundary?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): Privacy, security, and legal each have a say. Security is usually practical; legal wants definitions; privacy asks where the data goes and for how long.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We should include those stakeholders early. What would you use as a pilot?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): A national professional education program. It has event registration, attendance, content follow-up, and a sales or partnership handoff. It is large enough to test the model but separate from patient communications.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What outcome would convince you the pilot worked?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): Better response-time visibility and more accepted meetings, not simply higher email engagement. We also need to know whether regional teams can reuse the model without breaking governance.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would a centralized template library help?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): Potentially. Central operations should control brand and compliance modules. Regions need flexibility for local language and audience context.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How much local flexibility is acceptable?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): They can change approved content blocks and timing within a range. They should not be able to remove required consent language or invent a new data field without review.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That sounds like permissioned modular design. We can demonstrate it, but first I want to understand integration. What is your CRM?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): Salesforce, with several acquired-business instances feeding into it. The architecture team is consolidating, but the timetable is uncertain.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would the project need to wait for consolidation?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): No, but any proposal that assumes consolidation is already finished will be rejected. We need a phased design that works with the current state.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. Could the first phase use one instance and a controlled audience?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): Yes. We can prove value in the national program, then decide whether to expand. The funding is available, but procurement will require a business case and security review.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What is the internal decision timeline?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): I want a recommendation in front of the steering committee in six weeks. That means technical discovery must happen quickly, but stakeholder availability is a risk.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I can propose three sessions: data and architecture, campaign operations, and measurement. Each should have a written decision log.

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): Add privacy to the first one. If they discover a problem later, the entire schedule resets.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Good addition. How should attribution handle delayed opportunities?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): We need multiple touches and an agreed lookback window. Last-touch reporting overcredits whichever team sent the last email.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would you be comfortable starting with first-touch, influence, and meeting conversion, then refining after the pilot?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): Yes, as long as the dashboard labels assumptions. I do not want a polished number treated as fact just because it has two decimal places.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Completely fair. Are there any existing tools you expect us to replace?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): Not immediately. The web analytics and patient communications tools stay. The question is whether your platform becomes the orchestration layer for the approved professional-audience programs.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a reasonable scope. What would make the initiative fail even if the software works?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): No executive owner, unclear regional incentives, or a pilot that is too generic to matter. Also, if operations has to manually reconcile every report, adoption will collapse.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will reflect those as implementation risks, not bury them in a features slide. Can we schedule the architecture session for Monday?

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): Monday afternoon is possible. Send the data-flow questions beforehand; the architecture team will not respond well to a surprise demo.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will send them today, along with a proposed pilot outline and licensing assumptions.

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): Good. I am open to continuing, but the steering committee will need evidence from our environment, not generic healthcare examples.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is the right standard. Thank you, Rashad. I will follow up with the agenda and owners.

Rashad Morar (Demand Generation): Thanks, Amelia. Talk Monday.
