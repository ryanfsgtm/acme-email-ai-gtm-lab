Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Florine, thanks for making the time. Before we get into systems, how is your week looking?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Full, but survivable. We are closing out a supplier review and apparently every department chose this week to remember procurement exists.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Then I'll keep us focused. My understanding is that Bernhard is assessing marketing automation for a very large manufacturing organization, with regional teams and a fair amount of supplier governance. Is that broadly right?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Broadly. I should clarify that I am not the business owner. I manage commercial review, security coordination, and the purchase path. Marketing owns the requirements.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is useful. Who else is involved?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Global demand generation, three regional marketing leads, IT architecture, information security, legal, and finance. The number changes depending on which meeting you attend.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What prompted the evaluation now?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): The current platform is fragmented. Some regions use Mailchimp, some use internal tools, and one division has built a collection of scripts that only one analyst understands. The marketing team wants a common operating model.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would a common model need to do first?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Consolidate campaign execution, maintain regional control, and provide reliable reporting to leadership. They also want automated follow-up for distributor inquiries and event leads. Procurement's concern is that the proposed solution not create a new set of unmanaged vendors.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): When you say regional control, do you mean separate instances or shared governance?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): That is still being debated. Global wants a shared environment. Some regions are worried that global templates will flatten local language and regulatory requirements.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can explore a shared structure with permissions and regional workspaces, but I don't want to assume that matches your operating model. How many markets are in scope for the initial phase?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): North America and Western Europe for phase one. Asia-Pacific may follow, but it has its own data residency and translation questions.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): And what volume are we talking about?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Several million contacts eventually. The first phase is smaller, perhaps a few hundred thousand active business contacts, though the number is not final.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What is the most painful part of the current process for marketing?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Reporting. They cannot reliably connect an inquiry to a campaign, a distributor, and a revenue outcome. The second pain is timing. Event follow-up can take days because lists are cleaned manually.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Does procurement see that as a cost issue, a risk issue, or both?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Both, but risk comes first. We need clear ownership for data, access, retention, and incident response. A cheap tool with unclear obligations is not cheap.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That makes sense. What contractual terms are usually difficult for your team?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Data processing terms, audit rights, subcontractors, liability, termination assistance, and the ability to retrieve data in a usable format. Standard paper is a starting point, not an answer.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How early should those topics be addressed?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Earlier than vendors usually want. If we wait until the business has selected a tool, everyone assumes the deal is done and legal becomes the villain for raising normal questions.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can bring a preliminary security and legal packet into the evaluation. What does finance need to see?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): A transparent cost model. License tiers, contact overages, implementation, regional support, training, and any required professional services. The headline subscription price will not be enough.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would a multi-year agreement be considered?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Possibly, but only if the ramp and termination language are sensible. We have been burned by buying future capacity before adoption was proven.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What adoption measures would matter?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Active regional teams, campaign launch time, percentage of leads handled within the agreed service level, and reporting completeness. Marketing may add conversion metrics.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Where does Mailchimp fit into the discussion?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): It is familiar and inexpensive for smaller teams. It is also the competitor most people mention because they know it, not necessarily because it meets the global governance requirement.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would a lower-cost regional solution remain viable if the global program does not move?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Yes. That is why the evaluation is not just a procurement exercise. We need to understand whether global standardization creates enough value to justify migration and change management.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would make a pilot credible?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): One global campaign, one distributor workflow, and one event follow-up process across at least two regions. We need to see permissions, localization, reporting, and the operational burden.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How long would you expect a pilot to run?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Six to eight weeks, with preparation before that. A demo is not evidence of operating fit.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Who signs off commercially?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): The category director and finance controller, with executive sponsorship from the chief marketing officer. IT and security have veto power on their domains.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): And the target timing?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): The business would like a decision in the third quarter. That is an aspiration. The actual timing depends on the data inventory and legal review.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would delay it most?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Data mapping, regional consent requirements, and contract language. Not the demo. Everyone can make a demo look good.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): If we schedule the next meeting, who should attend?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Global demand generation, an IT architect, security, and someone from legal. Please send the security materials first so they can review asynchronously.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will. I'll also send a draft pilot scorecard and a total-cost template. Are there any assumptions you want me to avoid?

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Do not assume every contact can be migrated. Do not assume regional teams want identical workflows. And do not call a verbal preference a requirement until the business owner confirms it.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Those are fair guardrails. I'll mark them as open validation points.

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Good. One more thing: the business wants speed, but procurement will not approve a process that leaves data ownership vague.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. We can move quickly on discovery while keeping ownership explicit. Thank you, Florine.

Florine Smitham (Procurement): Thank you. Send the packet, and I will route it to the right people.
