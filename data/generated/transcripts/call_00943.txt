Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Hi, Serenity. I see you joined from a room with remarkably good lighting. That is a strong start for an onboarding call.

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): It is the one room where nobody can see the cables. We are a 26-person software company, so every room is also storage. Can you hear me?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Clearly. Today I would like to confirm scope, users, data, and the first launch. I know Collier Group chose us after evaluating Marketo, and the goal is to get the core process live without overwhelming a small team.

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): Correct. We need a usable system quickly. We have a few people who will touch it every day, and many more who will consume reports. We cannot spend six months designing the perfect architecture.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What does “live” mean to you?

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): Website forms connected, existing contacts loaded, basic lead routing working, and one nurture journey running. Also, sales needs to see what happened before a lead arrived. If that last piece is missing, they will say the system is just an email sender.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Good definition. Who owns the technical side?

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): I do the process and CRM mapping. Our implementation partner, Lucía, will handle the migration and some of the integration work. She is certified on the platform and has done Marketo migrations before. Our engineer, Tom, will review anything touching the product site.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That partner involvement is useful, especially because migrations from Marketo can look risky when you consider all the old programs together. We should keep the first cut narrow and let Lucía validate the mapping.

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): That is why we brought her in. We have about 18,000 contacts, but only perhaps 6,000 are active prospects. The rest are customers, former trials, vendors, or records nobody can explain.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Are you expecting to migrate all 18,000?

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): Not operationally. We need a retained archive for the rest, but we do not want dormant records entering every segment. Marketo has folders and programs that made sense to someone at the time. Some names are like “New New Trial Followup FINAL.”

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): A familiar archaeological layer. We can classify active, suppressed, archived, and ambiguous records. What consent fields need to come across?

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): Subscription status, source, opt-out date, and region. We sell mainly in the UK and Europe, with a small US presence. We also need to preserve the original source for our partner channel because those leads are handled differently.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What should happen when a form is submitted?

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): Validate the email, check for an existing account, assign by territory, and create a task if the person requests a demo. For lower-intent content downloads, they should enter nurture. We currently do some of that with rules and some with a Slack message that gets missed.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How quickly should a demo request be assigned?

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): Within fifteen minutes during business hours. That is ambitious for us, but the current delay is bad. Sometimes the alert goes to one person on holiday and just sits there.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can create round-robin ownership with absence coverage. Who approves the definitions?

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): I do, with our head of sales. Marketing owns content and journey timing. Tom signs off on the form integration. Finance does not need to configure anything, but they want monthly pipeline reporting.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Let us talk about the first nurture. What audience is most important?

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): Trial users who did not activate. We want educational messages, a use-case email, and then a human check-in. We have been sending one generic email to everyone, which is not exactly sophisticated.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What counts as activation?

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): Three projects created and at least one teammate invited. Actually, wait, we changed that last month to two projects and one teammate. The product team has the current definition; I will confirm it rather than pretend.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Excellent. We will mark that as an open item. What should remove someone from nurture?

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): Activation, a sales-qualified opportunity, unsubscribe, or a request for support. We do not want marketing emails continuing while a customer is waiting on a technical answer.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is a good suppression rule. Do you need Marketo assets rebuilt one-for-one?

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): No. We need the useful behavior, not a museum. The partner has already flagged that several old programs can be retired. She is more confident about the migration than we are because she knows where the bodies are buried.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is exactly the kind of confidence we want grounded in a map. We will have Lucía produce a source-to-target inventory and identify anything that cannot be carried over cleanly.

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): Please include scoring. Our current score is a black box. Sales does not trust it, and I cannot explain why one lead is 72 and another is 68.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can begin with transparent signals: fit, demo request, activation, product engagement, and meaningful content behavior. We should avoid making a score the only routing condition.

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): Agreed. A high score from downloading five PDFs is not the same as a demo request from a target account.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What reports are required for launch?

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): Lead volume by source, response time, demo conversion, nurture progression, and pipeline by source. Monthly is enough initially. We can improve attribution once the team agrees on opportunity stages.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Any concern about user permissions?

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): Yes. We want sales to see assigned leads and activity, but not alter global fields. Marketing needs to build journeys. The partner needs temporary admin access, then it should be removed or reduced.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We will document role profiles and an access review at the end of implementation. How will you train the team?

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): Short sessions by job. Sales gets alerts, queues, and activity history. Marketing gets segments and journeys. Leadership gets reports. If everyone attends one two-hour lecture, half will be answering email.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Sensible. What could delay launch?

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): Bad data, unclear activation rules, and our engineer being pulled into an outage. Also, if we keep adding “one small thing” every week.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We will keep a launch scope and a phase-two list. The first milestone can be a migration sample, followed by validation and a test form.

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): That works. Lucía can join the technical session next week. I will bring the current field map and the activation definition.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I will send the project plan, responsibilities, migration checklist, and proposed journey outline. Anything I missed?

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): Only the fact that I would like to sleep before launch. Otherwise, no.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We will aim for a launch that supports sleep. Thanks, Serenity.

Serenity Corkery (Sales Operations): Thank you. The certified partner makes this feel considerably less alarming.
