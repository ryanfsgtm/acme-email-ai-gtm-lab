Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Chandler, thanks for taking the time. I saw the note that Robel is looking at a marketing automation replacement, but I would rather hear the story from you. What prompted the review now?

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): We have reached the point where our current Marketo instance is more of a museum than a system. It contains years of programmes, half-finished fields, and documentation nobody trusts. The immediate trigger is a new services campaign that we cannot launch cleanly.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): When you say “cannot launch cleanly,” what breaks?

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): The audience definition is split across CRM fields, spreadsheets, and old Marketo smart lists. We can eventually get the list, but it takes several checks. Then the sales team asks why a contact was included, and we have to reconstruct the logic. It is slow and embarrassing.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How large is the team managing that today?

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): Two people directly, plus a part-time CRM administrator. We are an SMB professional-services firm, so nobody has a spare quarter to become a migration specialist.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is important context. What would a successful replacement change in the first ninety days?

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): Reliable segmentation, simpler campaign building, and reporting that can survive a meeting with finance. I do not need a moon landing. I need to know which programmes generated meaningful conversations and whether the handoffs happened.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Which CRM are you using?

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): Salesforce. It is not pristine, but it is the system sales believes. The opportunity stages are reasonably consistent. Contact roles are less so.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): And what kinds of campaigns matter most?

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): Webinars, partner referrals, and nurture around our consulting packages. The partner referrals are especially sensitive because partners expect us to follow up quickly, but we cannot always tell whether an inquiry came from a current partner agreement or a public event.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would routing and response-time measurement be part of the evaluation?

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): Yes. Although I should say the business problem is not only technology. We have a sales coordinator who handles most inbound requests, and when she is away the queue gets messy.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That distinction helps. We can improve routing visibility, but we should not pretend a platform creates capacity. What makes a Marketo migration feel risky to the wider team?

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): The database is old and the programme library is tangled. People are afraid we will lose historical activity or accidentally trigger something during the move. Our managing partner remembers a previous migration that took months and produced broken forms.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Has anyone estimated what must actually be migrated versus archived?

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): Not rigorously. That is part of why the project feels bigger than it may be. We have active programmes, inactive programmes that are still referenced, and reports built on fields nobody can explain.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I would suggest starting with an inventory and a migration decision tree. Active journeys, compliant contact records, and reporting history get one treatment. Dormant assets can be archived with a searchable export. Before discussing a contract, we should identify dependencies and test a representative campaign.

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): That sounds sensible. The question is who does the work. We do not have the capacity to catalogue everything ourselves.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can provide an implementation team, and we also work with certified implementation partners for larger or more specialised migrations. A partner could handle the programme inventory, field mapping, and rehearsal while your team makes the business decisions.

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): I have heard partner proposals before. Sometimes the partner is excellent in the sales presentation and then disappears behind a queue.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a fair concern. I would want the partner named in the plan, with responsibilities, milestones, and escalation points. You should not be asked to accept an abstract promise that “services will help.”

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): The last Marketo agency did exactly that. They knew the product, but they did not understand how our consultants sell. We ended up with elaborate scoring that nobody used.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would a useful scoring model look like for Robel?

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): Probably modest. Attendance, repeat engagement, service-area interest, and a few account attributes. We do not want a score that says someone is hot because they opened six newsletters.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Then the model should be explainable and tied to an agreed action. For example, a high-intent webinar attendee from a target account gets a task within one business day; a newsletter opener does not. Would sales agree to that kind of rule?

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): Some would. The partner team may resist because they have their own follow-up process. That is why I want the workflow tested with real users before launch.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can make user acceptance testing a gate. I also want to ask about data quality. Are there duplicates or consent gaps that would affect migration?

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): Duplicates, yes. Consent is mostly captured, but older records have inconsistent source values. We cannot assume every historical contact can be mailed.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We should preserve those records for operational history but exclude them from marketing until consent is validated. That may mean the first launch audience is smaller than the current database count.

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): Smaller is fine if it is trustworthy. Our problem is not lack of names.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What is the internal decision process?

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): I will recommend a shortlist. The managing partner approves the spend, the CRM administrator approves integration changes, and sales operations signs off on handoff reporting. We also need the finance lead comfortable with attribution definitions.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): And timing?

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): We would like a decision in six weeks, but that depends on seeing a migration plan before we commit. The new services campaign is planned for late spring, though the date has moved twice already.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I can organise a discovery workshop with a solutions consultant and a certified implementation partner. We would use your active programme inventory, one partner-referral workflow, and one webinar campaign as the test cases. The deliverable would include scope, risks, estimated effort, and a phased launch plan.

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): I want the partner involved early, not added after the commercial proposal. Otherwise we are pricing a fantasy.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Agreed. I will include the partner in the workshop and identify who would actually lead the work. We will also show how a simpler first phase can coexist with archived Marketo history.

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): Good. I cannot promise that Robel will move. If the audit says our process is the main problem, we may fix that and stay put.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a reasonable outcome. The audit should help you decide, not force a platform conclusion. Are there any non-negotiables we have missed?

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): No black-box migration, no automatic mailing of questionable records, and no scoring without a documented sales action. If the partner can respect those, we can have a productive next step.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will capture those as evaluation criteria and send times for the workshop. Thanks, Chandler.

Chandler Adams (Marketing Operations): Thanks. Send the draft scope first. I will circulate it internally and bring the CRM administrator.
