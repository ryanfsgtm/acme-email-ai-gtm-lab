Priya Shah (Account Executive): Hi Ariel, thanks for making time. How are things in France today?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): Busy, but manageable. We have a student recruitment campaign launching, so the timing is useful. I’m joining from a room that is apparently also being used for a faculty meeting.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I’ll try not to compete with the faculty. Before we get into the platform, could you give me the short version of what prompted the conversation?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): We’re trying to make prospective-student communications less manual. Our marketing team is small relative to the number of programs we support. We have inquiries coming from web forms, events, partner schools, and some imported lists. Right now, a lot of follow-up depends on someone remembering to send the next email.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): So the issue is not just sending campaigns; it’s maintaining a consistent journey after the first interaction.

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): Exactly. A person can request information about a postgraduate course, attend an open day, and then receive a generic undergraduate newsletter. That is not a great experience.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What happens after a form submission today?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): The form goes into our CRM. Marketing gets an alert, and admissions has its own queue. Some programs have automated acknowledgments, but most of the useful follow-up is still a spreadsheet and a series of reminders. If somebody is on leave, the gap is obvious.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How quickly do you usually respond?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): On a good day, within one business day. During application peaks, it can be three or four. We have not measured every program consistently, so that number is directional.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. Which audiences matter most for the next six to twelve months?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): International students, continuing-education prospects, and people comparing several programs. We need different messaging, but we cannot ask the team to build every sequence from scratch.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds like a case for reusable journeys with local variation. What systems would need to connect?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): Our CRM, the website forms, webinar registration, and probably the events tool. We also have a data warehouse, although I would not want to make that the first integration. It tends to turn every project into an IT project.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Fair. We can start with the operational sources and leave the warehouse for later. How are you thinking about consent, especially across countries?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): Carefully. We have different consent language by market, and our privacy office does not want marketing assuming that an event attendee has opted into everything. We need an audit trail and clear suppression rules.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would your privacy team participate in the evaluation?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): Yes, probably after marketing confirms the use cases. They do not want a broad technical demo. They want to see how consent is recorded, how a deletion request propagates, and who can access contact history.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I can tailor a session around those questions. On segmentation, how precise do you need to be?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): Program interest, country, stage in the recruitment process, and whether the person has attended an event. We also need to avoid over-contacting people who are already speaking with admissions.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Could admissions update that stage in the CRM, or would marketing own it?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): Admissions owns the official stage. Marketing can use it, but we should not create a parallel truth. That is one of my concerns with automation: if a field is stale, the automation simply makes the mistake faster.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a good point. We would want entry criteria, exclusions, and a review path before activating a journey. What would success look like?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): Faster first response, fewer missed follow-ups, and better visibility into which programs generate engaged prospects. I would also like a reduction in the number of one-off email requests landing with my team.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Do you have target numbers yet?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): Not firm ones. Maybe first response under four hours for priority inquiries, but I need admissions to agree. For engagement, we care more about completed applications and event attendance than open rate.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Makes sense. Are there existing tools you are comparing?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): Customer.io is the name that keeps coming up. Some colleagues like its flexibility. I am less interested in a feature checklist than in whether our team can govern it without becoming dependent on one technical person.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How many people would build and monitor journeys?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): Maybe six regularly, with another ten needing reporting or review access. We have around eighteen hundred employees overall, but that is not the marketing user base.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): And who signs off commercially?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): I can sponsor the business case. Finance and procurement will review the contract. IT and privacy need to be comfortable. The executive committee will want a clear implementation timeline.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): If we planned a working session, what would be most useful to bring?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): A sample inquiry journey. Show the form submission, consent capture, branching by program and country, an admissions handoff, and reporting. Please include failure cases. Our leadership is tired of demos where everything works perfectly.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Agreed. We can use a realistic path and deliberately pause or suppress a contact to show what happens. Would you be able to provide field names in advance?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): I can send a sanitized list. I cannot share real student data. Also, our forms are not all uniform, which may be the messy part.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is normal. We can map the differences and identify where a common field is needed. Do you have a preferred launch window?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): If we proceed, I would like a pilot before the next major recruitment cycle. But I do not want a rushed launch in the name of a date.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): So a controlled pilot, with privacy review and admissions sign-off, then expansion.

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): Yes. And I need to understand the workload on our side. If the first phase requires three months of custom data work, we need to know that now.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We’ll make implementation assumptions explicit, including what your team owns and what our team supports. I’ll send a recap with the proposed workshop, integration questions, and a draft success scorecard.

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): Good. Add the data-retention question. It came up in our last internal meeting and I do not have a crisp answer.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will. Anything else before we let the faculty have the room?

Ariel Halvorson (VP Marketing): No, that covers it. The important thing is that this remains useful to admissions, not just a marketing automation project.
