Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Antoinette, thanks for meeting before the year closes. How was the holiday break?

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): Short. We’re still cleaning up the implementation backlog, so I’m glad we could talk.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’d like to review adoption, open issues, expansion possibilities, and the renewal decision. I know the account has been evaluating alternatives.

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): That’s accurate. The platform delivered value for several teams, but the rollout was uneven across our 17,090 employees and multiple UK business units.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Which parts are working best?

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): The central lifecycle programs are stable. The retail loyalty team also improved follow-up speed. Local teams, however, still depend on spreadsheets and occasionally rebuild audiences outside the governed process.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Is that primarily a usability issue or a governance issue?

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): Both. Some users find the interface dense. More importantly, the rollout gave them a global template without enough local design time. They feel the central team is imposing rules that don’t fit every market.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can propose role-based training and regional office hours. Would that address the adoption gap?

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): It might help, but training alone won’t fix the approval queue. A campaign can wait days for a central review, so people create a manual workaround.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That’s fair. We should examine approval routing and risk-based exceptions. Which campaigns truly need central approval?

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): Anything involving sensitive loyalty attributes or large sends. Routine local event follow-up could be delegated.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll capture that distinction. How are the current integrations performing?

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): CRM sync is mostly fine. The loyalty feed has intermittent delays, and the team doesn’t always know whether the issue is upstream or in the connector. Incident communication has been inconsistent.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Do you have examples from the last quarter?

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): Three incidents. None caused a major customer event, but one delayed a product message by nearly a day. The business remembers that more than the successful runs.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. We can review monitoring, ownership, and escalation with support. Would an expansion into the remaining brands depend on those fixes?

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): Absolutely. We will not widen the footprint while the foundation is still noisy.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We’ve also heard Eloqua mentioned in the evaluation. What is driving that comparison?

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): Familiarity and existing relationships. Some teams believe Eloqua is easier to govern because they already know its processes. Others prefer your platform’s event-driven workflows.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): So the decision is not simply feature versus feature.

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): No. It’s implementation confidence, local autonomy, support quality, and whether the total cost matches the value. License price matters, but it isn’t the only issue.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would the renewal committee need to see?

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): A measured adoption report, incident trend, integration remediation plan, and a realistic expansion sequence. Also, named customer-side owners. We have too much dependency on two people.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can build that jointly. For expansion, would one additional brand be a reasonable next phase?

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): Maybe, if it has a motivated owner and a bounded use case. We should not repeat the original “big bang” approach.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What use case would be bounded enough?

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): Post-purchase education for one loyalty tier, with clear consent and a small number of journeys. It would test local control without exposing every brand to the change.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That’s specific. I’ll include it as a pilot option, along with the approval redesign.

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): Please include an exit criterion. If adoption or reliability doesn’t improve, we need permission to stop expansion.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Agreed. I’ll also avoid presenting the pilot as an automatic renewal commitment.

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): Good, because the committee has not decided. We may renew the existing footprint, reduce it, or move to another platform.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I appreciate the candor. What is the next internal milestone?

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): I’ll present the operational review in January. Before then I need your remediation proposal and commercial scenarios.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll send those with separate pricing for the current footprint and the pilot. Can we review them together before the committee meeting?

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): Yes, provided the incident data is included and the assumptions are visible.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. Thanks for the thoughtful feedback.

Antoinette Weissnat (Implementation Partner): Thank you. We need a plan that works for the people operating it, not only a good executive presentation.
