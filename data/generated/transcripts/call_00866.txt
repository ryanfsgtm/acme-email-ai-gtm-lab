Letha MacGyver (Revenue Operations): I’d like to look at adoption by the commercial team and whether the reporting is giving us a useful picture. We’re a fairly small healthcare business—seventy-four people—so I don’t need a giant dashboard that nobody trusts.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That’s fair. We should distinguish activity from useful outcomes. How are you currently measuring those referrals?

Letha MacGyver (Revenue Operations): Mostly a spreadsheet and a weekly email. We record source, organisation, assigned owner, and whether someone followed up. Then I reconcile it against finance once a month. It’s not elegant, but it works.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What makes it difficult when it doesn’t work?

Letha MacGyver (Revenue Operations): Duplicate organisations, mainly. A hospital trust might appear under a short name, a legal name, and a local branch. The team then thinks we have three opportunities. Also, ownership changes aren’t always reflected in the weekly report.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can review the matching rules and the owner-change workflow. I don’t want to promise that every legal-entity variation will resolve automatically, but we can reduce the manual cleanup.

Letha MacGyver (Revenue Operations): Good. I’m less concerned about a perfect match than about knowing where the exceptions are. If the system tells me, “These twelve records need attention,” that’s better than quietly making a bad decision.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That’s a useful requirement. On the current quarter’s reporting, the conversion view shows forty-one new referrals, fourteen qualified conversations, and five proposals. Does that match your internal numbers?

Letha MacGyver (Revenue Operations): Roughly. We count two of the proposals differently because one was paused. The finance team calls it open; the account manager calls it dormant.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That definition mismatch may be worth resolving. Would a shared stage definition help, or is the issue more about timing?

Letha MacGyver (Revenue Operations): Both. People use “qualified” to mean “someone replied,” while I mean there’s a confirmed need, budget discussion, and an owner. If we don’t agree on that, the dashboard is just a prettier argument.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I like that phrasing. I can send a proposed stage glossary after the call. We can keep it lightweight: entry criteria, exit criteria, and who is responsible for updating it.

Letha MacGyver (Revenue Operations): Please do. And include the handoff from partnerships to sales. That’s where information disappears.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Looking at response time, the median is two business days. The target we set was one day. Are there capacity issues, or is the notification process the problem?

Letha MacGyver (Revenue Operations): Capacity at the moment. January is always noisy because renewals and new referrals land together. But the alert also gets buried. Some reps receive a digest instead of an immediate prompt.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can test immediate alerts for high-priority referrals while leaving the digest for lower-priority items. Would that fit your team’s tolerance?

Letha MacGyver (Revenue Operations): Probably, provided we don’t create a hundred notifications. Define high priority as an existing customer, a regulated organisation, or a referral with a requested start date.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We could make the field structured but allow an “unspecified” value, so the report distinguishes missing from genuinely unknown.

Letha MacGyver (Revenue Operations): Yes, that would be better than forcing a made-up date. Please don’t make it mandatory without that option.
