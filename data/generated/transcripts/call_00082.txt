Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Hi Patricia, thanks for making time. Before we get into systems, how has your week been?

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): Busy in the usual healthcare way. We are trying to prepare for the next regional outreach cycle while cleaning up the last one.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That sounds familiar. I understand you are evaluating marketing automation and currently have ActiveCampaign in the mix. What prompted the review?

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): We have ActiveCampaign, but adoption is uneven. Marketing uses it for newsletters, sales gets spreadsheets, and operations spends time reconciling the two. Nobody would say it is unusable; it is just not producing one reliable process.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): When you say reliable, where does it break most often?

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): Lead ownership and follow-up. A contact may attend a webinar, download a clinical guide, and then sit untouched because the alert went to a general inbox. We discover the gap when a manager asks for a report.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Do you have a defined routing model today?

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): In theory. Region, specialty, and account tier should determine ownership. In practice, territory changes are updated in three places, sometimes four. The CRM field is the one people trust least.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That suggests the automation design needs to account for data stewardship, not simply add more triggers.

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): Exactly. I do not want another platform creating elegant workflows around dirty fields.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What does the sales team need to see when a qualified interaction occurs?

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): A concise alert, source activity, suggested next step, and ownership. They do not want a ten-line email. They want to know, “This person from this organization did this, and you own the follow-up.”

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Would alerts go to individual reps or a queue?

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): Usually the individual. For smaller accounts, probably a pooled queue. That distinction is still being debated.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): How are consent and communication preferences managed?

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): We have a global preference center, but regional exceptions exist. Australia and New Zealand are the immediate focus. The legal team wants a visible record of why a person entered a campaign.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Is that record available now?

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): Partly. We can see the form submission, but not always the campaign version or the approval that was in effect at the time.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That may be an important implementation question. We should map the source event, consent status, and campaign revision together.

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): Yes, and please do not assume one consent language works everywhere. We have been burned by that assumption.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Understood. How many people would build and manage the programs?

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): Six regular users, maybe twelve occasional users. Most occasional users should not publish anything. They need to request changes, not launch campaigns.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What would success look like ninety days after implementation?

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): First, fewer unassigned leads. Second, a report that marketing and sales both accept. Third, less manual work before every campaign. I would like measurable time saved, but we have not established a baseline.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can help define one. For example, time from campaign close to accepted lead report, or hours spent correcting routing each week.

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): The correction time is easier to measure. The reporting definition is political. Different leaders want different numbers.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What is the buying process?

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): Marketing owns the budget, sales operations owns requirements, IT reviews integration, and privacy can stop the project. The final approval is a steering group.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): And the target timing?

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): The opportunity is open, but I would not call October firm. We have an internal planning cycle, and ActiveCampaign renewal timing may influence the decision.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What would make you stay with ActiveCampaign?

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): If we can fix routing and reporting without a migration, the switching cost matters. The team knows the interface. But if we keep paying for manual reconciliation, that becomes its own cost.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Are there capabilities you consider mandatory?

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): CRM synchronization, field-level permissions, preference handling, and a way to test workflows before sending. Also, support during launch. We do not have spare operations capacity.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What integration is most sensitive?

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): The CRM ownership field. If your system overwrites it incorrectly, sales will reject the project immediately.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We should make that a technical validation item, with sample records and a rollback plan.

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): Agreed. I also want to test duplicate contacts. We have a messy history there.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will capture that. Would a follow-up workshop with marketing, IT, and privacy be practical?

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): Yes, but keep it structured. A general demo will not answer the questions that matter.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Then we will prepare a process map, an integration checklist, and a small set of acceptance tests.

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): That is the right next step. I am interested, but the project needs to prove operational safety before anyone gets excited about features.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I appreciate that. I will send a recap with the open decisions and proposed attendees.

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): Good. And please mark the timing as tentative.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will. Thanks again, Patricia.

Patricia Herman-Goyette (Sales Operations): Thank you. Talk soon.
