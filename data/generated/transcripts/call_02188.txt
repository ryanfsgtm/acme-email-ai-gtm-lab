Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Tyrese, thanks for taking the call. I know this is late in Toronto, so I appreciate it.

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): It is fine. Our board meeting ran long, which is becoming a recurring marketing technology feature. How are you?

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Doing well, thank you. I wanted to revisit how your team thinks about marketing automation and what prevented the earlier proposal from moving forward.

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): The proposal did not move forward because we chose Marketo. That was the decision in April. I am happy to explain the decision, but I do not want this treated as an active buying cycle.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Absolutely. My goal is to learn, not reopen a closed deal. What problem was the team trying to solve?

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): We wanted better visibility from first inquiry through a consulting engagement. Our current process loses context between the website, events, and the partner channel. Marketing can see activity, but sales often gets a flat spreadsheet and a note saying, “please follow up.”

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That sounds like a handoff and attribution problem more than a simple email problem.

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): Correct. We have only seventy-two employees, so people assume our processes are simple. They are not. We serve professional-services clients with long consideration periods, and a single opportunity can involve several stakeholders.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What did Marketo offer that you felt was decisive?

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): The reporting depth and the confidence our operations lead had from using it at a previous company. Marketo was more expensive and definitely more complex, but the team believed it would support account-level engagement and our existing governance model.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Was the purchase primarily driven by reporting, orchestration, or integration?

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): Reporting opened the door. Integration closed it. We needed the CRM sync to behave predictably, particularly around campaign influence and lifecycle status. One vendor demonstrated a pretty journey builder but could not answer what happened when a contact belonged to two accounts or changed firms.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Those edge cases can become central in professional services. How are they handled today?

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): Manually, mostly. Our operations manager maintains a set of rules in a spreadsheet. It works because she knows every exception. That is also why it does not scale. If she takes a holiday, the process slows down.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Did budget play a role in losing the opportunity?

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): Yes, but not in the simplistic sense. Your amount was around fourteen thousand seven hundred forty-nine dollars annually, and Marketo was higher. The board approved the larger amount because the implementation plan included data cleanup and executive reporting. A cheaper tool with unclear migration work would not have been cheaper for us.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is useful context. Was there anything in our proposal that was strong?

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): The team liked the speed of creating campaigns and the interface. Our campaign managers are not technical, and they could understand it quickly. The concern was that ease of use might come at the expense of governance and reporting granularity.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Did we make that tradeoff clear enough?

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): No. The demos moved quickly from segment creation to a finished email. Nobody showed a messy real-world dataset, a duplicate contact, or an executive report with caveats. That made the product look polished but did not answer our hardest questions.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): If you could change one part of the evaluation, what would it be?

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): Start with a working session using our actual process map, without personal data. Show where the system is authoritative, what is synchronized, and what remains manual. Then let us see a failure case. For example, a partner contact opts out, the account has three active opportunities, and a new employee imports an old list. What happens?

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is a much better test than a standard feature checklist.

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): Exactly. We also underestimated change management. Marketo is not magically successful because it has more capability. We have to train people, define ownership, and stop five teams from inventing their own lifecycle stages.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Has the implementation started?

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): Yes, though slower than planned. We are still reconciling account hierarchies. Our opportunity is closed-lost in your records, and that is accurate. I do not want a follow-up sequence suggesting we are about to sign.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. I will record the outcome as closed-lost and keep this as a retrospective conversation. Are there any circumstances under which you would reconsider later?

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): If the implementation becomes too heavy, we might review alternatives, but I am not inviting a sales cadence. In twelve months, perhaps, after we know which requirements were real and which were assumptions.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Fair enough. What should a vendor avoid saying in a future conversation?

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): “One platform will solve attribution.” It will not. Attribution depends on definitions, source quality, and whether leadership agrees on what a qualified interaction means. Also avoid promising that a small company has a small data problem.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will take that back. Is there anything else about the lost opportunity that would be valuable for our team?

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): Ask who will maintain the system after launch. During the evaluation, everyone talked about what the software could do. Nobody asked whether our marketing coordinator had time to administer it. That was a significant gap.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is an excellent point. Thank you for being candid, Tyrese. I will not send a proposal or schedule a product demo. I will send a brief thank-you note and close the loop internally.

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): Good. I appreciate that. And please include the lesson about failure cases; polished demos are easy to compare, operational realism is not.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Agreed. Thanks again, and I hope the board meeting is less dramatic next time.

Tyrese Roberts (CMO): From your lips to the board chair’s ears. Good night, Luis.
