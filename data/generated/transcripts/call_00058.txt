Priya Shah (Account Executive): Kirstin, good morning—or afternoon for you, I think. Can you hear me clearly?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): Clearly enough. It’s just after two here in the UK. I’ve got thirty-nine minutes, so let’s keep the dashboard honest.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I appreciate that. Since the last quarter, I’d like to review adoption, campaign performance, open risks, and whether the platform is still aligned with your lifecycle program. I know the renewal decision went to Marketo and the opportunity is recorded as lost.

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): Yes. We’re moving to Marketo. The decision is made, although the migration is not finished. I’m happy to discuss the quarter because the product has still been useful.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That distinction is exactly why I wanted the conversation. Let’s start with usage. Which teams are active?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): The central lifecycle team is active. We built onboarding, trial conversion, and re-engagement programs. Product marketing uses the email editor occasionally. Regional demand teams mostly consume audiences rather than build journeys. Usage is not uniform across Watsica - Hettinger.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): The account has a little over eleven hundred employees, but the marketing organization is much smaller. Did the smaller team find the workflow manageable?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): Manageable, yes. Elegant, not always. The campaign builders could launch quickly once the templates existed. The challenge was governance. We had to create naming conventions, document exclusions, and maintain a separate spreadsheet for which segment belonged to which motion. That’s process debt, though the system didn’t remove it.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How did performance look for the onboarding sequence?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): Better than before. Completion of the first three messages improved, and the timing was more consistent. I don’t want to attribute every improvement to the platform; we also rewrote the content. The new subject lines did a lot of work.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Fair. What about the trial-to-paid motion?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): Mixed. We increased qualified handoffs, but sales complained that the alerts were too frequent. We adjusted the threshold twice. The reporting showed activity, but it didn’t always explain whether the account was actually ready for a conversation.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Was that a scoring issue or a data-quality issue?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): Both. Scoring was based on behaviors we could observe, while sales had context we couldn’t see. A person might download several technical papers because they were researching for an existing customer, not because they wanted a contract. That created false urgency.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Did the Marketo evaluation focus on that gap?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): Yes, partly. Marketo was already familiar to our demand-generation leadership from a previous company. They believed it would give them stronger control over the operating model and reporting. Familiarity was an advantage, but it wasn’t the only reason.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What were the biggest practical issues during the quarter?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): Data sync delays, mostly. The CRM updates did not always appear when the campaign manager expected. Sometimes the delay was harmless; sometimes it affected suppression or routing. We also had an integration change that required more coordination than anticipated.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Were those resolved by support?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): Support was responsive. The problem was not that tickets were ignored. The problem was that the operational answer often required us to understand three systems at once. A small team can handle that for a while, but it becomes fragile when someone is away.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Did your team use the support documentation and training resources?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): The help center, yes. Formal training, less so. We recorded the original sessions, but new people tended to learn from a colleague. That was our choice, though a more opinionated onboarding path would have helped.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I see a drop in active users in January. Is that expected?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): Mostly. Two contractors rolled off, and one person moved to product marketing. The drop does not mean the program stopped. In fact, the core team was still running more campaigns. A user count without workload context is misleading.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That’s a good counterpoint. What should we measure instead?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): Time from brief to launch, number of manual corrections, percentage of audiences passing governance checks, and whether sales accepts the handoffs. Opens and clicks are useful but not sufficient.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How did the platform perform against those measures?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): Launch time improved for repeatable programs. Manual corrections were still too high for ad hoc campaigns. Governance checks were performed, but outside the tool. Sales acceptance improved after we stopped sending every engagement alert. So there’s progress, but not a clean win.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Were there any features you particularly valued?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): The visual journey builder was easy to explain to stakeholders. The versioning and testing controls were also useful when we remembered to use them. Templates helped a lot. When the data was clean, the system was predictable.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): And where did it fall short?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): Cross-account reporting and permission design. We have several product lines and regional teams. We wanted local autonomy with centralized guardrails. We ended up creating conventions and checking them manually. Marketo’s team presented a model that looked closer to how leadership wants to operate.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Do you expect a direct migration of every journey?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): No. We’re going to rebuild the important programs. Some old journeys have outdated assumptions and should be retired. The migration will likely expose data issues that are not really platform-specific.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would you want us to improve for a future evaluation?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): Be clearer about the full operating model. The demo showed how to build a journey, but not enough about who maintains segments, how permissions scale, and what happens when a sync fails at eight in the morning on launch day. Also, make reporting examples closer to a multi-product software company rather than a single funnel.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): If Marketo implementation becomes difficult, would you reconsider?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): We have a signed direction and budget, so I wouldn’t expect that. But the platform remains in our history, and I would give a reference if asked—as long as the reference includes both the wins and the caveats.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That’s fair. Is there anything we should do before the transition?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): Help us export the journey definitions, suppression information, and performance history. I don’t need another pitch. I do need a clean handoff and confirmation of the retention window.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I’ll coordinate that with support and send a checklist. Are there any outstanding incidents?

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): One reporting discrepancy is still being checked. It may be a timezone issue. The numbers in the dashboard and our warehouse differ by a day for one campaign. Don’t mark it as a critical incident yet, but don’t close it without evidence.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. We’ll keep it open and document the comparison.

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): Good. To summarize my view: the platform helped us improve repeatable lifecycle work, but the surrounding operating model became too manual for where the company is heading. Marketo may fit that direction better. That is not the same as saying this platform failed.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That’s a nuanced and useful conclusion. Thanks for giving us the time, Kirstin.

Kirstin Gibson (Lifecycle Marketing): Thank you. Send the transition checklist by Friday, please.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Will do. Speak soon.
