Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Hadley, welcome. Before we start, are you working from the plant or the London office today?

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): London office. The plant is louder, although sometimes the dashboards are quieter there. We have 1,903 employees now, and our marketing operations feel like they belong to three different companies.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is a good place to begin. This is our quarterly review for Gislason Inc, with the broader marketing automation evaluation still in discovery. I would like to cover performance, adoption, data quality, open risks, and the priorities for the next quarter. Anything you want added?

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): Regional adoption and attribution. We can see activity, but I am not confident we can explain pipeline contribution consistently across the UK, Germany, and the North American teams.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Let us start with activity. What changed since the last review?

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): We launched two product campaigns and one distributor campaign. The product campaigns were reasonably coordinated. The distributor campaign was a different story: three regions used different naming conventions, and the reporting had to be reconciled manually.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Was the issue campaign setup, source data, or reporting configuration?

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): A little of each. The regional teams have autonomy, which they value. But autonomy without a common structure becomes translation work for my team.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How much of that reconciliation is happening in spreadsheets?

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): Too much. We export campaign activity, CRM opportunity data, and distributor responses, then join them. The result is useful, but it is not timely. By the time leadership asks a question, the answer is already historical.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That suggests the next design decision should be a shared campaign taxonomy and ownership model. A platform can automate reporting only when the inputs mean the same thing.

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): Correct. I do not want another dashboard layered on top of inconsistent definitions. “Influenced” means three things here depending on who is talking.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What definitions would you want standardized first?

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): Campaign membership, response, sourced opportunity, influenced opportunity, and partner-assisted opportunity. Also the date logic. Sales wants to count the date an opportunity was created; finance wants the date it moved stages.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Those are not just configuration questions. They are governance questions involving sales operations and finance. We can document alternative views, but the organization still needs an agreed primary view.

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): Exactly. Could the system preserve multiple views without making the user choose one forever?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Yes, if the underlying events and timestamps are retained. We can build reports for different stakeholders, but I would label them clearly so a board report does not accidentally mix definitions.

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): Good. Adoption is the other issue. The UK team uses the platform consistently. Germany uses it for email but not for landing pages. North America has a separate event process.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What explains the difference?

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): Training, mostly. The UK team had a local champion. Germany had turnover. North America says the standard forms are too rigid for distributor programs.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would a champion model help, or are the workflows materially different?

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): Both. We need champions, but we also need an exception path. If the standard process cannot support a legitimate regional requirement, people will build around it.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is reasonable. We could define a standard campaign template with required fields, then allow approved extensions for regional attributes. The extension should still feed common reporting fields.

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): I like that, but I need to know who approves an extension. My team cannot become a bottleneck for 1,900 employees.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We could propose a small governance group: demand generation, sales operations, and one regional representative. Approval thresholds can separate minor field additions from changes to attribution logic.

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): Put that in the action plan. Also, our database has duplicate company records. Some are subsidiaries, some are distributors, and some are just stale entries.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Are duplicates affecting campaign eligibility or mostly reporting?

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): Both. A contact may appear tied to a parent company in one system and a plant location in another. If we deduplicate aggressively, we risk losing useful plant-level context. If we do nothing, the account roll-up is unreliable.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then a blanket merge is probably unsafe. We should define the business identity rules first, perhaps distinguishing legal entity, operating site, and buying group.

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): That is the conversation I have been trying to start. Vendors tend to offer a button called “clean data” as if the button knows our structure.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): The button can identify patterns; it cannot decide whether two facilities buy independently. That judgment belongs with your business owners.

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): Fine. On performance, email engagement is up, but opportunity progression is flat. The executive team will ask whether the platform is producing anything beyond nicer activity reports.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What changed in the target audience?

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): We expanded into colder distributor lists. Engagement naturally fell. So the flat progression may reflect audience mix rather than campaign quality.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is an important confounder. We should compare like-for-like cohorts and separate new-market prospecting from existing-account programs.

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): Yes, and include sales follow-up time. A good response is not useful if nobody calls the account.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Agreed. We can add a handoff measure: accepted leads, time to first action, and disposition. That will require sales operations participation.

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): I can bring them into the next meeting, although they may bring a spreadsheet of their own.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We will give it a name and call it a data source. That usually makes people happier.

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): Maybe. What do you need from me before the next review?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Three things: examples of the regional campaign names, your current attribution definitions, and one duplicate-account case that illustrates the plant-versus-parent issue. We will use those to design a practical governance workshop.

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): I can send those by next Friday. I also want a proposed dashboard layout, but marked as a draft.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I will send a draft with a note that metrics depend on agreed definitions. We should not present it as finished while the taxonomy is unresolved.

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): Good. The account is still in discovery from my perspective. We have enough evidence to continue, not enough to choose a final architecture.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That matches my notes. We will keep the next step focused on governance, adoption, and attribution feasibility. Thanks for the candid review, Hadley.

Hadley Treutel (Demand Generation): Thanks, Marcus. I appreciate that the messy parts made it into the notes.
