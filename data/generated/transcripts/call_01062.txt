Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Gennaro, good morning—or good afternoon for you, I think. I always have to check the Melbourne time zone twice.

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): It is morning here, just barely. The holiday calendar made scheduling this one a bit odd.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I appreciate you joining during the break. I understand Herzog - Shanahan has been comparing us with HubSpot for marketing automation, and the opportunity is now closed-lost. I’d still like to understand what drove the decision.

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): Yes, we selected HubSpot. We are a retail group with several brands and about three hundred and fifty employees. The main issue was not that your product looked poor. It was that HubSpot felt easier for our marketing team to own without depending on sales operations.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is helpful. When you say easier to own, was it campaign creation, administration, reporting, or the overall rollout?

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): Mostly rollout and familiarity. Our regional marketers already know HubSpot. They could see how to build emails and landing pages immediately. With your platform, we liked the control, but we expected more design and governance work up front.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): During the demo, I emphasized the governance features. Did that come across as too much complexity?

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): A little. We have central operations, but the brands need autonomy. If every newsletter requires a ticket to a central administrator, adoption drops. At the same time, we cannot let each region invent its own customer fields.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): So the tension was autonomy versus consistency.

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): Exactly. Also, our customer data is not clean. Loyalty records, ecommerce records, store signups, and sales contacts overlap. We did not want to start a six-month data project before sending anything useful.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Did HubSpot give you confidence that it could handle the duplicates better, or did its implementation proposal simply feel more familiar?

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): More the second. Their partner showed us a phased plan with one brand first. It was concrete. Your proposal was more flexible, but we had to imagine the phases ourselves.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is fair criticism. Was pricing a deciding factor?

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): It mattered, but it was not the only factor. The commercial numbers were close enough that we were really buying confidence and speed. Our leadership wanted a visible campaign live this quarter.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What campaign did you have in mind?

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): A post-purchase series for online customers, followed by store-specific offers. We needed consent by channel and region. Australia and New Zealand are not identical operationally, even when the team treats them that way.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Were you able to test that regional consent logic in our demo?

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): Not properly. We saw segmentation, but not enough of the exception handling. Someone asked about a customer shopping online in one region and visiting a store in another, and the answer was “we can configure it.” That may be true, but it did not lower the risk.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. Did the sales integration create concern?

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): Some. Sales operations wanted reliable ownership and activity visibility. Marketing wanted fewer approvals. The two groups were not aligned on what should sync back to the CRM. HubSpot already had a working connector in our environment, so that reduced friction.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Was there anything you preferred in our approach?

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): The audit history and permissions were stronger. For a regulated promotion or a high-value customer segment, I liked being able to see who changed a rule. Your reporting also seemed more flexible once configured.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): If we had offered a narrower pilot, would the outcome have changed?

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): Maybe, but timing was decisive. We had an internal deadline. We could not run two pilots and delay the launch. If you had proposed a four-week pilot with one brand, a defined data set, and an implementation owner, we would have evaluated it seriously.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is very specific. What would success have meant after four weeks?

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): First, a working post-purchase journey. Second, consent and suppression behaving correctly. Third, reporting that a regional manager could understand without a training session. Fourth, proof that our operations team could administer it after handoff.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): And what would have failed the pilot?

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): If basic changes required professional services, or if the duplicate rules created obvious customer errors. Also, if the connector duplicated activities in the CRM. We had that happen with a previous tool and nobody wants a repeat.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Did your team consider revisiting after the HubSpot rollout?

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): Not in the near term. The opportunity is closed. We are committed to getting the new system live. That said, I would not call the decision permanent. If governance becomes painful at scale, we may reassess.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I appreciate the candor. One thing I want to clarify: was the product itself missing a required capability?

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): No single fatal gap. It was accumulated uncertainty—migration, training, ownership, and timeline. HubSpot won by being the safer operational decision for us, even though we saw advantages in your controls.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That distinction is useful for our team. Is there anything we should not assume from this loss?

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): Do not assume the largest feature list wins. Our marketers actually wanted fewer decisions. Also, do not assume central operations wants more control; we wanted clean boundaries, not control over every email.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a valuable counterpoint. I’ll record the feedback and send a concise summary, with no pressure to reopen the deal.

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): Good. If you do update the implementation story, make it concrete. A sample plan would help more than another broad demo.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Agreed. Thanks for making time, Gennaro, and enjoy the rest of the holiday period.

Gennaro Carter (Sales Operations): You too, Amelia. Take care.
