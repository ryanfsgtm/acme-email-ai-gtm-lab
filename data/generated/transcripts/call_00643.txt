Darby Terry (Privacy): For every outbound customer campaign, yes. Transactional messages are handled elsewhere. Also, the refresh must account for withdrawals recorded after audience creation.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is feasible as a launch gate. We should define what happens if the refresh fails—hold the send, or use the last successful snapshot?

Darby Terry (Privacy): Hold the send. A stale permission snapshot is not an acceptable fallback for our higher-risk regions.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Clear. What qualifies as higher risk?

Darby Terry (Privacy): Any campaign using behavioral data, partner-provided contacts, or sensitive product-interest categories. Basic newsletter sends may have a simpler approval route, but I don’t want that distinction improvised by individual campaign managers.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then we’ll make the classification part of the request form. I’ll have our solution team propose a rule matrix for review.

Darby Terry (Privacy): Good. Another issue is deletion. We can remove a person from active audiences, but our legal team wants confirmation that event history is treated according to the applicable retention schedule.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can plan a deletion and suppression test with representative identities. Should that include a person appearing in multiple regional systems?

Darby Terry (Privacy): Definitely. That is where our current process is weakest. A French customer may have a service relationship in Germany and a marketing record in Spain. The systems do not always agree on the identifier.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We’ll need a cross-system identity strategy before promising end-to-end deletion. I’ll mark that as a dependency rather than burying it in the connector task.
