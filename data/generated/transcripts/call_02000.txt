Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Norbert, thanks for joining. I understand you’re evaluating ways to coordinate lifecycle communication across the education programs.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Yes, although “evaluating” may be generous. We have a problem, but the solution is not yet agreed internally.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That’s a good place to start. What is breaking today?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): We have prospective students attending webinars, downloading course guides, and speaking with advisors, but those signals sit in different places. Campaigns are mostly manual. A coordinator exports a list, cleans it, and sends an email.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How often does that happen?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Weekly for the main programs, more often during enrollment periods. The work is not impossible, but it is fragile. If the coordinator is away, everything slows down.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): And what would you want automated first?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Follow-up after a webinar, reminders for incomplete applications, and a re-engagement series for people who have gone quiet. The admissions team also wants better visibility into which messages lead to advisor conversations.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Let’s look at a webinar follow-up. A registration event enters the platform, consent is checked, and the contact receives a message based on attendance. Non-attendees receive a recording link; attendees receive next-step content.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): We do not always know whether someone attended. The webinar provider’s export arrives the next day.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then the initial message could use registration status, with a later branch when attendance data arrives. We should not imply real-time personalization if the source is delayed.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Good. That distinction matters. Our last vendor demo made everything sound instantaneous.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What system owns the contact record?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): The student information system owns enrolled students. Prospects are in the admissions CRM. Marketing has its own mailing lists, some of which are older than anyone wants to admit.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We would need rules for matching and for consent. Are marketing permissions captured centrally?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Not consistently. Some forms record an explicit opt-in; older imports just have a source and date. The legal team is reviewing those records.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can keep unknown permission states suppressed until legal decides how they should be treated. That may reduce the initial reachable audience, but it avoids building a journey on questionable data.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): That could frustrate admissions. They already complain that marketing is too cautious.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then the business discussion should include the size of the suppressed audience and the reason, so the tradeoff is visible.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Can an advisor see what a prospect has received?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Yes, if the CRM integration passes activity and the advisor has the right access. We can show campaign engagement and journey status without exposing unrelated private data.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): We have regional programs in England, Wales, and Scotland. The content and deadlines differ.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can use program and region attributes for branching, with localized templates and approval rules. How much control should regional teams have?

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): They need to edit copy, but central marketing must own the data rules and unsubscribe language.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That maps well to role-based access and shared templates. We would test that with a regional editor before going live.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Reporting is another concern. Leadership asks for enrollment impact, but we can only show opens and clicks today.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can pass engagement events to the CRM and connect them to advisor activity or application milestones, assuming those events have stable identifiers. Attribution still needs a defined window.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Our finance team will ask whether that is a proven causal effect.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We should be careful there. The platform can report influence and sequence, but it cannot by itself prove causation. A holdout test may be more defensible for one program.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): I appreciate that answer. We have a small analytics team, though. I doubt they want a complex experiment immediately.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then start with a baseline: time to launch, coordinator hours, application completion, and advisor follow-up. We can add experimentation after the operating process is stable.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): What does implementation require from us?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Data access, field definitions, consent policy, content owners, and a pilot audience. For a first phase, I would suggest one program and two journeys rather than all departments.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): The enrollment window begins in September. We need something useful before then, but procurement has not opened a project.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then the immediate outcome should be a feasibility package, not a promised launch date. We can outline the pilot, assumptions, and a budget range for procurement.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Budget range is important. We are a mid-market institution, and the board does not like open-ended services.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll separate subscription, implementation, and optional services. I’ll also identify which tasks your team can perform after enablement.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Can you send a sample project plan?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Yes, with milestones for discovery, data mapping, configuration, testing, training, and launch readiness. The dates will remain illustrative until we know your review cycle.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): I need to involve admissions operations and legal. They may disagree about the priority.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is useful now. If admissions wants incomplete-application reminders and legal wants consent remediation first, the pilot needs an explicit decision rather than quietly trying to do both.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): I’ll schedule an internal discussion. I cannot promise they will agree.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): No problem. I’ll send the feasibility package by Monday, including the delayed-attendance example and the consent caveat.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): Good. I’m interested, but we are still at the information-gathering stage.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. We’ll treat the next meeting as a requirements review, not a commercial commitment.

Norbert Mante (Lifecycle Marketing): That works. Thanks, Marcus.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Thanks, Norbert. I’ll follow up with the materials and leave the launch date open until the internal owners are aligned.
