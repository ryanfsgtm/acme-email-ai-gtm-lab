Cordie Prohaska (Revenue Operations): The autumn store-partner campaign. It is representative enough to be useful, but not so critical that a testing delay would damage revenue.

Cordie Prohaska (Revenue Operations): The current list is around twelve thousand, though I expect duplicates and old addresses. I would rather clean a smaller pilot first than load everything and discover problems afterward.

Cordie Prohaska (Revenue Operations): Marketing owns campaign membership. Sales operations owns account and contact ownership. Data protection reviews consent. The tricky part is that none of those owners reports to me.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Then the plan should include decision owners, not just task owners. If there is a conflict on a field, who has the final say?

Cordie Prohaska (Revenue Operations): For contact fields, sales operations. For marketing eligibility, data protection. For campaign naming, me. If the issue crosses two areas, our commercial director decides.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Excellent. Let's document that. What is your current definition of a valid marketing contact?

Cordie Prohaska (Revenue Operations): An opted-in business contact with a current email, a country, and a partner relationship. But “current” is subjective. Some records were verified last year; others are from trade-show scans.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We should separate eligibility from freshness. A record can have consent and still need verification before it enters a high-volume send.

Cordie Prohaska (Revenue Operations): Yes. I also want a quarantine status for records that are incomplete, instead of letting people fix them ad hoc during a launch.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is workable. We can create an exception queue and agree on who clears it. How quickly can the team review exceptions?

Cordie Prohaska (Revenue Operations): During launch weeks, maybe one business day. Outside those windows, two or three days. I do not want us promising same-day review if nobody is staffed for it.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Good. We should set a service expectation that reflects reality. Now, on assignment rules: is ownership based on territory, account, or product interest?

Cordie Prohaska (Revenue Operations): Territory first, then named-account ownership. Product interest is a notification, not the owner. We learned that assigning by product creates confusion when one customer asks about several categories.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will note territory first, named-account override second. What happens when there is no territory?

Cordie Prohaska (Revenue Operations): Put it in the unassigned queue. Do not round-robin it automatically. Our team is small, and an invisible random owner is worse than a visible queue.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a valuable constraint. We can include an alert for the queue without assigning the record.

Cordie Prohaska (Revenue Operations): And please make the alert visible to me and sales ops. Last time, a queue existed but nobody knew where to look.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. For the first build, I suggest we configure the workflow, then run three tests: a normal territory match, a named-account override, and a missing-territory exception.

Cordie Prohaska (Revenue Operations): Add a fourth: an existing contact with a new campaign response. We need to prove it updates the activity without creating a duplicate.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Good catch. We will include that. What should the follow-up sequence be?

Cordie Prohaska (Revenue Operations): A confirmation email immediately, a task for sales within one business day, and a reminder if the task is not completed after two days. But the reminder should stop if the contact opts out.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can test that branch explicitly. Do you want marketing or sales to own the confirmation email?

Cordie Prohaska (Revenue Operations): Marketing owns the email content. Sales owns the personal follow-up. Compliance needs to approve the template before it goes live.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How long does that approval usually take?

Cordie Prohaska (Revenue Operations): If the language is standard, three to five business days. If someone changes the claims or adds a promotion, it can take much longer. We should not schedule launch before approval.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Agreed. We can prepare the workflow with a placeholder template, but launch readiness requires the approved version.

Cordie Prohaska (Revenue Operations): Exactly. Another concern is reporting. Leadership wants conversion by campaign, but sales activity is not always logged consistently.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What minimum event would count as a conversion for the pilot?

Cordie Prohaska (Revenue Operations): A qualified conversation, meaning a sales rep accepted the task and recorded an outcome. An email open or a click is engagement, not conversion.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We will keep those metrics separate. We should also show records missing an outcome so the report does not hide the data-quality issue.

Cordie Prohaska (Revenue Operations): Yes. If the dashboard only shows clean records, people will think the process is healthier than it is.
