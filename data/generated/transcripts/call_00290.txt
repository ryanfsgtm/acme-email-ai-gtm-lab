Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Lera, thanks for making time. I understand you are joining from revenue operations, and that Stiedemann - Franey has already looked at ActiveCampaign. I would like to understand the operating problem before discussing a replacement.

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): That is the right order. We have 960 employees, several service lines, and a lot of regional variation. The platform conversation gets noisy quickly.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What made the conversation urgent now?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Our lead lifecycle is no longer consistent. One region marks a lead qualified after a form submission, another waits for a sales conversation, and a third has its own spreadsheet. Forecasting is affected because marketing and sales do not mean the same thing by “active.”

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Is the main pain data quality, process design, or adoption?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): All three, but process design comes first. Better software will not fix three definitions of qualification.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is a useful boundary. How are campaigns built today?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Central marketing creates some programs, while regional teams create others. The central team can move quickly. Regional teams are slower because they wait for approval and often lack confidence in the audience rules.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What happens when a regional team requests a change?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): It enters a queue. Sometimes the request is urgent, but there is no consistent way to tell whether it is urgent because of a revenue event or simply because someone remembered it late.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would a shared prioritization process help, or do you need decentralized control?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): A balance. We need centralized governance for definitions and compliance, but local teams need to build within guardrails. If every email requires central operations, adoption will remain low.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What systems are involved in the lifecycle?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): CRM, website forms, webinar registration, billing, and a data warehouse. The CRM is the source for opportunity stages, but the warehouse is better for historical reporting.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How fresh does the data need to be?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Opportunity status should be close to real time. Historical attribution can be daily. A dashboard that looks live but is actually a day behind would create more arguments.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. Which metrics do leadership ask for?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Pipeline influenced by marketing, conversion by source, speed from inquiry to first contact, and regional performance. The problem is that each metric has exceptions nobody documents.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Can you give an example?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): A partner referral may be credited to marketing even though marketing did not generate it. An event attendee may be counted as a new inquiry even when the person already had an open opportunity. The numbers are not necessarily wrong, but the rules are unclear.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would you want the system to enforce one attribution model?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Eventually, perhaps. First I want it to show the assumptions and exceptions. A precise wrong answer is worse than an honest qualified answer.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is a strong principle. On the operator side, what takes the most time?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Audience preparation and QA. People duplicate a segment, edit one condition, and forget to update the exclusion. Then someone catches it in review.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How is testing handled?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Mostly manually. We can test subject lines, but multistep journey tests are difficult because the sample is small and the timing is uneven.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would a visual journey builder be enough to change that?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Not by itself. We need version history, approvals, and a way to preview who qualifies. Otherwise a prettier builder just makes mistakes easier to create.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is fair. Who would administer those controls?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Revenue operations would own the framework. Regional marketing leads would own their content. Legal needs to approve consent language. Sales operations needs to validate the handoff.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How do sales receive a handoff today?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): An alert and a task, but the alert sometimes arrives without useful context. Sales wants the source, recent activity, and reason for qualification in one place.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What response time is expected?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): For high-intent requests, under one business hour. For lower-intent content engagement, it should enter nurture rather than create a task.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How do you distinguish those categories now?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): A combination of form type, account tier, and declared need. It is not perfect. Some high-value accounts submit a generic form.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): So the routing model needs an exception path, not just a score threshold.

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Exactly. I am cautious about scoring because people treat a score as a fact.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would a successful evaluation look like?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Use two regions, one central campaign, and one partner referral example. Show the qualification rules, the exclusion behavior, the sales handoff, and the resulting report. We should include a deliberately messy contact sample.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How large a sample?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Maybe 10,000 records, with duplicates and old consent states. If the sample is clean, we learn nothing.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What could prevent a purchase even if the evaluation works?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Implementation burden. Our team is already committed to a CRM redesign. We cannot run two transformations with overlapping ownership.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then timing and responsibility need to be part of the evaluation. Would a phased rollout reduce the risk?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Potentially. Start with definitions and one region, then add the rest. But the architecture must not make expansion a rewrite.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Who else should join the next meeting?

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): One regional marketing lead, sales operations, IT integration, and someone from legal for consent. The VP Marketing should see the output but probably does not need to attend the technical workshop.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That gives us a good group. I will send a discovery summary with the assumptions I heard and the questions still open.

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Please distinguish facts from proposed decisions. That is another habit we need to improve.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I will. I will also include ActiveCampaign as a comparison point without assuming it is the only alternative.

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Good. We are not looking for a winner in a feature checklist. We are looking for a lifecycle process people can operate and leadership can trust.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is clear. I will propose a ninety-minute evaluation session next week.

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Send two options. My calendar is unpleasant at the moment.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Will do. Thanks, Lera.

Lera Hackett (Revenue Operations): Thank you. The conversation was focused, which is a promising start.
