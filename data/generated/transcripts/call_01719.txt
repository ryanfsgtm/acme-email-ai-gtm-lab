Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Isaias, thanks for taking the call. I know procurement is usually brought in after a business team has already decided what it wants, so I’d like to understand your role in this evaluation.

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): That is generally true, although we get involved earlier when a platform could affect several regions or create a large implementation commitment. We are not at vendor selection yet. This is still exploratory.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Helpful distinction. What triggered the exploration?

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): The marketing organization is consolidating tools. There are too many contracts, overlapping capabilities, and inconsistent renewal dates. They want a more coherent automation environment, but procurement is concerned that “consolidation” can become a justification for an oversized project.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What does the current environment look like?

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): Several business units use different systems. The primary comparison at the moment includes your platform and Pardot. Some groups have custom integrations that they describe as essential, though we have not validated every one.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would you need validated first?

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): Total cost over three years, implementation ownership, security obligations, user licensing, and exit terms. I also need to understand what is included versus what becomes a paid service. “Easy to implement” is not a useful phrase without assumptions.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Fair. Do you have a target timeline?

Isaias Fisher (Account Executive): The marketing team would like a recommendation in the second half of the year. The close date currently in the system is not a commitment; it is an internal placeholder. Budget planning begins before that.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How many users are likely to need access?

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): That is unsettled. Full administrators may be a small group, but regional marketers and sales managers want some visibility. If licensing is based on every occasional user, the economics change quickly.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Are you evaluating campaign volume as well as seats?

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): Yes. We have high-volume periods around product launches and events. A model that looks affordable in an average month may be expensive during those peaks. Please show the assumptions clearly.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I will. Apart from pricing, what operational outcomes are being requested?

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): Marketing wants faster campaign launches and less dependency on IT. Sales wants better lead routing. Executives want credible attribution. I am not sure those outcomes all require the same configuration.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is often where scope expands. Which outcome is non-negotiable?

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): Compliance and continuity. We cannot disrupt active programs during migration. Data residency and access controls are also material because we operate internationally.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would your security team provide a requirements checklist?

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): Eventually. We have a standard questionnaire, but it is not the first gate. First we need a business owner and a preliminary architecture. Otherwise security reviews a hypothetical solution.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Who is the business owner today?

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): The head of marketing operations is sponsoring the work, but regional leadership has not signed off. One large region believes its current process is efficient enough. Another wants change immediately.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That sounds like a stakeholder alignment issue. What happens if the regions disagree?

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): The project may be phased. A central deployment could start with two regions, then expand. But a phased plan can cost more if the initial design cannot support the later requirements.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We could model both a centralized and phased approach. Would a workshop with marketing operations and IT be useful?

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): Yes, provided it produces artifacts: requirements, assumptions, dependencies, and a decision log. We do not need another broad presentation.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What should we bring to that workshop?

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): A sample implementation plan, licensing scenarios, integration boundaries, and a clear list of customer responsibilities. Also explain how data is exported if the relationship ends. That question is sometimes skipped until legal asks it.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can include it. Do you have a preferred contracting structure?

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): We prefer an initial term that gives us confidence without locking us into an untested global rollout. Renewal caps matter. So do service-level commitments and remedies if implementation milestones slip.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would a pilot be acceptable?

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): Potentially. The pilot must use a real process and have a defined success measure. A demonstration environment with perfect data will not tell us enough.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Which process would be representative?

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): Event follow-up across two regions, with lead routing, consent handling, and opportunity reporting. It is complex enough to expose the gaps but contained enough to evaluate.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is a good candidate. I’ll propose a ninety-minute workshop and send a pricing framework beforehand.

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): Send it, but mark it as indicative. I do not want internal stakeholders treating an early estimate as a quote.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. One last question: if the solution meets the functional requirements, what could still prevent approval?

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): Unclear implementation risk, weak references for organizations of our scale, or a commercial structure that penalizes growth. Also, if the regional teams cannot operate independently after deployment.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): And what would make the proposal compelling?

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): Transparent economics, a credible migration sequence, and evidence that the platform can support both central governance and local execution.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll capture those points and coordinate with your marketing operations sponsor.

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): Good. Please do not imply that procurement has endorsed a vendor. We have not.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Absolutely. Thanks for the clarity, Isaias.

Isaias Fisher (Procurement): Thank you. I’ll review the materials when they arrive, and then we can decide whether the workshop is justified.
