Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Hi Myrtie, thanks for making time. Can you hear me clearly? I’m getting a little echo on my side.

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): I can hear you. There’s a slight delay, but it’s manageable. We’re between offices today, so the conference room equipment is doing its best.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That sounds familiar. I understand Bahringer is evaluating marketing automation, with Pardot in the comparison. Before I make assumptions, what prompted the review now?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): The immediate trigger is that our regional teams are running campaigns differently. We have about twelve hundred and twenty employees across professional services, and sales operations is spending too much time stitching together campaign responses.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What does stitching together mean in practice?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): We export from the current marketing tools, clean the files, compare them with CRM activity, and then send lists to sales leaders. A campaign can look successful because it generated forms, but we can’t always tell whether the right accounts engaged.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): So account-level visibility matters more than raw lead volume.

Myrtie Heidenreich (Account Sales Operations): Exactly. One consultant downloading three white papers shouldn’t necessarily outrank an entire account attending a briefing. We need a way to see the pattern without asking every seller to become a data analyst.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How are campaigns organised today—by region, service line, or both?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): Both, and that’s part of the trouble. Australia and New Zealand have different naming conventions, and service lines create their own campaign structures. There are several versions of “risk advisory webinar” in the system.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would standardised campaign templates be acceptable, or do the regions need full control?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): A controlled template with local fields would work. If you tell them they can’t customise anything, they’ll work around the process. If you let them customise everything, we’re back where we started.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That balance comes up often. We can separate required governance fields from optional local attributes. Required might include service line, region, audience, and fiscal period.

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): That sounds sensible. We also need approval steps. At the moment, a campaign can go live before legal or brand has seen it.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Is the approval issue mostly timing, or have there been actual incidents?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): Both. We had one event page with an outdated disclaimer. It was corrected quickly, but it created a lot of internal noise. The current system doesn’t make the approval state obvious.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can map approvals and permissions, but I’d want to understand who approves what. Marketing operations, brand, legal, and sometimes a service-line partner?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): Correct, and it varies by campaign type. That variation is why a single approval chain won’t work.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Let’s talk lead management. What happens after someone responds to a campaign?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): They’re scored, but the score isn’t trusted. A junior contact at a large target account may score lower than an individual who downloaded several assets. Sales leaders then ask us to override the list manually.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would make the scoring credible?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): It needs to include account fit, engagement recency, role, and known opportunity status. Also, the reason for the score has to be visible. “Score: eighty” tells a seller almost nothing.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can show contributing signals and use account-level rules. Would you want a model that sales operations can adjust without a technical ticket?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): Yes, within guardrails. We don’t want every regional manager editing scoring logic, but I need to test a rule and see what changes.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. How does Pardot fit into the conversation?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): It’s familiar to some of our teams and has a strong ecosystem. The concern is whether it solves our governance problem or just gives us another place to manage assets. We’re not choosing based on a feature checklist.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That’s helpful. What would a useful evaluation involve?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): A sample campaign from briefing through handoff. We’d want three regions represented, a service-line dimension, approval history, account engagement, and a report that a sales director can understand.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can build that as a working session. Which data can we use?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): Sanitised records are fine. We can provide campaign names, account tiers, job functions, and outcomes. We can’t provide personal details until procurement and privacy approve it.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): No problem. What systems need to connect?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): CRM first, then webinar and event platforms, then our data warehouse. The warehouse connection is important, but it’s not phase one if it adds months.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What timeline are you working toward?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): The opportunity is open, but our planning cycle closes in April. We’d need a recommendation before then, though implementation could be later.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): And who else should be involved?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): Marketing operations, one regional marketing lead, a sales director, IT integration, and someone from legal. Procurement comes in after we know there’s a fit.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That sounds like a reasonable group. Are there any non-negotiables?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): Auditability, account-level reporting, permission controls, and a clear handoff into sales. Also, we need to avoid a long implementation that depends on one administrator.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): If the evaluation demonstrated those four areas and the team could operate the system without constant consulting, would that be enough for a recommendation?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): It would get us to a serious recommendation. Cost and migration effort still matter. And I want the regions to challenge the workflow, not just watch a prepared demo.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Agreed. I’ll propose a ninety-minute workshop with an imperfect sample campaign and time for objections. We’ll document what we can support immediately and what would need a later phase.

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): Good. Please include a comparison against our current Pardot process, but keep it outcome-based.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I will. I’ll send an agenda and data template this week. Anything else before we wrap?

Myrtie Heidenreich (Sales Operations): No, that covers it. The echo has improved, at least.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): A successful technical milestone. Thanks, Myrtie. I’ll follow up with the workshop options.
