Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Cassandra, thanks for joining. I understand you are evaluating ways to improve marketing operations at Reynolds LLC. Before I share the product, could you describe what happens after a new contact enters your process?

Cassandra Brown (Marketing Operations): Usually, the honest answer is that three things happen depending on who notices it first. A referral may be emailed to me, a web inquiry goes into a form tool, and a hospital event list arrives as a spreadsheet. Then I normalize it by hand and try to decide what should happen next.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How much of that is automated today?

Cassandra Brown (Marketing Operations): Basic notifications and a few email sequences. The rest is homegrown. We are a forty-five-person healthcare company, so we cannot behave as if we have a large data team. Our system is functional, but it depends heavily on me remembering the exceptions.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What kind of exceptions are most common?

Cassandra Brown (Marketing Operations): Missing organization names, duplicate contacts, old job titles, and people who should not be marketed to because their consent is unclear. Also, a salesperson may say a referral is urgent, while the record has no source or permission information.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Let's use that last example. Would you want the system to hold the record until required fields are complete?

Cassandra Brown (Marketing Operations): For some sources, yes. But not for every inquiry. If a hospital administrator submits a request for information, I do not want a rigid form rule to make the response wait two days because an optional field is blank.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): So you need different service levels: immediate response for a high-intent inquiry, controlled review for a questionable imported list, and perhaps a normal nurture path for known-consent contacts.

Cassandra Brown (Marketing Operations): Exactly. The words 'automate everything' make me nervous. We need automation where the rule is clear and a human handoff where the risk is meaningful.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is a good design principle. In this workflow, the source and consent status determine the initial route. A direct inquiry can receive an acknowledgment and create a task, while an imported list is held for review.

Cassandra Brown (Marketing Operations): Can the task show the reason for the route? Sales will ask why one contact got a task and another did not.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Yes. The task can include source, consent status, requested information, and the condition that triggered it. You can also add a short explanation field for manual decisions.

Cassandra Brown (Marketing Operations): Good. Now duplicates. We have two people at the same hospital with similar names, and an old record sometimes gets matched to the wrong person. I do not want an automatic merge based only on email domain.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then email domain should be a supporting signal, not a merge rule. You can require multiple matching criteria and route uncertain matches to a review queue.

Cassandra Brown (Marketing Operations): Show me that queue.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Here it is. It lists the candidate records, matching signals, source dates, and the proposed action. A reviewer can merge, keep separate, or mark the suggestion incorrect. The decision is logged.

Cassandra Brown (Marketing Operations): That is better. We have a homegrown spreadsheet with colored cells, but the color does not tell me who made the decision or why.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Auditability is especially important when the record may contain sensitive business context. We should also define which fields are safe to include in notifications and exports.

Cassandra Brown (Marketing Operations): Yes. We do not put patient information in marketing systems, but people occasionally paste too much into a free-text note. The platform cannot fix careless behavior by itself.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can restrict fields, add guidance, and create validation rules, but training and policy still matter. I would recommend an explicit rule that patient information never belongs in campaign notes or contact properties.

Cassandra Brown (Marketing Operations): That will need to be in the implementation checklist. Our compliance team will ask about it.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can set precedence rules and test them against known examples. For a customer, the account relationship should take priority over a generic event source. But again, we would want your team to approve that hierarchy.
