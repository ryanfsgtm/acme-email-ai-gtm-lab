Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Hi Maud, welcome to the onboarding session. Before we get into screens, how did the internal project meeting go?

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Productive, but crowded. Everyone wants automation, and nobody wants to own the definitions behind it.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is usually the first implementation task disguised as a philosophical debate. Today we can establish ownership, the first use case, and the technical validation path.

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Good. Our opportunity is still open because we haven’t decided whether to proceed with this platform or keep the current process. The “no decision” option is very real.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. What process should we use as the pilot?

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): New-account follow-up after an inquiry. Sales operations currently exports a list, cleans it, and sends it to regional teams. They then decide who gets contacted. It takes too long and the handoff is inconsistent.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Do you want automation to assign every inquiry immediately?

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): No. We have strategic accounts, existing customers, distributors, and low-fit requests. A blind assignment would create noise.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then we should define a qualification and routing matrix first. What fields are reliable today?

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Country and account owner are fairly reliable. Industry is inconsistent. Product interest is often free text, and employee count is missing for smaller companies.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can start with reliable fields and use review queues for incomplete records. Do you have a preferred system of record?

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): CRM for account ownership and opportunity status. Marketing owns campaign membership and engagement. Sales operations owns routing rules.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Excellent. I’ll record those ownership decisions. What should happen when a contact submits a second inquiry?

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): It should attach to the existing contact if confidence is high, but not create a duplicate task every time. We need a way to see the new activity without restarting the whole sequence.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can use an event update and a conditional branch. The duplicate logic depends on the matching keys and your CRM configuration.

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): We have multiple people at the same company using personal-looking addresses, so email domain isn’t enough.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then CRM ID or a governed matching service may be necessary. We’ll test common and ambiguous cases in the sandbox.

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Please include a subsidiary. The parent account has one owner, but subsidiaries may route by region.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We’ll add parent-child behavior to the test plan. Should the automation create an opportunity, or only a task?

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Only a task in the pilot. Sales should qualify first. Automatically opening opportunities would distort forecasting.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That’s a sensible boundary. We can require a qualification outcome before the opportunity stage changes.

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Can the regional team see why a task was assigned?

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Yes, if we include the routing reason in the task or associated activity. We can also expose the source campaign and inquiry type.

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): I want the reason visible, but not a paragraph generated by a model. Keep it structured.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can use structured values such as region, account tier, and product interest, with an optional note.

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Better. Now, permissions. Regional administrators need to see their queues, while corporate operations needs the aggregate view.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We’ll define regional workspaces and corporate reporting access. Are there users who should approve changes to routing logic?

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Me and one backup. Regional teams can request changes but should not publish them directly.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I’ll include that approval rule. How often do you expect to revise the matrix?

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Frequently at first. New products and territories are being introduced. After the first quarter, perhaps monthly.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then change history and version naming will matter. We can test rolling back a rule, but we should agree who signs off on each version.

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Put that in the operating guide. We’ve lost time before because nobody remembered which routing sheet was current.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What reporting would tell you the pilot is working?

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Time from inquiry to assignment, duplicate-task rate, percentage routed without manual cleanup, and accepted versus rejected tasks. I also want a queue-aging report.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can build those as baseline metrics. For accepted tasks, should “accepted” mean a salesperson clicked a button or entered an outcome?

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Entered an outcome. Clicks are too easy to misunderstand.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Noted. We’ll also separate no-response from unqualified and bad data.

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): That distinction will help. Otherwise leadership sees one low number and assumes the whole system failed.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Let’s discuss the technical connection. Which CRM objects are in scope?

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Contacts, accounts, activities, and possibly custom territory objects. The territory object is still being redesigned.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then we should avoid making the pilot depend on the unfinished object. We can use a temporary territory field and document the later migration.

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Agreed, though I don’t want a temporary field becoming permanent by accident.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We’ll put an expiry review on it and assign an owner.

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): How are failures surfaced? Our concern is silent failure between systems.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We’ll configure integration alerts, review logs during the pilot, and run a daily reconciliation. The exact retry behavior will be confirmed with the integration specialist.

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Good. Do not call the process real time until we measure it.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Agreed. We’ll record actual latency and define a service expectation after testing.

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): What would cause us to stop after the pilot?

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That decision is yours, but possible stop conditions include unacceptable duplicate rates, incomplete audit history, or more administration than the current process.

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Add user adoption. If regional teams bypass the queue, the metrics will be meaningless.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can monitor bypasses and interview a sample of users, not just count system events.

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Exactly. A perfect workflow nobody uses is not a successful workflow.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I’ll send a thirty-day onboarding plan with the data map, permissions, test cases, metrics, and decision gates. We can schedule the data workshop next Thursday.

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Thursday works. Include the CRM architect and one regional manager who is skeptical. If the process survives that conversation, it has a chance.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Skeptical users are excellent testers.

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): They are also excellent at finding the one missing exception at 4:55 on Friday.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We’ll leave room for that exception. Thanks, Maud.

Maud Larson (Sales Operations): Thanks, Luis. Send the draft before Thursday so I can mark it up.
