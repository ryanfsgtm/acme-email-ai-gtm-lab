Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Hilda, thanks for taking the time. I saw the rain finally stopped in Sydney, which feels like a minor operational victory.

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): It did, briefly. We are planning around weather and retail launches at the moment, so the calendar is full.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is why I would like to understand the planning rhythm before talking about a solution. You have a large regional operation and an active marketing automation program. What prompted the review now?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): We won last year with the current rollout, but the teams still build campaigns differently by country. Australia and New Zealand share some processes; our stores and online group do not. Reporting is slow, and my team spends too much time reconciling numbers.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): When you say reconciling, are you comparing campaign responses, store activity, and online revenue in separate systems?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Exactly. The campaign report says one thing, commerce says another, and the regional manager has a spreadsheet that becomes the unofficial truth by Friday.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That sounds painful. How often does it affect a decision rather than just create administrative work?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Weekly. We change spend when a launch underperforms, but sometimes we discover the tracking was late or the audience definition changed. Then the postmortem becomes a debate about whose number was real.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would a useful operating model look like?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): A shared definition of audience, a clean handoff to sales and stores, and dashboards that show freshness. I do not need another pretty dashboard that nobody trusts.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is helpful. I can show how teams use a common campaign and lifecycle model, but first, where are the biggest gaps in the current process?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Consent is one. We have loyalty members, store purchasers, and newsletter subscribers. They do not all have the same permissions. Another is regional ownership: a national campaign can overlap with a local one.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How do you resolve an overlap today?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Usually by email. Sometimes the national team finds out after the local send. There is no elegant control, just people trying to be considerate.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can look at a shared calendar and suppression rules. I also want to understand your journey design. For example, a customer could receive different content after browsing, purchasing, or joining a loyalty program. Would advanced journey branching be relevant to how you operate?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): It could be. We do have different paths for first-time buyers and returning members, although the current setup is fairly simple.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can model branches by region, customer status, purchase category, or engagement. The idea is to give the team a controlled way to add conditions without rebuilding every campaign.

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Right. But I do not want branching for its own sake. Our smaller regions may need one standard path because they do not have the staff to maintain a tree of exceptions.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a good counterweight. We can make the default journey simple and reserve branches for decisions that change the message, timing, or owner. Would that help?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Yes, especially if the reporting still rolls up. I want to see the overall journey and then drill into a regional branch when something is different.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can discuss reporting at both levels. What is the handoff point to sales or store teams?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): For online leads, sales gets a queue. For stores, the campaign team sends a weekly list to regional managers. The list is not always acted on, so we need a better definition of what “qualified” means.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Who owns that definition today?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): It depends. Sales says a form fill is enough; store teams want purchase intent; marketing wants engagement. We have not agreed on one threshold.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Then technology alone will not solve the handoff. We should facilitate a working session with those groups and define stages, required fields, and response timing.

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Please include operations. They are the ones who discover the missing fields after launch.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Absolutely. What would success look like six months after implementation?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Campaign build time down by a third, reporting available within a day, and fewer duplicate sends. I would also like regional teams to launch approved local campaigns without opening a ticket every time.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How would you measure duplicate sends?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): A monthly audit of recipients, campaign IDs, and consent status. We currently do that manually for a sample, which is not enough.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can propose an audit view, but we should confirm the exact fields and whether store identifiers are consistent. What systems need to connect?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Our commerce platform, loyalty database, CRM, and the analytics warehouse. The warehouse is the most reliable for revenue, but it updates overnight.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): If overnight revenue is the constraint, we should not promise real-time revenue-triggered journeys without an architecture change. We could use near-real-time behavioral events for messaging and use the warehouse for validated reporting.

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): That distinction matters. The board wants accurate revenue numbers, while campaign managers want timely signals. They are not the same requirement.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Exactly. What concerns would block expansion of the existing program?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Governance, training, and the risk of creating a central team bottleneck. Also, the previous rollout succeeded partly because one regional lead was unusually persistent. We cannot assume every region has that person.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We should design ownership and enablement so the process does not depend on a hero. A pilot with one national and two local teams could test that.

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): That is sensible. Let us include a region with strong adoption and one that is less mature.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Good comparison. I will send a discovery summary with the reporting definitions, consent and overlap problem, handoff workshop, journey design principles, and pilot structure. What should happen next?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Bring our CRM lead and analytics owner to a technical workshop. I will invite the regional leads after we agree on the pilot.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will propose two times and list the decisions needed. We have interest, but the operating model needs to be clear before we widen the rollout.

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): That is where we are. The technology is only useful if the teams can govern it.
