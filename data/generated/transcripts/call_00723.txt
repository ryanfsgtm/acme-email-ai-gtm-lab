Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): When you say inconsistency, where does it show up most visibly?

Jordon Heidenreich (VP Marketing): Campaign follow-up. A subscriber may download a report, attend a webinar, and talk to sales, but those interactions sit in different places. Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Are those brands operating from a shared CRM?

Jordon Heidenreich (VP Marketing): Mostly. “Mostly” is doing a lot of work there. The advertising division has an older database, the subscription team has a newer CRM, and our event platform is separate. We are also acquiring a small publication, so the map keeps changing.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That makes an integration-first discovery important. Which system should be considered authoritative for identity and consent?

Jordon Heidenreich (VP Marketing): Consent is supposed to be centralized, but the practical answer is not one system. Germany and Austria are particularly sensitive. Some local teams maintain their own records because they do not trust the central process to preserve the right language and purpose.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What happens when a person changes their preference?

Jordon Heidenreich (VP Marketing): Ideally it propagates everywhere. In reality, an email may stop in one system and continue in another if the synchronization fails. We have not had a major incident, but I do not like relying on luck.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): How are teams notified about those failures?

Jordon Heidenreich (VP Marketing): That is part of the issue. There are logs, but nobody owns them every morning. The technical team will say the job completed, and marketing will say the audience still looked wrong.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): So an important success criterion may be operational visibility, not only building journeys.

Jordon Heidenreich (VP Marketing): Exactly. Our vendors all show beautiful journey diagrams. The hard question is who notices when a condition is wrong at 7:30 on a Tuesday.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): How many people would build or approve campaigns?

Jordon Heidenreich (VP Marketing): Perhaps twenty-five across the group, though only six are central marketing. Local editors need some autonomy, but brand and legal approvals must be consistent.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Would you prefer one global workspace with permissions, or separate environments by brand?

Jordon Heidenreich (VP Marketing): I do not know yet. Separate environments reduce accidental mixing, but they also create duplicate audiences and reporting. We are trying to avoid another tool that becomes a collection of little islands.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What does leadership measure today?

Jordon Heidenreich (VP Marketing): Revenue attribution is the headline, although it is not very trustworthy. We also watch subscription conversion, event attendance, engagement by publication, and opt-out rates. The teams argue about definitions more than results.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Would a common lifecycle model help?

Jordon Heidenreich (VP Marketing): Maybe. A subscriber is not necessarily a prospect, and an advertising contact may be valuable without ever buying a subscription. We need the model to reflect media economics, not force everyone into a SaaS funnel.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That is a useful constraint. We can discuss multiple relationship types and event-based audiences instead of a single linear stage.

Jordon Heidenreich (VP Marketing): Please do. Also, do not assume every customer wants personalization. Some readers prefer a simple weekly digest and consider behavioral targeting creepy.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): How do you decide the boundary?

Jordon Heidenreich (VP Marketing): Purpose and expectation. If someone registered for a logistics webinar, an invitation to the next logistics webinar is reasonable. If we infer their political interests because they read three articles, that is not acceptable.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Would your legal team define prohibited attributes and approved use cases?

Jordon Heidenreich (VP Marketing): They should, but legal is already overloaded. We need tools that make the approved path easy, not a system that expects every editor to be a privacy lawyer.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Understood. What is the current campaign production process?

Jordon Heidenreich (VP Marketing): Brief, copy, translation, legal check, build, test, send. For urgent news events, the process compresses. We sometimes skip the formal brief, which is when inconsistent targeting appears.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Can you give me an example of a campaign you wish had been automated?

Jordon Heidenreich (VP Marketing): A conference attendee sequence. Registration confirmation was timely, but the reminder list included people who had cancelled because the event platform export was stale. Then the follow-up went to attendees and no-shows together. Nobody died, but it looked amateurish.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What would the ideal sequence do?

Jordon Heidenreich (VP Marketing): Confirm registration, update on cancellation, send a reminder based on actual attendance status, and offer different follow-up content. It should stop if a sales conversation is already active.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): How important is speed to you?

Jordon Heidenreich (VP Marketing): Important, but not the only thing. We have an incumbent platform that could probably send a basic nurture tomorrow. The value would be reliable orchestration across brands, which takes longer.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Who else would need to participate in evaluating that?

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What would make you decide not to proceed?
