Evelyn Bartell (IT): CRM, website forms, email engagement, and probably the ERP for customer and product information. The ERP is sensitive, and I would not want a broad replication of it.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can begin with a limited field set and avoid copying unnecessary records. What does the current integration architecture look like?

Evelyn Bartell (IT): Some APIs, some scheduled files, and a few old scripts. It is documented, but not beautifully. There is also a plant-level distinction in the customer data that marketing does not always understand.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What kind of distinction?

Evelyn Bartell (IT): A global account may have several plants, distributors, or service relationships. A campaign can be relevant to one facility but not another. If we treat the account as a single flat record, the targeting will be wrong.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That sounds like an important modeling question. How does marketing handle it today?

Evelyn Bartell (IT): They ask sales for lists. Sometimes a salesperson knows the local context; sometimes the list is based on a broad industry filter. It is not elegant.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What journeys are on the near-term roadmap?

Evelyn Bartell (IT): New product education, event follow-up, and service reminders. Different stakeholders own them. Marketing owns the first two; customer service has strong opinions about reminders.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Do executives currently ask for attribution?

Evelyn Bartell (IT): Yes, but the request is vague. They want to know what worked. A channel report is not enough, but they have not agreed on a model. Sales operations wants campaign influence; finance wants a connection to booked revenue.
