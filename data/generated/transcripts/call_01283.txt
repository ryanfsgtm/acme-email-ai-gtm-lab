Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Augustus, thank you for taking the call. Before we get into software, could you give me the short version of what prompted the search?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): The short version is that the team is spending too much time copying lists between systems. The longer version is that nobody agrees on which list is the right one. We are a small manufacturing business, forty-four employees, and the marketing function is essentially two people plus borrowed help.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What happens when a campaign needs to go out?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): Someone exports contacts from the CRM, checks a spreadsheet, removes a few names manually, and uploads the file. Then sales says three contacts should not have been included, or a distributor was missed. It is not catastrophic, but it is embarrassing and slow.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Is the primary goal campaign execution, lead management, or reporting?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): Campaign execution first. Reporting is a close second because management keeps asking which trade shows produce inquiries. Right now the answer is based on whoever remembers the source.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Do you have a defined lifecycle today?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): Not really. We have “new,” “contacted,” and “customer,” plus a few custom labels. Different people use them differently. I would not want to pretend there is a clean process waiting to be automated.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That is helpful. Automation works best when we start with a small agreed process. What would be the first journey?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): A follow-up for people who request a technical brochure, then a separate path for trade-show leads. The audience is not huge. Maybe a few hundred usable contacts, not thousands.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): And who approves messaging?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): The marketing manager drafts it, the managing director wants visibility, and sales occasionally rewrites the call to action at the last minute. You can hear the gears grinding already.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): I can. How much time could the team realistically spend on implementation each week?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): Two or three hours, maybe. During the busy season, less. We need something that does not assume a dedicated administrator.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We could phase this: clean the core contacts, agree on consent and source fields, build two simple journeys, and train one owner. Advanced scoring can wait. Would that fit?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): It sounds closer. My concern is that vendors hear “small team” and give us a miniature version of an enterprise project. We cannot run a six-week committee process.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Understood. What systems need to connect?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): The CRM, the website form, and our email tool. We also have an accounting package, but that is outside marketing. We do not need every machine in the building connected on day one.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What data fields are essential?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): Name, company, industry, product interest, consent, and the person who owns the follow-up. We have too many optional fields that nobody fills in.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We can make the minimum fields explicit and use progressive profiling later. How would you know the project succeeded after ninety days?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): The team stops doing manual uploads for the two main campaigns. We can see the source of an inquiry without opening three spreadsheets. Sales gets a notification with enough context to have a useful conversation.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Would response time be measurable?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): Yes, if the owner field is accurate. At the moment, a notification can go to a shared inbox and disappear into the fog.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We should include an assignment rule and an exception report. Do you have regional or language requirements?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): Mostly United Kingdom customers, English only for now. Some distributors operate internationally, but that is not a separate program yet.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Any compliance constraints?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): Consent has to be clear, and we need to honor unsubscribes. We do not have a large legal department, so the controls need to be understandable by a normal person.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That is a good design principle. What alternatives are you considering?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): We have not selected a competitor. The finance director has asked whether we can solve this with the tools we already own. There is pressure to avoid another subscription.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): If the existing tools could handle the workflow cleanly, would you prefer that?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): Probably. But we have tried assembling pieces before. The problem is not that each tool is useless. It is that the handoffs depend on memory.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): So the value is in a shared operating process, not just sending email.

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): Exactly. I need to be careful, though: a nicer process will not create demand by itself. Management may expect magic because the word “automation” sounds expensive and impressive.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That expectation is worth resetting. We can improve consistency and measurement, but the content and follow-up still matter. What would cause the project to stall?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): If setup requires too much data cleanup, or if the marketing manager cannot make routine changes without calling someone. Also if the monthly cost is out of proportion to the audience.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): I will prepare a scoped option with implementation hours separated from subscription cost. We can show the first two journeys and a simple reporting view. Would you want your managing director in the next conversation?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): Yes, but not for a feature tour. Show the before-and-after workflow and the amount of manual work removed.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Agreed. One last question: is this urgent because of a specific event?

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): The autumn trade shows. If we wait until after those, we will repeat the same spreadsheet routine. But the final decision is not mine, and there is a chance management decides to defer.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): I will capture that as a timing risk, not an assumed commitment. Thank you, Augustus.

Augustus Kirlin (Implementation Partner): Thanks. The next step should be practical, or I will lose the room before the first slide.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Then I will bring fewer slides and more workflow.
