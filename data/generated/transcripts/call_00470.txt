Priya Shah (Account Executive): Lilliana, thanks for joining. I understand you lead demand generation for Beahan - Predovic’s manufacturing business in the UK. Before we talk about a platform, what prompted the review now?

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): Two things collided. We’re expanding the product portfolio, and our regional teams are running campaigns in different tools. The enterprise team has one process, the plants have another, and nobody trusts the global funnel report.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): When you say nobody trusts it, is that a data problem, a definition problem, or both?

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): Both, mostly. A distributor can be counted as a lead in one region and an account relationship in another. A form submission can be an engineer looking for a specification, not a buying signal. Then the dashboard calls everything “pipeline influenced.”

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That distinction matters. What would the demand-generation team want the system to do differently?

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): Give us a common operating model with local control. We need global campaign standards, but our UK, German, and North American teams can’t wait three weeks for headquarters to change a subject line or audience.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How would you separate those responsibilities?

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): Central owns taxonomy, consent policy, core lifecycle definitions, and reporting. Regions own content variants, event timing, and local suppression decisions within those guardrails.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds like a federated model. What happens today when a regional team creates a new field or scoring rule?

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): It appears in the CRM, then somebody discovers it during a quarterly audit. We have around 44,650 employees globally, but the marketing operations team is not sized like that. We need change control without making every change a committee meeting.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can explore approval tiers: governed changes for lifecycle and consent, delegated changes for content and regional audiences, and an audit trail for both. I’d want to understand which systems are authoritative before saying how much can be automated.

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): CRM is authoritative for account ownership. Our web platform is authoritative for form submissions. The event system is authoritative for attendance, although its API sometimes drops a field. Product telemetry belongs to the digital team and should not automatically become marketing data.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Good boundaries. What are the important buyer journeys?

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): New equipment inquiries, retrofit opportunities, and service renewals. They have different sales cycles. An equipment request can take months, while a service renewal may be an existing commercial relationship.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would you score them in one model or maintain separate models?

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): Separate models with a common reporting layer. If you flatten everything into one score, the renewal team gets noise from top-of-funnel content and the equipment team misses account context.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a useful counterpoint to a single global score. How does sales want handoffs represented?

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): They want an explicit reason, recent activity, account status, and recommended next action. They do not want a mysterious alert saying “hot lead.”

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Could a handoff require fit plus a defined intent event, rather than activity alone?

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): Yes, for most segments. But there are exceptions. A named strategic account may deserve human review after a single executive event, even if the contact’s digital activity is light.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): So the model needs both automated qualification and account-level overlays.

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): Correct. And please don’t describe that as replacing judgment. Our best opportunities are sometimes discovered through field sales conversations that never show up as clicks.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. How are those conversations captured today?

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): In notes, email, and sometimes a spreadsheet. It’s inconsistent, but forcing every rep to complete twenty fields would reduce adoption.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We should identify the minimum required context and let the rest remain optional. What would you measure in a pilot?

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): Acceptance rate by region, time from qualification to first sales action, duplicate rate, campaign-to-opportunity conversion, and the percentage of records that sales sends back as not relevant.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would you compare the automated process with a control group or historical baseline?

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): Both if possible. Historical baseline tells us whether the funnel improved; a control group tells us whether seasonality or a product launch caused the change.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can design that with your analytics team. We should also agree on exclusions: existing service customers, students or job applicants if they enter the same web properties, unsubscribed contacts, and distributor records that are not direct prospects.

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): Add suppliers and employees. Our public forms get surprising traffic. A global nurture that includes an employee because they downloaded a product sheet would be embarrassing.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Absolutely. What’s the current consent structure across regions?

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): It varies. The UK team has clear topic subscriptions. Some other regions use a broader marketing permission with local wording. Central wants a common taxonomy, but legal has warned us not to erase the source language.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then we should preserve source and map to a normalized reporting category without pretending the original permissions were identical.

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): Yes. That’s a key requirement. We had a previous vendor migration where “marketing consent” was imported as a simple yes/no and we spent months reconstructing what it meant.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We’ll put consent mapping and evidence retention into discovery. What internal resources are available?

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): Two marketing-operations analysts, a CRM architect part-time, regional campaign owners, and legal for scheduled reviews. IT can support integration, but not an indefinite stream of custom tickets.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then maintainability should be a selection criterion, not a promise at the end. How much autonomy should regional users have during the first release?

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): Start with UK and Germany, but build the model so North America can adopt it later. We want to prove governance in a contained scope, not design every global exception up front.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would make leadership call the pilot successful?

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): A more credible funnel, fewer manual reconciliation meetings, and sales saying the handoff context is actually useful. If the dashboard is prettier but the data is still disputed, we’ve failed.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That’s a clear outcome. I’ll propose discovery workstreams for data ownership, lifecycle definitions, consent mapping, account overlays, pilot journeys, and measurement. We can then compare the approach with Eloqua on governance and operational effort, not only feature checklists.

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): Good. Eloqua is familiar here, so migration risk will come up. We also need an honest view of what doesn’t fit.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I’ll include open risks and dependencies. No platform removes the need for agreed definitions or clean ownership.

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): That’s probably the most important thing said today.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I’ll send the discovery outline and a data-inventory template. Who should review first?

Lilliana Frami (Demand Generation): Me and the CRM architect. Then I’ll bring in regional owners and legal once we have the first draft.
