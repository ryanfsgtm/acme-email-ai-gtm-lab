Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Kiana, thanks for joining. Before we get into systems, how are things in the plant this week?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): Loud, mostly. We have a production launch and a demand-gen launch competing for the same calendar, so I am hoping this conversation helps us remove a few manual steps.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a good place to start. What is currently consuming the most time?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): Segmenting audiences across Germany, Austria, and Switzerland. We have different language, consent, and distributor rules. The team exports lists, checks them, then imports them into the sending tool. By the time it is ready, the campaign window has moved.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Is the main source your CRM?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): The CRM, product-registration data, and a distributor file. The distributor file is the least trustworthy, but commercially it is important.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What makes it least trustworthy?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): Missing country codes, duplicate contacts, old job titles. Not malicious; just maintained by several people with different ideas about what “complete” means.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can establish validation rules, duplicate review, and source-specific permissions. How do you decide who can receive a campaign?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): It depends on product interest, country, language, consent basis, and whether they are already assigned to a sales owner. Some of that is in fields, some is in notes, and some exists only in a spreadsheet.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Which criteria are non-negotiable?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): Consent and country first. Then language. Sales ownership matters because a campaign can conflict with an active opportunity.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can make those first conditions mandatory and use ownership as an exclusion or review rule. Would marketing own the final send approval?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): Marketing should, but sales wants veto power for strategic accounts. We have not solved that governance question.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): A staged approval could work: marketing prepares the audience, account owners review only their assigned strategic contacts, then marketing sends. It adds a step, so we should measure whether it is worth it.

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): Exactly. I do not want automation that simply moves the bottleneck.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How many people would use the system initially?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): About forty across demand generation, regional marketing, and selected sales operations. The broader company is much larger, but most employees should not touch campaign data.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That aligns with role-based access. Are there regional hosting or data-processing requirements?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): Our legal team is reviewing those. We need clear answers for DACH, especially around processors and deletion. I do not want the evaluation to stall because we discovered a missing document at the end.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can bring the privacy and security materials into the evaluation now. What does success look like six months after launch?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): Campaign preparation cut from several days to same-day for standard sends, fewer duplicates, and a reliable report showing why a contact was excluded. The last part is underrated.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Why is it important?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): When sales asks why a customer did not receive an invitation, we currently inspect three systems and shrug. If the answer is “country restriction” or “consent missing,” we can act. If the answer is “someone used an old spreadsheet,” we cannot.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): ACME records audience criteria and activity history, so an operator can inspect the decision path. We should verify which details are retained for your compliance policy.

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): Good. Please do not promise every internal decision is magically explainable.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Fair. Let us talk integrations. Which CRM do you use?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): Salesforce. Product registration is in a separate manufacturing application. The distributor file arrives as a spreadsheet every month.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We could begin with Salesforce and the monthly file, then assess the registration integration. Would a manual upload with validation be acceptable for phase one?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): Yes, if the upload is controlled and the source is visible. We do not need a grand integration diagram on day one.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That could reduce launch risk. What about templates and localization?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): German and English first. French later. We need regional reviewers to edit copy without changing the audience logic.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can separate content permissions from data and audience permissions. Would regional teams approve their own translations?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): Usually. But central marketing owns brand language. Again, governance, not software, is the messy bit.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can document the intended operating model and configure around it. Are there reporting metrics beyond send and open rates?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): Pipeline influence, meeting creation, and engagement by product family. I know attribution is imperfect. We need directional evidence, not a fantasy of causality.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a healthy expectation. We can agree on definitions before dashboards are built.

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): I also want to avoid scoring every touch as a sales signal. The field team already distrusts that.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Then scoring should be tested against outcomes and adjusted, not treated as a truth machine.

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): Good. What would implementation require from us?

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): A data owner, a privacy reviewer, sample exports, consent definitions, and two regional marketers for workflow testing. We would propose a pilot with one product family and two countries.

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Account Executive): Germany and Switzerland, probably. Austria has a distributor complication we need to untangle.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a manageable boundary. What could stop the project?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Account Executive): If the migration requires perfect historical data, if regional teams lose control, or if legal answers arrive too late. Budget is a consideration, but the manual cost is visible.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will send a pilot outline, data assumptions, and the compliance packet. Can we schedule a working session with sales operations next week?

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): Yes. Send the outline first. If it says “all data sources integrated in phase one,” I will return it with a red pen.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Noted. Phase one will be Salesforce plus controlled distributor upload, with registration data as a later decision.

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): Then we have a useful path. I am interested, but the details will decide it.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is exactly what the next session is for. Thanks, Kiana.

Kiana Fisher-Bailey (Demand Generation): Thanks, Amelia. I have to get back to the production launch before it gets louder.
