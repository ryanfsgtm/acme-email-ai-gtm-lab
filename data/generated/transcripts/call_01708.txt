Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Kasandra, hello. Is the audio okay?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): It is now. My laptop decided to update five minutes before the meeting, so I’m working from a borrowed headset.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): A familiar kind of excitement. How is your morning in Lyon?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): Rainy and full of spreadsheets. We’re a small education provider, but our reporting has become surprisingly complicated.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That’s a good place to start. What led you to evaluate marketing automation?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): We run courses for universities and professional learners. Leads come from events, partner referrals, paid search, and a few email lists. We can send newsletters, but follow-up is mostly manual.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What does manual follow-up look like?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): Marketing exports a list, sales filters it, and then someone remembers to create tasks. Sometimes a prospect downloads a curriculum guide and hears from us two weeks later. Sometimes nobody knows who owns the lead.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Where is the lead record maintained?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): Our CRM, mostly. There are duplicates, though, especially when someone registers for an event with a different email address. The school team also keeps a local spreadsheet.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would deduplication and routing be part of the project?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): They should be. But we need to be realistic. I’m one person, and our CRM administrator is shared with finance. We cannot spend six months cleaning everything before seeing a result.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can begin with a defined segment and improve the model over time. Which segment matters most?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): Professional learners. Their enrollment cycle is shorter, and the commercial team wants reminders based on course interest. University procurement is slower and more relationship-driven.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would a useful journey do for professional learners?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): If someone attends a webinar on project management, send a recap, wait a few days, offer the relevant course, and alert an advisor if they engage. We also need different treatment for people who already enrolled.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How is enrollment status updated today?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): Nightly from the student system, but the field names are inconsistent. “Active,” “paid,” and “registered” are used by different teams.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That sounds like a data-mapping exercise before automation can be trusted.

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): Yes, and that is the part people underestimate. The demo always looks easy until you ask which system owns the truth.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Which system should own consent?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): The CRM for marketing preferences, with the student system supplying enrollment suppression. If someone enrolls, they should not continue receiving a promotional sequence for that same course.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. How do you measure campaign success?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): The marketing team reports opens and clicks. Leadership asks about enrollments and revenue. Sales asks whether the lead was qualified. Those numbers do not currently line up.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would you want dashboards for leadership and operational queues for advisors?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): Exactly. Leadership needs a clean view, while the team needs to know what to do next. We also need to distinguish assisted enrollment from a direct conversion.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can discuss attribution models, but I want to avoid promising a perfect answer before we see the data.

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): Thank you. We have been promised “single source of truth” before, and it usually means another report.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What other tools are you considering?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): Mailchimp is familiar and inexpensive. The issue is whether it can handle branching workflows and CRM handoffs without custom work. We are not looking for a huge enterprise suite.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What budget range has been discussed?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): Somewhere around fifteen to twenty thousand annually, depending on implementation. The close date in the system says March 28, but no one has committed to that date.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Who will make the decision?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): Our commercial director, with input from finance and me. Marketing owns the campaign requirements, but Revenue Operations owns the integration risk.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Could we schedule a working session with marketing and the CRM administrator?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): Yes, but not this week. We have an enrollment deadline. Next Tuesday afternoon might work.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I’ll propose Tuesday. What should we use as the working example?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): The webinar-to-course journey. Please include a duplicate record, an enrolled learner, and someone who unsubscribed. If the workflow handles those cases, people will pay attention.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a better test than a polished happy path. Do you have sample data we can use?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): Anonymized records, yes. I’ll ask IT to create a small export. They may object to sending it outside the company, so I’ll confirm the process.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can also use fabricated examples if that is easier for the first session.

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): Good. One concern: the sales team does not want every click to create a task. They already have too many notifications.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can define thresholds and only create tasks for meaningful activity.

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): And can advisors pause a journey manually? A learner may call us and ask for a different start date.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Yes, with appropriate permissions and audit history. We should confirm how that status is written back to the CRM.

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): Please do. That is another detail that sounds small but becomes important.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I’ll include it in the agenda. Anything that would cause you to stop evaluating?

Kasandra Fritsch (Revenue Operations): If implementation requires a developer for every change, or if reporting cannot reconcile to enrollment. Also if pricing jumps because we need several workspaces.
