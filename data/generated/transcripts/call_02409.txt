Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Gaetano, thanks for taking the call. I see McKenzie Inc is an education organization in France with roughly one hundred eleven employees. Before we discuss any particular solution, I would like to understand how demand generation works today and where the team is under pressure.

Gaetano Erdman (Demand Generation): That is a good starting point. We sell training programs to schools and professional learners, and the buying cycle is uneven. September is huge, summer is quiet, and then a government tender can appear with almost no warning.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How do inquiries arrive?

Gaetano Erdman (Demand Generation): Website forms, webinars, partner referrals, and phone calls to regional offices. The phone calls are the awkward bit because they often never become records. We know demand exists, but we cannot always trace it.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What happens after someone fills out a form?

Gaetano Erdman (Demand Generation): The central team sends an acknowledgment. If the person asks for a course brochure, someone usually follows up within two days. Usually. During enrollment season, it can be a week. The regional teams have their own priorities and do not always see the same information.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Is the main issue speed, visibility, or consistency?

Gaetano Erdman (Demand Generation): All three, though consistency is probably the root. We have different definitions of a qualified inquiry. One region calls a school administrator a lead; another calls it an opportunity only after a meeting. Reporting then becomes an argument about vocabulary.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Who owns those definitions?

Gaetano Erdman (Demand Generation): Marketing proposes them, sales directors negotiate them, and nobody enjoys maintaining them. Our director is supportive, but she wants evidence before approving a large process change.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What evidence would convince her?

Gaetano Erdman (Demand Generation): A small pilot around one program. We could compare response times and enrollment inquiries before and after. I would not promise revenue attribution immediately because our courses are often purchased offline.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is reasonable. Which program has enough volume for a useful pilot?

Gaetano Erdman (Demand Generation): The continuing-education catalog. It has recurring webinars and downloadable guides. The school partnerships are more strategic and have too few records for a clean test.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What tools are involved today?

Gaetano Erdman (Demand Generation): A CRM, an email platform, web analytics, and shared drives. I know everyone says “integrate everything,” but the CRM has years of inconsistent fields. We need to decide what not to bring forward.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How much of your week goes into preparing lists and reports?

Gaetano Erdman (Demand Generation): Two or three days in a normal month, more during enrollment. The reports are manually reconciled. I can produce a number, but I cannot always explain why it changed.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): If the pilot succeeded, what would change for you personally?

Gaetano Erdman (Demand Generation): I would spend less time checking whether a webinar attendee got a follow-up and more time improving the webinar. It would also give the regional teams a common queue instead of five inboxes.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What could prevent the pilot?

Gaetano Erdman (Demand Generation): Privacy review, mostly. We handle learner information, and some programs involve younger participants. Marketing contacts are not all the same risk category. Our legal team will want a clear retention and consent story.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Would legal need to approve the process before technical work begins?

Gaetano Erdman (Demand Generation): They can review in parallel, but they will not approve a vague diagram. We need to show where data enters, who can see it, how consent is recorded, and how someone is removed.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Good detail. Who else should be in a follow-up session?

Gaetano Erdman (Demand Generation): The CRM administrator, a regional sales director, and our privacy manager. Maybe the enrollment operations lead. If all of them attend, we will need a miracle and a larger calendar.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can make the first session forty-five minutes and send questions ahead. Would you prefer a workflow mapping session or a product demonstration?

Gaetano Erdman (Demand Generation): Workflow mapping first. A demo without our definitions will just make everyone enthusiastic about buttons.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Fair point. We can document the continuing-education inquiry path, identify the minimum fields, and define a baseline for response time. Then we can determine whether the platform fits.

Gaetano Erdman (Demand Generation): That is the right order. Also, French-language support matters. Our internal team can work in English, but the public messages must be reviewed in French.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can include bilingual templates in the discussion, with your team owning final wording. I do not want to imply automatic translation is a substitute for review.

Gaetano Erdman (Demand Generation): Good. We have had machine-translated course names that sounded ridiculous.

Gaetano Erdman (Demand Generation): Yes. Add that the director wants a baseline before she approves anything. And I need to check whether the enrollment season gives us enough time for a meaningful comparison.
