Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Hi Lacy, can you hear me? You froze for a second.

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): I can hear you now. The joys of July video calls. I’m in Lyon, and apparently the Wi-Fi has chosen a slower life.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): No worries. I’ll keep this fairly tight. I understand Walker, Swaniawski and Heathcote is looking at marketing automation for lifecycle programmes. What prompted the search?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): We’ve outgrown our current setup. We’re a retail business with a fairly broad customer base, and our emails are still too manual. A campaign manager builds a list, sends a batch, then someone checks the numbers in three different places.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What does the ideal process look like?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): Triggered journeys. Welcome series, abandoned basket, post-purchase education, maybe win-back. The basics, honestly. The problem is that each one currently depends on a spreadsheet and a reminder in somebody’s calendar.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How are customers getting into those programmes today?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): Our commerce platform has the order data, the CRM has customer details, and our email tool has subscriptions. They do not agree on fields. One system calls it “consent,” another calls it “marketing permission,” and a third has a checkbox nobody trusts.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That sounds like a data-model issue as much as a campaign issue. How many active contacts are you managing?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): Roughly one hundred and fifty thousand marketable contacts, but it varies. We also have dormant records and store-only customers. I don’t want to pay to activate every historic record immediately.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That makes sense. We can begin with the active segment and expand as the use cases prove themselves. Which journey would you prioritise?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): Post-purchase. It’s the easiest to explain internally and it should help with repeat purchase. We sell products that need a little guidance, so the first few messages should be genuinely useful, not just another discount.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What kind of guidance?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): Setup tips, care instructions, complementary products, and a reminder when replacement might be due. Timing depends on the product category. A new customer buying a lamp is different from someone buying a seasonal item.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would those rules come from order attributes?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): Yes, ideally. Today a merchandiser exports orders by category every Monday. If she’s away, the journey waits. That is why our current process feels fragile.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): And what would success look like after the first quarter?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): Less manual work, obviously, but we need measurable outcomes. Repeat purchase rate, unsubscribes, conversion from the educational series, and whether store and online customers behave differently.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Do you have a baseline for those metrics?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): For repeat purchase, approximately twelve percent in the relevant ninety-day window. I’m not sure that number is perfectly clean because returns are handled separately. Unsubscribe is about three-tenths of a percent per send, depending on the promotion.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That baseline is enough to start, provided we document definitions. What has been working in the current tool?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): The simple newsletters work. The team knows how to use them, and our creative templates are good. I don’t want a platform that gives us a powerful canvas but makes every small change require a developer.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How technical is the team?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): Two lifecycle marketers, one CRM manager, and a part-time analyst. We can manage logic and fields, but not maintain custom code every week.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That’s helpful. Would you need a visual journey builder with reusable templates?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): Yes, and previews. We have had embarrassing cases where a personalised product name was blank and the email displayed punctuation instead. So testing is not a nice-to-have.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. Do you currently run holdout tests?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): Occasionally. Mostly subject lines. We’ve tried broader tests, but the audience selection was manual and the result was not statistically useful. We need a cleaner way to define a control group.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That could be part of the evaluation. Are there any compliance requirements we should know about?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): France is our main operating market for this team, but we also serve neighbouring countries. Consent and suppression need to be reliable. We don’t want a loyalty member receiving marketing after opting out because one source did not update.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How quickly should an opt-out propagate across systems?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): As close to immediate as possible. I’m not asking for magic, but a delay of several days would be unacceptable.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We’ll include that in the technical discovery. What integrations are essential for a first phase?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): Commerce orders, customer profile, and subscription status. Product catalogue would be useful but maybe not mandatory on day one. We are comparing you with Braze, and they have more than enough capabilities. Our concern is implementation effort.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): So the decision is less about whether the platform can do everything and more about whether your team can operate it consistently.

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): Exactly. A feature list will not win this for us. If the first journey takes four months, everyone will conclude automation is too complicated.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What is your target timeline?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): We would like a recommendation in August and a pilot before the holiday planning cycle. I’m not promising production launch before then, because merchandising will want to approve every message.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That approval process is worth designing around. How involved is IT?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): They’ll support the connections and security review, but they will not own campaign operations. Marketing has to be able to make changes without opening a ticket.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I can arrange a working session with a solutions specialist focused on the post-purchase journey, consent flow, and testing. Would you bring the CRM manager?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): Yes. Also our analyst, if she is not travelling. She will challenge every metric definition, which is healthy, though not always quick.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Healthy and slightly dangerous—fair enough. Before we wrap, is there anything that would make you stop the evaluation?

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): If you cannot handle our regional consent rules, if the integration requires a custom project before we see value, or if pricing is based on every historical record. Also, I need confidence that store-only customers can be excluded or treated differently.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll capture those as decision criteria. I’ll send a short summary and propose the working session. We can use your post-purchase example as the centre of the demo rather than walking through unrelated features.

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): Good. And please send the technical requirements in plain language. Our IT team will ignore a forty-page brochure.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Plain language it is. Thanks for the time, Lacy.

Lacy Marks (Lifecycle Marketing): Thank you. Hopefully next time the Wi-Fi behaves.
