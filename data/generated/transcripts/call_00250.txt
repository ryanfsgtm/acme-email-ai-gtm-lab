Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Good morning, Emery. Thanks for speaking with me. Before we get into the platform, how is your week going?

Emery Huels (Privacy): Busy, and probably about to get busier. I’m joining because privacy needs to understand the proposed use cases before Marketing starts discussing implementation.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That makes sense. I have Muller LLC as a 1,342-employee manufacturing company in the DACH region, and you’re leading the privacy review. The opportunity is for marketing automation at €84,295, with Eloqua listed as an alternative. Is that the right context?

Emery Huels (Privacy): Yes, although I’m not the purchasing owner. I need to establish whether the use is acceptable and what controls would be required.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. What prompted Muller to look at a new system?

Emery Huels (Privacy): Marketing wants a common platform across several business units. They currently export lists from different systems, and nobody has a complete picture of consent, retention, or who can access what.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): So the problem is fragmented governance as much as campaign execution.

Emery Huels (Privacy): Correct. Better automation could improve control, but adding another platform could also create another copy of personal data.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That’s an important risk. Which data categories are being considered?

Emery Huels (Privacy): Business contact details, engagement history, event attendance, and inferred interests. In some markets, there are distributor contacts and technical users. We are not planning to put employee records or sensitive health information into the platform.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can discuss field-level minimization and exclude categories that do not support a defined purpose. Are the contacts all existing relationships, or do you also collect leads through public forms and events?

Emery Huels (Privacy): Both. Events are particularly messy. A person may attend a trade show, scan a badge, receive a follow-up, and then appear in a regional list without a clear record of what they agreed to.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would a consent-status requirement on campaign entry address part of that issue?

Emery Huels (Privacy): It could address sending, but not necessarily collection. I don’t want the system to make a questionable record look compliant merely because a field says “yes.”

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Agreed. The source and timestamp should be retained, and the capture language needs to be reviewed separately. We can help map those fields, but your legal interpretation should govern.

Emery Huels (Privacy): Good. What happens when someone withdraws consent? I need suppression to be immediate enough for ordinary campaign operations, and I need evidence that the suppression occurred.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): The platform can update subscription status and exclude the person from eligible sends. It also records changes in an audit log. I need to confirm the exact synchronization interval with your source CRM before promising “immediate.”

Emery Huels (Privacy): Please confirm it. “Near real time” is usually marketing language.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Fair criticism. We’ll document the measured or contractual interval.

Emery Huels (Privacy): Cross-border access is another concern. Muller is in DACH, but our support and marketing teams include people outside the EEA. Where is the data hosted, and who can access it?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I don’t want to guess on hosting from memory. I’ll bring in our security specialist with the regional hosting documentation, subprocessors list, and transfer mechanisms. Role-based access can restrict users by team, but hosting and international access are separate questions.

Emery Huels (Privacy): Exactly. Also, role-based access does not solve excessive retention.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What retention periods are you considering?

Emery Huels (Privacy): Marketing has not settled them. We may need different periods for active customers, prospects, event attendees, and people who have opted out. The platform should support deletion or anonymization without breaking aggregate reporting.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can explore retention policies and whether reporting can use aggregated records after deletion. That may require a design decision about which identifiers are truly necessary.

Emery Huels (Privacy): That is the kind of conversation I need. Eloqua already has controls, so the new platform cannot be evaluated only on ease of use.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would a successful privacy review look like?

Emery Huels (Privacy): A documented data-flow diagram, processing purposes, access roles, retention behavior, subprocessors, transfer safeguards, and a test of consent withdrawal. I also need clarity on whether the vendor uses customer data for product training or other secondary purposes.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll include that as a specific contractual question. We can schedule a technical workshop with your security and legal colleagues.

Emery Huels (Privacy): We are not ready to schedule implementation. First I need the documentation, and Marketing needs to define the use cases. They currently say “personalization,” which is too broad.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would it help if we separated the use cases into event follow-up, customer education, and distributor communication, then assessed each one?

Emery Huels (Privacy): Yes. That would expose whether the same data and legal basis are being assumed across unrelated activities.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll send a discovery worksheet with those categories and leave the legal-basis fields for your team to complete.

Emery Huels (Privacy): Good. And please do not mark this as privacy-approved because we had an introductory call.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll record it as discovery with privacy requirements identified. No approval implied.

Emery Huels (Privacy): Then we have a useful next step. Send the data-flow and hosting materials first. After I review them, I’ll tell you whether a technical validation is worthwhile.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll do that by Friday, along with the consent and retention questions. Thanks for the candid discussion, Emery.

Emery Huels (Privacy): Thank you. The controls may be workable, but the decision is not just about automation.
