Brooke Metz (Marketing Operations): We are a small healthcare business in France. There are 79 employees overall, but only four people touch marketing systems regularly. Brooke Metz (Marketing Operations): We cannot reliably answer simple questions. How many people attended a webinar and became an opportunity? Which nurture email influenced a request? Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds frustrating. Is the main issue reporting, execution, or data quality?

Brooke Metz (Marketing Operations): All three, although data quality is the root. We have duplicates from trade shows, incomplete job titles, and old contacts who moved companies. The sales team says marketing sends irrelevant messages. Marketing says sales does not update statuses. Both statements are true sometimes.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How are leads currently passed to sales?

Brooke Metz (Marketing Operations): Usually by email. If somebody fills out a form that looks promising, I forward the notification to a salesperson. For a campaign list, I export a CSV and ask sales to call the important names. There is no shared definition of qualified.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would a useful definition include?

Brooke Metz (Marketing Operations): Maybe role, organization type, engagement, and a request for information. But we do not want a score that turns every click into a “hot lead.” We have had that before, and people stopped trusting the alerts.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): A score should support judgment, not replace it. We could start with a few explicit signals and review the results with sales. What happens after a lead is sent over?

Brooke Metz (Marketing Operations): Sometimes the salesperson follows up. Sometimes they say it is not their territory. Sometimes the contact is already an account but the systems do not connect the person to the right account. Then the lead goes quiet.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Do you have a territory field in the CRM?

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How confident are you that opt-outs are consistently honored?

Brooke Metz (Marketing Operations): More confident than I was last year, but not completely. The email tool has its own suppression list. The CRM has a consent field. The web agency has a form setting. There are three places to look, and I am the only person who knows all three.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a key risk. What does your privacy process require for marketing consent?

Brooke Metz (Marketing Operations): Consent must be clear, recorded, and tied to the purpose. Our privacy counsel does not want us to assume that a business card at an event equals permission for every campaign. We also need a way to honor deletion requests.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would you prefer the CRM to be the source of truth, or the marketing platform?

Brooke Metz (Marketing Operations): I do not know yet. The CRM team will say CRM, marketing will say the system that actually sends the email. I suspect the answer is different for identity and consent.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Are opportunities linked to campaigns today?
