Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Hi Ike, thanks for making time. How’s the morning going?

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That combination is common. I’d like to understand your current email programme, what you want to improve, and how you’re comparing options. I understand Mailchimp is in the mix.

Ike Davis (CMO): It is. We’ve used Mailchimp for years, and nobody hates it. That’s part of the problem—we have no crisis forcing a change.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What prompted the conversation?

Ike Davis (CMO): We publish several newsletters and sell sponsorships around them. The audience is growing, but our segmentation is primitive. We know who clicked, not necessarily what they care about over time.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Can you describe the audiences?

Ike Davis (CMO): Subscribers, event attendees, podcast listeners, and commercial contacts. There’s overlap. Someone may attend an event, subscribe to the newsletter, and later download a media kit.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): And today those signals are stored separately?

Ike Davis (CMO): Mostly. Mailchimp has the newsletter activity. Event data is in a registration tool. The website has forms. We export things and reconcile them when we need a campaign.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): How often does that reconciliation happen?

Ike Davis (CMO): Before major sends, perhaps monthly. It’s not elegant. But we’re not sending millions of emails, so the manual work hasn’t become intolerable.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What would a better process enable?

Ike Davis (CMO): More relevant sponsorship offers. If someone repeatedly reads our technology coverage, we should know that when a technology advertiser asks for an audience. Also, we want to avoid promoting an event to someone who already attended it.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That sounds like a combination of behavioural scoring and suppression rules. How important is real-time data?

Ike Davis (CMO): Helpful, but not essential. Daily is probably enough. I don’t want to buy a race car for a bicycle commute.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Fair. What about reporting?

Ike Davis (CMO): We need campaign performance, audience growth, and some connection to sponsorship revenue. Attribution will be approximate; I’m not expecting magic.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): How would you evaluate a platform?

Ike Davis (CMO): Ease of use first. Then cost, deliverability, integrations, and the ability to see a person’s history without exporting three files. Mailchimp wins on familiarity. A new platform has to earn its place.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Who else would participate in a decision?

Ike Davis (CMO): Me, our editor who owns newsletters, and our finance director. The editor will care about workflow. Finance will focus on the annual number, which I believe is £9,730 for your proposal.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Correct. We’d start with the newsletter and event audiences, then add sponsorship journeys after the data model is proven.

Ike Davis (CMO): That phased approach is sensible. I don’t want a six-month implementation before anyone sees value.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What concerns would block a change?

Ike Davis (CMO): Migration risk. If we lose consent history or accidentally mail people who opted out, that’s serious. Also, the editor has limited technical patience.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We can import consent fields, preserve unsubscribe status, and run a staged validation. The editor would work in visual workflows rather than code.
