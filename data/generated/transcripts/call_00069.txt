Jayce Bogisich-Treutel (Implementation Partner): More than people expect. Corporate marketing, regional sales, distributor relations, and product specialists. Jayce Bogisich-Treutel (Implementation Partner): The CRM holds customer accounts, a separate event tool holds badge scans, and regional teams keep spreadsheets. There is also an old email platform that may still be connected to a few lists. I would not call any one system complete.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What happens after an event list arrives?

Jayce Bogisich-Treutel (Implementation Partner): Someone cleans it manually, removes obvious duplicates, and sends it to marketing. Then marketing asks sales which contacts are relevant. By that time, the event is two weeks old. Sometimes a sales rep has already called someone; sometimes the record is just parked.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Would faster routing be the main value?

Jayce Bogisich-Treutel (Implementation Partner): It would help, but speed is not the only issue. A distributor may submit a contact on behalf of an end customer. If the system sends an automated message as though the person opted in directly, we create a compliance and relationship problem.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): How is that distinction recorded today?

Jayce Bogisich-Treutel (Implementation Partner): Usually in a notes field or in the event coordinator's email. That is not a reliable control. We need a policy decision before we build workflows.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Who makes that decision?

Jayce Bogisich-Treutel (Implementation Partner): Legal and regional marketing together, with IT advising on the data model. I can convene them, but I cannot approve the policy myself.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Understood. What would a successful first phase include?

Jayce Bogisich-Treutel (Implementation Partner): Probably one region and one event type. We should prove that records can be imported, consent source preserved, duplicates handled, and follow-up assigned to the right owner. If that works, we can expand.

Jayce Bogisich-Treutel (Implementation Partner): There is a major industry event in the second half of the year, but nobody has formally tied the project to it. The event creates urgency, although it could also create a bad rush. If the workflow is not ready, people will bypass it.
