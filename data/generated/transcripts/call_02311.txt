Priya Shah (Account Executive): Hi Emilio, thanks for making the time. How are things in Toronto today?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): A little grey, but not terrible. We have a production review in an hour, so I’m glad we could get this in before the day gets noisy.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I’ll keep us moving. I understand Sporer, Reynolds and Zemlak has several media brands and a fairly broad marketing remit. Before I make assumptions, what prompted the conversation about marketing automation now?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): We’ve grown by acquisition, and every brand has developed its own habits. One team uses Mailchimp, another exports lists from the CRM, and a third still has a spreadsheet that somebody calls the “master.” It’s workable until a campaign needs to cross brands.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): So the issue is less sending an email and more creating a dependable operating model across the portfolio?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Exactly. We can send. We can’t always prove who should have received something, why they received it, or whether the sales team saw the response. And when an editor asks for a quick audience pull, marketing operations becomes a service desk.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How many people are involved in campaign execution?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Directly, maybe thirty-five across corporate and the brands. Indirectly, hundreds of content, subscription, and sales people touch the data. Our headcount is around 1,449, but the marketing group is not 1,449 people, thankfully.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. What does the current Mailchimp setup handle well?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): It’s familiar and inexpensive. Brand managers can get a newsletter out without calling IT. That matters. The tradeoff is that preferences, scoring, and reporting are fragmented. We have three different definitions of an engaged contact.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): When you say scoring, are you looking for lead handoff to sales, subscriber engagement, or both?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Both, though not with equal urgency. The subscription business wants engagement and churn signals. The B2B advertising side wants a way to identify accounts that are actually active, not just one person who opened a white paper.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What happens today when a promising response comes in?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Someone forwards it. Sometimes to the right seller, sometimes to a general inbox. We have a weekly report, but it is mostly retrospective. By the time an account appears on it, the moment may be gone.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds like a timing problem. Is there a specific campaign or launch driving the evaluation?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Our fall media summit. We want registration, nurture, partner promotion, and post-event follow-up to be one program. Last year the registration list was in one system, attendance in another, and the sales follow-up was manually assigned. We lost at least a week.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would success look like for that event?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): A single audience definition, automatic routing for priority accounts, visible consent status, and reporting my leadership team can understand without a glossary. Also, no heroic spreadsheet work the night before launch.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): On consent, are your brands operating under one policy?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): The policy is central. The implementation is not. Canada is the complicated piece, and we have contacts who interact with more than one brand. I don’t want a global unsubscribe accidentally interpreted as permission to keep sending from another property.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We should include that in the data and governance workshop. Which systems would need to connect?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Our CRM, the subscription platform, the event tool, and probably the warehouse. The CRM is the system of record for accounts, but not every contact is clean. We have duplicates from acquisitions, stale job titles, and inconsistent industry values.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): If we started with one brand, which would be the best proving ground?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): The B2B advertising group. They have enough complexity to demonstrate value, but a leader who is willing to make decisions. Starting enterprise-wide sounds impressive and usually means nobody agrees on the first workflow.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That’s a sensible boundary. Who else would participate in the decision?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Marketing operations, our CRM owner, privacy counsel, and procurement. Sales leadership will want to see routing and account visibility. Finance will ask whether this replaces Mailchimp or sits beside it for a while.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): And your role in the approval?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): I sponsor the business case. I won’t approve an architecture that creates a second disconnected database. Procurement will manage the commercial process, but they won’t decide whether the workflows are useful.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We have a budgetary estimate around $47,396 annually for the scope we’ve discussed. Is that in the range you expected?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): It’s higher than Mailchimp, obviously. It’s not automatically a problem if the reporting and routing reduce enough manual work. I need a practical comparison, including implementation and the cost of running two tools during transition.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can model that. What timeline would be meaningful?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): We’d like a decision before the summer planning cycle closes, then a pilot in late summer. But I’m wary of a vendor promising a six-week transformation. We have a lot of inherited data.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would a technical discovery next week with marketing operations and the CRM owner help validate the pilot?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Yes, provided it is not a generic demo. Bring a sample event journey, consent edge cases, and an example of account-level reporting. We can bring our current field map.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I’ll send an agenda and a data checklist. Any risks I should capture now?

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Adoption. Brand teams will resist if they lose autonomy. Also, we have a merger integration competing for CRM resources. If your plan assumes unlimited admin time, it won’t survive contact with reality.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Fair point. We should show where central standards end and local execution remains flexible. I’ll include an adoption track, not just configuration.

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Good. And please don’t position this as replacing creativity with automation. Our teams will shut down if they hear that.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. The goal is to remove repetitive coordination so they can spend more time on the content and audience strategy.

Emilio Kulas (CMO): That’s the right framing. Send the materials, and I’ll get the CRM owner and privacy counsel invited. I can’t promise everyone will attend; calendars are a bit of a zoo.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I’ll make the session useful even if one person has to join late. We’ll reconvene after the workshop with a pilot recommendation and a clearer total-cost comparison.

Emilio Kulas (CMO): Sounds good. I need to jump to that production review.
