Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Jacinto, I appreciate you joining. I understand you are evaluating the marketing automation project from the privacy side.

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): Correct. I am not the business sponsor. My job is to find the assumptions that become incidents later.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is exactly the perspective we need. What are the first questions you would ask about the proposed use?

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): Purpose, lawful basis, data sources, retention, international transfers, and whether marketing can explain the logic to a person who asks. We are an education organization, so the audience includes students, parents, staff, and partners. They are not interchangeable.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How are those groups identified today?

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): Imperfectly. Some forms ask audience type. Some do not. A parent may download a staff guide, and a student may use a personal email. If the system guesses, the guess must not become a consequential decision.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can use declared attributes where available and keep uncertain records in a neutral state. I would not recommend inferring a sensitive category from browsing behavior.

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): Good. Marketing has asked about scoring engagement. I need to know whether that means a person is profiled automatically.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): It can mean several things. We could start with operational triggers, such as sending a relevant follow-up after a confirmed form submission, without assigning a broad individual score. If scoring is used, the purpose and effects need to be documented.

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): That distinction matters. A sequence based on a request is easier to justify than a hidden ranking based on visits.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Agreed. Which data sources are in scope?

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): Website forms, event registrations, existing CRM contacts, and possibly alumni records. Alumni are the sensitive area. We have historic permissions that vary by year and by program.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would you prefer historic alumni data excluded from the initial rollout?

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): Unless we can establish the permission history, yes. Exclusion is not a failure; it is a control. The business may disagree because the list is large.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can define a verified cohort and hold the remainder outside promotional automation pending review. I will need to confirm the import and suppression mechanics with our technical team.

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): Please do not tell the business it is compliant because we can configure a checkbox. Configuration is not an assessment.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. Our materials can describe capabilities, while your team determines whether the proposed processing is appropriate.

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): What is the retention behavior? If a person unsubscribes, do you delete them, suppress them, or retain them for another purpose?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Those are separate controls. A record may need to be retained for suppression or legal documentation while being excluded from marketing. The exact default periods should be reviewed against your policy and contract.

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): I want deletion requests to be traceable across connected systems. We have had one vendor say deletion completed when an export still existed in a shared folder.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then the workflow should include source systems, exports, backups where applicable, and an owner for verification. I will capture that as a validation requirement rather than a checkbox in the project plan.

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): Good. Now transfers. Where is the service hosted, and which subprocessors handle email or analytics?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I can provide the current subprocessor and hosting documentation. I do not want to recite a location from memory, especially because contractual terms may differ by service component.

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): That answer is better than an overconfident answer. We also require access logging and least privilege.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can review role-based access, administrator controls, audit logs, and the process for removing a departing employee. Who will own periodic access reviews on your side?

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): IT security normally performs the review, but privacy needs visibility into marketing roles. A contractor should not inherit the same access as the campaign administrator.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can map those personas separately. For the pilot, would a limited administrator group and no contractor access be acceptable as a starting point?

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): Potentially. But I need the data-flow diagram first. Our organization is large enough that “small pilot” can still involve several departments.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is fair. I will send a diagram showing collection, synchronization, segmentation, sending, suppression, and deletion paths. We can annotate the uncertain areas.

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): Include manual CSV uploads. People always forget the manual path because it is not an integration.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Excellent catch. We will include uploads and downloaded reports as well.

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): What is the project status? I saw a discovery-stage opportunity, but one document mentioned a September date.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): September is an existing planning reference, not an approved launch. The opportunity remains in discovery, and your review is one of the prerequisites. I will avoid presenting the date as committed.

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): That is important. A deadline can pressure people into accepting an incomplete assessment.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can instead define gates: documented purposes and sources, approved retention, transfer review, access model, and tested deletion and suppression behavior.

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): Add a test for mixed audiences. A single contact should not receive a student campaign and a partner campaign because two systems disagree.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Added. We should also test an unsubscribe arriving shortly before a scheduled send.

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): Yes, and an imported record with ambiguous consent. Those are the real cases.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I will send the requested documents and propose a working session with IT security and marketing operations. After that, you can decide whether the design is reviewable.

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): Correct. I am interested, but I am not approving the product today.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. Thanks for making the requirements concrete.

Jacinto Ernser (Privacy): Thank you. Send the diagram before the workshop, please.
