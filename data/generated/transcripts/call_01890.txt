Dolores Monahan (Procurement): Marketing wants faster campaign creation and testing. Executives want reliable reports. Operations wants fewer handoff problems. Procurement wants a scope that does not expand every time someone sees another feature.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Those are different definitions of value. We can make the first phase explicit: journey building, testing, workflow handoffs, and reporting tied to agreed business questions.

Dolores Monahan (Procurement): Who owns those questions? The CMO is interested, but the operators will live in the system.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I would recommend a sponsor from executive marketing, an operational owner from Marketing Operations, and representatives from Sales Operations and IT. Procurement can then evaluate a plan with named dependencies.

Dolores Monahan (Procurement): We have a large organization, nearly two thousand employees, and several regional teams. A global design can become a committee exercise.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then begin with a representative region and a governance model for expansion. The implementation should define what is standardized and what remains local.

Dolores Monahan (Procurement): Marketing keeps saying attribution. I need to understand whether that is a dashboard request or an actual implementation requirement.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): It is both. Executive buyers usually need attribution and reporting that connect activity to pipeline and outcomes. That requires agreed source definitions, opportunity stages, and ownership of the reporting model.

Dolores Monahan (Procurement): And the practitioners?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Operators generally care about building journeys, testing variations, and moving through workflows quickly. If the system produces an impressive executive dashboard but makes routine campaign work slow, adoption will suffer.

Dolores Monahan (Procurement): So the business case has two audiences.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Exactly. We should demonstrate the executive view and have operators complete a real task: create a journey, set a test, define a suppression condition, and hand an engaged contact to sales.

Dolores Monahan (Procurement): That is more useful than a feature tour. Can the test be scored?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can define acceptance criteria such as time to build, number of manual steps, test coverage, and whether the resulting report answers the agreed question. Your team can sign off before expansion.

Dolores Monahan (Procurement): What implementation work is not included?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We need to distinguish configuration from content creation, data cleansing, custom integrations, and local process redesign. Those may be separate workstreams. The statement of work should list assumptions and out-of-scope items plainly.

Dolores Monahan (Procurement): Good. Our legal team will also ask about data processing, security, and termination assistance.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can provide the standard documents for review. For termination assistance, the agreement should specify available export formats, timing, and any services that carry a fee.

Dolores Monahan (Procurement): I will need pricing in a format finance can compare. They do not want a subscription number without services and internal effort.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can separate license, implementation, optional services, and estimated internal roles. I would also include the cost of governance and reporting ownership because those are necessary for a credible program.

Dolores Monahan (Procurement): The marketing team may ask for advanced automation before the basics are proven.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can place advanced capabilities in a later phase unless they are required for the initial acceptance criteria. That keeps the first release focused while preserving a roadmap.

Dolores Monahan (Procurement): What would you put in phase one?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): One lead-capture source, two representative journeys, a test-and-approval workflow, defined sales handoff rules, suppression handling, and an executive report using agreed pipeline fields. We would also train the operators who maintain those assets.

Dolores Monahan (Procurement): And phase two?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Additional regions, more complex branching, deeper integrations, and expanded attribution if the data foundation supports it. I would not promise a global rollout before the pilot produces evidence.

Dolores Monahan (Procurement): That will help. There is pressure to call this a global transformation, but the purchase order needs a deliverable.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then the deliverable should be a working, measurable operating slice, with a documented path to scale.

Dolores Monahan (Procurement): Who needs to attend the next meeting?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): A Marketing Operations lead to perform the workflow, a CMO or delegate to validate reporting outcomes, Sales Operations for the handoff, and IT or security for integration constraints.
