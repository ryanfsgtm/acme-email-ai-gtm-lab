Priya Shah (Account Executive): Hi, Nelle. Thanks for making time. Is this still a good half hour, or did the factory schedule win?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): The factory schedule always wins eventually, but I have twenty-eight minutes. We manufacture components in the Benelux region, and marketing is a very small team, so I wanted to be direct about that.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Direct is perfect. I understand Schuppe Group has about one hundred fifty-six employees. What prompted the conversation now?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): We have grown through referrals and repeat business, but that has plateaued. Our sales team says marketing does not generate enough qualified opportunities. Marketing says sales does not follow up consistently. Both statements contain some truth.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How are new inquiries handled today?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Mostly email, phone calls, and trade shows. A salesperson enters some activity into the CRM. A trade-show list may sit in a spreadsheet for two weeks because everyone is busy. Then we wonder why the campaign did not work.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What happens when a prospect visits the website or downloads a technical document?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): We see aggregate traffic, but not much useful identity. Our website forms are basic. They send an email to me or to whoever remembers to check the shared inbox.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): So the first issue may be visibility and routing rather than a lack of content.

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Correct. We have plenty of technical content. It is not glamorous, but engineers like it. The problem is knowing whether the person reading it is a student, a supplier, or an actual buyer.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How do you define a qualified inquiry?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Company fit, an identifiable project, and some indication of timing. Industry alone is not enough. We sell to manufacturing companies, but a request for a catalog is very different from a request for a tolerance review.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That distinction could become a form question or a scoring signal. Who decides whether the inquiry is ready for sales?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Usually me, unless I am traveling. Then it becomes whoever notices it. There is no agreed service level. Sales says they need more context, but they do not always tell us what context was missing.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would a better handoff look like?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): The salesperson receives the inquiry with the source, product area, company, stated project, and next action. If it is not ready, the reason should come back to marketing so we can improve the process.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That feedback loop is important. How many salespeople would use it?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Four, maybe five. The owner is also involved in larger accounts, so not every lead follows a neat route.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Tell me about your buying cycles.

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Existing customers can move quickly, sometimes within weeks. New industrial accounts can take six months or more because engineering, quality, and procurement all weigh in. Marketing cannot force that timeline.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Of course. Are there particular segments you want to grow?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): We want more business from mid-sized machinery manufacturers and systems integrators. We are not trying to become a mass-market lead machine. Ten good opportunities are worth more than two hundred random downloads.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What channels have worked best?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Referrals and trade shows. LinkedIn produces attention but not many conversations. Email newsletters are inconsistent. We have one excellent case study, though it is in Dutch and has never been promoted properly.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would localized campaigns help?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Maybe. But I do not want to create three languages of content before we know which message works. We should start with a clear English version and adapt based on actual demand.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Sensible. What reporting do you have today?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Website visits, email opens, and a monthly spreadsheet of inquiries. The spreadsheet is hand-built. It tells me volume, not whether an inquiry became revenue.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Can you connect opportunities back to the original source?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Sometimes. The CRM has an original-source field, but it is often blank or overwritten. The sales team prioritizes customer conversations, understandably, not data hygiene.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): If you had one dashboard, what would it show?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Qualified inquiries by source, conversion to a real sales conversation, time to follow-up, and revenue influenced. I would also want to see reasons for disqualification. Otherwise we optimize for the wrong thing.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How comfortable is the sales team with structured processes?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Mixed. One salesperson loves checklists. Another says every deal is different. They are both partly right. We need a minimum process that does not feel like bureaucracy.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What might stop you from changing the current setup?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Time. We cannot spend six months implementing a system. Also, I do not want to become the administrator for every field, workflow, and user.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is helpful. A sensible first phase might focus on forms, routing, basic nurture, and closed-loop reporting, with a small number of fields. Administration can be shared.

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Yes, but nurture has to respect how technical buyers behave. Someone may download three documents over a year and still not be ready for a sales call.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We could use engagement as a signal, not an automatic handoff. A threshold could create a review task rather than assigning every active reader to sales.

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): I like that. And existing customers need a separate path. They are often asking for a product extension, not beginning a new buying journey.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We should separate customer expansion from net-new demand. Who else should be involved in evaluating a solution?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Our operations manager, one salesperson, and probably the owner. Finance will want to understand cost, but they should not design the workflow.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would success look like after ninety days?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Every inquiry has an owner within one business day, sales gives a reason when it is rejected, and I can see which campaigns create conversations. If we also reduce spreadsheet work, that is a strong result.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): And what would be a misleading success metric?

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): More form fills. We could buy those tomorrow with a generic guide. The quality and follow-up matter.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will prepare a focused proposal around that definition, including a phased rollout and the data fields we would leave out initially.

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Include implementation effort. If it requires a dedicated person every week, we need to know.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will. I will also include a workshop using one trade-show list and one website inquiry, so the team can see the process with real examples.

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Good. I am interested, but I am not ready to say we have a project until the operations manager sees it.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is reasonable. I will send the summary and a short agenda, and we can decide whether to continue after that review.

Nelle Collins (VP Marketing): Perfect. I have to join the production meeting now.
