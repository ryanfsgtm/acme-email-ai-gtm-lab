Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Katrine, hello. Can you hear me clearly? I have a little echo on my side.

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): I can hear you. It is not studio quality, but we will survive. I have twenty minutes before another forecast meeting.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will keep us moving. I understand Rolfson - Purdy is a 742-person financial-services organization in the Benelux, and you are evaluating marketing automation from a revenue-operations perspective.

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): That is accurate. We have multiple business units, different definitions of “qualified,” and a heroic number of spreadsheets. The spreadsheets are not the root problem, but they are the visible symptom.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What creates the most friction today?

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): Handoffs. Marketing says a lead is ready, sales says it is not, and operations is asked to explain why the numbers do not reconcile. Then someone changes a field and we lose the history.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How are leads routed now?

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): A mix of CRM rules, inbox forwarding, and regional habits. The Netherlands team follows one process. Belgium follows a slightly different process. Luxembourg has a shared queue that nobody loves.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would a common scoring and routing model help?

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): Possibly, but I would not begin with scoring. If the underlying account ownership and consent data are inconsistent, a sophisticated score just creates a more confident mistake.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a good caution. We could begin by mapping definitions, ownership, and required fields before adding behavioral scoring.

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): Yes. Also, our leadership wants a single funnel view, but a single funnel can hide meaningful regional differences.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Then the reporting model should have shared stages with documented local exceptions. The goal is comparability without pretending every market behaves identically.

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): Exactly. Can your platform preserve the source and timestamp of a status change?

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): It can track activity and field history for supported records. We would need to confirm the exact history available for each integrated object and how long it is retained.

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): Please mark that as a question, not a feature we can assume.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will. What happens when a salesperson rejects a marketing-qualified lead?

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): Usually they write “not ready” in a note. That note is not structured, so marketing cannot learn whether the issue was timing, fit, duplicate, or bad data.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We could use structured rejection reasons and trigger different nurture paths. Would sales accept that?

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): They may accept it if it takes five seconds. If it becomes a mini-form, adoption will collapse.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Then speed is part of the design requirement. We can test the minimum required fields with a pilot group.

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): Good. Another issue is reporting latency. Executives see yesterday’s numbers while regional managers want near-real-time activity.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Those are different use cases. We should identify which metrics need freshness and which can be reconciled overnight for accuracy.

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): That answer is less exciting than “real time,” but more useful. We have spent money on dashboards nobody trusts.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Trust usually depends on definitions, ownership, and reconciliation, not only refresh speed.

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): What implementation effort should I assume? Our opportunity is roughly fifty-four thousand ARR, but the internal cost could be larger than the subscription in year one.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I would estimate in phases: process and data mapping, initial routing and nurture, reporting alignment, then expansion. The range depends on the number of business units and integrations.

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): We have more integrations than anyone admits in the first meeting.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Then discovery should include an inventory exercise. We should identify system owners, data direction, failure handling, and the fields that must remain authoritative.

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): Failure handling is often omitted. If synchronization breaks overnight, who knows, and what happens to the queue?

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We would define monitoring and escalation, but I need to confirm which alerts are native and which require an external monitoring process.

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): Again, thank you for separating confirmation from assumption.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would success look like after ninety days?

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): Fewer unassigned leads, consistent rejection reasons, a funnel report that regional leaders accept, and less manual reconciliation. I would not promise revenue lift in ninety days.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is measurable. We can establish a baseline before launch and review operational indicators first.

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): Who would own the program internally?

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I would recommend a business owner in revenue operations, a marketing operations counterpart, and named owners for CRM and regional rollout.

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): That is probably right, but I need to see whether those people have actual capacity. “Named owner” is not the same as available owner.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Absolutely. Capacity and decision rights should be part of the readiness check.

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): We are at eighteen minutes. What do you need from me next?

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): A high-level process map, integration list, current funnel definitions, and examples of rejected leads. In return, I will send a discovery outline, assumptions, and an implementation-range worksheet.

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): Send the worksheet. I will collect the data, though the integration list may be embarrassing.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Embarrassing inventories are usually the useful ones.

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): One last thing: do not bring a generic demo next time. Show the routing exception and the rejection workflow.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Agreed. We will tailor the next session around those two moments and leave pricing until the scope is credible.

Katrine Mayert (Revenue Operations): Fine. I am not saying we will buy anything, but this is relevant enough to continue.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a good outcome for discovery. Thanks, Katrine.
