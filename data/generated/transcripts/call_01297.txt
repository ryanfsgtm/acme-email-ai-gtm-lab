Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Then I arrived at exactly the right moment. I understand you support implementations for smaller professional-services firms, and Kreiger - Effertz is evaluating marketing automation. What prompted the review?

Kurtis Grady (Implementation Partner): Their current setup is a mix of spreadsheets, email sequences, and ActiveCampaign. It works if one person remembers everything. That person is leaving in two months.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): So continuity and repeatability are the main drivers?

Kurtis Grady (Implementation Partner): Those are the immediate drivers. They also want better visibility into which prospects attended a consultation, which proposal is open, and which clients might need a follow-up. They are not trying to build a giant ecommerce machine.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What kind of services do they sell?

Kurtis Grady (Implementation Partner): Advisory and implementation work, mostly project-based. Sales cycles vary. A small engagement might close in three weeks; a larger one can sit for a quarter while several partners review the scope.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): And who owns marketing today?

Kurtis Grady (Implementation Partner): Practically, one coordinator. Formally, the managing partner signs off. I am external, so I can recommend a design, but I cannot approve policy or spend.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Helpful distinction. What did they like about ActiveCampaign, and where does it fall short?

Kurtis Grady (Implementation Partner): It is familiar and inexpensive at their current scale. The problem is that reporting is fragmented, and the handoff from an automation to a human is not consistent. Also, different consultants have invented different tags.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would you want us to reproduce those tags or simplify the model?

Kurtis Grady (Implementation Partner): Simplify. A lifecycle stage, service interest, lead source, owner, and next action would cover most of it. There are about fifty-two employees, but only a subset should touch the system.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How many active users do you estimate?

Kurtis Grady (Implementation Partner): Maybe eight, perhaps ten. The wider staff should receive internal notifications, not edit campaigns.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What channels matter in phase one?

Kurtis Grady (Implementation Partner): Email and forms. Possibly webinar attendance later. They are not asking for social publishing. Their website is being refreshed, so forms need to be easy to replace without calling a developer every week.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can show the form editor and approval workflow. What would a successful first ninety days look like?

Kurtis Grady (Implementation Partner): First, migrate the useful contacts without importing years of junk. Second, create an inquiry-to-consultation sequence. Third, give partners a dashboard that tells them what needs attention. If the dashboard is decorative, they will abandon it.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What data is available for migration?

Kurtis Grady (Implementation Partner): CSV exports, the ActiveCampaign account, and some information in the CRM. The exports use inconsistent country and industry values. I can clean them, but I need a clear target model.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We would begin with a field inventory and a mapping workshop. We should also decide whether old contacts are stored for reference or eligible for marketing.

Kurtis Grady (Implementation Partner): Yes, that distinction matters. They have a lot of historic contacts who should not suddenly receive a campaign because a field was blank.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How involved do you expect to be after launch?

Kurtis Grady (Implementation Partner): I can lead configuration and training. I would like office hours for the first month, because the managing partner changes priorities verbally and then forgets the original request.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a familiar implementation risk. We can use a decision log and require approval for changes to live journeys.

Kurtis Grady (Implementation Partner): Good. I also want to avoid overengineering. A small firm does not need twelve nurture branches to prove the platform is powerful.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Agreed. We can start with a few reusable templates and add complexity when there is evidence it helps. What concerns do you anticipate from the team?

Kurtis Grady (Implementation Partner): Cost, mostly. They will compare the subscription with ActiveCampaign. But if you present value only as “more automation,” they will push back. The value is fewer missed follow-ups and less dependence on one coordinator.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. Is ActiveCampaign still the primary alternative?

Kurtis Grady (Implementation Partner): Yes, although the team is not emotionally attached to it. They are attached to not disrupting their work.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a useful way to frame the evaluation. Who should attend the next session?

Kurtis Grady (Implementation Partner): The coordinator, one managing partner, and someone who owns the website. Maybe the finance person for pricing, but not for a two-hour feature tour.
