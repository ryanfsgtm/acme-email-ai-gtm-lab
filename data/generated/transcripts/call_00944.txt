Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Sibyl, thanks for making the time. I know implementation planning is not always the most exciting meeting, especially when procurement is asked to discuss field mappings.

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): You would be surprised. Field mappings are more exciting than some of the contracts I review. We are a small healthcare company, so everyone wears two hats, sometimes three.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That helps explain why the attendee list is short. I have Willms - Kihn as a 16-person organization in the United States, and I understand there is no active opportunity attached yet. Are we planning around a potential purchase or an internal rollout exercise?

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): A potential purchase. We need to understand what implementation would involve before we decide whether the subscription is sensible. Our current process is a combination of spreadsheets, a basic CRM, and manual reminders.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What prompted the review?

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): We have a growing referral network and more patient-facing education campaigns. The team is losing track of follow-up, and compliance questions are handled inconsistently. We do not want a system that sends the wrong message to the wrong person.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Is the primary audience patients, providers, or referral partners?

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): Mostly referral partners and healthcare professionals. Patient communications may come later, but we are cautious. The initial project should focus on professional contacts and educational events.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Good boundary. What would the first ninety days need to accomplish?

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): Import a clean contact list, set consent and suppression rules, create event registration and reminders, route follow-up tasks, and produce a report our leadership can understand. We also need an audit trail of who changed what.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Who maintains your current data?

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): I coordinate it, though the source files belong to marketing, clinical partnerships, and operations. That is part of the trouble. We have duplicate organizations, different spellings, and contacts who changed roles.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would you want a full migration or a curated import?

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): Curated. We estimate 4,000 records, but perhaps 1,500 are genuinely active. I would rather start with a smaller trusted set than import every historical address and then spend months suppressing people.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How do you verify consent today?

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): There is a field in one spreadsheet, a note in another, and an opt-out mailbox. It is not ideal. We need someone from compliance involved before any campaign is sent.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can make compliance review a launch gate. What types of integrations are required?

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): Website forms, our CRM, calendar invitations, and perhaps the event platform. We do not want to connect clinical systems. That needs to be very clear in the plan.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Agreed. No clinical system data in the initial scope. What permissions should you have?

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): I need to approve vendors and review audit records, not necessarily build every campaign. Marketing should manage content. Operations should manage task queues. No one should casually export the entire database.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can define role-based access and export controls. Would single sign-on be required?

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): Required eventually. For the pilot, our IT contractor may accept a temporary login, but I would prefer not to establish a pattern we later have to unwind.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is reasonable. We can document a temporary access period and review it at handoff.

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): What does implementation cost beyond the subscription? That is usually where estimates become slippery.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We would scope data preparation, configuration, integration, training, and launch support separately. The exact number depends on the quality of the source data and whether the event platform has a supported connector. I do not want to invent a fixed figure before that is checked.

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): Good. I need assumptions and exclusions. If you say implementation is included and later charge for every mapping workshop, the relationship starts badly.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will provide an assumptions register. We can also use a sample of 200 records to estimate cleanup effort before the proposal is finalized.

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): That would help. What is the minimum internal team?

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): An executive sponsor, a process owner, one technical reviewer, and representatives from marketing and operations. Compliance should review consent and message categories.

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): Our executive sponsor will likely be the COO. Marketing has one full-time person. Operations can give us a few hours a week. The technical reviewer is external, so scheduling may be a constraint.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can work asynchronously on field documentation. Which workflow should be tested first?

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): Event attendance to follow-up. Registration confirmation, reminder, attendance status, a task for the right owner, and a short educational sequence. If someone opts out, every future message must stop.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What determines the right owner?

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): Referral territory and relationship type. The current rules are in a spreadsheet that only I understand, which is not a good control.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We will translate that into documented routing rules and test exceptions. What would make you stop the project?

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): If the vendor cannot explain where data is stored, if audit history is insufficient, or if the team cannot operate the system without a consultant forever. Also if the first use case expands into patient communications without a separate review.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Those are clear gates. We can address data handling, audit controls, training, and scope separately. We should not treat a successful event workflow as authorization for patient messaging.

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): Exactly. People hear “automation” and assume we want to automate everything.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What timing are you considering?

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): If the sample data review is clean, a pilot in January. But our budget cycle is not flexible, and our compliance reviewer is unavailable for two weeks in December.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Then the next step is a data sample and security questionnaire, followed by a scoped proposal. I will include a pilot schedule with a compliance gate.

Sibyl Koepp (Procurement): Send it to me first. I will circulate internally and collect the missing owners.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Will do. Thanks, Sibyl.
