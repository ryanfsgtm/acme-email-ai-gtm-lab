Priya Shah (Account Executive): Rollin, good morning. Am I saying your name correctly—Rollin?

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): Yes, that is right. And good morning from Munich, although the weather is doing its usual gray thing.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We have sunshine here, so I will try not to sound smug. Thanks for joining. I understand Romaguera - Senger is evaluating marketing automation for a 162-person education organization. I would like to understand what prompted the review, how your team works today, and what a successful change would look like.

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): The immediate trigger is that our campaigns are growing faster than our process. We have regional programs, continuing education, partner events, and some recruitment work. The team is not huge, but the audience structure is complicated.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How many people are actively creating or sending campaigns?

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): Four regular users, perhaps seven occasional users. The occasional users are where the trouble begins. Someone in a regional office asks for a list, somebody exports it, and then the central team discovers the audience was based on an old spreadsheet.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What happens after that?

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): Sometimes nothing dramatic. Sometimes a regional coordinator receives a complaint because a person who opted out of one program gets an unrelated invitation. We fix it manually and send apologies. It is embarrassing more than catastrophic, but it consumes time.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): When you say opted out of one program, are preferences currently managed separately?

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): Partly. We have a general unsubscribe in our email tool, but program-level preferences live in spreadsheets or in the event system. They do not always agree. We need a cleaner model.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds like both a data-governance problem and an execution problem. Which is more urgent?

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): Governance is the risk. Execution is the daily pain. If you ask me to choose, I would solve the governance first, because automating a bad process just makes mistakes faster.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I agree with that framing. What systems would need to participate?

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): Our website forms, the event registration platform, a student relationship database, and the email tool. Finance also has a reporting system, though I do not expect marketing automation to own that.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Is the student relationship database the authoritative system for a person’s status?

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): Mostly. Mostly is the dangerous word. The database is authoritative for enrolled status, but the event platform knows attendance and the email tool knows engagement. We need to decide what should update what.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Exactly. We would map field ownership before building journeys. For example, enrollment could come from the student database, while campaign subscription status might be maintained in the marketing layer, with clear synchronization rules.

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): Can marketing users override a field?

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That depends on the field. We can restrict sensitive status fields while allowing marketers to add campaign notes or internal classifications. The important part is distinguishing operational attributes from identity and consent data.

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): Our IT manager will ask whether this means a large implementation project.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): It can become one if every historical inconsistency is treated as a prerequisite. I would recommend starting with two journeys: continuing-education event follow-up and a general prospective-student nurture. That gives us a controlled scope.

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): We already have a nurture program. It is not sophisticated, but people know how it works.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is useful. We do not need to replace something merely because it is old. We can document what is working, identify where the handoffs fail, and migrate only when the new version is safer or easier to operate.

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): That is a surprisingly non-sales answer.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): A failed migration is expensive. I would rather earn the right to expand after the first use case is stable.

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): What would you measure?

Priya Shah (Account Executive): For the event journey, delivery and conversion to a defined next action, but also suppression accuracy and time spent preparing the audience. For the nurture, progression between stages and unsubscribe patterns. We should measure operational effort, not just opens and clicks.

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): Good. We have been burned by dashboards full of numbers that do not answer whether the team can keep up.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How is the buying decision made?

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): I own the recommendation. IT validates integrations and security. The academic program directors validate the audience rules. Procurement will review the contract. The executive sponsor wants a business case, not a tool comparison.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would make the business case credible?

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): Fewer manual list builds, fewer preference errors, and a clear way to prove who received what. If you tell me only that campaigns can be automated, that is not enough.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then our next step should be a working session using one real, anonymized journey. We can map the trigger, audience, consent condition, content, exit criteria, and owner. We can also show how an exception is handled.

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): I want our regional coordinator in that meeting. She understands the messy reality better than I do.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Absolutely. The people closest to the work often know where the documented process differs from the actual process.

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): We also need to discuss localization. Some communications are in German, some in English, and a few in French. The translation process is external.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can treat language as an audience attribute or campaign branch, but the content approval process remains yours. Automation should route approved content; it should not silently translate or invent it.

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): Thank you. Another vendor implied the system could solve that automatically.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): It can reduce routing work, but human review still matters, especially for education and regulatory language.

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): All right. Send me a proposed agenda for the working session. Include the data ownership questions and the reporting measures you mentioned. I am not ready for pricing yet.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is fine. We can keep the next step focused on fit and feasibility. After the workshop, we will know whether a commercial discussion is justified.

Rollin Abernathy (VP Marketing): Good. I have another meeting in two minutes, and it is, naturally, about a spreadsheet.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will try to help you retire at least one of them. Thanks, Rollin.
