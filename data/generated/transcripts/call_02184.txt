Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Colton, thanks for staying with us through another commercial discussion. I know these meetings can become a spreadsheet with voices.

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): That is exactly what it has felt like this week. I am calling from a site office, so apologies if a forklift beeps.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We can work around it. I have the proposal for Howell and Sons open. You are listed as the implementation partner, and the opportunity is closed-lost with an estimated value of $58,099. I want to understand what happened and make sure the implementation implications are clear.

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): The short version is that the customer selected Pardot. The longer version is that price was part of it, but not the only part. We should not reduce the loss to a discount problem.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That is useful. Where did the commercial proposal create friction?

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): The first proposal looked affordable at the headline level. Once we added regional onboarding, data migration assistance, and the connector work, the customer saw a much larger number. They felt the scope was moving underneath them.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Were those services mandatory for the use case?

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): Some were recommended, some were necessary if they wanted us to do the work. Their internal team could have handled part of the migration. They did not have the capacity, however, and that was known fairly early.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): So the issue was not simply the subscription price; it was uncertainty around total cost and ownership.

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): Correct. Procurement wanted a comparable five-year view. We supplied annual pricing, but the implementation assumptions were in a separate document. That made comparison difficult.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Did Pardot include those services?

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): Not exactly. Their implementation quote was lower on paper, but they also assumed the customer would provide more internal labour. The customer preferred that model, at least in the final meeting.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That is an important distinction. What did the customer say about capability?

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): They already had people familiar with the Salesforce ecosystem. Their team believed that reduced risk, even though I had concerns about the migration workload.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Were there technical objections to our platform?

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): A few. They wanted a particular CRM field hierarchy and a legacy ERP feed. We said both were possible, but the answer sounded conditional. Pardot had a reference architecture that looked closer to what their architects already knew.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Could we have demonstrated that architecture sooner?

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): Probably. The technical workshop happened after commercial negotiations had already created anxiety. By then, every caveat sounded like a new risk.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What did the customer expect from you as implementation partner?

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): A fixed plan, named resources, and a firm answer on migration duration. I could give a range, but not a guarantee without profiling their data. They interpreted that as uncertainty.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What was the data condition?

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): Mixed. About a third of the records had incomplete industry or region values, and there were duplicates from two acquisitions. We recommended a cleanse before activation. The customer wanted to migrate first and tidy later.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That is a material risk. Did they accept it?

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): They accepted it in conversation, then became nervous when the plan showed the dependencies. People often say they are comfortable with risk until it has a row and a date.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Very true. Was there a timeline constraint?

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): Yes. They wanted the first campaigns running before their autumn trade show. The date was aggressive. We said we could meet it with a narrow pilot, but the customer wanted the full regional setup from day one.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Did we propose a phased rollout clearly enough?

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): We proposed it, but too late. Also, the word “pilot” worried them. They heard “unfinished product,” not “controlled launch.”

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What could have changed the decision?

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): A single commercial document showing license, services, assumptions, customer effort, and optional items. A reference call with a manufacturer of similar size would have helped. And the executive sponsor wanted proof that local teams would actually adopt it.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We have manufacturing customers, but perhaps we did not bring the right story.

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): Yes. The customer liked the workflow demonstration. They were less convinced that their regional managers would use it consistently.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Did procurement negotiate aggressively at the end?

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): They asked for a reduction, certainly. But when we offered one, it did not restart the evaluation. That tells me the blocker had moved to perceived delivery risk.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That is helpful evidence against treating discount as the remedy. Is there anything we should avoid saying in future proposals?

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): Avoid “quick implementation” unless the conditions are explicit. Their data and regional requirements were not quick. Also avoid promising that configuration is simple when the governance work is complex.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What should we lead with instead?

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): A readiness assessment. Give the customer a view of data quality, decision owners, integration dependencies, and internal hours before pricing is final.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That may make the first conversation longer, but it could prevent the late surprise.

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): Exactly. And show two plans: a focused launch and a full rollout. Let the customer choose the trade-off consciously.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): I will document that. Is the loss final, or could there be a future re-entry point?

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): They signed with Pardot, so it is final for now. If the internal effort proves heavier than expected, there may be a review later, but we should not plan around that.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Understood. Any final point?

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): The product was not rejected. The buying process lost confidence. That is a different lesson.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Agreed. Thank you, Colton. I will share the revised proposal guidance with the account team.

Colton Prohaska (Implementation Partner): Thanks, Nina. And the forklift has finally stopped, so we have achieved something today.
