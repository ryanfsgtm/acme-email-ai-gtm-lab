Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): That would be useful. We moved quickly at the end, perhaps too quickly. The commercial side is closed, but the operating model is still being assembled.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Before we get into the platform, how is the business doing? Retail has been a noisy market this year.

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Noisy is polite. We sell across Australia and New Zealand, with a mix of stores, online sales, and partner channels. The brand team wants consistency, while local teams want freedom. Those two goals fight each other every week.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What prompted the marketing automation project?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): We are growing, but our customer communications are still too manual. A campaign manager exports a list, someone checks it in a spreadsheet, another person builds the email, and then we hope the follow-up happens. We can execute a launch, but we cannot reliably nurture someone over several weeks.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How many people are involved in a normal campaign?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Six or seven, depending on the channel. Marketing, ecommerce, a local manager, sometimes customer service. The number is not huge; the handoffs are the problem. A spelling mistake or an old segment can survive until the final review.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): And what would success look like six months after implementation?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Fewer manual exports, faster launch cycles, and a clear view of which journeys are actually producing revenue. We want a welcome series, an abandoned-cart flow, post-purchase education, and reactivation. Nothing exotic, but right now each one is a small project.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Which audience is most urgent?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Existing customers. Acquisition is expensive, and we have a lot of first-time buyers who never receive a thoughtful second message. The local teams would probably say abandoned cart, but I think retention is the bigger opportunity.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is helpful. We can map the customer lifecycle and then build the first two journeys around the data you already trust. What systems need to connect?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Ecommerce is on one platform, loyalty on another, and point-of-sale data comes through a nightly file. The agency owns some of the website tags. There is no single clean customer record.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Do you have a central customer ID?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Usually. Usually is doing a lot of work in that sentence. Email is reliable for some customers, loyalty ID for others, and store purchases can arrive without either. We cannot hold implementation hostage to perfect identity resolution, but we do need sensible rules.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can start with deterministic matches and keep an unmatched population separate. That avoids pretending the data is cleaner than it is.

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Exactly. I would rather have a smaller trustworthy audience than a large audience assembled by guesswork.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How do local teams approve content and segments today?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Each country has a marketing manager. Australia is more centralized; New Zealand is leaner and depends on the agency. We need templates with guardrails, but not a system so locked down that the local team cannot respond to a weekend promotion.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can set shared templates, reusable blocks, and permission levels. The central team can own the brand components while local users assemble approved variations.

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): That sounds right. I also want an audit trail. We have had arguments about who changed a subject line after approval.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Version history and approval steps can help there. We should define which changes require reapproval rather than making every edit equally heavy.

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Good. Measurement is another issue. Our board asks for return on marketing spend, but our current reporting mostly counts clicks and last-touch conversions.

Marcus Chen (VP Marketing): What would you like to see instead?

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): Revenue by journey, repeat purchase rate, and a reasonable view of influence across email, SMS, and paid media. I do not need a magic attribution number. I need a consistent methodology that the finance team will accept.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can configure campaign and journey reporting, then agree on attribution rules with finance. I would suggest starting with a simple primary model and a secondary comparison, rather than promising perfect multi-touch attribution on day one.

Hilda Rutherford (VP Marketing): That is the first answer today that sounds implementable. The danger is that executives want a dashboard before the event definitions are settled.
