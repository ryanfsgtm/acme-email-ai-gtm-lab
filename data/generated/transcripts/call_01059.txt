Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Giovanna, thanks for taking a few minutes. I know the evaluation is already closed, so I am hoping to understand what happened and whether there is anything useful for a future conversation.

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): That is fine. I can give you the short version, although I may be a little blunt. We are a small media company, about one hundred twenty-nine people, and this was not a huge transformation budget.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Blunt is useful. What problem were you trying to solve?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): Our campaigns are scattered. We have newsletters, sponsorship promotions, and audience reactivation happening in different places. I wanted one view of what is working and a simpler way for the team to manage follow-up.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Was the main pressure reporting, campaign execution, or both?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): Both, but reporting was the thing I personally cared about. The team wanted workflow speed. They are tired of copying lists between tools, but I need to explain spend and pipeline—or subscriptions, in our case—to the board.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That sounds familiar. Did you evaluate HubSpot as well?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): Yes. HubSpot was the competitor listed in the process. We already use some of it in another part of the business, so there was a comfort factor.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Comfort factor as in existing relationships, or familiarity with the interface?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): Both. The marketing manager knows it, our operations person knows where to find things, and finance already recognizes the vendor. A new platform would have needed a stronger reason to displace that familiarity.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What did your team like about the alternatives?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): They liked that they could build a basic sequence without asking an engineer. That matters because we do not have a dedicated marketing-automation administrator. One person is doing email, web updates, and audience lists.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): And what did you think was missing?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): I was skeptical about the attribution story. Every vendor shows a beautiful dashboard. Then you ask where the numbers came from and someone says, well, it depends on tracking, field hygiene, and definitions.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is a fair concern. What would a credible report need to show?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): Subscriber growth by source, campaign cost, conversion to a paid product, and the time between a campaign touch and a purchase. Not just opens and clicks. Those are interesting, but they are not the board's question.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Could your current systems provide those fields?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): Some of them. The purchase data is in a separate subscription system. The campaign data is split. We would have needed an integration project, not just a license.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Did anyone estimate the integration effort?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): Not with confidence. That became part of the issue. We were discussing a seven-thousand-five-hundred-forty-seven-dollar annual opportunity, but even a modest implementation could have consumed more time than the software cost justified.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): So the total cost was more than the quote.

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): Exactly. Internal time is real cost. If my marketing manager spends two weeks cleaning historical lists, we have lost two weeks of audience work.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Did the team have a must-have use case they could launch first?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): A reactivation journey for lapsed subscribers. It sounds simple, but our consent language differs by market, and some people are both subscribers and event attendees. We did not want to send the wrong message to the wrong segment.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is a good example of why a small pilot still needs careful data definitions.

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): Right. People call it a quick win, then discover four meanings of “inactive.”

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would have made you more comfortable?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): A concrete pilot with our data, not a generic demo. Show us the subscriber source report, suppress a contact, change a consent state, and explain what happens. Also show the team how long it takes to create a journey after the initial setup.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Would you have involved your operations person in that pilot?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): Absolutely. I was not the operator. I can approve a strategy, but I cannot tell whether the interface is tolerable at 4:45 on a Friday when a sponsor changes the deadline.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That distinction is important. How did the evaluation end?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): We chose not to proceed. It was not a dramatic technical failure. HubSpot was already present, the switching case was not strong enough, and our budget was modest. The decision was marked lost rather than delayed.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Was the competitor the decisive factor?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): Partly. Existing adoption reduced the perceived risk. But do not write down “HubSpot won because it had more features.” That was not the conversation. We did not complete a deep feature comparison.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. What about timing?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): Timing mattered. We were entering a busy publishing cycle. A new implementation would have competed with revenue-producing work.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): If the project resurfaced, who would need to be involved early?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): Me for the business case, the marketing manager for workflow, operations for data, and someone from finance for subscription reporting. Possibly our web developer, but only if the integration cannot be handled through existing tools.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That sounds like a multi-threaded evaluation rather than a single executive sign-off.

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): It is. I can say “yes” to a budget, but if the operator says the process is painful, adoption will fail quietly.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What is one thing vendors usually misunderstand about a company your size?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): They assume small means simple. We have fewer people, but each person carries more systems and more exceptions. We need less administrative overhead, not a smaller version of enterprise complexity.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is a useful takeaway. Is there any reason to revisit this before next year?

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): Only if our subscription reporting project moves ahead and the existing tools stop being adequate. For now, we are staying with what we know.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will record that the opportunity is closed-lost, with existing HubSpot adoption, limited switching economics, implementation uncertainty, and timing as the main factors—not a blanket statement that the product could not meet the need.

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): Yes, that is accurate. And if you contact us again, bring a pilot outline and include the operator from the first meeting.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will. Thanks for the candor, Giovanna.

Giovanna Wolf (CMO): You are welcome. Good luck explaining all those dashboard numbers to the board.
