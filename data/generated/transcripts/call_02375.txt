Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Norma, thanks for meeting. I know you have a full schedule, so perhaps we can begin with what prompted the review of marketing automation now.

Norma Lesch (CMO): We’re growing, but our marketing motion still feels improvised. We’re a UK financial services firm with about 189 employees. The team can produce campaigns, yet I don’t have a dependable view of which activity creates qualified conversations.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Is the challenge mainly reporting, execution speed, or the handoff to sales?

Norma Lesch (CMO): All three, though reporting is the one I hear about in leadership meetings. We have email metrics, website metrics, and CRM stages, but they don’t line up neatly. A campaign can show strong engagement and no obvious pipeline.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What would you want to see in an executive report?

Norma Lesch (CMO): Source, influenced opportunities, conversion by segment, and time to progression. I want enough detail to make budget decisions without asking an analyst to reconstruct every campaign manually.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): And what does the operating team need to deliver that?

Norma Lesch (CMO): They need to build journeys faster. Right now a simple nurture requires a spreadsheet, a request to data operations, and several checks in different systems. That slows us down, especially when regulation or market conditions change.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Can you walk me through a recent campaign?

Norma Lesch (CMO): We promoted a guide for small-business lending. The form captured interest, but the follow-up list was assembled the next morning. Some people received a generic message, and the sales team couldn’t see what they had downloaded. It wasn’t disastrous, just inefficient.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Where did ownership sit during that process?

Norma Lesch (CMO): Marketing owned the form. Sales operations owned the list. A relationship manager decided who followed up. There were three handoffs, and nobody had the complete picture.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): A journey could create the record, preserve the campaign and asset details, and assign a task based on the declared interest. Would the relationship manager need to approve every task?

Norma Lesch (CMO): Not every task. High-intent requests should route quickly. Lower-intent activity can go into nurture. I’m wary of flooding sales with people who downloaded one document and never engaged again.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That sounds like a scoring or qualification rule. How do you define high intent today?

Norma Lesch (CMO): Form language, repeat visits, and sometimes a referral from an existing client. The rule is partly documented and partly in people’s heads, which is not ideal.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We can make the rule explicit and test it against historical examples. It may not be accurate on the first version, but the team can review false positives and false negatives.

Norma Lesch (CMO): Good. I want testing built into the process. We’ve launched campaigns where the email looked fine in preview but the mobile rendering was poor.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): You can use internal test audiences, approval steps, and variant testing. What sample size or decision rule would your team use?

Norma Lesch (CMO): That depends on the audience. Our volume isn’t enormous, so I don’t want a dashboard pretending that a two-percent difference is meaningful after one send.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Then the reporting should show counts and confidence limits, or at least a warning when the sample is too small. Automation can execute the test; it can’t make a sound statistical conclusion from inadequate data.

Norma Lesch (CMO): That answer is reassuring. Vendors usually lead with “optimize everything.”

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Optimization should follow a clear decision framework. What concerns do you have around privacy and financial services data?

Norma Lesch (CMO): Consent, retention, and access by region. We don’t want a campaign manager exporting a broad list simply because the tool makes it easy. We also need a defensible record of why a person entered a communication.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We should include those controls in discovery, not bolt them on after the demo. Different purposes can have different subscription rules, and permissions can limit export and publishing rights.

Norma Lesch (CMO): Would the system integrate with our CRM without making it the only source of truth?

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Yes, but we’d need to agree on ownership for contacts, accounts, consent, opportunity stages, and campaign membership. Ambiguous ownership is a bigger risk than the integration itself.

Norma Lesch (CMO): Our CRM is authoritative for opportunity stage. Marketing should own campaign content and audience logic. Consent is shared with compliance.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That gives us a starting model. Do you have an existing competitor or a project already in procurement?

Norma Lesch (CMO): No formal competitor at this point. Procurement will want at least two options, and our CFO will ask how quickly the investment pays back. I’m not ready to promise a purchase date.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That’s reasonable. What would make the next step worthwhile?

Norma Lesch (CMO): A working session with marketing operations and sales operations, using one real but sanitized campaign. I want to see the build, the approval path, the handoff, and the report. If we can’t agree on those pieces, a polished demo won’t help.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We can prepare that. Who should attend from your side?

Norma Lesch (CMO): Our campaign manager, CRM lead, compliance partner, and someone from sales. Perhaps six people total. Keep it practical; we don’t need a two-hour architecture lecture.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): I’ll plan for sixty minutes and send a proposed agenda. We’ll also list the data fields required so your team can decide what can be masked.

Norma Lesch (CMO): Include attribution definitions. Leadership uses “source” and “influence” almost interchangeably, and that creates arguments.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): I’ll separate first-touch source, campaign membership, and influenced opportunity, then ask your finance team which numbers may be used for planning.

Norma Lesch (CMO): Excellent. One final question: how much work remains with our team after implementation?

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): There will always be ownership work—maintaining fields, approving templates, reviewing performance, and handling exceptions. The goal is to reduce repetitive coordination, not eliminate judgment.

Norma Lesch (CMO): That’s realistic. Send the agenda and an indicative scope. I’ll bring the right people together, but procurement timing remains open.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): I’ll do that. Thanks, Norma.

Norma Lesch (CMO): Thank you. If the next session connects operational speed to credible reporting, we’ll have something worth evaluating.
