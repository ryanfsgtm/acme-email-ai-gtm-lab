Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Good morning, Meagan. Thanks for joining. I understand you’re representing privacy for Hudson’s marketing automation review.

Meagan Schoen (Privacy): That’s right. I’m not the system owner, and I should say upfront that the opportunity was marked closed-lost with no decision. We are documenting what would be required if the project comes back.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That context is helpful. What triggered the privacy review originally?

Meagan Schoen (Privacy): Marketing wanted more coordinated retail campaigns across a large organization—about 1,080 employees—and they expected to connect several customer and loyalty data sources. Privacy was asked to assess the proposed flow, not to recommend a vendor.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What was the main concern?

Meagan Schoen (Privacy): Purpose limitation. A customer who bought from one brand or channel should not automatically become eligible for every campaign. The draft diagrams treated “customer” as one broad audience, which is too loose.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Would consent segmentation solve that by itself?

Meagan Schoen (Privacy): No. Consent is necessary but not sufficient. We also need lawful purpose, retention rules, access controls, and a clear explanation of which team is using which data. People often jump straight to a preference center.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Understood. How is the current process governed?

Meagan Schoen (Privacy): Inconsistently. Some regions have mature review practices; others rely on local spreadsheets and manager judgment. There is a central policy, but operational evidence is uneven.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Does that mean a centralized platform would be difficult?

Meagan Schoen (Privacy): Not necessarily. Centralization could improve visibility. But it could also make a bad data model operate at much greater scale. That is the part I wanted the project team to hear.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What would you require before a technical validation?

Meagan Schoen (Privacy): A data inventory, field-level classification, documented roles, and a retention schedule. I’d also need to know where processing occurs and whether support personnel can access production records.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): We can provide security documentation and discuss regional controls. Would pseudonymous identifiers help with the early pilot?

Meagan Schoen (Privacy): They might, depending on the workflow. If the system still receives enough information to target a person, pseudonymization does not remove the privacy obligation. It changes the risk profile; it does not make the issue disappear.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That’s a fair distinction. How would you define a safe first use case?

Meagan Schoen (Privacy): A narrow audience with an established purpose, limited users, no sensitive attributes, and a measurable deletion process. A campaign to existing loyalty members could be easier than importing every historical customer.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): What blocked the previous decision?

Meagan Schoen (Privacy): No single blocker. The business case was not strong enough to prioritize the work, and the data owners disagreed about who should fund cleanup. So “no decision” was more accurate than “privacy rejected it.”

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That nuance matters. If the initiative returns, should privacy join discovery from the beginning?

Meagan Schoen (Privacy): Yes, but not every meeting. Give me the proposed data flow and decisions requiring review. I do not need a product tour.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): I’ll send a concise flow template and a list of control questions. Are there specific regions we should use as examples?

Meagan Schoen (Privacy): Australia and New Zealand would be useful, but don’t infer that their requirements are identical. We have group policy plus local interpretations.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Noted. Would a file-based test be preferable to an integration?

Meagan Schoen (Privacy): For privacy assessment, yes. It limits the scope and makes the fields visible. For operations, it may be less realistic, so both phases should be described honestly.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): And if marketing says speed is the priority?

Meagan Schoen (Privacy): Then show the controls that preserve speed. Approval queues, audience versioning, audit logs, and deletion evidence are practical features. But they need owners; a control nobody operates is decorative.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): I’ll capture owners as an explicit workstream. Is there anything we should avoid promising?

Meagan Schoen (Privacy): Avoid saying the platform makes us compliant. Compliance is a program and a set of decisions. A vendor can support it, but cannot certify our whole use case.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Agreed. I’ll frame the materials as inputs for assessment. Any final point?

Meagan Schoen (Privacy): Keep the project status accurate. It is not approved onboarding, and there is no committed date. If the business revives it, we can revisit the narrow use case first.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): That gives us a clear path. I’ll send the flow template and follow-up questions today.

Meagan Schoen (Privacy): Good. Thanks, Nina.

Nina Okafor (Solutions Consultant): Thank you.
