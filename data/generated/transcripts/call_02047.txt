Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Melvina, hello. Thanks for joining. Before we begin, are you still in the hospital office, or have you escaped to somewhere quieter?

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): Still in the office, unfortunately. There is construction on the floor below, so if you hear drilling, that is not a new marketing automation feature.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will try not to compete with it. I understand Grimes has a very large and distributed healthcare organization. Could you describe how marketing operates today?

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): We have central marketing, regional teams, service-line leaders, and a substantial communications function. The headcount is around forty-five thousand, though not all of them touch campaigns. We support hospitals, outpatient clinics, and research programs. Every group thinks its audience is unique, and occasionally they are right.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What made this a priority for your team?

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): Fragmentation. We have several databases, inconsistent consent histories, and no reliable way to coordinate a patient education journey across service lines. One team sends an email, another sends a text, and the patient calls asking why we know so much about them.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That last point sounds especially important. Is the primary concern compliance, experience, or operational efficiency?

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): All three, but compliance is the floor. We cannot optimize a process that should not exist. Experience comes next. Efficiency matters because our marketers spend too much time preparing lists and reconciling reports.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): How are approvals handled?

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): It depends on the campaign. Clinical content requires subject-matter review. Legal reviews claims and privacy language. Regional teams may need approval for local providers. We have a central calendar, but people still maintain private calendars in Excel.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): So a shared calendar alone will not solve governance unless ownership and approvals are visible too.

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): Correct. We tried a shared calendar. It became a graveyard of colors.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What audiences are you most interested in initially?

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): Existing patients who have opted into educational communications, caregivers, and prospective patients who request information. We need strict separation between care-related communications and promotional activity. Someone may attend a webinar without wanting a product-style nurture sequence.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That distinction should be represented in the data model and permissions. Which systems would need to exchange information?

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): CRM, our web forms, the patient portal in a limited way, event registration, and the analytics warehouse. I am deliberately saying “limited way” because clinical systems are not marketing playgrounds.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Understood. We can begin with consented marketing data and keep clinical records out of scope. What does your IT and security review require?

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): Data-flow diagrams, subprocessor details, encryption information, incident response, role-based access, and deletion procedures. Procurement also needs a measurable business case. The amount in the opportunity is about three hundred twenty thousand annually, so nobody will approve a hand-waving promise.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What measures would be credible?

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): Reduction in campaign preparation time, fewer duplicate contacts, faster approval cycles, and better attendance for education events. I am wary of using open rates as the headline metric. Healthcare audiences behave differently, and privacy tools distort opens.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is sensible. Could we define a baseline for the first ninety days?

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): Yes, if we can obtain it. The trouble is that each region reports differently. In one report, “campaign sent” means queued; in another, it means delivered.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Then measurement alignment is itself a deliverable. We can agree on definitions before comparing results.

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): I would appreciate that. Also, do not assume the central team can mandate adoption. We can sponsor a standard, but service lines will need a reason to use it.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What reasons have worked in prior change programs?

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): Removing work. If a regional marketer sees that the system handles suppression and reporting better than their spreadsheet, they will try it. If we tell them only that headquarters wants consistency, we lose.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Would a pilot with two contrasting service lines help?

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): Possibly. Choose one mature region and one that is currently struggling. A pilot made only of enthusiasts produces a beautiful result nobody can reproduce.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I like that design. Who else should be involved?

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): Privacy, information security, the patient communications lead, one regional marketing director, analytics, and someone from the call center. The call center hears complaints before the dashboard does.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is a useful perspective. Do patients currently have a clear preference center?

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): Not consistently. Some forms have a global opt-out, others have separate topics, and older records have a source but no timestamp. We need a policy for ambiguity.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We should not silently infer consent. We can separate records by confidence and route uncertain cases for review.

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): Good. I will need that in the implementation approach, not buried in an appendix.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Noted. What timeline is realistic?

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): The executive committee wants a recommendation this quarter. A production rollout before then would be unrealistic. We can complete discovery, security, and a controlled pilot, then decide.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That aligns with the opportunity being closed-won on June seventeenth, but today’s work should still test the assumptions behind the purchase.

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): Exactly. A closed deal is not proof that every assumption was right. We still have to make it useful.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Let me summarize: start with consented educational communications, pilot two service lines, map approvals and data flows, define baseline metrics, and keep clinical systems outside scope unless governance changes.

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): Add adoption and call-center feedback. And document who can stop a campaign.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Yes, campaign-stop authority and escalation. I will send a discovery brief and schedule a workshop with privacy and security. Is there a preferred time?

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): Wednesday afternoon works, unless the construction crew discovers another floor.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will put Wednesday at two and include a backup dial-in. Any final concern?

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): The platform must support regional autonomy without creating twenty-seven incompatible processes. If we cannot find that balance, the technology will be blamed for an operating-model problem.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is an important boundary. We will surface it early.

Melvina Rice (VP Marketing): Then we have a useful next step. Thanks, Evelyn.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Thank you, Melvina. I will send the notes today.
