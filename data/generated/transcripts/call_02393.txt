Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Petra, hello. I see you joined from Melbourne. Is this still a good time, or has the day already run away from you?

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): It is late afternoon here, so the day is attempting to escape. I have thirty-seven minutes, though. I want to be candid: the project is currently marked closed-lost on our side, but I am willing to explain why.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I appreciate that. I saw the opportunity for Klein - Halvorson Marketing Automation, valued at 29,689, and the outcome listed as lost with a March close date. My goal is to understand the decision, not restart a sales cycle by force.

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): Good, because the main issue was not that your team was difficult. We are a manufacturing company with a lot of regional variation, and our process looked simpler in the initial brief than it really is.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Tell me what was hidden in the brief.

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): We have one corporate marketing team, several product divisions, and distributors who receive different campaigns. The database is not clean. Some contacts belong to a distributor relationship, some to a plant, and some to a trade-show list that nobody owns anymore.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): So the first challenge is governance and segmentation, before automation.

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): Exactly. The sales team wanted automated nurture immediately. I wanted a survivable data model. Those are not the same thing.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What did you need the system to do in the first phase?

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): Basic lead capture, routing, and campaign reporting. We also wanted to score engagement, but our leadership uses “qualified lead” to mean three different things. One director means a form fill; another means a conversation with a known project.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That ambiguity can make automation look broken even when the workflow is behaving as configured.

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): Right. We had a workshop where everyone agreed on the words and disagreed on the definitions. Then our data engineer showed us that the same company appears under four names.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Did you choose another platform?

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): We expanded an existing tool instead. It is not perfect, but the procurement path was familiar and the regional teams already had logins. The CFO preferred a smaller near-term commitment.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Was cost the deciding factor?

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): Partly. The 29,689 proposal was within the transformation budget, but implementation effort was harder to estimate. The cheaper option looked less risky because we could start with a narrow set of campaigns.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That sounds like risk perception more than feature comparison.

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): Yes. Also, our IT group was worried about another integration project. We have a CRM, ERP, service system, and an old event application. Marketing says “just connect it,” and IT hears “support four new failure modes.”

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would have made the risk more manageable?

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): A phased implementation plan with a firm boundary. For example, start with two regions and one product line, define the record owner, and prove reporting before connecting every source.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We could have proposed that, although I understand why the broader business case made the original proposal look more complete.

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): It looked complete on paper. In the meeting, people asked whether we could preserve local consent language and different fiscal calendars. Those questions were not in the requirements document.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How are you handling consent now?

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): Manually for the most part. It is not elegant. Each region maintains an export, and marketing operations reconciles it before a campaign. That takes time, but people trust the process because they know who checks it.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would automation be acceptable if the approval step remained visible?

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): Probably. The fear was not automation itself. It was a silent suppression or an accidental send that nobody could explain.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is useful distinction. What did you think of our reporting capabilities?

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): Strong in the demonstration, but we never validated whether the attribution model matched our long sales cycles. A distributor may influence a deal for months before the opportunity is created. Last-touch reporting would make marketing look unproductive.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can support multiple attribution views, but the model still depends on agreed events and clean timestamps.

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): Exactly. I wanted that caveat in the proposal. It is better to say “we can measure this after instrumentation” than to show a beautiful dashboard with invented certainty.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would you measure first if the project reopened?

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): Conversion from named account to engaged contact, response to product-specific content, and the time from marketing handoff to sales acceptance. I would not begin with revenue attribution.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Why not?

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): Because our revenue data is delayed and split across systems. We could spend six months arguing about credit instead of improving routing.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a pragmatic sequence. Is there anything in our approach that you would actively avoid next time?

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): Avoid assuming the central team represents all regions. Our Australian operation is not a copy of our German operation. Also, do not schedule a technical workshop before confirming who owns the taxonomy.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Who owns it today?

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): Nobody entirely. Marketing operations maintains campaign fields, sales operations owns opportunity stages, and IT owns integrations. That gap was a larger blocker than we admitted.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Could a short governance project precede the software decision?

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): Yes, but it would need an executive sponsor. I can coordinate, not compel regional leaders.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will record that the lost outcome was driven by implementation confidence, internal ownership, and near-term budget risk—not a simple lack of interest.

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): That is accurate. We liked the product. We did not believe we were ready to operate it well.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will send a brief summary with a phased outline, and I will not attach a new quote unless you ask.

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): Thank you. If our governance work produces a real owner, I may come back. For now, the existing-tool expansion is underway.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. Good luck with the regional cleanup—and the escaped day.

Petra Schumm (Marketing Operations): It has been contained for now. Thanks, Amelia.
