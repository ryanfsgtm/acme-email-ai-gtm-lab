Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Jewel, good morning. I see it’s six forty-five for you, so I appreciate the early start.

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): It’s fine. The coffee is doing most of the attending, but I’m here.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We’ll keep the review focused. I’d like to cover adoption, outcomes, open issues, and priorities for the next quarter. Anything you want to add?

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): Data quality. It’s improved, but we still have duplicate accounts and inconsistent lifecycle values. The dashboards look better than the underlying records in some places.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Let’s start there. What changed since the last review?

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): We cleaned about eight hundred records and standardized the main stages. But acquisitions are still entering accounts with different naming conventions. Also, sales reps create opportunities before the buying committee is properly associated.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That would affect both reporting and routing. Are the duplicates concentrated in one region?

Jewel Sawayn (Account Executive): Mostly North America, although the source systems are global. The issue isn’t just geography; it’s that different teams define “account” differently.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can review the matching rules and establish an exception queue. How is the team using the platform day to day?

Jewel Sawayn (Account Executive): Operations uses it heavily. Sales adoption is mixed. The reps like the alerts when they are relevant, but they ignore them when every website visit becomes a notification.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Can we look at the alert configuration?

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): Yes. We need to separate research activity from a meaningful buying signal. An executive visiting once should not trigger the same alert as a known opportunity returning to a pricing page.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That’s a good distinction. We could revise thresholds by lifecycle stage and account tier.

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): And by role, possibly. A developer downloading documentation behaves differently from a procurement contact reviewing security content.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How confident are you in the campaign reporting?

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): Directionally confident. I would not use it for a compensation decision yet. Campaign members are missing from older opportunities, and the CRM timestamps are not always trustworthy.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would make it usable for that purpose?

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): A defined attribution policy, mandatory campaign association for new opportunities, and a reconciliation report. We need to know when a number is incomplete rather than presenting it with false precision.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can help document the policy and build the reconciliation view. Are there any integrations causing trouble?

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): The webinar connector occasionally creates duplicate contacts. The support integration is stable. Our warehouse sync also has a delay, which means yesterday’s activity can appear in today’s report.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Is that delay acceptable operationally?

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): For weekly reporting, yes. For routing, no. A sales alert based on yesterday’s activity is sometimes too late.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then we should keep routing on the direct event feed and reserve the warehouse for analysis.

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): That’s what I’d prefer, but we need to check whether all relevant events are available directly.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Let’s put that in the technical action list. What outcomes did the team see this quarter?

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): Build time improved. A campaign that used to take two days now takes most of a morning. The bigger benefit is that operations can make changes without waiting for engineering.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That’s significant. Any downside?

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): More people can build things, so governance matters. We found three journeys with slightly different suppression rules. None caused a major incident, but they could have produced inconsistent communication.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We should review templates and add a prelaunch checklist.

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): Yes, plus an owner for every active journey. “Team” ownership sounds democratic until something breaks.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Agreed. How are you handling testing?

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): We test content and timing, but we’re not yet testing holdout groups consistently. The team wants a quick answer, while the sales cycle is too long for a same-week conclusion.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can help define a minimum sample and a review cadence.

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): That would help. Also, please don’t frame every result as revenue. Some programs are for education or retention.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Point taken. We’ll separate operational, engagement, pipeline, and retention outcomes.

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): Good. Next quarter, my priorities are deduplication, alert redesign, governance, and a cleaner opportunity history.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll capture those. From our side, we’ll provide a data model workshop, review the direct event feed, and share a campaign governance template.

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): Add training for regional administrators. Central operations can’t be the only group that understands the rules.

Jewel Sawayn (Revenue Operations): Yes. We have several launches in the same month. If the workshop requires a large committee, it will slip.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can start with you and one systems owner, then circulate the decisions. No need to make every stakeholder attend every session.
