Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Jarrell, thanks for making time. Before we start, are you still in the office or have I caught you between trains?

Jarrell Wilkinson (Sales Operations): Between meetings, fortunately. I’m at home today, and the dog has already objected to one call, so if you hear a bark, that’s my colleague from the next room.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll treat that as an early stakeholder introduction. I’d like to understand how sales operations supports the revenue process, where marketing automation fits, and whether there is a project worth exploring. I have a few assumptions, but I’d rather not make you correct all of them.

Jarrell Wilkinson (Sales Operations): That’s fair. Our business is spread across several media properties. We have a central sales team, regional teams, and a fairly complicated partner channel. The systems work, but they do not always talk to each other cleanly.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Which system causes the most friction?

Jarrell Wilkinson (Sales Operations): The CRM is the source of truth in theory. In practice, campaign responses arrive from several platforms, and the definitions differ. A click in one system is a response in another. Sales managers then ask why the numbers do not match.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Is the pressure primarily around reporting, execution, or both?

Jarrell Wilkinson (Sales Operations): Both, but reporting is louder. Leadership wants to know which programs create qualified conversations. Operations wants fewer lists, imports, and manual corrections. Marketing wants to move quickly and says operations slows them down. Everyone has a reasonable complaint.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That sounds familiar. How are campaigns built today?

Jarrell Wilkinson (Account Executive): Some are built centrally. Some are built by a property team using a separate tool. The central team supplies templates, but local teams customize them. We have naming standards, although adherence is—well, aspirational.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What happens when a campaign needs a change after launch?

Jarrell Wilkinson (Account Executive): Depends who owns it. The central team can make the change. Local teams sometimes duplicate the campaign because they do not want to wait. Then we have three versions, different suppression rules, and no one is sure which audience was final.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Have you seen a business impact from that, or is it mainly an administrative headache?

Jarrell Wilkinson (Sales Operations): Both. We have sent follow-ups to people who had already spoken with sales. We have also missed a handoff because a partner lead was classified as a newsletter response. Nothing catastrophic, but enough to create distrust.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would a better process look like?

Jarrell Wilkinson (Sales Operations): Shared definitions, controlled templates, and a reliable handoff. I would also like visibility into the actual journey, not just a campaign record saying “active.” If a lead is waiting three weeks between steps, we should know why.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How much control should local teams have?

Jarrell Wilkinson (Sales Operations): More than they have now, but within boundaries. They know their audiences better. Operations should define required fields, consent rules, and routing. Local marketers should be able to create the content and timing without opening a ticket for every minor adjustment.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That balance is usually important. What are the consent requirements across regions?

Jarrell Wilkinson (Sales Operations): We operate in several jurisdictions, so consent and opt-out handling matter. Our legal team reviews the policy, but the implementation is spread across tools. The most serious concern is inconsistent suppression. If someone opts out in one property, we need to understand whether that suppresses them everywhere.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Is that documented today?

Jarrell Wilkinson (Sales Operations): Partly. We have documentation, but it is not always current. The people who built the original integrations have moved to other roles.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): If you were evaluating a platform, who would be involved?

Jarrell Wilkinson (Sales Operations): Marketing operations, sales operations, IT, legal, and probably one regional marketing lead. Finance would want a business case. The sales leaders would not attend every demo, but they would ask whether the handoff actually improves conversion.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would make the project urgent?

Jarrell Wilkinson (Sales Operations): A major CRM cleanup is planned for next year. If we wait until then, we may carry the current problems forward. But I should be clear: no budget has been allocated specifically for a replacement.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That’s useful. Would a process-mapping workshop be a reasonable next step, before talking about a full platform decision?

Jarrell Wilkinson (Sales Operations): Yes, if it is practical. I do not need a generic maturity assessment with green, yellow, and red boxes. Map one campaign from intake through routing and reporting. Show where ownership changes.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can do that. Which campaign would be representative?

Jarrell Wilkinson (Sales Operations): A partner-sponsored webinar. It has registration, attendance, follow-up, account matching, and sales routing. It also crosses the central and local teams.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Excellent. I’ll send a short agenda and ask our solutions consultant to prepare questions around consent and attribution. Is Tuesday morning too soon?

Jarrell Wilkinson (Sales Operations): Tuesday is full. Thursday at ten might work, but I need to verify with marketing operations.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll hold Thursday tentatively and send the materials today.

Jarrell Wilkinson (Sales Operations): Please do. And send an example of how you distinguish engagement from a qualified response. That debate will come up immediately.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. Thanks for the candid overview, Jarrell. I’ll let you get to the next meeting—and I hope the canine stakeholder has approved the conversation.

Jarrell Wilkinson (Sales Operations): He has not, but he has stopped barking. That is progress. Talk Thursday, pending confirmation.
