Lonie Friesen (IT): No, but it went to a broad entertainment audience. The creative team thinks the subject line was the issue. IT thinks the frequency cap was not applied across channels.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Which channels were active for those recipients?

Lonie Friesen (IT): Email and in-app. The in-app notification was scheduled separately, so I cannot say whether the same people received both on the same day without querying the event table.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That query would be worthwhile before deciding the cap failed. Do you have a recent example of a customer receiving too many messages?

Lonie Friesen (Account Executive): Yes, but the account was shared by two household members. One person received the email, and another received the in-app message. That is still a customer-experience concern, but it is not necessarily a duplicate-contact problem.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Exactly. It weakens a simple “the platform sent duplicates” explanation. Can we separate individual identity from household behavior in the report?

Lonie Friesen (Account Executive): We can, if the household ID is present. Some older records do not have it.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Good to distinguish. Let us move to adoption. Which teams are using the system regularly?
