Priya Shah (Account Executive): What tends to take the most time for you personally?

Tatum Schowalter (Lifecycle Marketing): Building and maintaining journeys. A simple nurture is fine. Anything with product interest, geography, language, and account status becomes a small archaeological dig. We duplicate programs, change one branch, then discover another region has an older version. Testing is also painful because we cannot easily see the whole path in one place.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): When you say testing, are you mostly checking message rendering, or the decision logic as well?

Tatum Schowalter (Lifecycle Marketing): Both. Rendering is the visible part. The dangerous part is the logic. Someone changes a field definition in the CRM, and suddenly a segment is empty. Or a contact has two product interests and gets routed down the wrong path. We usually find out from a sales rep forwarding an email and saying, “Why did my customer get this?”

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds like a governance issue as much as an execution issue. Who owns the definitions?

Tatum Schowalter (Lifecycle Marketing): Marketing owns the campaigns, sales operations owns some CRM fields, and IT manages integrations. No one owns the entire customer journey. We meet about it, but the meeting ends with three action items and an attachment nobody opens.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. How are leads handed to sales today?

Tatum Schowalter (Lifecycle Marketing): A score, mostly. It is not useless, but it is not trusted. A download can create a high score even when the person is not in a buying cycle. Meanwhile, an existing customer asking about a new line may look cold because they did not fill out the expected form. Sales wants fewer alerts, and marketing wants evidence that the nurture is doing something.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): If you could improve one measurement first, what would it be?

Tatum Schowalter (Lifecycle Marketing): Influence by source and journey. We can report opens and clicks, but executives ask which programs produce qualified conversations and revenue. Priya Shah (Account Executive): That makes sense. In a long cycle, you need both the operational view and the executive view. On the operational side, teams can build reusable journeys, inspect every branch, and test variations before activation.
