Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Hi, Kayla. Thanks for joining. I understand you support implementations for Grimes Group, so I would like to focus on what makes projects succeed or stall.

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): That is a fair focus. We are a small professional-services firm, and the people implementing the system are usually also delivering client work.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): How many people would be involved in a rollout?

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): Probably three directly, with another four contributing part-time. We have about sixty employees total, but only a small operations team.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What prompted the discussion now?

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): We need a better way to coordinate campaigns for our clients. At present, every consultant has a slightly different spreadsheet and email process. It works until someone is on holiday.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Are you looking for an internal system or something you would deploy for clients?

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): Initially internal. Later, perhaps client-facing services. I do not want us to promise a managed service before we understand the administration effort.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What would the first workflow look like?

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): A client signs off on a campaign, we schedule it, collect responses, and report results back to the account lead. The approval step is important. We cannot have a draft going out because somebody clicked the wrong button.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Would clients need direct access?

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): Not at first. They need email summaries and perhaps a review link. Direct access creates permission questions that we are not ready to answer.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): How do you currently track approvals?

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): Email threads, mostly. Sometimes a PDF gets attached and someone replies “approved” without specifying which version. It is not ideal.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What would make a new platform unacceptable?

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): If setup takes weeks for each client, or if consultants need admin training before they can do basic work. Also, we handle confidential client information, so access controls and audit history matter.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Would a standard template with client-specific fields reduce the setup time?

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): Probably. But templates need guardrails. A consultant should be able to adapt copy and dates without changing the approval policy.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): How many client programs might you run at once?

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): Ten to fifteen, depending on the season. Some are tiny and only need a monthly newsletter. Others have multiple audiences and a weekly cadence.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Would you need separate workspaces for each client?

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): That is one option, although I am not sure the cost would make sense for our smaller clients. We need to compare isolation with a simpler folder or permission model.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That makes sense. Do you already have an implementation timeline?

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): No fixed date. We are gathering requirements now. The partners want a recommendation before the next quarterly planning meeting.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Who will approve that recommendation?

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): One managing partner and our operations lead. Finance will review the spend, but the partners care more about delivery risk than feature count.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What would you show them?

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): A short demonstration using a fictional client, including approval, revision, launch, and reporting. If you show only the happy path, they will assume the messy parts do not exist.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can include a rejected draft and a changed audience. Are there integration requirements?

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): Calendar and CRM would be useful. We also use a document repository for client assets. None of those integrations are perfectly standard because each client has a different setup.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): So a flexible process may matter more than deep out-of-the-box integration.

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): Exactly. We cannot rebuild our business around one client’s software stack.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What about reporting?

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): We need a client-friendly summary and an internal view of effort, status, and risk. Open rates are useful, but they are not enough to judge whether the project was delivered well.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That distinction is helpful. Would you be open to a requirements session next week?

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): Yes, but keep it to forty-five minutes. We are a small team and billable work is already booked.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will send an agenda with four sections: approval controls, templates, permissions, and reporting.

Kayla Roberts (Implementation Partner): Add onboarding effort, please. If the first month requires a dedicated administrator, we need to know that before recommending anything.
