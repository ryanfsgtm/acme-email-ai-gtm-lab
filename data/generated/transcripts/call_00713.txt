Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Hi, Kathlyn. Thanks for joining. How has the week been treating you?

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): Busy in the predictable way—three meetings about simplifying the number of meetings. But I am glad we could talk.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I promise to keep this one useful. I understand Carroll, Sauer and Pollich is reviewing marketing automation while the opportunity is in procurement. What prompted the review now?

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): We have grown enough that our current tools are starting to fight each other. Email lives in one system, event registrations in another, and the sales team keeps a private spreadsheet as the unofficial source of truth.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That sounds painful. What breaks most often?

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): Handoffs. Marketing sends a contact to sales, sales says the person is not ready, then the contact continues receiving the same nurture because nobody updates the status. Sometimes the data is technically present but nobody trusts it.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Is the main objective better data quality, faster campaign execution, or visibility for leadership?

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): All three, but in that order for my team. Leadership asks for pipeline contribution, while the operators need to build a journey without filing a ticket for every small change.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How many people are actually building campaigns today?

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): Six regularly, perhaps ten if you count occasional contributors. They are good marketers, not dedicated automation engineers. The system needs guardrails, but it also needs to be understandable.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What does a typical campaign look like?

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): A webinar or a product education series. We segment by country, customer type, and engagement. Then there is usually an approval step, except when the deadline is close and someone decides the approval can happen in spirit.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That last part is familiar. Where does the process slow down?

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): Copy review and list preparation. Building the email is not the bottleneck. Getting confidence that the right audience is included and the wrong audience is excluded takes most of the time.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How are lists created today?

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): A mixture of saved filters and exports. The exports are the problem. Once a list leaves the system, we lose a little context every time it gets edited. Then someone asks why the count changed and we reconstruct the answer from email threads.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would you want a centralized audience model or more autonomy for each country team?

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): A controlled middle. Country teams need local language and legal rules, but definitions like active prospect or customer should not mean five different things.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That points to shared data definitions with local content and permissions. What systems would need to connect?

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): Our CRM, the event platform, and the website forms. Finance is not in scope. Sales operations owns the CRM, and they are supportive in principle, which is not the same as having someone available for implementation.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Who else should be involved in a decision?

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): Sales operations, IT for security and integration, and probably one regional marketing lead. Our managing director will want a short business case, not a forty-slide feature inventory.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would make the business case credible?

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): Less manual list work, clearer attribution, and proof that campaigns can be launched faster without increasing mistakes. If you tell me only that the platform has more buttons, we will not get far.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Fair. How do you measure attribution now?

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): Mostly first-touch and last-touch reports from the CRM. Neither is convincing. We can say a campaign touched an opportunity, but we cannot explain the sequence of interactions well enough for leadership.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would an influence model based on contact activity and opportunity stages help?

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): It would, provided sales accepts the definitions. We have had reporting projects fail because marketing built a beautiful dashboard and sales considered it fictional.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then adoption is partly a governance question, not just a reporting question.

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): Exactly. Also, do not assume automation means “set and forget.” We need testing, exception handling, and someone accountable when a journey behaves strangely.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Can you give an example of strange behavior?

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): Last month, a contact attended an event and entered the post-event series twice because the registration record arrived late. It was not catastrophic, but it looked careless. We need visibility into why someone entered a journey.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is useful. We should show entry criteria, change history, and suppression logic in a working session. Are there timing constraints around the procurement review?

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): Procurement wants a recommendation this quarter. Implementation would likely start with one region, not the entire company. We need a realistic rollout plan and a clear estimate of internal effort.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I can bring an implementation lead and prepare a discovery workshop with your sales-operations partner. We can map the current process before discussing configuration.

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): Good. Please include the regional lead early. Otherwise we will design something elegant that cannot handle local consent requirements.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. What would make you say no?

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): If the platform requires too much specialist administration, if reporting is impossible to explain, or if the implementation assumes everyone changes behavior overnight. We can change process, but not by pretending the old process does not exist.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is a reasonable test. I will send a proposed agenda: current-state mapping, audience definitions, CRM handoff, reporting, governance, and a phased rollout.

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): Add testing and ownership. Those are the unglamorous parts that determine whether the program works after launch.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Added. I will also keep the commercial discussion tied to the first phase so the estimate does not become abstract.

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): Perfect. Send dates for the workshop and I will coordinate the other participants.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Thanks, Kathlyn. This was helpful.

Kathlyn Kuhn (VP Marketing): Likewise. And if the next meeting has forty slides, I reserve the right to leave after slide three.
