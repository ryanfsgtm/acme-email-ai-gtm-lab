Felicity Stiedemann (Revenue Operations): Nearly impossible. We have plants in France and other European markets, distributors, direct accounts, and long buying cycles. A campaign may influence an opportunity six months later, but nobody agrees how to record that.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Are you looking for campaign orchestration, governance, reporting, or all three?

Felicity Stiedemann (Revenue Operations): Governance first. Orchestration is useful only if we can trust the data. I want common lifecycle stages, ownership rules, and an audit trail when someone changes a field or imports a list.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): ACME can provide centralized lifecycle definitions, permissions, approval workflows, and reporting across business units. How centralized is your CRM today?

Felicity Stiedemann (Revenue Operations): Technically centralized, operationally not. France has one set of fields, Germany another, and the distributor team has a workaround that everyone pretends is temporary. It has been temporary for three years.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What is driving the current timing?

Felicity Stiedemann (Revenue Operations): The executive team wants a reliable forecast and marketing contribution report before the next planning cycle. Procurement lists January 30 as the target close date, but I’m not treating that as a promise. We still need architecture, data protection, finance, and regional sign-off.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Who owns the final decision?

Felicity Stiedemann (Revenue Operations): The chief revenue officer sponsors it. IT security can veto it. Finance controls the business case. Regional leaders can delay it by asking for exceptions.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would the first phase include?

Felicity Stiedemann (Revenue Operations): Standardize definitions, connect CRM and marketing activity, and create dashboards for pipeline influence and handoff speed. We would probably start with France and Germany, then expand.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Why those regions?

Felicity Stiedemann (Revenue Operations): Enough volume to prove value, but not every exception at once. France has the executive attention; Germany has the strongest sales-ops manager. If they cannot agree, the global rollout is fantasy.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That’s a useful test. What data quality issues are most serious?

Felicity Stiedemann (Revenue Operations): Duplicates, missing industry codes, inconsistent parent accounts, and old leads with no owner. Also, marketing imports lists without telling sales. Nothing malicious. Just a process that rewards speed.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can establish import permissions and required fields, then create a remediation queue. Would you want regional administrators to retain some control?

Felicity Stiedemann (Revenue Operations): Yes, but controlled control. They need local flexibility for language and consent, not permission to invent a new lifecycle stage called “hot-ish.”

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can distinguish local campaign content from global data definitions. How do you measure success?

Felicity Stiedemann (Revenue Operations): Fewer unowned leads, faster accepted handoffs, fewer duplicate records, and a report leadership trusts. Revenue attribution would be helpful, but if we promise perfect attribution in a manufacturing cycle, everyone will lose credibility.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Agreed. We should define an initial measurement layer and label longer-term attribution as a later maturity step.

Felicity Stiedemann (Revenue Operations): Exactly. I’m wary of vendors who claim the dashboard solves the organization. It doesn’t.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would make the business case persuasive to finance?

Felicity Stiedemann (Revenue Operations): Less manual reporting, fewer wasted follow-ups, and a defensible operating model. They’ll ask how the annual investment—about seventy-seven thousand two hundred eighty dollars—relates to measurable savings and pipeline.
