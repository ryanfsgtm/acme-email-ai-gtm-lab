Isadore Kuhn-Stracke (VP Marketing): Exactly. And there is a cultural wrinkle. Our consultants are very protective of client relationships. Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is helpful. We can talk about controls and suppression rules, but first, what prompted the evaluation now?

Isadore Kuhn-Stracke (VP Marketing): The board asked for a clearer view of demand generation. We have grown through referrals for years, but referrals are difficult to forecast. Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Understood. What does the current process look like for a campaign?

Isadore Kuhn-Stracke (VP Marketing): Marketing creates an audience export, an agency formats the email, and then someone in operations manually uploads responses. If a person clicks several times, that is usually visible. If they attend an event, someone may reconcile the list later. If they reply directly, it disappears into an inbox. There is no shared scoring model.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Does the team have a CRM that can be used as the source of truth?

Isadore Kuhn-Stracke (VP Marketing): We have one, but adoption is uneven. The CRM is strongest for active opportunities and weakest for early engagement. Some people call it the system of record; others call it the system they update before a quarterly review.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Fair distinction. If you had a connected marketing layer, what would be the first workflow you would build?

Isadore Kuhn-Stracke (VP Marketing): A post-event journey. Someone registers for a breakfast briefing, receives useful follow-up, and then the right adviser is alerted if the person shows continued interest. Not a hard sales handoff after one click. A measured sequence.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We could define engagement thresholds and route based on service line, territory, and relationship owner. Would different offices need different messaging?
