Priya Shah (Account Executive): Evie, good to see you. Congratulations again on getting the program approved. How are things in Sydney?

Evie Yundt (CMO): Busy, but in the good way. We have leadership alignment now, which is a relief. The teams are still asking whether “approved” means they can start building tomorrow.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We should probably prevent that interpretation before it becomes a support ticket. Today I would like to turn the agreement into an implementation plan: owners, milestones, data, training, and the first measurable outcomes.

Evie Yundt (CMO): Please do. The commercial decision is complete, but operationally we are not ready. We have 842 employees across Australia and New Zealand, and every regional leader believes their campaign is the urgent one.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is common. We can create a release sequence instead of trying to launch every use case at once. What is the first business outcome leadership expects?

Evie Yundt (CMO): Better follow-up on qualified inquiries and clearer visibility into marketing contribution to pipeline. We also want to reduce the handoff delay between marketing and business development.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Those are good anchors. Which regions and service lines should be in the first release?

Evie Yundt (CMO): Australia professional services first, then New Zealand. But our New Zealand team has a strong existing event program, and they will object if they are treated as a later experiment.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can include a representative New Zealand event journey in the pilot while keeping the main rollout focused. That gives us a counterexample to a purely Australia-first design without doubling the scope.

Evie Yundt (CMO): I like that. The pilot should feel fair, not like headquarters is taking the best tools for itself.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Let us talk data. Which system is authoritative for contacts and account relationships?

Evie Yundt (CMO): CRM for accounts and contacts. Our event platform holds registrations and attendance. The CRM data is better than it was last year, but there are duplicates and old role titles.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We will need a data inventory and a definition of readiness. I suggest profiling duplicates, consent status, email validity, and the fields required for routing.

Evie Yundt (CMO): Consent is important. Australia and New Zealand do not have identical practices, and our legal team will not approve a blanket assumption.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then jurisdiction and consent source should travel with the contact. We can create a pre-send validation and an exception queue for records that need review.

Evie Yundt (CMO): Who owns the queue? Marketing operations is two people, and they are already overcommitted.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We need a named business owner and a technical owner, with an escalation path. I can help define the process, but the customer-side ownership has to be real or the queue will become invisible.

Evie Yundt (CMO): I can assign our marketing operations manager as business owner. IT will nominate the technical owner next week. I want that dependency visible in the plan.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will mark it as a launch dependency. For the first journey, would you prefer event follow-up or inquiry routing?

Evie Yundt (CMO): Inquiry routing. Event follow-up is valuable, but our largest complaint is that qualified inquiries wait too long. If we improve that, leadership will see the benefit quickly.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then the initial journey can score the inquiry, check consent and region, assign the appropriate business-development queue, and notify the owner. We should agree what happens when no one accepts the task.

Evie Yundt (CMO): Escalate to the regional lead after one business day. But do not create a flood of reminders for low-value inquiries.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can apply the escalation only above the agreed score threshold. Lower-priority inquiries can receive automated information and remain in nurture.

Evie Yundt (CMO): That is the balance. Sales will ask why every form fill is not routed immediately, so we need a simple explanation they can repeat.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can include routing rules in enablement and show the reason for each assignment. Transparency usually helps adoption more than a complicated score model.

Evie Yundt (CMO): Training is a major concern. We have enthusiastic marketers who build quickly, then forget the governance rules. I do not want training to be a single webinar that everyone attends while answering email.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can use role-based sessions: administrators, campaign builders, approvers, and business-development users. We should include exercises with your own inquiry examples and record the decisions in a playbook.

Evie Yundt (CMO): Make the playbook short. If it is forty pages, no one will open it after launch.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Agreed. A concise operating guide with links to deeper references. We can also establish office hours during the first few weeks.

Evie Yundt (CMO): Good. What should success look like after the pilot?

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I would measure time from qualified inquiry to assignment, acceptance rate, routing accuracy, conversion to a meaningful sales conversation, and the percentage of records blocked by consent or missing data.

Evie Yundt (CMO): Include adoption. If the numbers improve because everyone bypasses the platform manually, that is not success.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Absolutely. We can track usage of the approved journey and the proportion of qualified inquiries handled through it.

Evie Yundt (CMO): And pipeline attribution? Leadership will ask for it in the first dashboard.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can provide an initial influence view, but I recommend labeling it clearly while the definitions mature. Sourced pipeline, influenced pipeline, and campaign response are not interchangeable.

Evie Yundt (CMO): Thank you. We have had dashboards create more debate than insight. Give leadership something useful, but do not pretend the model is final.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That will be in the measurement plan. The remaining open points are the technical owner, the exact score threshold, and the legal review of consent rules.

Evie Yundt (CMO): I will get the threshold from sales leadership. Legal has a review slot Thursday. IT should confirm the owner by Friday, although “should” is doing a lot of work there.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will note Friday as the target and the dependency as at risk until confirmed. Next week we can review the data profile and approve the pilot design.

Evie Yundt (CMO): That works. I am pleased we are starting with one outcome rather than trying to automate the entire company in month one.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): It gives us something measurable and a pattern we can safely extend. I will send the plan, owners, milestones, and decisions today.

Evie Yundt (CMO): Excellent. And please include the New Zealand event journey so the regional team sees itself in the rollout.
