Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Hello, Dillan. Thanks for joining. I know it is late afternoon in the Nordics, so I will keep us focused.

Dillan Howe (CMO): Thank you. It is one of those days where the calendar is full and the decisions are empty, if that makes sense.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): It does. Today is an initial working session, not a final implementation commitment. I would like to understand the business priorities, current process, and who should be involved if the evaluation continues.

Dillan Howe (CMO): Good. We are looking at marketing automation because our regional teams are sending campaigns independently. Some are excellent, some are basically a spreadsheet with a logo pasted on top.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would you want a shared system to improve first?

Dillan Howe (CMO): Visibility and consistency. We need a common calendar, reusable templates, and a way to distinguish a real buying signal from someone downloading a report for research.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Those are good initial outcomes. How many teams would be involved?

Dillan Howe (CMO): Four country teams, plus our central marketing group. The central team is only seven people, and they cannot become the service desk for every local campaign.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): So governance matters as much as functionality.

Dillan Howe (CMO): Probably more. We bought a collaboration tool two years ago and discovered that nobody agreed on who could create a workspace. We had twelve versions of the truth in about six weeks.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can design roles around central governance with local execution. Before recommending a structure, what systems are you using today?

Dillan Howe (CMO): CRM, a separate email tool in two countries, spreadsheets in the other two, and an event platform that exports files whenever it feels like it. The CRM is the official record, though not always the freshest one.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What makes it stale?

Dillan Howe (CMO): Local teams update their spreadsheets first because they are faster. Then someone reconciles them into the CRM at month-end. That creates duplicate contacts and awkward attribution.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): A phased approach could start with one country and a controlled CRM sync. Which country is representative without being the most politically complicated?

Dillan Howe (CMO): Denmark is operationally mature. Sweden has more volume, but also more debate. Norway has a different approval process. Denmark would give us a clean test.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What campaign would make a useful pilot?

Dillan Howe (CMO): A renewal-readiness campaign for existing customers. It is measurable, and the audience is known. I do not want our first test to depend on buying new data.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is a strong pilot candidate. What would success look like?

Dillan Howe (CMO): More completed health reviews and earlier conversations with accounts at risk. I would not judge it only on opens or clicks.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Could we define a baseline for review completion, time from signal to owner activity, and renewal-risk movement?

Dillan Howe (CMO): Yes, although renewal-risk movement is partly subjective today. Each account team scores it differently.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then the pilot can expose that inconsistency. We can report the current definition and separately recommend a common rubric.

Dillan Howe (CMO): Fine. Please do not promise that automation will fix the rubric by itself. Technology tends to get blamed for policy problems.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Agreed. Who owns the commercial decision if the pilot is successful?

Dillan Howe (CMO): I sponsor the business case, but finance owns the budget approval. Operations will care about data governance, and the country lead in Denmark must agree to the pilot.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Would it be useful to invite all three to the next session?

Dillan Howe (CMO): Not all three at once. Finance will turn a working session into a procurement interrogation, and then we will spend forty minutes discussing invoice timing.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can separate them: a process workshop with Denmark and operations, followed by a short finance review once scope and effort are clear.

Dillan Howe (CMO): Better. I also need something I can show the executive committee. They will ask whether this is a replacement for the existing email tools or another layer.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can map the current tools and show the possible end state. It may be a staged consolidation, not an immediate rip-and-replace.

Dillan Howe (CMO): That wording matters. If they hear “replacement,” they will assume a migration program and freeze the discussion.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. We will frame the pilot as a controlled test of governance, campaign execution, and measurable follow-up.

Dillan Howe (CMO): Good. Security will ask about regional data handling too. We have customers in several jurisdictions, and the legal team is cautious about sending personal data across boundaries.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We should include legal and security requirements before choosing the data flow. I can provide the documentation for their review, but your team will need to confirm the applicable internal policy.

Dillan Howe (CMO): Yes. And do not send them a hundred-page packet without a summary. They will ignore it.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will send a concise overview with links to the detailed documents.

Dillan Howe (CMO): What timeline would you suggest?

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): First, a ninety-minute process workshop in February. Then a pilot design, data review, and security checkpoint. If those are positive, a limited launch could follow, but I would not attach a commercial date yet.

Dillan Howe (CMO): I agree. The opportunity record has a June date because someone needed a forecast field populated. It is not an approved deadline.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will treat it as provisional and base progress on completed milestones.

Dillan Howe (CMO): Excellent. Please include clear owners in the recap. If tasks are assigned to “the team,” they belong to nobody.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will list you for the business case, the Denmark lead for pilot approval, operations for data mapping, and us for the workshop materials.

Dillan Howe (CMO): Add finance as a later reviewer, not a pilot owner.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Added. Any concern about adoption among local marketers?

Dillan Howe (CMO): They will resist if central marketing controls every word. They will support it if templates are flexible and the approval path is short.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Then we should involve one local marketer in template design and measure approval time.

Dillan Howe (CMO): Good idea. That will make the governance discussion practical instead of theoretical.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will send proposed workshop times, the current-state questionnaire, and the security summary. You will confirm Denmark and operations participants.

Dillan Howe (CMO): Correct. I will also brief the executive committee that this is an evaluation with a staged pilot, not a committed purchase.
