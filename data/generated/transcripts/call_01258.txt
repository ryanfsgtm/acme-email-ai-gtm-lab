Tom Stokes (Privacy): It would reduce it, not eliminate it. I also need a clear deletion path. If a person withdraws consent, the record must be suppressed across active journeys and removed or anonymised according to the retention schedule.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can map suppression and deletion separately. Suppression prevents further outreach; deletion is the retention action. I will ask our implementation lead to document the sequence rather than relying on a generic statement.

Tom Stokes (Privacy): Good. I have seen implementation plans that say “configure privacy settings” and leave everyone guessing who presses which button.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Agreed. On data hosting, our standard environment for this region may involve processing in more than one location. I do not want to give you a definitive answer from memory. I will send the current subprocessor and hosting documentation.

Tom Stokes (Privacy): Please do. We will need that for our transfer assessment. Are support personnel able to access customer records?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Limited support access may be possible under controlled conditions, with permissions and audit logging. The exact support model depends on the service tier. I will mark that as an open item.

Tom Stokes (Privacy): And administrators? We need role separation. Marketing should not automatically see every regional database.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): The platform supports role-based access and business-unit partitioning. During design, we would define which users can view, edit, export, or administer each area. We should test that with sample roles before production.

Tom Stokes (Privacy): That sounds right. The implementation timeline you showed last month was six weeks. Is that realistic if we need the transfer assessment and role model first?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Six weeks is possible for a constrained launch. It becomes less certain if all regions and historical data are included. I would recommend a first release with one business unit, a defined data set, and a short validation period.

Tom Stokes (Privacy): That may help the business, but privacy will still need to approve the overall pattern before a pilot uses live data.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. We can use masked records for configuration and keep production activation behind your approval gate.

Tom Stokes (Privacy): What about consent capture? Some teams use web forms, some use event scanners, and one office still imports spreadsheets.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We would inventory the sources, identify the lawful basis or permission indicator for each, and agree how that value maps into the platform. The spreadsheet import should have validation rules and an owner. It should not become an unreviewed back door.

Tom Stokes (Privacy): Exactly. Also, do not assume an unsubscribe request means we can erase every contractual record immediately. Some records have accounting or regulatory retention requirements.
