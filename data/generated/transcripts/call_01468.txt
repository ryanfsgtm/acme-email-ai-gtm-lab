Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Louisa, thanks for joining. I know implementation partners usually see the practical risks before anyone else does, so I’d like to understand how your team would fit into this project.

Louisa Hauck (Implementation Partner): Happy to help. I’m between two factories today, so if you hear a door close, that’s why.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): No problem. To start, what prompted the marketing automation discussion?

Louisa Hauck (Implementation Partner): Denesik, O’Reilly and Hoeger has grown through several plants, and each plant handles customer communications differently. The central team wants consistent campaigns, but implementation will succeed only if local teams can keep necessary exceptions.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What kinds of exceptions are legitimate rather than just preference?

Louisa Hauck (Implementation Partner): Regulatory language, distributor relationships, and product availability. A plant may need to suppress an offer because its inventory is different. That is not noise; it is operational reality.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That’s useful. Would you prefer one global template with controlled variants?

Louisa Hauck (Implementation Partner): Probably. But I would not begin with the template library. I would begin with ownership. Who approves content, who owns segments, and who can pause a send? If those are unclear, a beautiful configuration just makes confusion faster.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Agreed. How many systems would need to connect at launch?

Louisa Hauck (Implementation Partner): The CRM, the product catalog, and possibly the service desk. The catalog is the difficult one. It has plant-specific fields and inconsistent product codes.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can start with CRM data and add catalog enrichment later. Does that fit your rollout preference?

Louisa Hauck (Implementation Partner): It could, but only if the first use case does not depend on product availability. A welcome or educational series may be safer than a promotion.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would a realistic pilot look like?

Louisa Hauck (Implementation Partner): Two plants, one central marketing owner, and a narrow audience. Six to eight weeks including data mapping, training, and a live campaign. I would want a success measure beyond opens—perhaps fewer manual handoffs and faster approval time.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can measure those. Is there a procurement deadline tied to the July close date?

Louisa Hauck (Implementation Partner): The date is aspirational. The internal budget conversation is in progress, but nobody wants to commit to a deadline before the plant managers agree.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. What should I send after this call?

Louisa Hauck (Implementation Partner): A high-level architecture, assumptions about roles, and a sample pilot plan. Also identify what your team would need from us. The blank space around responsibilities is usually where projects slip.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll include a responsibility matrix and mark the catalog integration as an open technical question.

Louisa Hauck (Implementation Partner): Good. And please show how exceptions are handled. If the demo only shows a perfectly standardized company, the local teams will reject it immediately.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll ask our solutions architect to demonstrate a regional variant and a paused send. Would you be available for that session?

Louisa Hauck (Implementation Partner): Yes, with our data lead. Let’s avoid Friday afternoon, though; that is when everyone disappears into the plants.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Noted. I’ll propose Tuesday or Wednesday morning.

Louisa Hauck (Implementation Partner): Fine. I need to jump in about a minute.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then I’ll stop there. Thanks, Louisa. Your point about ownership is the main takeaway.

Louisa Hauck (Implementation Partner): And your point about starting with a low-risk use case is sensible. Send the materials and we’ll see who needs to be in the next room.
