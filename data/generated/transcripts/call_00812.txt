Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Who owns that review?

Stephen Wisozk (CMO): Compliance owns approval, marketing writes, and our advisers provide the practical wording. That is three calendars. One of them is always on fire.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would a smaller initial program help?

Stephen Wisozk (CMO): Yes, provided “smaller” means manageable rather than stripped of value. We could start with existing clients whose consent status is clear. A financial planning newsletter is less risky than a campaign to unknown prospects.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How important is integration with your client system?

Stephen Wisozk (CMO): Very. We use a CRM with adviser notes, and those notes are sensitive. Marketing should know lifecycle status and consent, not every personal detail. We need a clean boundary.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can map only approved fields and keep the rest excluded. Would you want advisers to see campaign activity?

Stephen Wisozk (CMO): A summary, perhaps. Not a stream of every open and click. If an adviser sees that someone opened an email at 2:00 a.m., they may invent a story around it.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a good example of a metric becoming a distraction. What would make a quarterly review valuable six months from now?

Stephen Wisozk (CMO): I want to see response time, qualified conversations, and whether follow-up tasks were completed. Revenue is the eventual measure, but our sales cycle is long and our volume is modest. Waiting for closed revenue would make the dashboard useless for several quarters.
