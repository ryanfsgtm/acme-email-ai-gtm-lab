Priya Shah (Account Executive): Kade, thanks for joining. Before we get into systems, how has the summer clinic schedule been? I imagine healthcare demand makes campaign timing a little unpredictable.

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): It does. We have several locations, and appointment availability changes quickly. A campaign can be accurate on Monday and wrong by Wednesday.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds like a useful place to start. What prompted you to look at marketing automation now?

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): Our sales coordinators are spending too much time moving leads between spreadsheets, email lists, and the CRM. We are a small organization—about one hundred and eight employees—and nobody has a spare afternoon to maintain a complicated machine.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): When a new inquiry arrives, what happens?

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): It lands in the website inbox, then someone checks whether the person is looking for a consultation, a referral, or general information. The relevant details get copied into the CRM. After that, reminders are mostly manual.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How quickly does the first response need to happen?

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): Same business day. For urgent services, faster. We do not promise a clinical outcome through marketing, obviously, but we do want the inquiry acknowledged and routed correctly.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would routing be based on location and service interest?

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): Location, service line, and whether the person is an existing patient. The last one is sensitive. We need to be careful about what is stored and who can see it.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What systems hold those details today?

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): The CRM holds contact records and referral status. The appointment system is separate. We do not want marketing automation writing into clinical records. That boundary is firm.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. We can keep the automation layer limited to consented marketing data and routing signals, with the appointment system remaining authoritative for clinical scheduling. Who owns consent today?

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): Marketing collects it on forms, but compliance reviews the language. Sometimes a coordinator receives consent by phone and records a note. We need a clearer method without pretending a note is the same as a formal subscription.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That distinction should be part of the design. What would success look like three months after launch?

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): Fewer manual handoffs, a visible queue for unassigned inquiries, and a report showing response time. I would like to know whether location teams are following up, not just how many emails were sent.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): So operational visibility matters more than a large campaign calendar.

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): Much more. We send occasional educational updates, but our main problem is follow-up. If the system is excellent at newsletters and poor at routing, we have bought the wrong thing.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is helpful. We could map an inquiry workflow with an acknowledgment, an internal assignment, and a reminder if no owner accepts it. Would the reminder go to a shared queue or an individual?

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): Shared queue first. Individual assignment can come later because staff coverage varies by location.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Are there cases where automation should stop rather than continue?

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): Yes. If the person asks not to be contacted, if the referral is outside our service area, or if the message suggests a clinical emergency. Those should be routed to trained staff, not placed in a nurture sequence.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Good safeguards. What reporting does leadership ask for?

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): Monthly totals, source, response time, and conversion to a scheduled consultation. They do not need twenty dashboards. They need numbers they can explain.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Can your CRM currently capture source and response timestamps reliably?

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): Source is inconsistent. Timestamp is there for some stages, not all. That may be a bigger project than the automation itself.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then we should not make reporting promises that the underlying data cannot support. We can start with the fields you trust, identify gaps, and use the implementation to establish a consistent process.

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): Exactly. Our previous vendor demo showed a perfect funnel using data we do not actually have. It looked impressive and was useless.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What level of involvement can you provide during implementation?

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): I can coordinate the workflows and test them. Compliance needs to approve the consent and emergency-routing rules. Location managers need to test assignment. I cannot be the only person signing off.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We would set up a working group with those three perspectives. How much configuration do you want to own afterward?

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): Routine edits, definitely. New routing logic should have a review step. I want the team empowered, but not able to accidentally contact an excluded population.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a sensible permission model. Are there budget parameters we should respect?

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): The approved opportunity is twenty thousand five hundred ninety-one dollars. We can consider that range, but hidden consulting charges will be difficult. We need a clear first-year total.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will provide a base subscription, implementation, and optional items separately. We can also identify what is explicitly out of scope, such as changes to the appointment platform.

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): Please do. If the project stays focused, I think we can move quickly.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would prevent a decision?

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): Compliance uncertainty, unclear ownership, or a workflow that requires too much technical maintenance. The product itself is not the hardest part.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then our next step should be a process workshop using one real inquiry path, including an exception case. I will bring a solutions consultant and a draft field map.

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): Bring the exception case first. If it handles the normal path but fails when someone says “don't contact me,” we will know immediately.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Agreed. I will schedule that for next week and send the assumptions beforehand.

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): Good. I am interested, but I want the design grounded in how our coordinators actually work.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is exactly how we will approach it. Thanks, Kade.

Kade Haag-Gottlieb (Sales Operations): Thanks, Priya. Send the draft, and I will circulate it to compliance.
