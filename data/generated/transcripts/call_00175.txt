I would like to understand how McClure Group handles revenue operations across the retail business and where a customer-data or campaign process might be slowing you down. Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): Mostly. I own the CRM, routing, forecasting inputs, and the handoffs with marketing. We sell through a mix of stores, a web shop, and a small wholesale channel. The systems are not terrible individually. Together they are a bit like a drawer full of cables.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What prompted the conversation now?

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): We are planning a loyalty relaunch. Marketing wants more personalized emails, sales wants visibility into high-value accounts, and finance wants proof that the work affects repeat purchases. At the moment, the web shop has purchase history, the CRM has account notes, and the email tool has a list that is not always current.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): When the list is not current, what happens operationally?

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): Usually someone exports a spreadsheet and cleans it manually. If there is a promotion, that can happen twice in a week. We have unsubscribes in the email tool, but a store customer can also opt out through a service desk form. Those do not always reconcile immediately.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Who first notices a mismatch?

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): Customer service, unfortunately. They get the complaint. Marketing then asks whether the customer was in the right segment, and I start looking through three systems.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That sounds stressful. What would a better process look like?

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): One customer record with consent, purchase category, location, and loyalty status. Then marketing can create a campaign without asking me for a file, but I still need controls. We cannot let everyone download the whole customer base.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): How many people need to build campaigns, and how many only need reporting?

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): Two marketing people build them. The commercial director wants reporting. Store managers might request local segments, but they should not be editing global rules.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What is the intended audience for the loyalty relaunch?

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): Existing customers who purchased in the last eighteen months, plus lapsed customers for a win-back test. We would exclude anyone with a service issue still open. That exclusion is currently manual.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Is purchase history reliable enough for those windows?

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): The web shop history is reliable. Store purchases arrive in a nightly file, and sometimes the receipt identifier is missing. We can match most records using email and postal code, but not all.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Would a pilot with the web-shop population be useful before including stores?

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): Yes. That would prove the workflow without pretending the data is complete. But leadership will ask why the first result excludes half our revenue.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Then the success plan can distinguish process validation from business coverage. What outcome would convince you the process is working?

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): Fewer manual list requests, zero known sends to opted-out customers, and a report that marketing and finance both accept. I do not need a miracle uplift in the first month.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is a grounded target. How do teams currently measure repeat purchase?

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): Finance has a cohort report, but it closes monthly. Marketing looks at clicks and revenue attributed inside the email tool. The numbers disagree because the attribution windows are different.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Would agreeing on one pilot attribution window be acceptable?

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): For the pilot, yes. We need to state the rule in plain language so nobody presents a click-through metric as incremental revenue.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I like that distinction. On integrations, do you have APIs available for the web shop and CRM?

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): The web shop has an API. The CRM is managed by a local partner, and they charge for changes. We can start with scheduled exports if the import process is reliable.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What cadence would be enough?

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): Daily for customer status and consent. Purchase data can be nightly. Near real time sounds nice, but it is not necessary for a weekly loyalty email.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): How would you handle a consent withdrawal between the nightly import and a send?

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): The send should check the most recent suppression file. If that is not possible, we need a short hold before dispatch. I would rather delay a campaign than create a legal problem.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will note that as a requirement to validate. I also want to ask about ownership. If the pilot works, who maintains segments and exceptions?

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): Marketing owns the content and standard segments. I own the data definitions and access. Customer service must be able to flag an open issue. The store teams should receive instructions, not admin rights.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That division is clear. What concerns would cause you to stop the evaluation?

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): If setup requires a developer for every change, or if the platform cannot show why a person entered a segment. We have had black-box tools before. They look efficient until somebody asks a basic question.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Auditability is important, especially with local teams. We can map an example segment from source field to campaign audience and identify where an override occurred.

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): Good. Also, do not assume all personalization is welcome. Some customers find product recommendations useful; others think we are being invasive.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Would preference controls or a conservative first message help test that?

Marlee Stamm (Revenue Operations): Yes. Start with category preference and loyalty status, not individual browsing behavior. We can learn before becoming clever.
