Kristoffer Aufderhar (Implementation Partner): The current process depends too much on individual coordinators. A prospective student may attend an event, download a course guide, and speak with an advisor, but those activities are not joined up. We then ask teams to reconstruct the history manually.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Is the primary goal recruitment, continuing education, or both?

Kristoffer Aufderhar (Implementation Partner): Both, although recruitment is the immediate priority. Continuing education has shorter campaigns and different audiences. If we build one generic journey, it will be wrong for half the organization.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How are leads routed today?

Kristoffer Aufderhar (Implementation Partner): A form creates a record in the central CRM, then regional staff decide who owns it. The rules vary by country and sometimes by faculty. Sweden is fairly disciplined. Norway has a clever spreadsheet. Denmark has three spreadsheets and a shared mailbox. I am not joking.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That variation will matter. Would you want the platform to enforce one process or support regional variations?

Kristoffer Aufderhar (Implementation Partner): A common framework with controlled variation. We need shared definitions for inquiry, application, and enrollment, but local teams need language, timing, and consent rules. We cannot tell a Danish coordinator to use a Swedish workflow simply because it is tidier.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What systems are involved?

Kristoffer Aufderhar (Implementation Partner): The CRM, the public website, event registration, and a student information system. The student system is the source of truth after enrollment. Marketing should not overwrite academic records. That boundary needs to be explicit.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Absolutely. What data would marketing need from the student system?

Kristoffer Aufderhar (Implementation Partner): Probably status and program information, not grades or sensitive support notes. We need to suppress communications when someone has enrolled or opted out. The exact fields need to be agreed with privacy and IT.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Who owns that decision internally?

Kristoffer Aufderhar (Implementation Partner): IT owns integration, admissions owns the lifecycle definitions, and privacy signs off on consent. I coordinate the implementation, but I cannot decide what the institution is legally allowed to use.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is helpful. How much content is ready?

Kristoffer Aufderhar (Implementation Partner): Quite a lot, but it is not organized as journeys. We have emails in shared folders, event follow-ups in documents, and web copy managed by another team. We would need a content inventory before asking people to build automation.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Would you start with one region?

Kristoffer Aufderhar (Implementation Partner): Yes, probably Sweden and one English-language program. That gives us a controlled test without pretending the Nordic model is uniform. If the pilot works, we can add Norway and Denmark with their local requirements.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would success look like in the first phase?

Kristoffer Aufderhar (Implementation Partner): Fewer missed follow-ups, clear ownership, and a trustworthy view of the journey. We can measure response time and progression from inquiry to application. I would be cautious about promising enrollment lift because program demand and admissions capacity affect that.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That distinction makes sense. Are there concerns about staff adoption?

Kristoffer Aufderhar (Implementation Partner): Definitely. Admissions advisers already have a CRM, email, and their own calendars. If the new system adds another screen without reducing work, they will route around it. Training must be tied to actual tasks, not a tour of every feature.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Could local champions help?

Kristoffer Aufderhar (Implementation Partner): Yes, one per region, but they need protected time. People often nominate a champion and then give them no hours to do the work. That is not a rollout plan; it is a title.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Very fair. What timeline are you considering?

Kristoffer Aufderhar (Implementation Partner): The student information integration and consent review. Also, if every faculty asks for a bespoke workflow before the first launch, we will lose the point of the pilot. We need a change-control conversation early.
