Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Willy, thanks for making time for the quarterly review. How are things in the UK?

Willy Rippin (Security): Wet, which is not a surprise. The business is busy, though.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I reviewed the account history and wanted to hear your perspective first. What has changed since the last review?

Willy Rippin (Security): We have reduced the number of marketing users, and the team is relying more on the CRM than on the automation platform. That is partly an adoption issue and partly a trust issue.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): When you say trust, do you mean security controls, data quality, or campaign behavior?

Willy Rippin (Security): Mostly behavior. A campaign was sent to a group that should have been excluded. It was stopped quickly, but the incident made everyone cautious.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Do you know whether the exclusion rule was missing, outdated, or overridden?

Willy Rippin (Security): The post-incident note says the segment was built from an old export. The suppression list had changed afterward. I do not think anyone deliberately bypassed a rule.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That sounds like a process and data-freshness problem, though I understand why it feels like a platform problem.

Willy Rippin (Security): Exactly. Security gets called after the fact, and then we are asked whether the tool is safe in general. “Safe in general” is not a useful control statement.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What evidence would help you regain confidence?

Willy Rippin (Security): A clear owner for suppression data, a visible timestamp, and an approval step before a high-risk audience can be activated. Also, I want to know who changed the audience and when.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can review those controls and build a small test. Would the business accept an approval step for every send?

Willy Rippin (Security): No, that would make them abandon the system. We need a risk-based threshold, perhaps based on audience size, region, or sensitive customer category.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a useful counterbalance. A universal approval queue would solve one concern by creating another.

Willy Rippin (Security): Right. Our London team sends small customer updates every week. Those should not require the same process as a nationwide promotional campaign.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What is the current status of the implementation?

Willy Rippin (Security): Technically, it is still available, but the team is not expanding usage. We have fewer active workflows than last quarter.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Is that because of the incident, or are there separate budget and staffing pressures?

Willy Rippin (Security): Both. The marketing manager left, and the replacement is covering several functions. They are also reviewing whether to consolidate tools next year.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): So even a clean security review would not automatically restore adoption.

Willy Rippin (Security): Correct. We need an owner who has time to use it. Security can remove blockers, but we cannot operate the campaigns.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would a guided reactivation plan help, or is the team too stretched?

Willy Rippin (Security): A short plan could help if it starts with one low-risk workflow. Do not propose ten new journeys. Give them something they can run and inspect.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We could use a customer renewal reminder with a narrow audience and no sensitive fields. Then we document the approval and audit trail.

Willy Rippin (Security): That would be a reasonable test. It should include a deliberate change so we can verify the history is understandable.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Good idea. We can add and remove a test record, then confirm the activity log shows both changes.

Willy Rippin (Security): And confirm what happens if the suppression source is unavailable. Does the workflow fail closed, or does it proceed with the last known list?

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will ask our technical team to verify that behavior for your configuration. The answer should be explicit, not implied.

Willy Rippin (Security): Please also clarify retention. Our privacy counsel asked how long campaign activity and contact history remain available.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will include the retention settings and deletion process in the review pack.

Willy Rippin (Security): Fine. Another practical issue: users are sharing screenshots instead of links because they cannot find the right audit page.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That suggests the control may exist but be difficult to use. We can include a short operator guide and identify the exact navigation path.

Willy Rippin (Security): Keep it short. If it reads like a forty-page manual, nobody will open it.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Agreed. One page for campaign approval, one for audit history, and one for incident response.

Willy Rippin (Security): That is enough for now. I am not recommending expansion this quarter, but I would support the controlled test.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will record that distinction. We will not treat the test as a commitment to broader rollout.

Willy Rippin (Security): Good. The renewal conversation should include the business owner, not just me.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Who should we invite?

Willy Rippin (Security): Our interim marketing lead and the CRM administrator. They can confirm whether the workflow is useful and whether the data is maintained.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will send a proposed agenda and the technical questions today.

Willy Rippin (Security): Thanks. I know this is not the most cheerful QBR, but a realistic one is more useful.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I agree. We will focus on evidence, ownership, and a manageable next step.

Willy Rippin (Security): Then we have a plan. Speak soon.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Speak soon, Willy.
