Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Zaria, thanks for joining. Before we get into software, how is the afternoon in France treating you?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): Busy, but not chaotic. We are between two productions, so I have a narrow window before another review.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I’ll make good use of it. I see Krajcik Group has several media brands and around five hundred seventy employees. What prompted the conversation?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): I should clarify that I did not initiate a purchase process. I was asked to advise on whether a marketing automation platform could be used safely across the brands. The commercial opportunity may be marked closed and lost, but the policy question is still active.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That distinction is useful. What are the brands hoping to accomplish?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): Better coordination of newsletters, event invitations, and subscription journeys. Today each brand has its own habits. Some use spreadsheets, some use a legacy mailing tool, and one team still asks me whether a CSV is acceptable for a campaign.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would success look like from your perspective?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): Clear accountability. We need to know why a person is being contacted, what consent or other legal basis applies, where the audience came from, and how quickly we can stop a campaign.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Are you looking for one global workspace or separate environments by brand?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): We have not decided. One workspace could improve governance, but it could also create accidental visibility across brands. Separation is safer conceptually, though more expensive and harder to administer.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): If we explored a shared workspace with strict business-unit permissions, would that address the concern?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): Perhaps. I would need to see whether permissions apply consistently to audiences, reports, templates, and exports. People often say “segmentation” and then discover that a user can export everything anyway.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a reasonable concern. We can walk through those controls. What data would enter the system?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): Contact details, professional interests, subscription preferences, event attendance, and engagement signals. We might also use inferred audience categories, but I would resist putting sensitive editorial or employment information into a marketing tool.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would your teams expect the platform to make automated decisions about who receives content?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): They would like recommendations, but we would need human review for anything that materially affects a person’s experience. A model choosing a newsletter is different from a model deciding eligibility for a paid service.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How are consent records maintained today?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): In the subscription database. The problem is that the mailing tools do not always update at the same speed. A person can unsubscribe in one place and still appear selectable in another for a while.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That timing gap sounds like a key discovery item. What duration would be acceptable?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): Minutes, not days. But I need the operational guarantee, not a demo that happens to update quickly.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We would need to map the source system, integration method, and failure handling. Would you require an alert for a failed consent sync?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): Yes, and an owner. An alert nobody reads is decorative.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Agreed. How do you handle deletion requests?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): The privacy team coordinates them. We identify systems holding the data, issue deletion or suppression instructions, and retain a record that the request was completed. Marketing cannot simply decide that a contact is “inactive” and leave the profile indefinitely.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would a retention rule based on inactivity be useful?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): Potentially, but inactivity is not the same as no legal reason to retain data. We would need exceptions for contractual records, complaints, and audit evidence.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. What happened with the previous evaluation?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): Procurement compared several platforms. One vendor looked attractive because it had many features, but its documentation was vague. Another was easier to understand but did not fit the brand structure. The project lost momentum when the business sponsor moved roles.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): So the loss was not necessarily a technical rejection.

Zaria Yost (Privacy): Correct. Please do not interpret it as approval either. We have unresolved governance questions.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Fair. If the project restarted, who would need to be involved?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): Privacy, IT, marketing operations, at least one brand lead, and procurement. Customer support should be consulted because they receive complaints when an unsubscribe fails.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a broader group than a standard marketing demo. Would a small pilot help, perhaps with one brand and a limited audience?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): It could, provided the pilot is governed like production. People sometimes use “pilot” to skip documentation, then the pilot becomes permanent.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What documentation would you require before testing?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): Data-flow diagram, processing purposes, roles and permissions, deletion behavior, subprocessors, hosting locations, incident obligations, and a test plan for consent withdrawal. Also, no real sensitive data in the first test.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can work from synthetic or anonymized records during validation. I would need your team to define the acceptance criteria.

Zaria Yost (Privacy): Yes. And I want an answer on cross-brand reporting. Executives may need aggregate numbers, but brand users should not browse another brand’s contact-level activity.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can investigate aggregate reporting options and whether contact-level access can be restricted. I will have a specialist confirm the exact boundaries.

Zaria Yost (Privacy): Good. “Can” is not enough; I need to know how it is configured and tested.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would make you comfortable recommending a next step?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): A written response that distinguishes standard capability from custom work, and identifies any assumptions. I also want a named owner for the consent-sync test.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I’ll send that response, along with a proposed pilot checklist. Since the opportunity is currently closed and lost, I won’t frame this as a forecasted deal. It is a governance review unless your business team reopens the commercial discussion.

Zaria Yost (Privacy): Thank you. That wording matters.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We have two minutes. Is there anything I have missed?

Zaria Yost (Privacy): Only that local teams may resist a central process. Adoption is a risk, not just a training task. If the controls are too cumbersome, people will create workarounds.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Then usability and governance need to be evaluated together.

Zaria Yost (Privacy): Exactly.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I’ll follow up with the written questions and leave the decision with your group.

Zaria Yost (Privacy): That is the right next step. Thanks, Amelia.
