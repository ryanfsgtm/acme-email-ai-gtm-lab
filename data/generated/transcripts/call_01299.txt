Priya Shah (Account Executive): Cory, thanks for joining. How is the new campaign launch treating you?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): It is treating me like a person who forgot to duplicate a spreadsheet. So, not gently.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I know the feeling. To start broadly, what made you look at marketing automation now?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): We are an eighty-four-person media company, and marketing is still coordinating campaigns manually. We need basic lifecycle follow-up, event invitations, and a better view of what sales is doing with inquiries.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What are you using today?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): Eloqua for some legacy programs, spreadsheets for planning, and email tools for newer work. It is too much for our team. We are not trying to create a NASA launch sequence.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would the first useful workflow be?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): A form submission should create a clean inquiry, notify the right person, and send a relevant follow-up. If someone attends an event, we need to know whether they requested a conversation. Right now that information is in three places.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How large is the marketable database?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): Around twelve thousand records, depending on how you count them. Finance looked at the number and said, “That is expensive.”

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Was the concern the total contract value or the contact-count model?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): Both, initially. The product is too expensive for us if the quote assumes every stored contact is an active marketing contact. But I also realized we were using the terms interchangeably.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Let us separate them. Stored contacts can remain in the database for history or sales context, while active marketing contacts are the people eligible for campaign communication. Does that reflect your use case?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): Yes. We have former subscribers, press contacts, vendors, and old event lists that should not all count as active marketing contacts. My concern is affordability, though. Even after cleaning, the budget is tight.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds like two different objections: one about the available budget and one about understanding what is counted. Which is the real blocker?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): The budget is the blocker. The counting confusion made the quote look worse, but it did not create money we do not have.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is helpful. What would the business need to see to justify the spend?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): Fewer missed inquiries and less manual coordination. We would need a phased plan, not a promise that every department transforms in month one.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Which teams would be in phase one?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): Marketing and two salespeople. Editorial might participate in event workflows later. We do not need every employee logging in.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How does Eloqua affect the decision?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): It has history and some sophisticated programs, but only one person really understands it. We are paying for capability we are not consistently using. Still, migration risk makes people nervous.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can inventory the programs and retire anything that is no longer needed. Would you prefer an assessment before discussing implementation dates?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): Yes. I want to know what gets migrated, what gets rebuilt, and what gets left behind.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What reporting does leadership ask for?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): Campaign influence, inquiry response time, and event follow-up. They do not want a dashboard with forty colorful tiles and no decision attached.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can define a small scorecard. Do you have an agreed lead-routing process?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): Not really. That is another issue. Sales says marketing sends incomplete inquiries; marketing says sales does not update statuses.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then routing and required fields should be part of discovery, not an afterthought.

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): Exactly. We need agreement on who owns a lead after the notification. The notification alone is not a process.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What timing are you working toward?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): We have a major industry event in about three months. I would like the event workflow ready before then, but our budget review is next month.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Could we prepare a small proof of value around inquiry capture and event follow-up for that review?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): Possibly, if the proof does not require a large implementation fee before we know whether the subscription fits.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will outline a lean phase-one scope and a separate migration assessment. We can also model active versus stored contacts so Finance sees the assumptions.

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): Good. Put the affordability question first. If the numbers cannot work, feature depth will not rescue the deal.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. Who else should review the proposal?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): Finance, our CRM administrator, and the sales director. Legal only needs to join once the commercial shape is credible.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will send a summary before the budget meeting. Any final concern?

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): Data cleanup. I do not want to pay to automate a mess. But I also do not want cleanup to become an endless project.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can set a threshold, document exceptions, and launch with the cleanest useful audience. Thanks, Cory.

Cory Cartwright (VP Marketing): Thanks, Priya. And please keep the pricing language plain. We have enough acronyms already.
