Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Jacynthe, thanks for taking the call. I saw the team has recently moved away from Marketo, so I would like to understand what actually made the transition manageable and where there are still gaps.

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): The transition is mostly complete. “Mostly” because our old nurture programs are still being archived, and nobody wants to touch the folder with 2019 in the name.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That folder is usually more dangerous than the live system. Who handled the migration?

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): We used an implementation partner certified on both platforms. That was a big factor. Our internal team understood the business rules, but the partner knew where Marketo’s oddities would show up in the export.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What kind of oddities?

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): Programs that looked identical but had different tokens, old custom fields that were still referenced, and scoring rules nobody remembered approving. We did not migrate everything. The partner helped us classify what was active, what was evidence, and what was simply historical clutter.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Did that affect the launch date?

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): It shortened it, actually. The first estimate was twelve weeks. We launched the core retail journeys in about seven, then added the wholesale segment later. Without the partner, I think we would still be debating the old scoring model.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is helpful context. What was the hardest part for your team?

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): Recreating the naming and ownership structure. Marketo had grown around individual specialists. Roob Group has 112 employees, and the new system is simpler, but simplicity exposes fuzzy ownership.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Who owns the active journeys now?

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): Marketing operations owns the framework. Regional marketers request changes. Sales operations owns the lifecycle definitions. Privacy reviews consent-related changes. We are still refining the handoffs.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Are the handoffs causing delays?

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): Sometimes. The delay is not the tool. It is that everyone agrees an approval is needed but nobody agrees who is the final approver.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can map that as a workflow issue. What is working better than in Marketo?

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): The audience building is faster. Also, the team can see why a person entered or left a journey. In Marketo, we could get the answer, but it required a small archaeological expedition.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): And what has not improved?

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): Reporting across retail and wholesale. We have different definitions of an engaged account. The dashboard is technically correct for each source, but the comparison is not clean.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Would a shared definition solve that, or are the businesses genuinely different?

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): Both. We need a common executive layer, but the operating metrics should remain different. A wholesale buyer may take months; a retail subscriber may convert in a week. If we force one funnel, somebody loses useful context.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is a good counterpoint to a single universal dashboard. We can use a shared taxonomy for high-level reporting and preserve segment-specific stages underneath.

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): Yes. The other thing I want to discuss is journey branching. We have basic branches live now. I do not want to build a maze just because the editor makes it easy.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can keep the first version simple.

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): Good, because advanced branching was mentioned in the original sales cycle, but it was not what caused us to buy. We bought because the migration path was credible and the partner could get us launched.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That distinction matters. For the current roadmap, what would justify adding more branches?

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): Evidence that the simpler journey is sending the wrong content. If product category, purchase history, and language materially change the next best message, then we will add a branch. Otherwise, the complexity is theater.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): How would you measure that?

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): Compare unsubscribe rate, assisted conversion, and support complaints by segment. Not just clicks. A high click rate can still mean the message was confusing.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Makes sense. Are there any Marketo-era assets you still depend on?

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): The lead-source mapping and a few compliance reports. The partner recreated the mapping, but we keep the old exports under restricted access for audit reference.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Has anyone asked to bring all that history into the new system?

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): Yes, usually someone who has not opened the archive. We decided that historical availability does not require operational migration.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That should reduce risk. What would you want from us over the next quarter?

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): Help us formalize the approval workflow and review the reporting taxonomy. I would also like a health check on the partner handoff. They did a good job, but there are still a few custom integrations that only one consultant understands.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can document the integration ownership and create runbooks. Would your team prefer workshops or written guidance?

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): Short workshops followed by written notes. Long manuals go unread until something breaks.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Agreed. We can schedule one session for workflow ownership, one for reporting definitions, and one for integration handoff.

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): That works. Please include sales operations in the reporting session. They are protective of the lifecycle fields, and honestly, they should be.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will. One final question: if another company asked whether a Marketo migration is risky, what would you tell them?

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): I would say the aggregate risk looks frightening because there are so many assets, but the risk is manageable when you have a certified partner, a clear cutover scope, and someone willing to delete or archive things. The tool alone does not make the migration fast.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is a useful summary. I will capture the partner-assisted launch as part of the success review, along with the remaining workflow gaps.

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): Fine, as long as you do not turn it into “branching solved everything.”

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I promise not to give branching credit for the partner’s work.

Jacynthe Morissette (Marketing Operations): Then we are aligned. Thanks, Evelyn.
