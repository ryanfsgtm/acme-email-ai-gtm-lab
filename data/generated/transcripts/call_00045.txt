Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Allison, good morning. How are things on your side?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): Busy in the usual way. We are closing the quarter, and three regional leaders have discovered new definitions of “urgent.”

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I know the feeling. Today I wanted to review Homenick Inc's current marketing automation program, the open renewal-and-expansion opportunity, and the remaining questions around the Mailchimp comparison. I have the account as a 1,914-person professional-services company in Canada, with an opportunity amount of 82,908 and a discovery-stage forecast.

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): That is right. The number is still a planning figure. We have not approved expansion.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What is working well enough to preserve?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): Central reporting is better than before. I can see campaign activity across our consulting, staffing, and advisory groups without collecting twelve spreadsheets. Lead handoff is also more visible. The issue is that visibility does not automatically make the regional teams consistent.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Where do they diverge?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): Each brand has its own audience, tone, calendar, and approval chain. One group wants a webinar follow-up in two days. Another wants a six-message nurture over six weeks. A third brand is prohibited from receiving contacts generated by the staffing business because of contractual boundaries.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Are those separate legal entities or internal brands?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): Mostly internal brands, but the contractual boundaries are real. And we have acquired two small practices that still operate almost independently.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How are you handling that today?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): We use separate lists and naming conventions. It works until someone imports a file into the wrong campaign. Then I spend Friday afternoon investigating why a healthcare contact received an engineering invitation.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That sounds like a workspace and governance question. Would isolated workspaces help?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): Probably, but I want to be precise. We have multiple brands and agencies. The agencies should not see every brand's contacts. Regional marketers need autonomy. At the same time, leadership wants one consolidated view of pipeline and engagement. If isolation means separate reporting forever, it creates another problem.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): So the requirement is separation for execution with consolidation for oversight.

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): Exactly. We raised it after purchase, when we understood the implications of sharing one operating space. It was not in the original buying checklist because we thought “multi-brand” meant a naming convention and a few permissions.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would a reusable template look like for you?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): A standard event-follow-up journey with approved timing, suppression, and sales alerts. Each brand should be able to swap in its own content and registration form. Right now, teams copy an old journey, then quietly change a branch. Six months later nobody knows which version is safe.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Would a central template library address that?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): It would address part of it. We also need version ownership and an expiration date. A reusable template without an owner becomes folklore.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How do agencies fit into the model?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): We use two agencies for creative and one for paid campaigns. They should work inside a limited area, with access to their brand's assets and contacts. They should not be able to export the full database or edit global suppression rules.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): And consolidated reporting?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): Leadership wants brand-level pipeline, total influenced revenue, cost per qualified inquiry, and trend lines. But the brands do not use identical funnel stages, so we need a common reporting layer that does not erase local detail.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Has finance agreed on the common definitions?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): Not fully. Finance likes the idea but has not signed off on influenced revenue. They say some practices have longer sales cycles, so comparing them directly may be misleading.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That could delay the expansion.

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): Yes. The ironic part is that the regional leaders want more autonomy because their businesses differ, while the executive team wants consolidation precisely because the businesses differ. We are trying to solve both with one structure.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What did Mailchimp offer that appealed to the team?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): Familiarity and a simple price story for a single brand. One regional manager said, “We only need newsletters.” That is true for her group, but not for the whole company. The comparison can look favorable if you ignore shared governance and reporting.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Would you recommend switching that brand separately?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): I would not decide by brand in isolation. Moving one brand could reduce immediate complexity but increase duplication, and then I would reconcile two systems. We need to model the operating cost, not just the license.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How many distinct workspaces do you think are appropriate?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): I do not know yet. Maybe one per regulated boundary, not one per logo. That is why I want an architecture session before we discuss seats or a larger package.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would the session need to produce?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): A brand and agency access map, a template ownership model, a consolidated reporting design, and a migration plan for the acquired practices. Also a list of what stays centralized: suppression, consent, taxonomy, and executive dashboards.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is a substantial agenda. Who should attend?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): Sales operations, marketing operations, one representative from each major brand, IT security, and procurement. The agencies can provide input, but they should not define the governance model.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Would a phased expansion be easier to approve?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): Yes. Start with the acquired practices and one established brand. Test isolated execution and consolidated reporting. If that works, extend it. Do not ask leadership to approve every brand at once.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What is the main success measure for phase one?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): No cross-brand access incidents, faster campaign launch, fewer duplicated templates, and a monthly report that leadership can understand without a translation meeting. We should also track how often my team has to repair a regional workflow.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That last metric is practical. Is there any reason the current opportunity would close quickly?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): No. Discovery is still the right stage. Our fiscal planning is underway, and the business case needs to account for agencies, migrations, and reporting work. The budget could support 82,908, but available budget is not the same as approved value.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will prepare an architecture workshop and a phased option. I will include the Mailchimp comparison as an operating-model comparison, not just a feature checklist.

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): Good. Please include that our needs were raised after purchase. People sometimes hear “we need workspaces” and assume we failed to identify a basic requirement. We understood the brand count; we had not understood the consequences.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That distinction will be in the summary. Anything else?

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): Yes. Do not promise that consolidated reporting means identical metrics. We need a shared executive layer and enough local detail for each brand to trust it.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Agreed. I will send a draft by tomorrow, then we can decide whether to invite procurement.

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): Invite them early. They will ask about agency access and renewal terms anyway, and it is better to answer before the proposal is polished.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Excellent. Thanks, Allison. I know this was a working session rather than a quick renewal check-in.

Allison Hoeger (Sales Operations): That is what we need. A quick renewal conversation would miss the reason expansion is complicated.
