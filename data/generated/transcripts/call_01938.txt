Priya Shah (Account Executive): Guiseppe, good to see you. How are things in Toronto? Still pretending spring has arrived?

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): It is sunny, which is usually the first stage of the lie. We may get snow next week.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then we will keep the QBR indoors. I have twenty-one minutes blocked. I thought we could look at outcomes since go-live, adoption, open items, and whether the current setup is ready for the next enrollment cycle.

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): Perfect. The quick headline is that the team is getting value. We closed the renewal as a won opportunity, and the marketing automation is finally connected to our sales process instead of sitting beside it.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is great to hear. What changed most for Sales Operations?

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): Visibility. Before, we had leads coming from forms, events, and referrals, but the source values were inconsistent. We could not tell whether a campaign produced a qualified conversation or just a lot of activity. Now the source mapping is much better, although not perfect.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What is still imperfect?

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): Partner referrals. They enter through a shared mailbox or a spreadsheet, and people sometimes select “other” because it is faster. We have a cleanup rule, but it catches the record after creation. It does not prevent the bad value at the start.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would a required field or controlled intake form be acceptable?

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): For the main web forms, yes. For partner referrals, I do not want to make the process so rigid that partners stop sending anything. A little mess is preferable to no pipeline.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a reasonable tradeoff. How has lead response time changed?

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): The median is down from roughly two days to same day. But that number hides a problem. During campaign peaks, the queue still builds because only two people monitor the alerts.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): So automation improved the normal case, but volume spikes are still a staffing issue.

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): Exactly. The system can notify people, but it cannot make them available. We have discussed rotating coverage, though that is an internal decision.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What about the nurture programs?

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): The adult-learning sequence is performing best. The webinar follow-up is also good, but we had to shorten it. The first version had too many emails and some prospects unsubscribed before reaching a person.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How did you decide to shorten it?

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): We looked at engagement and complaints, then asked two admissions managers to review the wording. The report alone would have favored keeping the sequence because opens were strong. The qualitative feedback changed our mind.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is an important point. Metrics need context. Are there particular results leadership cares about?

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): Qualified meetings and completed applications, not email opens. We have a dashboard for the first, but the application data arrives from another system with a delay. I would like a cleaner connection before the next board report.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Is the delay technical or process-related?

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): Both, probably. The application platform sends a nightly file, and the matching logic misses people who use a different email address. We need to decide whether to improve matching or change the intake process.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can review the integration design, but I do not want to promise that a platform change alone solves identity matching.

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): I appreciate that. We have a habit of buying another tool when the real issue is inconsistent data entry.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What are users asking for now?

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): More transparency into why a lead is in a sequence, and an easier way to pause someone when an admissions conversation starts. They also want fewer duplicate tasks. A person may get an automated reminder and a manual task for the same event.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Can you share an example?

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): If a prospect attends a webinar and then books a meeting, the webinar workflow still sends a reminder unless someone catches it. The meeting creates a task, so the prospect gets touched twice. We have a suppression rule in testing, but we have not rolled it out.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What is holding that up?

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): We are worried about suppressing too broadly. Some meetings are canceled, and the person should re-enter a follow-up path. We need a status that distinguishes booked, held, canceled, and no-show.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That sounds like a good next design session. Who owns the decision?

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): I do, with our admissions operations lead. Marketing can advise on messaging, but Sales Operations owns the lifecycle rules.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Good distinction. Any data governance concerns since the last review?

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): Consent status is better, but old records remain ambiguous. We are not using them in new campaigns, which is the safe choice, but the dashboard still counts them in the database total. Executives see the big number and assume it is marketable.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would a separate marketable-contact metric help?

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): Yes. Please do not just rename the total. I want definitions visible in the dashboard so nobody has to remember which number means what.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Agreed. For the next quarter, I hear four priorities: peak-volume coverage, application matching, lifecycle suppression, and clearer contact definitions.

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): That is right. I would add enablement. We have new coordinators joining, and if they do not understand the lifecycle, they will create workarounds.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can schedule a short role-based session rather than another broad product training. What would demonstrate that the QBR actions worked?

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): Response time stays within same day during ordinary weeks, duplicate touches decline, and the board report can distinguish inquiry, qualified meeting, and application without a manual reconciliation exercise.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will put those measures in the follow-up. We should review progress in six weeks, before the campaign peak.

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): That timing is good. One caveat: our next campaign could move, depending on the admissions calendar.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. I will make the review date flexible by one week. Anything else before we close?

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): No. The system is helping, but the useful part has been clarifying ownership. I would rather keep improving the operating model than chase another feature list.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a strong place to end. Thanks, Guiseppe. I will send the actions and owners this afternoon.

Guiseppe Stark (Sales Operations): Thanks, Priya. And if snow arrives, I am blaming the QBR.
