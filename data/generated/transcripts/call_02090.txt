Priya Shah (Account Executive): Clement, thanks for taking the call. How are things at McDermott Group?

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): Busy, but manageable. We are an 82-person retailer, so “team” can mean three people and a shared inbox.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That helps. What prompted you to look at marketing automation now?

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): We want better repeat-purchase campaigns and less manual work. We also need to understand the price before we get too enthusiastic.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Absolutely. What are you using today?

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): A newsletter tool, spreadsheets, and occasional paid social uploads. It is not elegant, but it is familiar.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How many people are involved in campaign execution?

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): Two regularly, with store managers sending us lists. We have customers, prospects, loyalty members, and old contacts mixed together.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): When you say mixed together, do you know roughly how many marketing contacts you have?

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): Not confidently. Finance heard one number and your pricing page seemed to use another. That is why I said the product looked too expensive.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Let us slow down there. The price can depend on active marketing contacts, while stored contacts may be retained without being eligible for campaigns. Do you know whether your estimate included every stored record?

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): Probably. We have around 31,000 rows in the old database, but a lot are former customers, duplicates, or people who have never consented.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then the initial estimate may be overstated. We would need to classify stored versus active marketing contacts and remove or suppress records according to your policy.

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): I want that in writing. I do not want a low first quote followed by a surprise when someone discovers the whole archive.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Agreed. We can model both scenarios: a broader active audience and a narrower consented audience. Neither should be treated as final until the data is reviewed.

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): That is the first useful pricing explanation I've had. But even the narrower number may still be hard for us.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What range has finance approved?

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): I do not have a signed range. I know they are comparing the cost with our current tools and asking whether the labor savings are real.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What manual work takes the most time?

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): Building lists, checking duplicates, and exporting audiences. The creative work is not the bottleneck.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): If we could reduce those tasks, what would you measure?

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): Hours saved per campaign, repeat-purchase revenue, and fewer mistakes in customer exclusions.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Those are measurable. Would store managers need direct access?

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): They want it, but I am nervous. One manager could upload a list with the wrong region and send an offer to the wrong customers.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can give them draft access and require central approval. That may preserve local input without allowing unreviewed sends.

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): Fine, although it adds some process.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Every control has a little friction. We should decide whether the friction is less costly than a misdirected campaign.

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): Exactly.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would a small first use case look like?

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): A lapsed-customer campaign for one product category, limited to consented contacts in two regions. If it works, we can expand.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a sensible pilot. We can compare the stored audience, active audience, and final eligible audience before sending.

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): Can we see that calculation ourselves?

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Yes, with the appropriate permissions. We can also export the counts for finance.

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): Good. I need a proposal that does not hide the contact assumptions.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will include the assumptions, two pricing scenarios, pilot scope, and measurement plan. What is the decision process?

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): I will recommend. Finance reviews the spend, and the owner approves. Timing depends on whether the price is explainable.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Should we schedule a pricing review with finance?

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): Send the proposal first. If it is clear, I will invite them.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. Before we finish, is the main objection affordability, or uncertainty about what is being priced?

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): Both, but they are not the same problem. We may genuinely be unable to afford the platform, or we may simply be pricing all stored contacts when we would not market to them.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That distinction will be central to the proposal.

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): Good. Send it next week, and please avoid implying that we have approved a purchase.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will. Thanks, Clement.

Clement Bernhard (VP Marketing): Thanks, Priya. This was more useful than the usual “here are all the features” call.
