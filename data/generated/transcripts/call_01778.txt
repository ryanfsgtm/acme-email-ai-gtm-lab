Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Flavio, thanks for joining. Before we start, I saw the note about your plant visit. Did you make it back to the office, or are you calling from a noisy production floor?

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): Back at my desk, thankfully. The plant was loud enough that I could feel the machinery in my teeth. We are trying to connect marketing more closely to the distributor network, which is why this conversation matters.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is a useful place to begin. I want to understand how the current program is supporting the business, what needs to change, and whether the renewal and expansion opportunity should be shaped around distributors, direct accounts, or both.

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): Both, but not identically. Direct accounts are managed by our team. Distributors own much of the day-to-day customer relationship, and they are protective of their lists.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): How are those lists handled today?

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): A mixture of CRM exports, distributor spreadsheets, and regional mailing tools. It is not elegant. Some distributors send us a file monthly; others only tell us that a campaign “went well,” which is not a metric I can put in a board deck.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Are distributors asking for shared campaigns, or is that an internal ambition?

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): A few larger ones are asking. They want co-branded product education and event invitations. Smaller distributors mainly want us to provide content they can reuse. The risk is that central marketing builds something that does not fit how the channel sells.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): So adoption depends on giving distributors control within boundaries?

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): Yes. They need to choose their audience and timing, but we cannot let them change compliance language or suppressions. We also need to know what was sent where.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That sounds like a strong case for governed workspaces or partner access. Before we jump to a solution, what is the current renewal experience for your internal users?

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): The core team is happy. The platform is reliable, and the manufacturing product launches are easier to coordinate. The complaints are about reporting, permissions, and the time it takes to get a new data source connected.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Which of those creates the most business impact?

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): Reporting. We can see opens and clicks, but sales wants pipeline. Our buying cycle is long, sometimes nine or twelve months. If a prospect downloads a specification sheet today and becomes an opportunity next year, the connection is difficult to explain.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Do you have a shared definition of marketing influence with sales?

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): Not really. We have a working definition, then sales challenges it at quarter end. I am not blaming them; the handoff is genuinely messy.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Would a simpler reporting model be more useful than a more detailed one?

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): At first, yes. I want three views: engagement, qualified account activity, and sourced or influenced pipeline. If we add twenty dimensions, nobody trusts any of them.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is a good constraint. We can review the existing reporting and identify what is actually connected to account and opportunity records. Are the CRM fields consistent across your 360 employees?

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): No. The company is not huge, but the field naming is old. One business unit calls a distributor “partner,” another calls it “reseller.” We are cleaning it up, slowly.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That cleanup may need to precede expansion. Otherwise the new automation would simply move inconsistent data faster.

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): Exactly. I do not want a shiny connector feeding a bad taxonomy. That said, we cannot wait until every field is perfect. Manufacturing is not known for moving quickly on data governance.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Then we could define a bounded data model for the first phase: account, region, partner type, consent status, product interest, and owner. Would those be enough for a distributor campaign?

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): Add language and product family. We sell equipment that looks similar to a casual observer but is not interchangeable. If someone gets the wrong technical material, the campaign becomes a sales problem.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Good point. What would success look like in the first ninety days?

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): Launch two distributor co-marketing programs, reduce manual list preparation, and give sales a report they will actually use. I would also like distributors to stop emailing us screenshots as proof that a campaign was sent.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is measurable. Would you start with two distributors or one pilot distributor and one internal business unit?

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): One large distributor and one mid-sized distributor. If we only use the sophisticated partner, we will learn the wrong lesson.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That is a useful counterbalance. What makes the mid-sized distributor different?

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): Less technical capacity, smaller list, and more reliance on our content team. They may need templates and a guided approval process. The large partner will push for flexibility.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): We can test both operating models. For the renewal opportunity, the record is in Discovery with a target close date in July. Is that still realistic?

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): July is realistic for a decision, not necessarily for a full rollout. We need procurement, security, and a distributor agreement. If those become one project, July will slip.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Then I will separate the commercial decision from rollout milestones. Would you prefer to renew the existing capability first and add the partner functionality later?

Flavio Walker (Customer Success Manager): Probably. The current program should not be held hostage by expansion. But I need expansion pricing now so leadership can see the full picture.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will prepare a base renewal, a governed partner-access option, and a services estimate. I will also include assumptions about data cleanup and distributor participation.

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): Include internal effort. My team is eight people and already overloaded. A solution that saves list work but requires a full-time administrator is not an improvement.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Understood. Who would own the program internally?

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): Marketing operations would coordinate it, but IT owns integrations and legal owns consent language. Sales operations needs to sign off on account reporting. I cannot make this a marketing-only project.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That governance sounds right. I can set up a working session with those groups, but I will keep the first meeting focused on use cases and ownership rather than a feature tour.

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): Please do. Feature tours make everyone nod and nobody commit.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will send the discovery summary and proposed pilot framework by Friday. What would make you stop the expansion entirely?

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): If partners cannot operate within our consent model, or if reporting cannot connect activity to accounts without manual reconstruction. Also if the effort estimate is vague.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Clear criteria. Anything else before we wrap?

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): One thing: do not promise that every distributor will adopt it. Some partners will keep their spreadsheets because that is what they know.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will model adoption as a range, not a universal assumption.

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): Good. That is the first sensible sentence I have heard this week.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I will take the compliment and let you get back to the quieter part of your day.

Flavio Walker (VP Marketing): Much appreciated. Send the summary, and I will bring in sales ops after I read it.
