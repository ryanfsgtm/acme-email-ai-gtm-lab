Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Cassie, thanks for joining on short notice. I know the quarter-end reporting cycle is not exactly relaxing.

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): It’s fine. We’re in the familiar state of having six dashboards that disagree by a few percentage points. I’m glad we’re reviewing this now.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): I want to look at adoption, reporting, and the lessons from the evaluation. Towne and Sons ultimately closed the marketing automation opportunity as lost, correct?

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): Correct. The amount was around 233,514, and the decision was made in April. We didn’t move forward. The loss wasn’t one dramatic failure; it was a combination of governance, timing, and doubts about how different teams would use the system.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Let’s unpack that. What did executives need to see?

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): The executive group wanted credible attribution and reporting. They were asking, “Which programs influenced pipeline, and can we compare regions?” The operations people were more interested in how quickly they could build journeys, test variants, and fix a workflow without opening a ticket.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): So different audiences were evaluating different kinds of value.

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): Exactly. The CMO cared about the board narrative. Marketing operations cared whether a practitioner could launch a journey before lunch. Those are related, but one dashboard doesn’t answer both.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Did we address both during the evaluation?

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): Partly. The demos were strong on journey construction. The reporting discussion came later, and by then the executive sponsor had already formed the view that migration risk was high. Also, the reporting examples were too generic for our regional structure.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That’s useful feedback. What would have made the reporting credible?

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): A sample using our actual funnel stages, regional ownership, and definitions for influenced versus sourced pipeline. We needed to see how an executive report connected to the records practitioners were changing.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): And for the operators?

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): Less clicking. The team liked the visual builder but worried about cloning journeys and keeping exceptions legible. They also wanted fast testing and rollback. A beautiful workflow is not useful if every edit requires a specialist.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Did the incumbent create any problems that might have pushed the team toward change?

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): Yes, but not consistently. Some regional campaigns were slow to update, and our data sync occasionally lagged. However, there are quarters where the existing tools perform adequately. That made the cost of switching harder to justify.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): So the pain was real but uneven.

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): Right. The team complained loudly in March, then had a relatively smooth April. Timing mattered. When the budget review arrived, the status quo looked less dangerous than it had a month earlier.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Were there any data concerns?

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): Duplicate contacts, regional consent fields, and inconsistent campaign names. None was impossible, but the cleanup effort was larger than the initial estimate. Our finance partner also challenged the proposed attribution window.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What window did they prefer?

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): They wanted a shorter window for executive reporting. Marketing wanted a longer period for considered purchases. We never reached a definition everyone accepted.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That sounds like a process issue as much as a product issue.

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): It was both. A platform can expose disagreements, but it can’t decide which business definition is politically acceptable.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Fair. Let’s talk about adoption signals. Which users engaged most with the trial?

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): The regional lifecycle managers. They built test journeys and asked detailed questions about branches. The senior leaders attended the overview but didn’t spend time in the product.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Did that create a communication gap?

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): Yes. The people with hands-on enthusiasm were not the people signing off on the investment. Their feedback traveled upward as “the tool is easier,” while the executive questions were “will this improve forecast confidence?”

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): If we had restarted, how would you structure the evaluation?

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): Start with two tracks. Give operators a constrained sandbox with a real journey and a timed build exercise. Give executives a reporting workshop using agreed definitions and a few real historical campaigns. Then connect the two tracks in one review.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Would you include the CMO earlier?

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): Definitely. Not for every configuration detail, but for the value model. The CMO should validate the questions the report must answer before anyone promises a dashboard.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): What about training?

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): Training wasn’t the main obstacle. We had plenty of training material. The obstacle was ownership: who maintains taxonomies, who approves new journey logic, and who resolves discrepancies.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That distinction is easy to miss.

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): It’s common. People say they need enablement when they actually need a decision forum.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): One more question on the loss. Was a competitor involved?

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): No named competitor in the final record. The practical competitor was delay. We postponed the decision, and the budget was allocated elsewhere.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): That’s clear. I’ll capture the need for persona-specific validation, agreed attribution definitions, and an operating model. Is there anything you would preserve from the evaluation?

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): The operator workshop. It surfaced useful friction quickly. I’d just pair it with an executive reporting session in the same week.

Evelyn Sullivan (Customer Success Manager): Good recommendation. I’ll send a concise recap and keep the account marked closed-lost. If priorities change, we can reopen with those conditions documented.

Cassie Hilpert (Revenue Operations): That works. And please don’t summarize this as “reporting was missing.” Reporting existed; confidence and shared definitions were missing.
