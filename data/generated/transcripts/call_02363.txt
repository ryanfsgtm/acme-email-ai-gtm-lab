Priya Shah (Account Executive): Hi Armani, good to see you. How are things in the middle of the summer term?

Armani Hessel (Lifecycle Marketing): A little chaotic. Enrollment communications are always louder than people expect, but overall the team is pleased. The dashboard looks healthy, at least on the surface.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a good place to start. We will review results, adoption, upcoming needs, and the commercial path. Your opportunity is still prospecting in our records, so I also want to make sure the account status reflects reality rather than an old administrative label.

Armani Hessel (Lifecycle Marketing): That label may be more accurate than you think. We have the system, but we are not using everything we discussed during evaluation.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What are you using consistently?

Armani Hessel (Lifecycle Marketing): Mostly email blasts. Weekly enrollment reminders, a monthly alumni update, and occasional event announcements. The sends go out reliably, and open rates are decent. We have not built the lifecycle journeys for applicants and accepted students.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What has prevented that?

Armani Hessel (Lifecycle Marketing): Time, mainly. We are a three-person marketing team. Also, our admissions data is not as clean as we hoped. If we automate too early, we could send the wrong message to someone whose status changed overnight.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That caution makes sense. We can start with a controlled segment and approval step rather than a fully automatic journey. Before that, how is the current contact population behaving?

Armani Hessel (Lifecycle Marketing): We have about eighteen thousand stored contacts, but only around seven thousand are actively mailed. The rest include old inquiries, former students, unsubscribed people, and records we keep for reporting.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Is the distinction between stored and active contacts clear to your finance team?

Armani Hessel (Lifecycle Marketing): Not really. That is one of our pricing objections. Someone looked at the stored total and assumed the next bill would be based on all eighteen thousand. If that is not how it works, we need a plain explanation. If it is, we need to rethink the plan.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will have our commercial team validate the exact terms. We should not treat it as one objection until we know whether the issue is contact-model confusion or a genuine budget limit. What budget limit has finance given you?

Armani Hessel (Lifecycle Marketing): The current amount is manageable. A major increase is not. We could perhaps absorb a modest change, but not a jump justified by records we are not contacting.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will record that as a boundary. In parallel, let us examine adoption. Which campaigns are producing the most value?

Armani Hessel (Lifecycle Marketing): The reminders before enrollment deadlines. They are simple, but they get people to act. The alumni newsletter is positive too, although it is more of a reputation channel than a conversion channel.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): And what would success look like for an applicant journey?

Armani Hessel (Lifecycle Marketing): Fewer manual reminders, better attendance at information sessions, and a clear handoff to admissions. But we need the admissions director to approve the messages, and she has not prioritized the project.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Could we schedule a forty-five-minute design session with her and your data owner? No build commitment—just map the status changes and approval points.

Armani Hessel (Lifecycle Marketing): Maybe. I do not want another workshop that ends with a beautiful diagram and no owner.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Fair. The output would include an owner, a first segment, and a date for a small pilot. If those cannot be named, we should not call it an implementation project.

Armani Hessel (Lifecycle Marketing): That is more practical. I can bring our admissions operations lead. She knows the data better than marketing does.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Excellent. On reporting, are you able to connect campaign activity to enrollment actions?

Armani Hessel (Lifecycle Marketing): Only loosely. We can see clicks and form submissions, but the final enrollment record lives elsewhere. People are enthusiastic about the engagement metrics, yet I am not comfortable calling them revenue impact.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Good distinction. We can label the current metrics as engagement and define a later reconciliation with the enrollment system. It may be less impressive, but it will be credible.

Armani Hessel (Lifecycle Marketing): Exactly. The team likes the platform, but enthusiasm is not the same as renewal confidence. If we remain a tool for sending newsletters, leadership will ask whether a simpler product is enough.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is important. Narrow product adoption is a stronger renewal warning than surface call sentiment. I want to make sure we address the usage pattern, not simply point to positive open rates.

Armani Hessel (Lifecycle Marketing): I agree. The product is easy to use, and support has been good. But the value case is narrow because we have not connected it to the student lifecycle.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then our plan has two tracks: resolve the stored-versus-active pricing question, and test one lifecycle workflow with a named owner. What is the smallest useful pilot?

Armani Hessel (Lifecycle Marketing): Applicants who register for an information session but do not attend. We have a clean event list and a clear follow-up window. We could send one reminder and one recording message, with admissions approval.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a sensible starting point. We can measure attendance, recording views, and subsequent inquiry quality without pretending it proves enrollment causality.

Armani Hessel (Lifecycle Marketing): Good. Also, we should keep the existing weekly blasts running. They are not sophisticated, but they are operationally important.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Agreed. I will not disrupt the reliable program while we test the pilot. I will send a recap with the commercial clarification, pilot outline, owners, and the decision date.
