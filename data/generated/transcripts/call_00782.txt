Allie Lind (Implementation Partner): Better coordination across our stores. We have 127 employees, with central marketing and regional managers doing different things. The goal was to make promotions more consistent and reduce the time spent assembling lists.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What does the current process look like?

Allie Lind (Implementation Partner): A mix of spreadsheets, point-of-sale exports, and an email tool. Store managers send requests by email. Central marketing checks them, removes obvious duplicates, and schedules the campaign. There are too many “final-final” files.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How often does that create a customer-facing problem?

Allie Lind (Implementation Partner): Not constantly. Most campaigns go out correctly. The issue is that corrections come late. A regional offer may need to exclude one store or one loyalty group, and the exclusion is sometimes buried in a message.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): So the pain is less sending email and more managing exceptions.

Allie Lind (Implementation Partner): Yes, although the team might describe it as a reporting problem. They want to know which stores contributed to a result, but the source data is not clean enough to answer quickly.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would store managers build campaigns themselves?

Allie Lind (Implementation Partner): No. That would create a governance headache. They should submit requests using a form or template. Central marketing remains responsible for approval and sending.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That’s a useful boundary. ActiveCampaign was attractive because people already knew it. What concerns remained?

Allie Lind (Implementation Partner): Migration effort, mostly. Also the number of separate audiences. We have country-level permissions, a loyalty audience, seasonal buyers, and some lists managed by agencies. It was hard to explain who owned what.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Did budget influence the decision?

Allie Lind (Implementation Partner): Of course, but I don’t have the final numbers. The amount discussed was around 17,995. Procurement wanted predictable costs. Marketing wanted flexibility. Those are not always the same thing.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): If the project restarted, what would need to be agreed first?

Allie Lind (Implementation Partner): Data ownership and the minimum viable structure. We should not begin by recreating every old list. We need a canonical customer record, a way to tag store affiliation, and a process for consent changes.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would a staged implementation help?

Allie Lind (Implementation Partner): Probably. One country, two campaign types, and a limited set of users. But the business wants a broad answer before approving a narrow test, which is backwards, in my opinion.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would success look like after the first month?

Allie Lind (Implementation Partner): A request arrives with the required information, an approver can see it, the audience is assembled without manual copying, and the result can be tied back to a store. If it takes three days to get a simple promotion out, the tool hasn’t helped.
