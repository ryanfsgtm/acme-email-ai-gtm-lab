Augusta Ullrich (IT): Lead imports, segmentation, email sends, and reporting. Some of it is in spreadsheets. Some is in our CRM. Some, I think, is in a tool that only one person knows how to use. I’m not close enough to the process to give you a perfect map.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That’s okay. When a campaign is ready to go today, what happens from the request to the send?

Augusta Ullrich (IT): Marketing prepares a list, then Sales checks it, then someone exports a file. There may be a second check for consent. Then the email is built and tested. If the list changes, we start again. It can be two days, or it can be two weeks if somebody is traveling.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): And reporting?

Augusta Ullrich (IT): The reports are assembled after the fact. Opens and clicks are easy enough. Connecting activity to a customer or opportunity is less clean. People argue about whose spreadsheet is correct.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Sounds familiar. What systems would a new platform need to connect with?

Augusta Ullrich (IT): Our CRM, Microsoft 365, and probably the website forms. I need to confirm the CRM edition; it is one of those questions that comes back to me later. We also have an internal identity provider.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): No worries. From an IT standpoint, are you mainly concerned about integration effort, data handling, user access, or ongoing support?

Augusta Ullrich (IT): All four, but not equally. Data handling is the first filter. We are a financial-services firm, even if we are not a bank. We cannot have customer information copied into five places without knowing where it goes. Then access controls. Then whether I am expected to become the help desk.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That makes sense. We can document the data flows, use role-based access, and keep administration delegated to business users where you want it. I don’t want to pretend integration is zero effort. Usually the difficult part is agreeing which fields are authoritative.

Augusta Ullrich (IT): Exactly. Vendors say “simple integration,” and then it becomes a project because the data is not simple.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How many people would need access if this moved ahead?

Augusta Ullrich (IT): I’d guess ten to fifteen initially. Marketing is small, Sales would need visibility, and maybe a compliance reviewer. But again, I’m giving you a rough number.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That’s enough for a first pass. Would the team want to replace the CRM, or sit alongside it?

Augusta Ullrich (IT): Sit alongside. Replacing the CRM is not happening. The CRM is unpopular in places, but it is embedded. Also, our finance team uses it for forecasts.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. What would make this worth pursuing rather than leaving the current process alone?

Augusta Ullrich (IT): If marketing could launch campaigns faster and we could trust the reporting. But the business case has to be visible. We are not going to buy something because it has a nice interface.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Is there a target for speed or volume?

Augusta Ullrich (IT): Not formally. Maybe reduce campaign preparation from several days to one day. More important is reducing rework. The same list being checked three times is not a good use of anyone’s time.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): And what would stop it?

Augusta Ullrich (IT): Budget, lack of ownership, or security review. Also, if Marketing cannot agree on a process, a platform will just make disagreement faster. Sorry, that sounded more pessimistic than intended.

Augusta Ullrich (IT): The CMO, probably, though our COO may care about efficiency. I would advise on technical fit, not approve the spend.
