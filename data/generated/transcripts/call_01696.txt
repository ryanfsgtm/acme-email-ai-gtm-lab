Amelia Hart (Account Executive): When a department wants a new platform, what normally happens?

Keyon Berge (Procurement): The department finds a shortlist. IT reviews integration and access. Legal reviews terms. Security sends a questionnaire. Procurement compares pricing and renewal terms. Sometimes the business sponsor has already chosen a vendor before I hear about it.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): And where does that break down?

Keyon Berge (Procurement): Visibility. We may see invoices but not usage. A team may say a tool is essential, while another has something similar. Renewal dates are spread out. Also, people underestimate the work required to migrate records.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What information would make a consolidation decision credible?

Keyon Berge (Procurement): Actual usage, user counts, owners, renewal dates, data location, and whether the replacement covers the same use cases. A slide saying “save money” will not get approved.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That makes sense. Our platform can centralize campaign and customer engagement work, with usage reporting and permissions. I do not want to imply that it automatically replaces every tool. Which teams would need to be involved?

Keyon Berge (Procurement): Marketing, customer teams, IT, security, and finance. Sales operations might have an opinion too. But I would not assume everyone agrees that the current setup is bad.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What is working well today?

Keyon Berge (Procurement): Local teams move quickly. They can launch an email or a small program without waiting for a central committee. That flexibility has value. The downside is duplication and inconsistent reporting.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): If a centralized system slowed those teams down, would that be a deal breaker?

Keyon Berge (Procurement): Probably. The business will reject a governance project that feels like a brake pedal.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How do you weigh price against operational control?

Keyon Berge (Procurement): Price is a threshold, not the only criterion. We look at total cost, implementation effort, support, contract flexibility, and risk. A cheaper license can be expensive if adoption fails.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Are there budget lines allocated for this?

Keyon Berge (Procurement): Not that I can confirm. We have an annual software review. If a proposal is strong, a department could sponsor it, but procurement does not create demand.
