Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Tony, thanks for joining. I know this is an unusual QBR because the marketing automation opportunity closed as lost last month, but I thought a short review would be useful. How has the transition been?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Busy. We have signed with ActiveCampaign, and the implementation is underway. I can be candid about the decision if that helps.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): It does. From our side, we understood the business case, but we may have missed something in procurement. What ultimately drove the decision?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Several things. The commercial proposal was not the only factor. ActiveCampaign was familiar to the marketing team, the initial package was easier to compare, and their legal terms were more straightforward for us.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Was price the deciding issue?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Not exactly. Your proposal was higher, but the gap alone would not have killed it. The bigger concern was that the total cost was harder to model. There were add-ons, onboarding assumptions, and questions about future contact growth.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): So predictability mattered more than the lowest number?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Predictability and speed. We had a deadline tied to the new service-line launch. Your team was responsive, but we had several internal stakeholders asking for revised scenarios. By the time we had a clean comparison, ActiveCampaign was already approved.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is helpful. Were there product gaps?

Tony Orn (Procurement): A few perceived gaps, mostly around ease of use. Marketing wanted to build simple campaigns without involving operations every time. Your demonstration was capable, perhaps too capable for the first meeting.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I can see how that could happen. Did security or data handling block us?

Tony Orn (Procurement): No. Security reviewed both vendors and did not reject either. We also did not have a major integration blocker. Procurement was comfortable with both from a risk perspective.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What should we have done differently?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Give us a one-page commercial model earlier. Separate mandatory costs from optional services. Also, make the first workshop about the three workflows marketing actually needed, not the full platform.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Which three workflows?

Tony Orn (Procurement): New inquiry routing, event follow-up, and a monthly client newsletter. We may add lead scoring later, but it wasn't central to the purchase.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Did our implementation estimate create concern?

Tony Orn (Procurement): A little. It looked professional, but it assumed more consulting time than our team expected. Our operations manager thought we could configure the basics ourselves.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): And ActiveCampaign's offer matched that expectation?

Tony Orn (Procurement): More closely. Their partner gave us a lean first phase. I am not saying it will remain lean forever; I am saying it was easier to approve.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That distinction matters. If you could revisit one part of the evaluation, would it be the demo, pricing, or implementation plan?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Pricing presentation first. The demo second. We needed confidence that the system would be adopted, not merely that it could do everything.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. Are there any lessons we can share with your team later, perhaps around integration or governance?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Possibly. We still have to connect the forms to our CRM, and that may be less simple than advertised. But for now, please don't treat this as an invitation to reopen the deal.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Absolutely. I respect the decision. May I check in after your first quarter?

Tony Orn (Procurement): Yes, a light-touch check-in is fine. By then we will know whether the adoption assumptions held.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I'll note that. Before we finish, is there anything about our process that worked well?

Tony Orn (Procurement): The people worked well. Responses were prompt and technically informed. The issue was that we had to translate too much of the information into an internal business case ourselves.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That's fair feedback. We should have made the internal case easier to carry forward.

Tony Orn (Procurement): Exactly. Procurement doesn't want to choose the cheapest vendor in a vacuum. We want a defensible decision with no surprises. In this case, the simpler proposal won.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Thanks for being direct, Tony. I'll send a brief recap with no sales follow-up attached.

Tony Orn (Procurement): Good. And if you send anything, keep it to the commercial-model lesson. The marketing team is already deep in configuration.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Will do. Thanks again.
