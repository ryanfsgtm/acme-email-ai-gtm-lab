Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Preston, thanks for joining. Before we get into the account, how is the new store opening going?

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): Busy. We opened the doors last weekend, and apparently customers do not care that the marketing team would like a quiet launch week. It went well, though.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Congratulations. I saw Grady - Dach has around 170 employees across the retail operation. Has the expansion changed how you think about marketing automation?

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): It has changed the volume more than the strategy. We have promotions, loyalty messages, local events, and the online store. At the moment those live in separate spreadsheets and email tools.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What happens when a campaign needs to go out quickly?

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): Someone pulls a list from the ecommerce system, someone else checks the loyalty file, and then we argue about which version is current. Sometimes the campaign goes out on time. Sometimes the promotion is over before the segment is ready.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That sounds painful. Where does the customer data live today?

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): The ecommerce platform has transactions. The stores have point-of-sale data. Loyalty has another system. We do have a CRM, but the store teams barely use it, so it is not the complete picture.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Are you looking for one customer record or primarily campaign orchestration?

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): Primarily orchestration. I do not want to launch a three-year data cleansing program before we can send a birthday offer. But I do want enough identity resolution that we do not send a new-customer discount to someone who has bought from us twelve times.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Understood. Which audiences matter first?

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): Lapsed loyalty members, recent online buyers, and people who browse a category without purchasing. Store managers also want local-event invitations, which creates a geography problem.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Geography by store postcode?

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): Usually postcode, sometimes the store selected in the loyalty profile. The data is inconsistent. One customer moved and still gets offers for a store two hours away.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can use the preferred-store field where available and fall back to postcode. Would the marketing team own the segments, or would each store manager build their own?

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): Marketing should own the rules. Managers can request a segment, but if everyone edits the logic, we will have forty definitions of “lapsed.”

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That governance point is important. How are approvals handled now?

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): Informally. The brand team reviews creative, legal reviews certain promotions, and the channel manager presses send. There is no single approval record.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would a useful approval flow look like?

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): Two levels. Routine loyalty messages can have one approver. Anything involving pricing, finance, or sensitive customer categories needs legal and commercial sign-off. I need an audit trail that survives staff changes.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): And what does success look like after ninety days?

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): Faster campaign preparation is the first measure. Then incremental revenue, unsubscribe rate, and whether store teams stop asking for manual lists. I am wary of vanity metrics such as opens without context.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That makes sense. Do you have a baseline for preparation time?

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): A simple promotion takes two or three days. A cross-channel promotion can take a week. The number is approximate because nobody logs the start time.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can establish a baseline during onboarding. What concerns would prevent adoption?

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): Integration reliability and training. A tool can look beautiful in a demo and still be too complicated for a regional marketing coordinator who uses it once a month.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Would a small pilot help?

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): Yes. One online category and three stores. We can test a lapsed-member journey and one local-event campaign. I would rather prove the operating model than import every historical contact on day one.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How would you choose the three stores?

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): One mature store, one new store, and one that has a strong manager but messy data. If it works there, it is probably usable elsewhere.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Good test design. We can also compare a manually prepared control audience with an automated audience, provided the promotion rules remain identical.

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): I like that. Our analysts will want to know whether the lift is real or simply the result of a better week.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What would you need from us before recommending a next step?

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): A clear data map, the minimum fields needed for the pilot, examples of failure handling, and pricing that does not assume we activate every channel immediately.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I can provide those. We should also include deliverability ownership and the escalation path.

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): Definitely. We have had a vendor say deliverability was our problem after changing a sending domain.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We will make that explicit. I will send a pilot outline and schedule a working session with your ecommerce and loyalty owners.

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): Good. I need to check whether they can join next Thursday. The store opening has made calendars a bit theoretical.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I know the feeling. If Thursday slips, we can hold the same slot the following week.

Preston Bogan (VP Marketing): That works. This is useful, Luis. We are not looking for another dashboard; we are trying to make campaign execution less dependent on three people remembering the same spreadsheet.
