Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Zula, good afternoon. Before we begin, I saw the photo of your team’s studio renovation. Is the wall actually that bright orange?

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): It is, unfortunately. The marketing director calls it “energizing.” The privacy team calls it a fire alarm with a budget.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll avoid choosing sides. Today I’d like to review the first year, upcoming priorities, and anything we should adjust before the next renewal cycle.

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): Overall, the rollout landed better than I expected. We had a lot of concern about consent and regional access, especially with teams in Belgium and the Netherlands using the same instance.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What helped those concerns settle?

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): The permission model and the audit trail. Our admins can see who changed a subscription status, and regional teams don’t automatically inherit every audience. That gave legal something concrete to review.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Are there still gaps in the process?

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): Partner-supplied lists. We have one agency that insists its consent language is sufficient, but the evidence arrives as a spreadsheet with a date and a sentence. It’s not ideal.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How are you handling those records today?

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): They go into a restricted audience until the agency provides documentation. That slows campaigns, but it’s safer than treating every uploaded contact as equivalent.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would a source-level approval workflow help?

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): Yes, if it remains visible to the people building campaigns. We’ve had controls that existed in policy but disappeared at the point of use.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll note that as a design requirement. How has the platform performed for the marketing team?

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): Journey building is much faster. They can make regional variants without waiting for a developer. The trade-off is that they sometimes create too many variants, and then nobody knows which one is authoritative.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Is that a governance problem or a workflow problem?

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): Both. The tool makes experimentation easy, which is good. It also makes clutter easy. We need naming standards and an archive routine.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What does leadership want from the program this year?

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): Better attribution. The board wants to understand whether the increase in qualified pipeline came from events, content, or lifecycle programs. The current dashboard is useful, but it still requires explanation.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would a single executive view solve that?

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): It would help, but only if the definitions are stable. I don’t want a beautiful chart that quietly changes when someone updates a campaign category.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can add a definitions panel and a change log. Finance should sign off on the revenue logic.

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): Finance will sign off if they are involved before the dashboard is built. They dislike being invited to validate numbers after launch.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Noted. We’ll schedule a working session with finance, marketing operations, and privacy.

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): Include the regional marketing leads. They know where the operational exceptions are.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Will do. Any performance issues since the last review?

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): One slow report in January. It turned out to be a very broad date range and a large event table. The campaign sends were fine, so it was inconvenient rather than an outage.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Did the workaround hold?

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): Yes, narrowing the date range fixed it. Still, if executives are looking at quarterly numbers, they won’t want to run three smaller reports and add them by hand.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can ask our data team to review the query pattern and recommend a more durable report.

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): Please do, but don’t frame it as an emergency. We have a workaround and other priorities.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. What about training? Are new users getting up to speed?

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): The basic training is fine. The harder part is judgment: when to use a broad audience, when to request approval, and when a regional exception needs legal review. That knowledge is mostly tribal.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We could turn the common cases into short playbooks.

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): That would be more useful than another two-hour webinar. Give people examples: an event list, a partner list, a repermission campaign, and a deletion request.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I like that. We’ll draft four scenarios and have you review the privacy language.

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): Good. Also, Braze keeps appearing in competitive conversations. Our marketing director asks whether we are missing anything.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What does she think Braze would improve?

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): Real-time engagement and more sophisticated mobile orchestration. Those are not currently our biggest gaps, but the comparison comes up whenever a vendor sends her a benchmark.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would you consider switching?

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): Not based on benchmarks alone. Migration would reopen the consent, access, and training work we just completed. If our requirements change materially, we’ll evaluate, but today the platform is meeting the core need.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That’s helpful context. For the next quarter, I’ll capture three actions: source approval workflow, executive attribution definitions, and practical privacy playbooks.

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): Add the report-performance review, with a non-urgent priority.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Added. I’ll send owners and dates by Friday.

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): Fine. And please tell the marketing director the orange wall is not a product requirement.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): I’ll put that in the unofficial notes.

Zula Kirlin (Privacy): Then we have a deal. Thanks, Marcus.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Thank you, Zula. Speak soon.
