Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Perfect. Since Homenick Inc is a large professional-services organization, I would like to use this session to establish the onboarding sequence rather than try to configure everything today. What is the first business process you want live?

Bill Hayes (Implementation Partner): New-client onboarding. We sell several service packages, and the handoff from signed agreement to kickoff is too dependent on an individual remembering the next email.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Who owns that handoff today?

Bill Hayes (Implementation Partner): Delivery operations owns the checklist, account leads own the relationship, and marketing sends a few standard messages. Nobody owns the complete customer journey.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is a useful distinction. What should happen immediately after signature?

Bill Hayes (Implementation Partner): The client should receive a confirmation, a request for required information, and a link to schedule kickoff. Internally, the delivery coordinator needs a task. If the client does not respond, someone should know before the kickoff date becomes a problem.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How many service packages need different versions?

Bill Hayes (Implementation Partner): Four main packages, plus custom engagements. The custom ones cannot be completely automated, but they can use the same first step.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would you prefer one shared workflow with branches or separate workflows by package?

Bill Hayes (Implementation Partner): One shared workflow if reporting remains understandable. We have been burned by complicated branches where nobody knows which version a client received.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can name each branch and include the package in the activity history. What system is the source of truth for signed agreements?

Bill Hayes (Implementation Partner): The CRM, although the contract tool updates it asynchronously. That is another place where timing matters.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): How long can the delay be?

Bill Hayes (Implementation Partner): Usually five minutes, occasionally an hour. If the first message fires before the package is populated, the wrong instructions go out.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then we should add a validation step and perhaps hold enrollment until the required fields are present.

Bill Hayes (Implementation Partner): Yes. I would rather delay ten minutes than send a client a form for the wrong service.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What information is mandatory?

Bill Hayes (Implementation Partner): Package, account lead, kickoff target date, billing contact, and region. Canada is the initial focus, but Homenick has teams elsewhere.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Are there differences in consent or language requirements by region?

Bill Hayes (Implementation Partner): There are differences in legal wording. English is enough for this phase, but the footer and unsubscribe behavior must be correct. Our legal team will provide approved text.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Great. Let us talk about exceptions. What if a client is already active and purchases an additional package?

Bill Hayes (Implementation Partner): They should not receive a first-time welcome sequence. The account lead may still want a package-specific kickoff message.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): So we need a customer-status check plus an expansion flag.

Bill Hayes (Implementation Partner): Correct. And please make the expansion path visible to delivery operations. Marketing cannot be the only team that knows what happened.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Who will administer the workflow after launch?

Bill Hayes (Implementation Partner): I will coordinate, but I do not want to be the permanent bottleneck. We have a marketing operations group of about six and a delivery-operations group much larger than that.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can define permissions by role and create a change-request process. How will you measure whether onboarding improves?

Bill Hayes (Implementation Partner): Time from signature to scheduled kickoff, completion of the required-information form, and the number of manual reminders. I would also like to know if clients drop out before kickoff.
