Amelia Hart (Account Executive): When you say less dependent on individuals, is the main issue process consistency, reporting, or the actual campaign work?

Brianne Emard (Implementation Partner): All three, but process consistency is the loudest one. If our marketing operations person is away, people ask me how to create a form, where a field is mapped, or whether a lead has been followed up. That’s not really implementation work anymore.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. What happens after someone submits a form today?

Brianne Emard (Implementation Partner): It depends on which site and which team owns it. Usually the record enters Pardot, gets an email, and then someone exports a list. If the person looks interesting, sales receives a task. The timing isn’t predictable. Sometimes the task is created the same day, sometimes three days later.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): And how are you measuring that?

Brianne Emard (Implementation Partner): We have reports, but I wouldn’t call them trusted. We can see campaign responses and opportunities, yet the source data is messy. A contact may be duplicated, or the campaign name doesn’t match the opportunity source. Finance wants a clean view of contribution, while the teams mostly want to know whether a campaign generated conversations.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That tension is common. Would a shared set of lifecycle definitions help, or is the bigger concern getting the systems to enforce them?

Brianne Emard (Implementation Partner): Enforcing them. We have definitions in a document. People interpret them differently. A marketing-qualified lead in one group is just a downloaded guide in another. Also, our sales process differs by region. The Nordics team is fairly consultative, while the UK group wants a shorter route to qualification.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can support different routing rules while keeping the underlying definitions consistent. I don’t want to pretend that one workflow fits everyone. How many people would need to administer the system?

Brianne Emard (Implementation Partner): Probably four directly, though around thirty users would touch it. We’re at 627 employees, but not all of them are commercial users. The tricky part is that our teams change often. A workflow that makes sense now may be wrong after a reorganization.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): So maintainability is as important as functionality.

Brianne Emard (Account Executive): Exactly. We’ve had automation projects where the launch looked successful and then nobody wanted to own the rules. Six months later, there were exceptions layered on exceptions.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would make you confident this time?

Brianne Emard (Implementation Partner): Clear ownership, an audit trail, and a way to test changes before they affect every business unit. Also, we need our implementation people to be able to troubleshoot without opening a support ticket for every small issue.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Let me show you how we usually approach that. We start with a central data model, then create controlled variations for regional routing. Brianne Emard (Implementation Partner): Does that include email templates? Our brand team is concerned about people making local edits that drift from approved language.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Yes, templates can be governed centrally, with permissions around who can edit content and who can only use approved versions. Local teams can still personalize selected sections.

Brianne Emard (Implementation Partner): Good. We also have consent requirements. We serve customers in several European markets, and privacy asks us to be careful about subscription status and regional preferences.
