Dallin Reichel (Marketing Operations): You caught me between both, actually. It was sunny ten minutes ago and now the windows look like we’re underwater. Typical. I’m glad we could speak, though. I’ve been asked to look properly at the automation options before we take anything to our director.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That makes sense. I saw that Dooley and Sons is a manufacturing business with roughly seventy-five employees, and that you’re evaluating Marketo alongside us. Before I show anything, could you talk me through what prompted the review?

Dallin Reichel (Marketing Operations): The short version is that our current setup has become a collection of workarounds. We run campaigns for distributors, direct buyers, and service partners, but the handoffs are mostly spreadsheets and calendar reminders. If someone downloads a specification sheet, the next action depends on whether I remember to create the task. That’s not a great operating model.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What happens when the handoff is missed?

Dallin Reichel (Marketing Operations): Sales says marketing sends unqualified names. Marketing says sales doesn’t follow up. Both statements are occasionally true. We also have duplicate contacts because a person might appear under a distributor email, a personal business address, and a trade-show scan. I spend a lot of time cleaning records instead of building journeys.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): And what would a better process look like for you?

Dallin Reichel (Marketing Operations): I want to build a journey, test it, and change it without asking an administrator for a ticket. Something like: enquiry comes in, check sector and product interest, send the right technical content, wait a sensible amount of time, then alert the right salesperson. If the person is not ready, they should go into a nurture stream rather than just sit in a queue.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That’s helpful. How important is testing? Are you currently doing much A/B testing?

Dallin Reichel (Marketing Operations): Barely. We can compare campaign results after the fact, but testing subject lines or branches is awkward. I’d like a visual workflow where I can test two paths and see whether a particular message or delay changes progression. “Awkward” is the polite word. It’s slow enough that people stop trying.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. Let me share a simple example. We can trigger a journey from a form completion, apply rules for sector, region, and product interest, and then branch into different messages or tasks. You can preview the path before activating it. If you change a delay or an audience rule, the next version can be tested separately rather than editing a live process blindly.

Dallin Reichel (Marketing Operations): Can I do that myself, or does it require your services team?

Priya Shah (Account Executive): You can do the day-to-day building yourself. We would help with the initial design, data mapping, and training. More complex integrations may need technical support, but routine journey creation, testing, and reporting are intended for the marketing operator.

Dallin Reichel (Marketing Operations): Good. The other thing is reporting. Our managing director asks which campaigns influenced enquiries and revenue, and I can give him clicks, opens, and form fills. That does not answer the question.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What systems contain the later-stage information—your CRM, finance system, or somewhere else?

Dallin Reichel (Marketing Operations): CRM has opportunities, but sales sometimes updates it late. Finance has invoices. We could probably start with CRM opportunity stages and amount, then improve the data quality. The director wants a view that is credible enough for budget discussions, not necessarily an academic model.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can connect campaign activity to contacts and opportunities and show influenced pipeline, sourced pipeline, and progression by campaign. I’d want to confirm your CRM fields before promising the exact dashboard. Attribution can be configured, but it is only as reliable as the opportunity and contact associations.

Dallin Reichel (Marketing Operations): That caveat is fair. I’d rather hear it now. Marketo has a strong reputation for reporting, though. The concern is whether it will be too heavy for a team of one and a half people.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is an important comparison. A senior buyer may care most about attribution and reporting—being able to explain which programs influenced pipeline and revenue—while the person operating the system usually cares about building journeys, testing variations, and moving quickly. If we only satisfy the reporting question, the system can still fail because nobody has time to use it. If we only make building easy, leadership may not trust the numbers. We should validate both sides.

Dallin Reichel (Marketing Operations): Exactly. I’m the half person, by the way. Our communications manager helps when she can, but she also does events and internal communications. I need the workflow to be quick enough that a campaign does not take three weeks to launch.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would your managing director join a follow-up focused on attribution?

Dallin Reichel (Marketing Operations): Probably, if I can show him something concrete. He won’t sit through a generic product tour. He’ll ask, “Can I see the return by product line, territory, and quarter?”

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then I’ll prepare two views: one for the operator, showing journey construction and testing, and one for leadership, showing campaign influence and pipeline.
