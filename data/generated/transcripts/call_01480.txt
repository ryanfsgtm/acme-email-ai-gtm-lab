Priya Shah (Account Executive): Hi, Unique. Thanks for taking the time. Before we begin, are you working from Melbourne or Sydney today?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): Melbourne, though my calendar is set to Sydney time for reasons nobody can explain. So if I look confused, that is why.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We will confirm times verbally. I would like to understand how sales operations supports the retail organization, where handoffs break down, and what you would want to improve. Does that sound right?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): Yes. We are a large organization, around eighteen hundred people, with several retail brands and regional teams. The scale is the issue, but it is also the reason simple answers rarely survive contact with the business.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How are marketing leads handed to sales today?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): It depends on the brand. One team uses CRM queues, another sends spreadsheets, and a third sends email alerts. We have tried to standardize it twice.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What happened?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): The central process was sensible, but local teams had different definitions of an active customer, a prospect, and a store account. They kept adding exceptions until the workflow became impossible to maintain.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Which problem has the greatest commercial impact?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): Slow follow-up on high-intent inquiries. We can identify people who request a quote or speak with an advisor, but the assignment rules do not always recognize the correct region or product category.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How long can follow-up take?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): Sometimes under an hour, sometimes two business days. The average hides the worst cases. A lead may sit in a queue because the territory field is blank.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Is the territory field entered by the customer?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): Occasionally. More often it comes from the form, the CRM account, or an enrichment service. Those sources do not always agree.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How do managers learn that a lead was delayed?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): They usually do not, unless someone complains. We have dashboards, but the dashboards report volume more reliably than accountability.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What would accountability look like?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): A timestamped handoff, a named owner, an escalation if there is no action, and a record of the disposition. We also need to distinguish a real opportunity from a service question or an existing-customer request.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What systems are involved?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): Our CRM, several marketing tools, a customer service platform, and data from the ecommerce sites. There are also regional spreadsheets that people insist are temporary, though some have been around for years.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a broad landscape. Are you seeking a replacement system or an orchestration layer?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): We have not decided. The current tools are not all bad. Replacing everything would be disruptive and probably politically impossible. We may need a common process with selective integrations.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What is driving the evaluation now?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): A new operating model. Leadership wants a consistent customer journey across brands, but the regional teams need room to adapt. We need to prove that consistency does not mean centralizing every decision.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How would you measure success?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): Time to first action, percentage of leads assigned correctly, conversion by source, and the number of manual touches per handoff. I would also track exceptions, because a process with too many exceptions is not really a process.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Do you have baselines?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): Partial ones. We have good data for the main brand, weaker data for two regional brands, and almost none for the smaller channels. That makes a company-wide business case difficult.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would a pilot with the main brand be acceptable?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): Possibly, but the pilot must include one complicated region. If we test only the cleanest path, leadership will approve something that fails where the complexity actually lives.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a strong design principle. Who would need to participate?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): Sales operations, marketing operations, one regional sales manager, customer service, and someone from data governance. IT will want to approve the integrations, even if they do not attend every session.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): What concerns would they raise?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): Data residency, access controls, auditability, and the risk of creating another system that nobody owns. Also, retail has seasonal peaks. A workflow that works in a quiet month may fail during holiday campaigns.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): When is your next peak period?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): November and December are the obvious ones, but some brands have school-year and sporting-event cycles. We would need load testing before any broad rollout.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Would you want automation to make assignments automatically, or recommend an owner for human confirmation?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): For straightforward cases, automatic assignment. For ambiguous cases, recommendation and an exception queue. We cannot let an opaque rule silently send a valuable lead to the wrong team.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): How much visibility should local managers have?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): Their own team and cross-brand summary metrics. Not every customer record across the entire group. Access should follow responsibility, not curiosity.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. What would be the next useful step?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): A process-mapping workshop using three actual lead paths: a quote request, an existing customer asking about a product, and a regionally ambiguous inquiry. Then we can see whether your approach handles reality.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I can prepare that workshop. Would next month work?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): Probably. I need to include our data governance lead, and she is traveling for the next two weeks. Do not put a firm date in the opportunity yet.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will leave timing as tentative. Is there an internal budget cycle we should understand?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): Budget discussions begin in the next planning round, but the project would likely require sponsorship from both commercial and technology leadership. I can influence the requirements; I cannot approve the spend alone.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That distinction is helpful. Would a successful pilot need a quantified return?

Unique Kulas (Sales Operations): Yes, but not only revenue. Avoided manual work, fewer routing errors, and better customer response times all matter. The CFO will still ask for revenue impact, though.
