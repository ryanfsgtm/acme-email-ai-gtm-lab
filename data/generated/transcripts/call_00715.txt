Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Hi, Norberto. Thanks for joining. I understand you are representing IT in the marketing automation review. Is that right?

Norberto Schmitt (IT): Yes. Marketing asked us to assess integration, security, and the amount of support they would need. I am not the final buyer, but I can make the decision easier or harder.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a useful role. What is driving the review at Klein - Halvorson?

Norberto Schmitt (IT): Marketing has outgrown its current email tool. They want behavioral journeys and better segmentation. From IT's side, the concern is that every new marketing platform becomes an integration project that nobody budgets properly.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What systems would be involved?

Norberto Schmitt (IT): The CRM, website forms, event registration, and our identity provider. There is also an ERP, but I hope that remains out of scope. If someone says the ERP is required on day one, the project will stop.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can keep it out of the initial design unless a use case proves otherwise. What does marketing need first?

Norberto Schmitt (IT): They want lead scoring, automated follow-up, and a way to stop sending messages when a prospect becomes an opportunity or customer. They also want sales to see the engagement history.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How reliable is the current CRM data?

Norberto Schmitt (IT): Mixed. Account records are decent. Contact roles are inconsistent. Some people use personal email addresses for event registration, then the CRM has a work address, so matching is not always straightforward.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That could affect suppression and scoring. Does marketing have a process for resolving duplicates?

Norberto Schmitt (IT): A manual one. They export a list, compare it, and ask sales reps to confirm. It works at low volume but will not scale. I have warned them that automation cannot manufacture clean identity data.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Fair point. Would a pilot with a defined audience be acceptable?

Norberto Schmitt (IT): Potentially. The manufacturing division has a clear campaign calendar, so that would be easier than trying to include every business unit. But the marketing director is pushing for a company-wide license because she wants consistency.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Is the disagreement about scope or budget?

Norberto Schmitt (IT): Both, though budget is the louder word. The team likes the idea of a broad platform, but nobody has calculated the internal hours for integration, testing, and governance. The subscription price is visible; the operating cost is not.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We should include internal effort in the business case. What security requirements are mandatory?

Norberto Schmitt (IT): SSO, role-based access, audit logs, and clear retention controls. We also need to know whether support staff can access customer data and how that access is approved.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I can provide those details and bring a technical specialist. Would your identity team need to review the setup?

Norberto Schmitt (IT): Yes, but they are overloaded. If the configuration is standard, it may be a short review. If it needs custom scripting, it goes into a queue that can last months.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Then we should validate the standard path early. How do you handle campaign approvals today?

Norberto Schmitt (IT): There is no consistent workflow. Marketing says the manager reviews campaigns; the manager says the campaign owner is responsible. Usually it is fine, until someone updates a list at the last minute.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would approval controls be useful, or would they slow the team down?

Norberto Schmitt (IT): Both. Controls help, but if every minor edit requires IT approval, people will work around the system. Marketing should own content and audiences within boundaries; IT should own identity, integrations, and security.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That division sounds practical. What would make this initiative fail?

Norberto Schmitt (IT): A broad rollout without data cleanup, unclear ownership, and a sales team that does not use the engagement information. Also, if the platform is difficult for marketers, they will return to spreadsheets even if the integration is technically perfect.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would convince you to proceed?

Norberto Schmitt (IT): A limited design that proves the CRM handoff, identity matching, suppression, and access model. I also need an honest implementation plan. Do not tell me a four-week launch is realistic if our approvals alone take six weeks.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. We can map dependencies and identify what can happen in parallel. Would you include marketing operations in the next session?

Norberto Schmitt (IT): Definitely. They know where the current process bends. Sales operations too, because the handoff only matters if sales agrees on the statuses.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will schedule a technical discovery with those teams. We can use the manufacturing campaign as the pilot example and leave ERP integration as a documented future consideration.

Norberto Schmitt (IT): That would be more credible. One correction: our event tool does not expose every attendance field in real time, so do not promise immediate triggers until we verify the connector.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Good correction. We will treat event latency as an open technical question and test it explicitly.

Norberto Schmitt (IT): Also, the identity provider is available, but access provisioning is managed by a separate group. That is another dependency.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will add that to the responsibility matrix. Are there any timing requirements?

Norberto Schmitt (IT): Marketing wants something running before the next planning cycle, but the date is not fixed. If the project requires a rushed decision, I would advise against it.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is helpful. I will send a summary with pilot scope, dependencies, estimated effort, and the questions for the technical session.

Norberto Schmitt (IT): Send it to me and the marketing director. If she sees the effort clearly, the conversation will be more productive.
