Marcus Chen (Account Executive): The initial scope is customer profile data, purchase events, loyalty status, browsing interactions, and communication preferences. We would exclude payment-card details and free-text customer-service notes.

Willard Marks (Privacy): Browsing interactions can still be personal data. Are you proposing individual-level tracking from day one?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Then the implementation needs separate consent signals, mapped to separate processing purposes. The journey engine should evaluate eligibility before activating a communication.

Willard Marks (Privacy): And if consent is withdrawn while someone is already in a journey?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): The withdrawal should remove them from eligible audiences and prevent future sends. We should test the timing with a real event and a controlled contact record.

Willard Marks (Privacy): I want that test documented, including logs. Our current problem is not only whether the system obeys a preference; it is proving that it did.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): We can include evidence capture in the acceptance criteria: source event, transformation, decision, and resulting suppression.

Willard Marks (Privacy): What is your retention model?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Data retention is configurable by object and purpose. We would propose a shorter period for behavioral events than for account records, with deletion and suppression handling documented in the operating procedure.

Willard Marks (Privacy): “Propose” is not enough. Who owns the final retention schedule?

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Your privacy team should approve the schedule, with IT confirming that the connectors and deletion jobs can enforce it. Marketing should not be the sole owner.

Willard Marks (Privacy): Correct. We have had marketing teams promise deletion behavior that the source system could not actually perform.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is why I would make source-system capability a dependency before committing to launch. We can create a matrix showing which system is authoritative for identity, consent, loyalty status, and purchase history.

Willard Marks (Privacy): Include Mailchimp in that comparison. Some teams still use it, and the migration cannot assume every list is clean.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Understood. We should inventory the Mailchimp audiences, identify duplicate and stale records, and decide whether they are migrated, archived, or rebuilt from an authoritative source.

Willard Marks (Privacy): Also identify how unsubscribes are handled there. A list that looks inactive may contain important suppression history.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Agreed. We will not treat inactivity as permission to message.
