Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is useful to know. What is difficult with the current tools?

Elizabeth Batz-Aufderhar (Marketing Operations): Audience building is slow, reporting is stitched together manually, and event leads often arrive after the salesperson has already followed up. We also have too many local email tools. They are not all bad, but they make frequency and consent controls hard to see centrally.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Which group feels that pain most sharply?

Elizabeth Batz-Aufderhar (Marketing Operations): Events. A conference can generate several thousand scans, and the follow-up is tactical: send a thank-you, send a brochure, assign a spreadsheet. Subscription marketing has more mature journeys and may not want to change. So the first use case would probably be event-to-sales follow-up.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That sounds like a focused starting point. Who owns the event process today?

Elizabeth Batz-Aufderhar (Marketing Operations): A campaign manager owns the email, events owns the scan file, sales operations assigns territories, and regional sales leaders decide whether the follow-up is good enough. I coordinate, but I do not control all of them.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Would a common workflow need executive sponsorship?

Elizabeth Batz-Aufderhar (Marketing Operations): Eventually, yes. The last evaluation had a sponsor who was enthusiastic at the beginning and then moved to another division. After that, calls became tactical. We kept discussing field mappings and never got a decision.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would make this evaluation different?

Elizabeth Batz-Aufderhar (Marketing Operations): A named business owner, a bounded pilot, and a decision date tied to a real event calendar. We should not run a discovery project indefinitely. The sponsor cannot just be copied on emails; they need to attend the important checkpoints and approve trade-offs.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): That is reasonable. Is there a candidate sponsor?

Elizabeth Batz-Aufderhar (Marketing Operations): The VP of Digital Revenue is interested because event leads affect subscription sales, but he has not committed to owning the program. The head of events is practical and influential, though not senior enough to clear every budget issue.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): So sponsorship is possible but not confirmed. What is the next major event?

Elizabeth Batz-Aufderhar (Marketing Operations): A September industry forum. The lead volume is usually high, but the exact attendance is not final. We could pilot with one event and one sales region. If the dates move, the pilot moves too.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would you measure?

Elizabeth Batz-Aufderhar (Marketing Operations): Time from scan to assignment, percentage with a valid owner, first-touch rate, and sales acceptance. I would also measure whether operations can produce the report without a spreadsheet exercise. Revenue attribution would be interesting but not a fair first gate.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What are the privacy or security requirements?

Elizabeth Batz-Aufderhar (Marketing Operations): Consent source and purpose must be retained. We operate across the Benelux, and some events collect consent on paper or through the organizer’s system. I need a process for proving what we are allowed to send. IT will ask about SSO, regional access, and data export.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Which systems are involved?

Elizabeth Batz-Aufderhar (Marketing Operations): CRM, the events registration platform, a subscription database, and several local email tools. I would exclude the subscription database from phase one. If we try to unify every audience, the project will become architectural rather than useful.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): Agreed. Are any vendors being considered?

Elizabeth Batz-Aufderhar (Marketing Operations): Mailchimp remains in use by a small team. It is familiar and inexpensive for basic sends. That is not the same as a strategic preference, but people compare against it whenever central tooling is discussed.

Marcus Chen (Account Executive): What would cause a no-decision outcome this time?

Elizabeth Batz-Aufderhar (Marketing Operations): If the sponsor never becomes accountable, if the pilot depends on data we cannot access, or if every region demands a different design before we prove one workflow.
