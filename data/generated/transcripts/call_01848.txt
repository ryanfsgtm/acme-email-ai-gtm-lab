Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Dax, thanks for joining. Before we get into workflows, could you describe your role in the implementation?

Dax Hessel (Implementation Partner): I coordinate the delivery partner, the university project team, and the vendor workstream. That sounds tidy on paper. In practice, it means I am usually the person asking which spreadsheet is the latest spreadsheet.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What systems are involved today?

Dax Hessel (Implementation Partner): CRM, student information system, a separate events platform, and two email tools inherited from different faculties. We are trying to standardize without making the faculties feel they have lost control.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): What prompted the search now?

Dax Hessel (Implementation Partner): The current tools can send messages, but the handoffs are manual. A prospective student attends an event, someone exports a list, another person cleans it, then a faculty coordinator decides what happens. It is slow and there is no shared history.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): How would you define success six months after launch?

Dax Hessel (Implementation Partner): A contact should have one usable timeline, consent should travel with the record, and coordinators should know why a message was sent. We also need reporting that the central team can trust.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I want to show you how we can map a contact through several audience conditions and send different content based on enrollment interest, event attendance, and faculty. We call that advanced journey branching.

Dax Hessel (Implementation Partner): That is useful, but I need to understand who will own it. A beautiful branch that nobody maintains becomes another abandoned diagram.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We would normally train central administrators and give faculty users controlled templates. The branches can be documented and versioned.

Dax Hessel (Implementation Partner): Show me the maintenance side, then. Suppose the engineering faculty changes its open-day date. How many places must someone edit?

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): If the date is stored as an event attribute, one update can feed the relevant paths. If it is embedded in copy, the email content needs review. We can flag both.

Dax Hessel (Implementation Partner): Good. Now the less glamorous issue: consent. Canadian and European applicants may be in the same system, and our policy is not perfectly uniform.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can preserve consent status and source, then use region or program rules to determine eligibility. Your privacy counsel should validate the policy mapping.

Dax Hessel (Implementation Partner): Yes, because the system should not become the policy. We need an audit trail showing the source, timestamp, and person or form that captured consent.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is available. We can also restrict edits and report on changes.

Dax Hessel (Implementation Partner): Can an administrator export that without involving your support team?

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Yes, subject to permissions. We can include an export exercise in the technical workshop.

Dax Hessel (Implementation Partner): Please do. Our team has been promised self-service by other vendors and then discovered the export button was hidden behind a premium role.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): I will verify the exact role requirements. On data integration, which system is the source of truth for program and applicant status?

Dax Hessel (Implementation Partner): The student information system for status, CRM for relationship activity, and the events platform for attendance. That sounds clear until an applicant changes programs midstream.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): We can define ownership by field and use a conflict rule. We should not allow a marketing workflow to overwrite an academic status.

Dax Hessel (Implementation Partner): Correct. Also, the implementation calendar is tight. We have a September intake and a December communications freeze.

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): Would a phased launch help—central recruitment first, then faculty journeys?

Luis Romero (Customer Success Manager): That is important context. What would be a credible first phase?

Dax Hessel (Implementation Partner): One program, one event type, one consent model, and a dashboard with delivery, engagement, and conversion. If that works, we expand.
