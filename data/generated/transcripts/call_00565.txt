Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Jaeden, thanks for joining. Before we start, are you still in the office, or have I caught you in the procurement version of a quiet Friday?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): I am in the office, though “quiet” may be generous. We are a 27-person software company, so procurement is also security, finance, and occasionally fixing the printer.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. I have Kovacek Inc in the United Kingdom, with the marketing automation opportunity closed and recorded as lost to Customer.io. I would like to understand what happened and whether there is a future fit. Is that fair?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): Yes. We selected Customer.io for this cycle. The decision was not dramatic. They were already familiar to our product team, and the initial package was easier to approve.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Was the main issue price, implementation effort, or capability?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): Simplicity and timing. We needed a basic onboarding sequence for trial users. Your proposal seemed more capable, but capability creates questions: who administers it, how long setup takes, and whether we would pay for things we do not use.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That makes sense. Who owns growth at Kovacek?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): No dedicated marketing operations person. The product marketer owns campaigns, one engineer helps with events, and sales follows up on accounts. I handle contracts and vendor review.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What did the first use case need to do?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): Capture trial signups, send a few educational messages, and tell sales when someone becomes active. We have a small volume, perhaps a few hundred new trials a month. We did not need complex account scoring.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How are trial events generated?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): Product events come through our application database. Website forms are separate. The product marketer wanted the automation tool connected directly, while engineering wanted an event queue in between.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Which approach won?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): The event queue. Engineering already operates it for product notifications, and they did not want another vendor reading the production database directly.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a reasonable security boundary. Did Customer.io support the queue easily?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): They had a straightforward example, yes. Your team could probably have handled it, but we were comparing the time to first test, not just the feature list.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What did the approval process look like?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): I checked the contract, finance checked the monthly spend, and our technical lead checked data access. Because the amount was under our small-vendor threshold, we did not need a formal committee. That made timing decisive.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Was our contract difficult?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): Not unusually difficult. There were more implementation assumptions and optional components. We had to ask what we were really buying. Customer.io's proposal was narrower.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): If we had offered a narrower package, would the result have changed?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): Maybe, but I would not say definitely. The product marketer also liked their interface, and the technical lead had seen it at a previous company. Familiarity carried weight.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What concerns remain with the chosen tool?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): We have not launched yet. So the decision is not proof that the implementation will be good. We still need to validate event naming, consent, and how to stop messages when a trial converts.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is important. What would a successful launch look like?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): A new trial receives a useful welcome message, advanced users receive different guidance, and converted accounts stop receiving trial prompts. We also need a clear audit trail when somebody changes a campaign.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What is your current consent model?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): We ask for product communications separately from marketing. The product marketer wanted a single checkbox originally, but legal rejected that. We are still finalizing wording.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Did that affect the vendor decision?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): Not directly. It affects launch. Both vendors would have needed to follow the same rule.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How many people will build campaigns?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): Two. The product marketer and me for basic changes. I am not a campaign specialist, so if the interface is too complicated, changes will wait for the product marketer.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Does Customer.io include onboarding help?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): Yes, but it is mostly documentation and office hours. We did not buy a large services package. We wanted to get moving.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): If the first phase works, what might you add later?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): Webinar follow-up, lead handoff, and perhaps customer expansion signals. But those are future ideas, not committed requirements. We are careful not to purchase the future before proving the present.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is a good procurement principle. Did the recorded close date in May reflect the actual decision date?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): It was close enough for the system. We selected the vendor around then, but contract signatures and implementation scheduling happened separately.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Is there anything we could have clarified earlier?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): The minimum viable package. Your discovery covered many possible departments, and that was useful, but it made the purchase feel larger than our immediate problem. For a small company, a broad roadmap can sound like a broad bill.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is helpful feedback. If we reconnect later, should the conversation start with a smaller trial-user workflow?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): Yes, if Customer.io does not meet our needs. But do not assume the problem will be the platform. It could be our event taxonomy or lack of ownership.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Understood. What checkpoint would tell you whether to revisit the market?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): If the team cannot launch the first journey within six weeks, or if we cannot reliably suppress converted users. Also if reporting is too limited to show which trial behaviors correlate with activation.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would it be appropriate for me to check in after that period?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): A light check-in is fine. Please do not make it a hard sales follow-up. We have chosen a vendor and need space to test it.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I will send a brief note and leave it there. Any final procurement lesson for us?

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): Make the smallest credible package visible early. Show the data boundary and the first implementation step. We were not rejecting sophistication; we were avoiding a project that our team could not own.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That is clear and useful. Thanks for being candid, Jaeden.

Jaeden Murazik (Procurement): Thanks, Amelia. The conversation was constructive, even if we did not buy.
