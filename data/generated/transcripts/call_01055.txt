Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): Yes, although I may need to step away briefly. We’re a small manufacturing company, and revenue operations sometimes means finding the missing shipping file.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That sounds familiar. What are you hoping to improve?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): Follow-up after trade shows and quote requests. We have a few hundred active accounts, but our process is mostly manual. Someone exports a list, sends an email, and then tries to remember who needs a call.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How are leads captured?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): Website forms, event scans, referrals, and salespeople forwarding emails. The event scans are the messiest. We get incomplete titles, duplicate records, and sometimes a company name that is just a brand abbreviation.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Does Braze handle any of that today?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): Braze is used for some customer communications, mostly by the product team. We’re considering whether it could support more of the commercial journey, but I’m not sure it fits our sales process.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What makes you uncertain?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): Our buyers are not impulse purchasers. A manufacturer may take months to evaluate a machine, and the person who downloads a specification sheet may be an engineer, not the decision-maker. Automated scoring could mistake research for intent.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): That’s a useful distinction. Would automation be more valuable for consistent follow-up than for predicting purchase readiness?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): Probably. We need reminders and useful context, not a robot declaring that somebody is hot.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What would a good follow-up look like after an event?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): Within a day, send a relevant note based on the session or product area. Then, if they engage, route the record to the right salesperson. If they don’t engage, maybe one more message. We don’t want a seven-email sequence.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How do you identify the right salesperson?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): Territory and product line, mostly. Sometimes an account spans both. The routing rules are in a spreadsheet maintained by one person, which is a risk.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would centralizing those rules help?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): Yes, as long as I can understand and change them. We have 170 employees, so I’m not looking for an enterprise administration project.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can start with a limited model: account, territory, product interest, and owner. Do you have a CRM that stores those fields?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): We do, but product interest is often free text. “Press line,” “presses,” and a particular model may all refer to the same family.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Then normalization may be a prerequisite. Could a controlled interest list replace free text for new records while preserving the old value?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): That would help. I don’t want to lose the original note, because sales sometimes includes useful context there.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We can preserve the note and add a structured field for routing. What metrics matter to you?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): Response time, event follow-up completion, meetings booked, and eventually influenced revenue. I’m cautious about attribution because our sales cycle is long and offline conversations matter.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Would an initial dashboard focus on operational metrics, with revenue attribution as a later phase?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): Yes. If you lead with a revenue number that nobody can validate, our managing director will lose interest.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What about consent and frequency limits?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): We sell in Australia and New Zealand, and our lists include people from other countries. We need opt-outs respected everywhere. Also, customers should not receive a marketing sequence while a service issue is being handled.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Could the service status suppress commercial messaging?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): It should, but only if the service status is reliable. Some of our support notes are still in email.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Then we should not promise a perfect suppression rule until we inspect that data. We could begin with explicit exclusions and add the service signal later.

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): I appreciate that. Vendors usually say “yes” and discover the limitation during implementation.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): What implementation resources do you have?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): Me, half an IT person, and a marketing coordinator who is very capable but already overloaded. We can’t spend months cleaning every historical record.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): A focused pilot could avoid that. One event type, one product family, and a small sales group. We would define the minimum fields and test routing before expanding.

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): That sounds realistic. What would it cost?

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): The exact amount depends on the integration and services scope. I can provide a range with assumptions, but I don’t want to pretend the number is final before seeing the CRM structure.

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): Fine. The budget is not approved yet. We’re comparing this with improving the existing Braze setup and possibly hiring a coordinator.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): How will you compare those options?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): Time to launch, ongoing administration, and whether sales actually uses the outputs. A cheaper system that creates more cleanup is not cheaper for us.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Could we include an operating-cost estimate in the comparison?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): Yes, especially the hours required each month. Also tell me what happens when the person who built a workflow leaves.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): We’ll include documentation, ownership, and administrator training. Workflows should be named and reviewed, not treated as personal property.

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): Good. Another concern is email tone. Our customers expect technical credibility. Generic marketing language would hurt us.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Could sales and product approve the initial templates together?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): They should. Marketing can own the process, but product engineers know whether a claim is accurate.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): I’ll propose a content review step for the pilot. Are there any technical requirements we haven’t covered?

Fredy Gleichner (Revenue Operations): We need CRM sync, event-import support, unsubscribe handling, and an export option. I don’t want our data trapped if priorities change.

Amelia Hart (Account Executive): Those are reasonable requirements. I’ll confirm the export formats and integration limits in the follow-up.
