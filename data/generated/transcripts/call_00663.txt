Priya Shah (Account Executive): Sophie, thanks for joining. Before we start, I understand it is late morning in the Nordics. Have you managed a coffee yet?

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Half a coffee. Procurement runs on interrupted beverages. We are a 90-person financial services firm, so everyone wears two hats, sometimes three.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is helpful context. What prompted you to look at marketing automation?

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Our marketing manager is spending too much time preparing lists and too little time planning campaigns. We need better follow-up for enquiries and events, but the budget is not unlimited.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): When you say the budget is limited, is there a number already approved?

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Not exactly. We could potentially support a modest annual subscription, but if the price is beyond our capacity, no amount of feature discussion fixes that. We are not trying to make a large enterprise purchase.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Understood. Is the concern the total investment, or uncertainty about how contacts are counted?

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Both, and they are getting mixed together. Someone told me we pay for every record in the database. If that means old, inactive records count the same as people we actively market to, that is a problem.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Let me separate those. We should confirm the commercial model and distinguish stored contacts from contacts actively used in marketing. The exact thresholds would be in the proposal, but the concepts are not necessarily the same.

Sophie Koch (Procurement): That distinction is useful. We have around 18,000 historical records, but perhaps 3,000 are currently receiving campaigns. I do not want to delete history just to control a bill.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): You should not have to make that decision blindly. We can review the records, consent status, and activity, then model a few scenarios.

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Scenario one is probably still too expensive. Our finance director has already said the marketing budget cannot absorb a major increase this year. That is an affordability constraint, not confusion.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is clear. If the active audience can fit a workable tier, we can address the contact-model question. If the approved budget remains below that, we should say so early.

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Exactly. I appreciate a straight answer.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Apart from cost, what would the team need day to day?

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Journey building, testing, and speed. The manager wants to create a short event follow-up without asking a technical person for help. She also wants to test subject lines and timing.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is the operator perspective. For an executive or finance stakeholder, the question may be whether campaigns can be tied to enquiries and revenue.

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Yes. Our managing director will ask what marketing contributed. He will not care whether a workflow has six branches if we cannot explain the result.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can demonstrate both: a practical journey builder and reporting that connects campaign activity to CRM outcomes.

Sophie Koch (Procurement): I should mention that our current process is not entirely broken. For a small event, a spreadsheet and a personal follow-up can work. We do not want to automate everything just because automation exists.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): That is a good counterpoint. The value may be highest for repeatable processes where timing and handoff matter, not every communication.

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Correct. We have one annual conference and several small seminars. The annual conference is where mistakes become visible.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Who owns the event data?

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Marketing collects registrations, sales owns the follow-up, and procurement gets involved when a new vendor is selected. The fields are inconsistent. Sometimes attendance is recorded, sometimes only registration.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Then the first workflow should probably use confirmed registration as one signal and attendance as a separate signal where available.

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Yes, although the attendance file arrives two days late. Please do not design a journey that assumes it is real time.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can build a delay into the plan and use a fallback message for people whose status is unknown.

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Another question: can we start with only the active marketing population and keep the historical records elsewhere?

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We can evaluate that, but I want to confirm retention, reporting, and consent implications before recommending a structure.

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Fair. Procurement will need a clear quote, implementation assumptions, renewal terms, and what happens if our contact volume changes.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will include those. I will also show pricing with the active population and with a larger population so the difference is visible.

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Good. Do not call 18,000 contacts “small” in the proposal.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Point taken. What is the decision process?

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Marketing will validate usability. Finance will validate affordability. I will review terms and risk. The managing director wants a short business case, not a product tour.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): We should schedule a working session with marketing and a separate commercial review with you and finance.

Sophie Koch (Procurement): That makes sense. I cannot promise a purchase, but I can promise that a transparent model will get a fair review.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): I will send a recap with the two pricing questions, the event use case, and the reporting example. We will leave advanced journey options until the core process is clear.

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Good. The important thing is that we distinguish “we cannot afford it” from “we do not understand what we are paying for.”

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Agreed. Those require different answers.

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Then send the model, and I will bring the marketing manager next time.

Priya Shah (Account Executive): Will do. Thanks, Sophie.

Sophie Koch (Procurement): Thank you, Priya.
